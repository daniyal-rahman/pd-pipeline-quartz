# Academic Programs with Drug Development Potential

#claude


**Last Updated:** January 2026

**IMPORTANT CAVEAT:** Academic programs are generally earlier stage than SBIR/STTR or MJFF company grants. However, the SPARK NS program specifically focuses on translating academic discoveries into drugs, with a 50% success rate of advancing to licensing or clinical trials.

---

## SPARK NS Translational Research Program (Highest Relevance)

SPARK NS is a non-profit translational research organization founded in 2023, based on the Stanford SPARK model which has achieved a 50% success rate advancing academic projects to licensing and/or clinical trials.

### Program Structure
- **Duration:** 2-year immersive program
- **Funding:** Up to $2M per project (milestone-based)
- **Includes:** Drug development education, 50+ industry mentors, networking

## ASAP (Aligning Science Across Parkinson's) - $290M+ Funded

ASAP is a global research initiative backed by the Sergey Brin Family Foundation, with MJFF as implementation partner. Led by Nobel Laureate Dr. Randy Schekman.

### Funding Overview
- **Total Funded:** 35 teams since 2021
- **Total Investment:** Over $290M
- **Major Grant:** $132M over three years (announced 2023)

### Research Tracks

#### Scientific Track (2025)
- **Focus:** Mechanisms of PD heterogeneity
- **Funding:** Up to $3M/year for 3 years
- **Six focus areas for applications**

#### Technical Track (2025)
- **Focus:** Tool development for emerging targets
- **Funding:** Up to $2M/year for 3 years ($6M total)
- **Goal:** Sustainable tools for validation and therapeutic discovery

### Notable ASAP-Funded Research

#### Duke University - $18M (Two Team Grants)
- Position Duke as national leader in understanding PD origins
- Multiple institution collaboration

#### GP2 (Global Parkinson's Genetics Program)
- Goal: Genotype >150,000 volunteers worldwide
- Understand genetic architecture of PD
- Data shared openly with research community

#### PPMI (Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative) Support
- Significant enrollment expansion
- More diverse participant groups
- Understanding disease development over time

## University Clinical Trials That Could Generate Drug Programs

### UCLA - NEU-411 Phase 2 Trial
- **Target:** LRRK2 pathway
- **Population:** Early PD patients with predicted LRRK2 pathway elevation
- **Approach:** Genotype-guided therapy

### UC Irvine - AAV2-GDNF Phase 2
- **Approach:** Gene therapy (GDNF delivery to putamen)
- **Population:** Moderate Parkinson's disease
- **Design:** Randomized, surgically controlled, double-blinded

### UCLA - ANPD001 Cell Therapy
- **Approach:** Dopamine-producing cell injection
- **Stage:** Safety and tolerability testing

---

## Gene Therapy Programs from Academia/Spinouts

### AskBio - AB-1005 (AAV2-GDNF)
- **Origin:** Academic gene therapy research
- **Stage:** Phase 1b completed (2024)
- **Result:** Met primary endpoint for putamen coverage, safe over 18 months

### MeiraGTx - AAV-GAD
- **Approach:** Reprogramming brain circuits via local GABA production
- **Stage:** Clinical bridging study (MGT-GAD-025) results October 2024
- **Delivery:** Single infusion via minimally invasive procedure

### Prevail Therapeutics - PR001 (AAV9-GBA1)
- **Target:** GBA1 gene replacement
- **Stage:** Phase I/II PROPEL trial
- **Population:** GBA1-mutation carriers with PD

## International Linked Clinical Trials (iLCT)

Created by Cure Parkinson's in partnership with Van Andel Institute.

- **Committee:** 20-30 world-leading PD experts
- **Meeting:** Annual prioritization of disease-modifying treatments
- **Impact:** 30%+ of drugs in 2023 clinical trials had been evaluated by iLCT
- **Now in:** 13th year of operation

---

## ACT-PD Project (UK)

Multi-arm, multi-stage clinical trial platform for disease-modifying PD trials in the UK.

- **Organizer:** Cure Parkinson's
- **Goal:** Accelerate and support disease-modifying trials
- **Model:** Platform trial allowing multiple drugs to be tested efficiently

## Translation Success Metrics

The SPARK model (now SPARK NS for neuroscience) has achieved:
- **50% success rate** advancing to licensing or clinical trials
- Model refined over **two decades** at Stanford

---

## Limitations

1. Academic programs are inherently early-stage
2. Timeline to drug: typically 10+ years from discovery
3. Many programs will fail during translation
4. Specific project details often not publicly disclosed
