## Critical Limitation: Patent != Active Development

**THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT CAVEAT OF THIS RESEARCH**

A patent filing does NOT mean:
- The company is actively developing the technology
- The compound will ever enter clinical trials
- The program hasn't been abandoned
- The assignee has the resources to develop the asset
- The mechanism is scientifically validated

Patents are filed for many reasons:
- Protecting early-stage research that may never advance
- Defensive IP to block competitors
- Licensing opportunities
- Academic publication requirements
- Acquisition value creation

**How to use this data:**
1. Use patents to IDENTIFY potential players, not to confirm pipeline status
2. Cross-reference with ClinicalTrials.gov for active development
3. Check company press releases and SEC filings
4. Look for conference presentations and peer-reviewed publications
5. Verify with industry databases (e.g., Evaluate Pharma, Cortellis)

## Data Extraction Protocol

For each relevant patent, we extracted:
1. **Patent Number** (e.g., WO2024108116A1, US20240246952A1)
2. **Assignee** (company or institution)
3. **Filing Date** and/or **Publication Date**
4. **Mechanism of Action** (from abstract/claims)
5. **Target Indication** (Parkinson's, specific subtypes)
6. **Inventors** (when notable for follow-up)

## Known Limitations of This Research

### Search Limitations
1. Google Patents search may not capture all recent filings (indexing delay)
2. Site-specific searches sometimes fail to return expected results
3. Non-English patents may be underrepresented
4. Provisional applications not always indexed

### Data Quality Issues
1. Assignee information on Google Patents can be outdated (transfers not reflected)
2. Patent status (granted, pending, abandoned) not always current
3. Family patents (same invention in multiple jurisdictions) may be counted separately
4. Priority dates vs. publication dates can be confusing

### Coverage Gaps
1. Recent filings (last 3-6 months) may not yet be published
2. Some companies file under subsidiary names not easily linked to parent
3. Academic patents may not indicate commercial interest
4. Defensive patents may not reflect actual development plans

## Notes on Specific Mechanisms

### LRRK2 Inhibitors
- Most active patent area in 2024
- Key challenge: peripheral toxicity (lung, kidney)
- Companies differentiating on brain penetration and selectivity
- G2019S mutation is most common LRRK2 variant

### Alpha-Synuclein Targeting
- Multiple modalities: antibodies, small molecules, gene therapy
- Phase 3 failures (prasinezumab) have not stopped new filings
- Diagnostic vs. therapeutic patents both active

### GBA/GCase
- Small molecules vs. gene therapy competition
- Targeting broader PD population beyond GBA1 mutation carriers
- Sanofi's venglustat failure is cautionary tale

### Gene Therapy
- AAV vectors dominate
- Delivery (IV vs. intrathecal vs. intranasal) is key differentiator
- Manufacturing and pricing challenges
