# Manifold Bio BBB Shuttles

## Summary

Manifold Bio is a Boston-based platform therapeutics company that combines massively parallel in vivo protein screening (via proprietary mCode barcoding) with AI-guided protein design (the mDesign engine) to discover tissue-targeted biologics. In November 2025, Roche signed a strategic collaboration worth $55M upfront and over $2B in total milestones plus tiered royalties to access Manifold's platform for discovering next-generation BBB shuttles for neurological and neurodegenerative diseases ([Manifold Bio press release, November 2025](https://www.manifold.bio/news/manifold-bio-announces-strategic-collaboration-with-roche-to-develop-multiple-next-generation-brain-shuttles-for-neurological-diseases)). This deal represents Roche seeking to expand beyond its existing BrainShuttle platform (which targets TfR1 and is clinically validated via [[trontinemab]]) by tapping into Manifold's ability to screen tens of thousands of shuttle designs iteratively in vivo and identify novel BBB-crossing molecules that may target different receptors or achieve superior pharmacological properties ([BioSpace, November 2025](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/roche-piles-55m-into-blood-brain-barrier-tech-with-manifold)). Manifold retains full rights to apply its BBB shuttles to non-licensed targets in internally developed and future partnered programs, and has the right to opt-in to co-fund development of one product candidate in exchange for enhanced royalties ([Manifold Bio press release, November 2025](https://www.manifold.bio/news/manifold-bio-announces-strategic-collaboration-with-roche-to-develop-multiple-next-generation-brain-shuttles-for-neurological-diseases)).

## Notes

### Science

#### The mDesign Platform

- **Core innovation — mCodes protein barcoding:** Each candidate therapeutic protein is tagged with a unique inert peptide barcode (an "mCode") that can be detected via high-affinity binding and read out by DNA sequencing. This converts protein biodistribution measurement into a DNA sequencing problem, enabling massively parallel quantification of hundreds to thousands of protein candidates across multiple tissues in a single animal ([Century of Bio — "Barcoded Biologics"](https://centuryofbio.com/p/manifold); [Wyss Institute technology page](https://wyss.harvard.edu/technology/multiplexed-protein-binding-and-barcoding-platform-for-drug-discovery/))
- **Multiplexing scale:** By 2022, Manifold had over 150 mCodes operational. The platform can now test over 100,000 antibody variants in pooled in vivo experiments that would traditionally test single molecules one at a time ([Century of Bio — "Barcoded Biologics"](https://centuryofbio.com/p/manifold))
- **Sensitivity:** The mCode detection system achieves sensitivity equivalent to detecting "a single grain of sugar in over 120 Olympic-size swimming pools" ([Century of Bio — "Barcoded Biologics"](https://centuryofbio.com/p/manifold))
- **Design-build-test-learn cycle:** (1) AI models design thousands of candidate shuttle molecules; (2) variants are synthesized as a combinatorial library; (3) the entire library is administered to a single animal with mCode barcodes enabling parallel tracking; (4) sequencing-based readout identifies which candidates reach the target tissue (e.g., brain); (5) data feeds back to the ML models, which refine predictions for the next design round ([Manifold Bio website](https://www.manifold.bio/); [Nucleate Signal — "Nature is the Master Multitasker"](https://signal.nucleate.xyz/master-multitasker-gleb-manifold/))
- **ML-squared (ML2) — Multiplex Libraries x Machine Learning:** The platform pairs combinatorial variant libraries with machine learning models trained on in vivo readouts to predict fitness landscapes and identify superior designs beyond local sequence neighborhoods. The in vivo data generated by mCodes is uniquely valuable because it captures the complexity of a living organism — pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, off-target distribution, clearance — that cannot be replicated in vitro ([Century of Bio — "Barcoded Biologics"](https://centuryofbio.com/p/manifold))
- **"Virtual organism" aspiration:** CTO Pierce Ogden stated that Manifold is "building the world's first virtual organism model" using the massive in vivo datasets generated by the platform — a predictive model of how biologics behave in the body ([Manifold Bio press release, November 2025](https://www.manifold.bio/news/manifold-bio-announces-strategic-collaboration-with-roche-to-develop-multiple-next-generation-brain-shuttles-for-neurological-diseases))

#### BBB Shuttle Application

- **Approach to BBB shuttles:** Manifold applies its platform to screen tens of thousands of shuttle designs iteratively in mice and non-human primates, measuring which molecules cross the BBB most efficiently while maintaining target specificity. This is a fundamentally different discovery paradigm from traditional shuttle engineering, which typically starts with a known receptor target (e.g., TfR1, IGF1R, CD98hc) and rationally engineers a binding domain ([Century of Bio — "Barcoded Biologics"](https://centuryofbio.com/p/manifold))
- **Potential advantage over receptor-targeted approaches:** Traditional BBB shuttles are limited to known receptor targets. Manifold's unbiased in vivo screening could identify entirely novel transcytosis pathways or shuttle architectures that do not depend on a single receptor — potentially avoiding the safety liabilities of existing approaches (e.g., TfR1-mediated reticulocyte toxicity for [[trontinemab]], or theoretical concerns about IGF1R pathway interference for [[bbb-delivery/grabody-b|Grabody-B]])
- **Roche's stated rationale:** Boris Zaitra, Head of Corporate Business Development at Roche, stated: "Roche has worked in the field of BBB shuttles for over 15 years and has demonstrated the significant impact BBB shuttles can have on improving antibody pharmacology. We are excited about our partnership with Manifold to identify the next generation of highly specific BBB shuttles, applicable across multiple therapeutic modalities" ([Manifold Bio press release, November 2025](https://www.manifold.bio/news/manifold-bio-announces-strategic-collaboration-with-roche-to-develop-multiple-next-generation-brain-shuttles-for-neurological-diseases))
- **Published abstract:** Manifold presented an abstract at a neurology conference titled "Inverting the Drug Discovery Funnel: In Vivo First Screening for mAb Blood-brain Barrier Penetration Yields Molecules with Novel Uptake Properties" (Neurology, 2025), describing their high-throughput in vivo screening methodology for identifying antibodies with novel BBB crossing mechanisms ([Neurology, 2025; DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000206390](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000206390))

### Competitive

**Why Roche partnered externally rather than iterating internally on BrainShuttle:**

- Roche's current BrainShuttle is built around a single receptor target — transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) — and a specific 2+1 bispecific antibody format. While clinically validated ([[trontinemab]] achieved 92% amyloid negativity at 28 weeks), this approach carries inherent limitations: TfR1-mediated reticulocyte toxicity (10-18% anemia in Phase 1b/2a) and receptor saturation constraints on dosing ([BioSpace, November 2025](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/roche-piles-55m-into-blood-brain-barrier-tech-with-manifold))
- Manifold's platform is receptor-agnostic — rather than engineering against a pre-selected receptor, it screens massive variant libraries in vivo and lets the biology reveal which molecules cross the BBB most efficiently. This could yield shuttles targeting entirely novel pathways or achieving superior safety/efficacy profiles compared to TfR1-based approaches
- The deal explicitly covers "multiple therapeutic modalities" — suggesting Roche wants shuttles that can carry different payload types (antibodies, enzymes, ASOs, gene therapies) beyond what the current BrainShuttle format accommodates
- The competitive BBB shuttle landscape is intensifying: Denali's Transport Vehicle (TfR1-based, BLA filed for tividenofusp alfa), ABL Bio's Grabody-B (IGF1R-based, $5.4B in pharma deals), and BioArctic's BrainTransporter (TfR-based, deals with BMS/Eisai/Novartis) all represent alternative platforms. Roche's move to diversify its shuttle portfolio through Manifold is a hedging strategy against the possibility that TfR1 alone is not the optimal receptor for all applications

**Manifold vs. traditional shuttle engineering:**

| Parameter | Manifold mDesign | Traditional Shuttle Engineering |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------------------|
| Starting point | Unbiased variant library | Known receptor target |
| Screening throughput | 1,000s-100,000s variants per experiment | 1-10 variants per animal |
| Receptor dependence | Agnostic — discovers mechanism empirically | Locked to chosen receptor (TfR1, IGF1R, CD98hc) |
| Data type | In vivo biodistribution at scale | In vitro binding + individual in vivo PK |
| AI integration | Generative design from massive in vivo datasets | Structure-based rational design |
| Discovery cycle | Iterative ML-guided rounds | Hypothesis-driven, sequential |

## Analysis

**Analytical estimate — Probability that Manifold-discovered shuttles enter IND-enabling studies within 4 years: ~45%. This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:** Manifold's platform has demonstrated the ability to screen massive variant libraries in vivo and has presented preliminary BBB shuttle data (Neurology 2025 abstract). However, the journey from discovering a lead shuttle to IND-enabling studies requires (1) identifying a lead molecule, (2) pairing it with a therapeutic payload, (3) manufacturing process development for a novel bispecific construct, and (4) IND-enabling toxicology in the context of a novel BBB-crossing mechanism. The Roche collaboration structure (Manifold leads discovery, Roche leads development) should accelerate the transition but also introduces coordination complexity. Upward adjustment: the $55M upfront provides substantial resourcing, and Roche's 15+ years of BrainShuttle experience means the development infrastructure already exists. Downward adjustment: AI-driven drug discovery platforms have historically overpromised on timelines; unbiased screening may yield molecules with unknown mechanisms of BBB crossing that require additional characterization and raise novel safety questions.

**Strategic significance for PD:** The Manifold-Roche deal is relevant to PD for two reasons: (1) the collaboration explicitly covers "neurological and neurodegenerative diseases," which includes PD; (2) if Manifold discovers novel BBB shuttles with superior properties to TfR1-based BrainShuttle, Roche could apply them to PD payloads (prasinezumab, anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies, GCase enzymes, or future targets). The key question is whether Manifold's platform can identify shuttles that avoid the reticulocyte toxicity associated with TfR1 targeting while achieving equivalent or better brain penetration — this would be a meaningful advance for any CNS-targeted PD therapeutic.

**Platform vs. asset risk:** This is a platform deal, not an asset deal. The $2B milestone ceiling reflects Roche's option value on multiple shuttle-payload combinations, but no specific therapeutic candidate has been disclosed. The $55M upfront is modest by big pharma standards (comparable to Roche's $50M upfront for Sangamo's STAC-BBB AAV capsid in August 2024), reflecting the early-stage, speculative nature of the program. The meaningful catalysts are not clinical but preclinical: (1) identification of a lead shuttle with novel BBB-crossing mechanism, (2) demonstration of superior brain exposure compared to BrainShuttle in head-to-head NHP studies, (3) selection of the first Roche therapeutic payload. None of these catalysts have been publicly disclosed as imminent.

**The "AI for biology" validation question:** Manifold's deal with Roche is notable as one of the largest partnerships ($2B total potential) for an AI-native biology platform. It validates the concept that AI-guided massively parallel in vivo screening can generate assets that big pharma values. However, the value remains entirely prospective — no Manifold-discovered molecule has entered clinical development, and the platform's ability to discover truly novel BBB-crossing mechanisms (as opposed to optimizing known approaches) is unproven. The company's Neurology 2025 abstract title ("molecules with novel uptake properties") is suggestive but not yet substantiated in peer-reviewed data.
