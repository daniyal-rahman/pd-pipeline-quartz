# Sironax Brain Delivery Module

## Summary

Sironax's Brain Delivery Module (BDM) is a proprietary blood-brain barrier crossing platform designed to enhance brain delivery of large molecule therapeutics across multiple modalities. In July 2025, Novartis secured an exclusive option to acquire the BDM platform for up to $175M in upfront and near-term payments. The deal structure is notable: Novartis evaluates the platform during an option period and, upon exercise, acquires full global rights — while Sironax retains rights to develop selected therapeutic assets using the platform. The BDM is at lead optimization stage. The specific molecular mechanism (receptor target, antibody format, engineering approach) has not been publicly disclosed, which is the single largest gap in evaluating this platform. What is publicly known: the BDM is designed for large molecule therapeutics and is modality-agnostic (applicable across different drug formats). Sironax was founded in 2017 by Xiaodong Wang (NIBS Beijing, co-founder of BeiGene, National Academy of Sciences member) and Zhiyuan Zhang, has raised over $376M, was named to the 2025 Endpoints 11 list of most promising biotech startups, and has three clinical-stage small molecule programs (SIR2501/SARM1, SIR9900/RIPK1, SIR4156/NAMPT) separate from the BDM platform. For Novartis, the Sironax deal is the second BBB delivery platform acquisition in 2025 — they also signed a $772M option deal with BioArctic for the BrainTransporter (TfR1) platform in August 2025 — suggesting Novartis is building a multi-platform BBB delivery strategy rather than betting on a single mechanism.
