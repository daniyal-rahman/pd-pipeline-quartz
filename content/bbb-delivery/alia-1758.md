# ALIA-1758

## Summary

ALIA-1758 is a bispecific anti-pyroglutamate-amyloid-beta (pGlu-Abeta) antibody built on the MODEL (Modular Delivery) platform, acquired by AbbVie (big pharma, ABBV) through its $1.4B all-cash acquisition of [[companies/aliada-therapeutics|Aliada Therapeutics]] completed December 2024. The Phase 1 single ascending dose trial in healthy volunteers (NCT06406348) commenced May 2024 and completed July 2025. The MODEL platform, originally developed by Janssen scientists and licensed to Aliada at founding in 2021, employs dual targeting of both transferrin receptor (TfR1) and CD98 heavy chain (CD98hc) at the blood-brain barrier -- a fundamentally different architecture from single-receptor shuttles like [[trontinemab|Roche's BrainShuttle]] (TfR1 only) or [[abl301|Grabody-B]] (IGF1R only). The dual-receptor approach is designed to combine TfR1's rapid brain uptake with CD98hc's slower, more prolonged retention, potentially achieving higher sustained brain concentrations than either receptor alone. AbbVie's willingness to pay $1.4B for a Phase 1 asset with $53M in total fundraising is among the strongest acquisition price signals in the BBB delivery space, suggesting internal data supporting differentiated brain penetration. If Phase 1 PK data confirm meaningfully superior brain exposure versus conventional anti-amyloid antibodies, ALIA-1758 could advance rapidly into Phase 2 AD trials; if the dual-receptor approach fails to outperform single-receptor shuttles like [[trontinemab]], the $1.4B premium over platform value becomes difficult to justify.

## Notes

### Science

- **Molecular architecture:** ALIA-1758 is a bispecific antibody construct comprising an anti-amyloid-beta antibody targeting N-terminal truncated Abeta pyroglutamated at position 3 (pGlu3-Abeta or 3pE-Abeta), fused with an optimized single-chain antibody to the transferrin receptor (TfR1) that facilitates BBB transcytosis ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/alia-1758))
- **pGlu-Abeta target biology:** Pyroglutamate-modified Abeta (AbetapE3) is enzymatically generated by glutaminyl cyclase, is abundantly present in AD brain, forms stable and soluble low-molecular weight oligomers, and acts as a seed for amyloid aggregation. This is the same epitope targeted by donanemab (Eli Lilly, approved as Kisunla), which achieved 76.8% amyloid plaque clearance at 18 months in TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 ([Nature, Molecular Psychiatry 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01409-2))
- **Reduced effector function Fc:** The Fc sequence has reduced effector function, which is a deliberate design choice. Once in the brain, the antibody is taken up by resident myeloid cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis through their transferrin receptors, leading to degradation of Abeta via a non-classical phagocytosis pathway -- not through Fc-mediated ADCC/CDC. This mechanism avoids microglial activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and the company has stated it does not anticipate ARIA incidence ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/alia-1758); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/amyloid-remains-a-key-target-in-next-generation-alzheimer-s-treatments))
- **MODEL platform -- dual receptor targeting:** The MODEL (Modular Delivery) platform targets both TfR and CD98hc receptors, which are highly expressed on brain endothelial cells. These are used as independent transcytosis-enabling modules (TEMs) that can be optimized separately and combined to deliver different cargo types including therapeutic antibodies and genetic medicines such as siRNA ([AbbVie press release, Oct 2024](https://news.abbvie.com/2024-10-28-AbbVie-to-Acquire-Aliada-Therapeutics,-Strengthening-Focus-in-Alzheimers-Disease-and-Neuroscience-Pipeline))
- **Complementary kinetics of TfR1 vs CD98hc:** TfR-mediated delivery promotes rapid, high brain uptake with faster catabolism. CD98hc-mediated delivery is slower with more prolonged exposure, colocalizing with AQP4-positive astrocyte processes and endfeet. Dual transport vehicles combining both receptors achieve significantly higher brain concentrations than vehicles targeting either receptor alone ([Cell Reports, Aug 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00809-5); [Nature Communications 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40681-4))
- **Stronger CD98hc affinity yields higher, more sustained brain concentrations** by reducing TfR-mediated neuronal internalization -- modulation of the two affinities shifts brain exposure kinetics and biodistribution, giving the platform a broader design landscape than single-receptor approaches ([Cell Reports, Aug 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00809-5))
- **Foundational publication:** Janssen scientists published the TEM concept in Med (Cell Press) in December 2022, demonstrating significantly enhanced brain exposure and efficient clearance of tau aggregates and HER2+ tumor cells via non-classical phagocytosis, with no inflammatory cytokine release in NHP ([Med 2022; 3(12):860-882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36257298/))
- **Key distinction vs [[trontinemab]]:** Roche's BrainShuttle uses monovalent TfR1 binding only, achieving 4-18x brain AUC enhancement (Kp ~0.5% vs ~0.03% for standard IgG). MODEL's dual TfR1+CD98hc approach theoretically achieves higher sustained concentrations by layering rapid TfR-mediated uptake onto prolonged CD98hc-mediated retention. No head-to-head data exist, but the dual-receptor concept is biologically distinct from any single-receptor shuttle
- **Key distinction vs [[abl301|Grabody-B]]:** ABL Bio's platform uses IGF1R (insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor) as the transcytosis receptor. MODEL uses the same TfR1 receptor as Roche/Denali but adds CD98hc as a second targeting arm -- complementary rather than competing with TfR1
- **Platform versatility beyond ALIA-1758:** MODEL can deliver diverse cargo types. Chiesi Global Rare Diseases licensed the platform (exclusive worldwide license, November 2025) for enzyme replacement therapies in lysosomal storage disorders, with Chiesi funding R&D and commercialization ([Chiesi press release, Nov 2025](https://chiesirarediseases.com/media/co-development-agreement-with-aliada-therapeutics))

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (First-in-Human, Single Ascending Dose)** | NCT06406348 | N=~52 | Healthy male and female volunteers
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single ascending dose (SAD) trial testing IV and SC administration routes
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics
- **Eligibility:** Ages 18-65, BMI 17.5-32, no hepatic/renal/cardiovascular/psychiatric conditions
- **Location:** Worldwide Clinical Trials Early Phase Services, San Antonio, Texas, USA
- **PI:** J. Michael Ryan, M.D. (previously Aliada CMO, now AbbVie)
- **Timeline:** Study commenced May 9, 2024; completed July 15, 2025 ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06406348](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06406348); [TipRanks, 2025](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/abbvie-completes-phase-1-trial-of-alia-1758-key-insights-for-investors))
- **Status:** Completed. No public data disclosure as of February 2026.
- **Interpretation:** Trial completion without early termination is a positive safety signal. The inclusion of both IV and SC dosing cohorts suggests AbbVie is evaluating subcutaneous delivery for potential chronic dosing convenience. Phase 1 data readout and decision on Phase 2 progression likely in 2026.

**Preclinical validation:**
- Janssen internal validation in both rodent models and NHP showed improved brain delivery with TEM modules attached relative to naked antibody. Aliada replicated all Janssen-generated data after licensing ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2024/10/abbvie-adds-another-alzheimers-prospect-with-1-4b-aliada-therapeutics-acquisition/))
- A tau/TfR bispecific antibody using the TEM approach gained widespread entry in NHP brain and reduced tau aggregates without inflammatory cytokine release ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/alia-1758))
- Reduced Abeta40 production demonstrated in mouse models ([Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/alia-1758))

### Financial

Completed December 11, 2024 ([AbbVie press release](https://news.abbvie.com/2024-10-28-AbbVie-to-Acquire-Aliada-Therapeutics,-Strengthening-Focus-in-Alzheimers-Disease-and-Neuroscience-Pipeline))

- **Return multiple:** ~26x on total invested capital ($1.4B / $53M) -- an exceptional VC return for a Phase 1 asset, reflecting platform value beyond ALIA-1758 alone
- **Investors:** JJDC (Johnson & Johnson venture arm), RA Capital Management, Raven (RA Capital incubator) as co-founders and seed co-leads. OrbiMed and Sanofi Ventures participated in seed extension (late 2022)
- **Acquisition context:** AbbVie paid $1.4B for a Phase 1 asset with one clinical-stage program. For comparison, AbbVie acquired Cerevel Therapeutics for $8.7B (Phase 3 assets across multiple indications) and Capsida Biotherapeutics for $665M+ (preclinical AAV capsid platform). The Aliada price-per-stage is among the highest in the BBB delivery space
- **Chiesi license (Nov 2025):** Exclusive worldwide license to apply MODEL platform to enzyme replacement therapies for LSDs, with Chiesi funding R&D/commercialization and Aliada eligible for milestones and royalties -- partial platform monetization post-acquisition ([Chiesi press release](https://chiesirarediseases.com/media/co-development-agreement-with-aliada-therapeutics))
- **Peak sales potential:** If ALIA-1758 matches donanemab's clinical profile with superior brain penetration (potentially lower dose, less ARIA, faster clearance), it could compete for a share of the $10B+ anti-amyloid Alzheimer's market. However, this is highly speculative given Phase 1 status

- **vs [[trontinemab]] (Roche BrainShuttle, Phase 3):** The most clinically advanced BBB shuttle, with 92% amyloid negativity at 28 weeks and <5% ARIA-E in Phase 1b/2a. Uses single TfR1 binding, achieving 4-18x brain AUC enhancement. ALIA-1758 claims the dual TfR1+CD98hc approach achieves higher sustained concentrations, but no head-to-head or equivalent-stage data exist. If trontinemab succeeds in TRONTIER Phase 3, it validates TfR-based transcytosis broadly but also sets a high efficacy/safety bar that ALIA-1758 must beat to justify its later development timeline
- **vs donanemab (Eli Lilly, Approved -- Kisunla):** Targets the same pGlu-Abeta epitope without BBB shuttle technology. Achieved 76.8% amyloid clearance at 18 months but with 24% ARIA-E incidence. ALIA-1758's theoretical advantage: higher brain concentration at lower systemic doses, potentially enabling faster plaque clearance with reduced ARIA. The non-classical phagocytosis mechanism (no Fc effector function) could be the key ARIA-reduction differentiator
- **vs [[dnl422|DNL422/DNL111]] (Denali, IND-enabling/Phase 1):** Denali's Transport Vehicle (TV) platform also targets TfR1, and their 2025 Cell Reports paper demonstrated dual TfR+CD98hc targeting in the academic/publication context. Denali has BLA submitted for tividenofusp alfa (Hunter syndrome) -- the closest any TfR-based platform is to approval. The Denali dual-targeting data directly validates the biological rationale underpinning MODEL
- **vs [[abl301|ABL301/Grabody-B]] (ABL Bio/Sanofi, Phase 1b -- deprioritized):** Uses IGF1R receptor instead of TfR1. Sanofi deprioritized ABL301 in January 2026 before Phase 2. The IGF1R approach has $5.4B in aggregate deal value but the least clinical evidence among BBB shuttles -- the deprioritization is a bearish signal for IGF1R as a transcytosis target
- **Key competitive question:** Does dual-receptor targeting (MODEL) outperform optimized single-receptor targeting (BrainShuttle, Denali TV) enough to justify the added molecular complexity? The Cell Reports 2025 data suggest yes in preclinical models, but human PK data from ALIA-1758 Phase 1 will be the first clinical test of this hypothesis

## Analysis

The $1.4B acquisition price for a Phase 1 asset with $53M in prior funding is the single most important data point for ALIA-1758's evaluation. AbbVie conducted extensive due diligence -- including access to all preclinical data, Phase 1 interim data (the trial was underway at acquisition), and the full MODEL platform IP -- before committing. The 26x return to investors in ~3 years from founding to exit suggests either exceptional platform value, acquisition-market froth, or both. The Chiesi license five months post-acquisition (November 2025) provides independent third-party validation of the MODEL platform's versatility beyond Alzheimer's.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of advancing to Phase 3: 35-40%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for Phase 1 to Phase 3 advancement in CNS: ~30%
- Adjustments upward: $1.4B acquisition signals strong internal data (+5%), dual-receptor mechanism is biologically differentiated (+5%), pGlu-Abeta target validated by donanemab approval (+5%), reduced effector function Fc may lower ARIA risk (+5%), Phase 1 completed without apparent safety signals (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: no public human PK/brain penetration data yet (-8%), BBB shuttle technology unproven in registrational AD trials (-5%), competitive landscape increasingly crowded with trontinemab in Phase 3 (-5%)
- Net: ~35-40%

**Signal analysis:**
- AbbVie's $1.4B all-cash deal for a single Phase 1 asset is not standard big pharma behavior -- it signals conviction in the platform, not just the lead molecule. The MODEL platform's application to siRNA delivery, enzyme replacement therapy, and other biologics makes it a strategic infrastructure play for AbbVie's entire neuroscience portfolio.
- The founding story is instructive: J&J developed the MODEL platform internally but licensed it to a Raven/RA Capital startup rather than advancing it themselves. This is a pattern seen when large pharma has promising technology that doesn't fit internal pipeline priorities. AbbVie then acquired the externally matured version -- a "license out, acquire back" dynamic that J&J has seen repeatedly (e.g., Actelion).
- The non-classical phagocytosis mechanism (clearing Abeta without Fc-mediated inflammation) is a potentially transformative safety differentiator. ARIA is the dominant safety concern for anti-amyloid antibodies -- donanemab's 24% ARIA-E and lecanemab's 13% ARIA-E drive mandatory monitoring, dose titration, and patient exclusion (ApoE4 homozygotes). If ALIA-1758 genuinely achieves near-zero ARIA, it could unlock the ~30% of AD patients currently excluded from anti-amyloid therapy.
- The PD relevance is indirect but real: if MODEL demonstrates superior brain delivery of anti-amyloid antibodies, the same platform could shuttle anti-alpha-synuclein payloads for PD. AbbVie has not announced such a program, but the modular architecture explicitly supports payload swapping. This makes MODEL a platform to watch for [[prasinezumab]]-class molecules that struggle with the ~0.1-0.2% CSF penetration of conventional IgG.
