# Grabody-B

## Summary

Grabody-B is ABL Bio's (Korean biotech, KOSDAQ:298380) IGF1R-based blood-brain barrier shuttle platform — the only clinically validated non-TfR receptor-mediated transcytosis (RMT) system for antibody brain delivery, now licensed to three top-10 pharma companies (Sanofi, GSK, Lilly) for a cumulative $6.4B+ in biobucks. The platform fuses an engineered anti-IGF1R scFv to the Fc region of any therapeutic antibody, creating a bispecific construct that hijacks IGF1R-mediated transcytosis across brain endothelial cells without interfering with endogenous IGF1R signaling ([Yun et al., Cell Reports Methods 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9701613/)). The first clinical application, [[abl301|SAR446159/ABL301]] (anti-alpha-synuclein + Grabody-B), completed Phase 1 in 91 healthy volunteers with clean safety (no SAEs) but was deprioritized by Sanofi in January 2026 before Phase 2 — a product-level setback that has not diminished platform-level deal velocity, with both GSK ($2.75B, April 2025) and Lilly ($2.6B, November 2025) licensing Grabody-B for multi-target neuroscience programs after the Phase 1 readout. The IGF1R shuttle's core advantage over TfR1-based competitors (Roche BrainShuttle, Denali ETV, BioArctic BrainTransporter) is the absence of hematologic effects: TfR1 antibodies deplete reticulocytes at high doses, while IGF1R targeting avoids this liability entirely, enabling chronic dosing regimens necessary for neurodegenerative diseases.

## Notes

### Science

- **Architecture:** Grabody-B is an engineered anti-IGF1R single-chain variable fragment (scFv) fused to the Fc domain of a therapeutic IgG1 antibody. The resulting bispecific has bivalent binding to the therapeutic target and monovalent binding to IGF1R, enabling receptor-mediated transcytosis (RMT) across brain endothelial cells without receptor-mediated dimerization or signaling activation ([Yun et al., Cell Reports Methods 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9701613/))
- **IGF1R binding:** Affinity-matured scFv with KD 1-6 nM to human IGF1R. Critically, Grabody-B is a **non-neutralizing** antibody: it binds IGF1R without activating receptor kinase, without interfering with endogenous IGF-1 ligand binding, and without disrupting the IGF1R signaling axis ([Yun et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9701613/)). This is essential for chronic dosing safety in neurodegenerative diseases
- **Cross-species reactivity:** IGF1R has ~98% amino acid homology across mouse, rat, monkey, and human — far higher than transferrin receptor (~76%). This enables direct translation from preclinical species without species-specific engineering, a significant development advantage ([Yun et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9701613/))
- **Brain penetration (preclinical):** In rats and NHPs, Grabody-B-fused constructs showed significantly higher and sustained brain and CSF concentrations vs. monospecific antibody controls over 1 week following single 30 mg/kg IV dose. Dose-dependent increases at 10, 30, 60 mg/kg showed linear CNS exposure — indicative of non-saturating transcytosis at therapeutic doses ([npj PD, Bhatt et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01117-6))
- **IGF1R aging stability advantage:** IGF1R expression on brain endothelium remains stable with aging, whereas TfR1 expression declines in aged mice, and age-related effects on TfR1-mediated brain delivery are not well characterized in humans ([Alata et al., FASEB Journal 2022](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1096/fj.202101644R)). For neurodegenerative diseases affecting patients aged 60-80+, an aging-stable receptor is a potentially decisive advantage
- **No hematologic effects:** TfR1 is heavily expressed on reticulocytes and erythroid precursors. TfR-targeted bispecifics cause dose-dependent reticulocyte depletion and anemia risk — a class-wide safety concern requiring dose limitations and monitoring ([Age, dose, and binding to TfR on blood cells influence brain delivery, Fluids Barriers CNS 2023](https://fluidsbarrierscns.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12987-023-00435-2)). IGF1R is predominantly expressed on brain vasculature and parenchyma with minimal peripheral hematopoietic expression, enabling higher doses and chronic regimens without hematologic liability
- **Modular platform:** Grabody-B can be fused to any IgG Fc, enabling application across antibodies, bispecific ADCs, and potentially oligonucleotide conjugates. The GSK and Lilly deals explicitly include siRNA and ASO payloads, extending the platform beyond protein therapeutics

### Clinical

**[[abl301|SAR446159/ABL301]] Phase 1 (SAD/MAD)** | NCT05756920 | N=91 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability -> No deaths, no SAEs. Clean safety profile
- **SAD (N=56):** 7.1% treatment-related AEs (TRAEs); 2 of 4 TRAE cases were in placebo group. 69.6% had mild TEAEs, all Grade 1-2
- **MAD (N=35):** 5.7% TRAEs. 57.1% had TEAEs, all Grade 1-2
- **PK:** Dose-dependent systemic exposure confirmed. Human CSF/brain PK data **not publicly disclosed** as of February 2026
- **Status:** Completed (December 2022 - April 2025). Sanofi assumed development responsibility post-Phase 1
- **Interpretation:** Validates Grabody-B platform safety for chronic IV dosing. The absence of published human CSF data is the most informative signal — if penetration data were strong, Sanofi would likely have advanced to Phase 2 rather than deprioritizing. See [[abl301]] for full analysis of the deprioritization

Source: [Korea Biomed Phase 1 report (Sept 2025)](https://www.koreabiomed.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=28799); [npj PD preclinical + clinical design (Aug 2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01117-6)

**SAR446159 Preclinical efficacy (PFF model)** | In vivo (WT mice + haSyn-overexpressing mice)
- Wild-type mice injected with alpha-synuclein PFFs in striatum: SAR446159 treatment **reduced the spread of alpha-synuclein pathology** (phospho-Ser129 staining) and **lessened motor phenotype severity** vs. control ([Bhatt et al., npj PD 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01117-6))
- Human alpha-synuclein overexpressing mice: 4 doses of SAR446159 (Q3D) **modestly reduced aggregated alpha-synuclein** (p-alpha-syn Ser129), whereas the non-brain-shuttled monospecific counterpart (1E4) **failed to reduce aggregates at molar-equivalent doses** ([Bhatt et al., npj PD 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01117-6))
- **Interpretation:** Proof-of-concept that the Grabody-B shuttle provides sufficient brain exposure to achieve pharmacodynamic effects that a standard antibody cannot. The effect was "modest" — not a dramatic clearance — consistent with the challenges of targeting extracellular alpha-synuclein

### Financial

- **ABL Bio (KOSDAQ:298380):** Founded 2016, IPO December 2018 on KOSDAQ. Raised ~$170M in first 3 years. Headquarters in Daejeon, South Korea. Three core platform families: Grabody-T (immuno-oncology, 4-1BB), Grabody-I (immune checkpoint), Grabody-B (BBB penetration) ([ABL Bio corporate](https://www.ablbio.com/en/company/main))
- **Stock impact:** ABL Bio shares dropped **19.5%** on January 30, 2026, following Sanofi's deprioritization announcement. Market interpreted this as milestone payment delay for the Sanofi deal, not as platform invalidation ([Asia Economy, Jan 2026](https://cm.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026013010503347399))
- **Deal velocity:** Three pharma deals in 4 years (2022-2025) for the same underlying platform technology, with escalating deal sizes ($1.06B -> $2.75B -> $2.6B), represents exceptional out-licensing execution. ABL Bio CEO has stated the company can continue licensing to additional partners since each deal covers non-overlapping target exclusivity — a "delivery-as-a-service" business model
- **Upfront payments reality check:** $165M in actual cash received across all three deals vs. $6.4B headline biobucks. Pharma is purchasing cheap options with capped downside (~$55M/partner), preserving upside worth billions if BBB delivery becomes an enabling technology for CNS biologics. The probability of the full $6.4B materializing is well below 50% ([synthesis-3-bbb-platforms](synthesis-3-bbb-platforms) analysis)
- **Lilly equity investment:** $15M for 175,079 new shares at 125,900 KRW/share gives Lilly a minority stake — strategic alignment signal, not financial control ([ABL Bio / Lilly equity announcement](https://www.ablbio.com/en/company/news_view/895?keyword=))
- **Comparable platform licensing:** Grabody-B's cumulative deal value ($6.4B) from 3 pharma partners exceeds BioArctic's BrainTransporter (~$2B+ across BMS, Eisai, Novartis). This makes Grabody-B the highest-valued BBB shuttle platform by deal metrics, despite having the least clinical validation in the class — a central paradox

- **Roche BrainShuttle (TfR1):** The clinical leader. Trontinemab (gantenerumab + BrainShuttle) achieved 91% amyloid plaque clearance in 28 weeks with <5% ARIA-E in Alzheimer's patients — moving to Phase 3 in 2025 ([Roche, July 2025](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-07-28)). Validates the BBB shuttle concept in humans, but TfR1 carries reticulocyte depletion risk and monovalent design constraints. Grabody-B's IGF1R shuttle avoids hematologic toxicity but has no comparable human efficacy data
- **Denali ETV (TfR1/CD98hc):** Closest to regulatory approval. Tividenofusp alfa (enzyme replacement for Hunter syndrome) BLA submitted, PDUFA April 2026. Validates RMT-based brain delivery for enzyme replacement but in a lysosomal storage disorder, not neurodegeneration. [[dnl422]] and [[dnl111]] use the same transport vehicle technology for PD-relevant targets
- **BioArctic BrainTransporter (TfR):** Three independent pharma licensees (BMS, Eisai, Novartis) validate the platform approach. Less public clinical data than Roche but strong partnership validation
- **IGF1R vs. TfR1 key differentiators:** (1) No hematologic effects for IGF1R vs. reticulocyte depletion for TfR1; (2) 98% cross-species homology for IGF1R vs. ~76% for TfR enabling better preclinical translation; (3) IGF1R expression is stable with aging vs. uncertain TfR1 trajectory in elderly; (4) TfR1 has human clinical proof-of-concept (trontinemab) while IGF1R does not yet
- **The competitive paradox:** Grabody-B has the most pharma deal value ($6.4B) but the least clinical validation of any major BBB shuttle. The inverse relationship between deal value and clinical evidence is the central tension in this platform's thesis

**PD-specific competitive context:**
- [[abl301|SAR446159]] is the **only brain-shuttled anti-alpha-synuclein antibody in clinical trials** — if the alpha-synuclein antibody modality is validated by [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3, SAR446159 has a differentiated next-gen positioning. If [[prasinezumab]] fails, no shuttle technology saves the payload
- The broader competitive threat to antibody-based alpha-synuclein strategies comes from production-inhibition approaches: [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (Arrowhead/Novartis siRNA, $2.2B deal) and Ionis/Biogen ASOs. Intriguingly, the GSK and Lilly Grabody-B deals explicitly include siRNA/ASO payloads — suggesting pharma may use the IGF1R shuttle to deliver nucleic acid therapeutics rather than antibodies

## Analysis

The Grabody-B platform embodies the sharpest version of a recurring pattern in CNS drug development: the delivery technology is more validated by pharma investment than any individual therapeutic payload it carries. Three top-10 pharma companies paying $6.4B in cumulative biobucks for rights to the same IGF1R-based shuttle is extraordinary external validation — yet the first clinical product (SAR446159, anti-alpha-synuclein for PD) was deprioritized before generating any human efficacy data. This is not contradictory: GSK and Lilly are licensing the platform for future payloads (LRRK2, GCase, neuroinflammation targets, oligonucleotides), not for alpha-synuclein. They may have reviewed Phase 1 data, concluded the shuttle works mechanistically, and recognized that the first payload bet on alpha-synuclein was a target problem rather than a delivery problem.

The IGF1R receptor biology offers genuine advantages over TfR1 that justify the differentiated deal interest. The absence of hematologic effects is not theoretical — TfR1-targeting antibodies cause measurable reticulocyte depletion in preclinical and clinical settings, forcing dose capping and monitoring protocols that limit therapeutic windows for chronic neurodegenerative indications. IGF1R sidesteps this entirely. The 98% cross-species homology enables direct translation from rodent and NHP studies without species-specific re-engineering, which de-risks the preclinical-to-clinical transition. And the aging-stability argument — that IGF1R expression on brain endothelium is maintained in elderly patients while TfR1 may decline — is particularly relevant for a Parkinson's population with median age at diagnosis of 60-65.

**Analytical estimate — Grabody-B platform probability of generating at least one approved CNS product across all partners by 2035: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for any individual biologic CNS program reaching approval from Phase 1: ~10%
- Adjustments upward: three independent pharma partners running multiple programs in parallel creates portfolio diversification (+15%); Roche BrainShuttle Phase 2a data validates the BBB shuttle concept in humans (+10%); clean Phase 1 safety profile with Grabody-B enables chronic dosing (+5%); platform's modality-agnostic architecture (antibodies, siRNA, ASOs) broadens the opportunity set (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: no human CSF PK data publicly disclosed despite Phase 1 completion (-10%); first clinical product deprioritized (-5%); IGF1R shuttle has less human clinical evidence than TfR1 competitors (-5%)
- Net: ~40-50% across all partner programs combined; any single program remains in the 10-15% range

**Signal analysis:** The most informative datapoint is the timing of the GSK and Lilly deals relative to Phase 1 completion. GSK signed in April 2025 — after Phase 1 was well underway — and Lilly signed in November 2025, after Phase 1 completed. Both would have had access to at least interim safety and PK data. The fact that they still committed $5.35B in combined biobucks suggests the shuttle mechanism showed sufficient proof-of-principle to justify platform bets, even as the alpha-synuclein payload looked increasingly uncertain. The alternative explanation — competitive herding, where each successive deal validated the previous one and created FOMO-driven urgency — cannot be ruled out but is less parsimonious given the technical due diligence these organizations conduct.

The decision tree for the platform is more favorable than for any individual product: if [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 succeeds (2027-2028), it validates extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance and [[abl301|SAR446159]] becomes a differentiated next-gen candidate with BBB-enhanced delivery. If [[prasinezumab]] fails, the alpha-synuclein antibody payload is dead but Grabody-B pivots to other payloads — GSK can apply it to LRRK2, GCase, or neuroinflammation targets; Lilly can use it for enzyme replacement or nucleic acid delivery. The platform survives either scenario; the first product does not.
