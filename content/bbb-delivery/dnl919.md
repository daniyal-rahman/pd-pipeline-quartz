# DNL919 (ATV:TREM2) -- Denali Therapeutics

DNL919 was a brain-penetrant TREM2 agonist antibody engineered with Denali Therapeutics' proprietary Antibody Transport Vehicle (ATV) platform, designed to cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) via monovalent binding to transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1). Developed in partnership with Takeda, DNL919 aimed to activate microglial function in Alzheimer's disease patients. The program was discontinued in August 2023 after Phase 1 data revealed a narrow therapeutic window: moderate, reversible hematologic effects emerged at the highest dose tested, despite robust target engagement at lower doses. DNL919 represents a critical case study in TfR1-mediated BBB shuttle safety and the challenges of TREM2 agonism.

---

## Mechanism of Action

### TREM2 Biology

TREM2 (triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2) is a transmembrane receptor of the immunoglobulin superfamily expressed primarily on microglia in the CNS. Upon ligand binding (lipids, apoptotic cells, amyloid-beta aggregates), TREM2 activates downstream DAP12/SYK signaling, modulating phagocytosis, inflammatory responses, and metabolic adaptation ([Shi et al., 2025, ASN Neuro](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10738584241254118); [Nature Reviews Neurology, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41582-018-0072-1)).

Loss-of-function variants in TREM2 (e.g., R47H) are among the strongest genetic risk factors for late-onset Alzheimer's disease, conferring a 2-4x increased risk comparable to carrying one APOE4 allele. TREM2 mutations have also been linked to frontotemporal dementia, Parkinson's disease, and Nasu-Hakola disease ([MDPI, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/15/7057); [Nature Cell Death Discovery, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41420-025-02538-1)).

The therapeutic rationale: agonizing TREM2 should restore protective microglial functions -- promoting amyloid-beta phagocytosis, supporting microglial survival, enhancing lipid metabolism, and shifting microglia toward a disease-associated responsive state capable of clearing pathological debris.

### ATV Platform Engineering

DNL919 used Denali's Antibody Transport Vehicle (ATV) format: a bispecific antibody with one arm binding the therapeutic target (TREM2) and an engineered Fc domain containing a monovalent TfR1-binding site. Key design features:

- **Monovalent TfR1 engagement**: One CH3 domain of the Fc is engineered to bind the apical domain of human TfR1, while the other CH3 remains unmodified. This asymmetric design uses knob-into-hole (KiH) technology to enforce monovalent binding, which is critical for efficient transcytosis without disrupting TfR1 intracellular trafficking ([Alzforum, 2020](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/molecular-transport-vehicle-shuttles-therapies-brain)).
- **Native IgG structure**: The TV was engineered using directed evolution to bind the apical domain of human TfR without amino acid insertions, deletions, or unnatural appendages, maintaining native immunoglobulin stability ([PubMed 32461332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32461332/)).
- **BBB transcytosis**: The antibody binds abundant transferrin receptors on BBB endothelial cells, facilitating active transport into brain parenchyma ([Alzforum DNL919](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/dnl919)).

### Preclinical Validation

A preclinical study published in *Nature Neuroscience* (January 2023) demonstrated that ATV:TREM2, upon peripheral delivery in mice, showed improved brain biodistribution and enhanced TREM2 signaling compared to a standard (non-ATV) anti-TREM2 antibody. In human iPSC-derived microglia, ATV:TREM2 induced proliferation and improved mitochondrial metabolism. The mouse surrogate promoted TREM2 receptor signaling, increased microglial survival and amyloid-beta phagocytosis, and enhanced plaque clearance in a mouse amyloidosis model ([PubMed 36635496](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36635496/); [MedicalXpress, 2023](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2023-01-trem2-antibody-vehicle-boost-metabolism.html)).

## Phase 1 Clinical Data

### Study Design

- **Trial**: Phase 1 single ascending dose (SAD) study
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**: NCT05450549
- **Location**: Netherlands (initiated to proceed outside US FDA clinical hold)
- **Population**: 80 healthy volunteers
- **Sponsor**: Denali Therapeutics / Takeda
- **Objectives**: Safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and target engagement

### Efficacy Signals (Encouraging)

At therapeutically relevant doses, DNL919 demonstrated:

- **Robust target engagement**: Confirmed TREM2 activation in humans consistent with preclinical predictions ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))
- **CSF biomarker changes**: Demonstrated changes in cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers, confirming brain penetration and CNS pharmacodynamic activity ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))
- **Microglial biomarker modulation**: Changes in CSF1R, SPP1, IL1RA, IP10, MIP1b, and MCP-1 -- consistent with induction of a "responsive microglial cell state" ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))
- **No serious adverse events**: No SAEs or severe treatment-emergent adverse events at lower doses

### Safety Signals (Dose-Limiting)

- **Moderate, reversible hematologic effects** observed at the highest dose tested ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))
- The specific hematologic finding was **anemia**, consistent with TfR1-mediated reticulocyte depletion ([Alzforum DNL919](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/dnl919); [BioSpace, Aug 2023](https://www.biospace.com/takeda-denali-scrap-phase-i-alzheimer-s-candidate-data-shows-narrow-therapeutic-window))
- Effects were **reversible** but created a **narrow therapeutic window**: the dose needed for full therapeutic benefit overlapped with the dose causing hematologic toxicity
- Specific dose levels (mg/kg) and detailed hematologic parameters (reticulocyte counts, hemoglobin changes) were not publicly disclosed

### Discontinuation Rationale

Denali cited two factors in the decision to discontinue:

1. **Narrow therapeutic window**: Hematologic effects at the highest dose tested limited the achievable therapeutic exposure in Alzheimer's patients ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))
2. **Evolving AD treatment landscape**: The approval of anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) shifted the competitive landscape, making understanding of combination therapy important ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html))

## Lessons Learned for BBB Shuttle Safety

### The Core TfR1 Safety Problem

TfR1 is not exclusively expressed on BBB endothelial cells. It is also highly expressed on **reticulocytes** (immature red blood cells) that rely on TfR1-mediated iron uptake for hemoglobin synthesis. Any anti-TfR1 antibody risks depleting reticulocytes through several mechanisms:

1. **Fc-mediated effector function**: The Fc domain can activate macrophages/monocytes via Fcgamma receptors, triggering antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) against TfR1-expressing reticulocytes ([Alzforum, 2013](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/can-transferrin-antibodies-safely-open-blood-brain-barrier))
2. **Complement activation**: A secondary mechanism causing longer-term reticulocyte loss, though this appears partly species-specific ([Alzforum, 2013](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/can-transferrin-antibodies-safely-open-blood-brain-barrier))
3. **Receptor saturation**: At high doses, extensive TfR1 occupancy can impair normal iron transport and reticulocyte maturation ([mAbs, 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19420862.2025.2592422))

In preclinical studies, anti-TfR antibodies caused reticulocyte levels to crash by as much as 90% within 24 hours of administration, recovering in approximately one week ([Alzforum, 2013](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/can-transferrin-antibodies-safely-open-blood-brain-barrier)).

### Engineering Mitigations and Their Limits

Denali and others have developed multiple strategies to mitigate TfR1-related hematologic toxicity:

| Strategy | Rationale | DNL919 Status |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Monovalent TfR1 binding** | Avoids receptor crosslinking, preserves TfR1 trafficking | Implemented |
| **Fc effector function attenuation** | Prevents ADCC against reticulocytes | Implemented (LALA mutations) |
| **Lowered TfR1 affinity** | Reduces peripheral reticulocyte engagement | Partially implemented |
| **Dose optimization** | Stay below reticulocyte depletion threshold | Failed -- therapeutic dose overlapped with toxic dose |

DNL919 employed both monovalent binding and Fc effector attenuation (ATVcisLALA format), yet still exhibited dose-limiting hematology at the highest dose tested. This indicates that **even with current engineering mitigations, TfR1-based delivery has an inherent dose ceiling** for antibody therapeutics requiring high brain exposure.

### Critical Distinction: Platform vs. Compound-Specific

Denali explicitly stated that "the Phase 1 safety findings are believed to be specific to properties of DNL919 and TREM2 biology" rather than the ATV platform itself ([GlobeNewsWire, Aug 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/08/2721096/0/en/Denali-Therapeutics-Reports-Second-Quarter-2023-Financial-Results-and-Business-Highlights.html)). This framing suggests that TREM2 agonism may require particularly high brain exposure (and thus high systemic dosing) compared to other targets, making the therapeutic window problem target-specific rather than a universal platform limitation.

However, the broader lesson remains: **any TfR1-shuttled therapeutic requiring high-dose chronic administration will face this same hematologic ceiling**. The margin of safety depends on how much brain exposure the target requires relative to the dose that triggers reticulocyte toxicity.

## Competitive Landscape: TREM2-Targeting Programs

The TREM2 field has suffered multiple setbacks, raising questions about whether TREM2 agonism is a viable therapeutic strategy:

| Program | Developer | Modality | Status | Key Findings |
|---------|-----------|----------|--------|--------------|
| **DNL919** | Denali / Takeda | ATV-enabled antibody (BBB-penetrant) | Discontinued (Aug 2023) | Narrow therapeutic window; hematologic toxicity at high dose |
| **AL002** | Alector / AbbVie | Conventional antibody (no BBB shuttle) | Phase 2 failed (Nov 2024) | INVOKE-2: failed all endpoints (CDR-SB primary, all secondary); ARIA observed in APOE4 homozygotes; LTE discontinued ([Alector, Nov 2024](https://investors.alector.com/news-releases/news-release-details/alector-announces-results-al002-invoke-2-phase-2-trial)) |
| **VGL101** (iluzanebart) | Vigil Neuroscience (ex-Amgen) | Conventional antibody | Returned to Amgen; not part of Sanofi acquisition | Tested in ALSP (rare neurodegeneration), not AD |
| **VG-3927** | Vigil / Sanofi | Oral small molecule TREM2 agonist (molecular glue) | Preclinical (acquired by Sanofi, Aug 2025, $470M) | Brain-penetrant; potentiates TREM2 signaling in response to natural damage ligands ([Sanofi/Vigil, 2025](https://www.eu-startups.com/2025/08/paris-based-sanofi-completes-the-acquisition-of-vigil-neuroscience-inc-to-add-to-alzheimers-treatment-pipeline/)) |
| **VHB937** | Novartis | Antibody (IgSF domain binder) | Preclinical | Binds ligand-binding domain; delays shedding without triggering internalization |

The failure of both DNL919 (safety-limited) and AL002 (efficacy-limited) raises a fundamental question: does TREM2 agonism require BBB-penetrant dosing to work, and if so, can any TfR1 shuttle deliver enough antibody safely? Sanofi's acquisition of Vigil's oral small molecule TREM2 agonist (VG-3927) represents a potential end-run around both the brain penetration and hematologic toxicity problems.

## Key Takeaways

1. **TfR1 shuttle dose ceiling is real**: Even with monovalent binding and Fc effector attenuation, DNL919 hit hematologic toxicity at the highest dose. This is an inherent limitation for high-dose TfR1-shuttled antibodies.

2. **Brain penetration proof-of-concept validated**: DNL919 demonstrated robust CSF biomarker changes and microglial target engagement in humans, confirming that the ATV platform does deliver functional antibody across the BBB.

3. **TREM2 may demand too much exposure**: The narrow therapeutic window may reflect that meaningful TREM2 agonism in humans requires doses that saturate peripheral TfR1 on reticulocytes. Lower-exposure targets may fare better with the ATV format.

4. **The TREM2 field is struggling**: Both DNL919 (safety) and AL002 (efficacy) have failed. The field is pivoting toward brain-penetrant small molecules (VG-3927) or back to preclinical engineering.

5. **Regulatory signal preceded clinical problems**: The FDA clinical hold in January 2022 (citing preclinical toxicology concerns) foreshadowed the clinical hematologic findings, suggesting the agency identified TfR1-related risk early.

6. **Strategic context matters**: The approval of lecanemab and donanemab changed the AD landscape, making standalone TREM2 agonism less attractive relative to combination approaches.
