## 1. Executive Summary

BBB delivery is the infrastructure layer for next-generation CNS biologics, and as of February 2026, the field has crossed from theoretical to clinically validated -- but no BBB-shuttled PD therapeutic has generated efficacy data in patients. The 15 assets in this folder span six approach categories, four receptor targets, and stages from discovery to approved. [[pabinafusp-alfa]] (JCR, approved Japan 2021) and [[tividenofusp-alfa]] (Denali, BLA filed, PDUFA April 5, 2026) prove TfR1-mediated transcytosis works for enzyme replacement. [[trontinemab]] (Roche, Phase 3) demonstrates that BBB shuttles can clear amyloid with <5% ARIA-E at one-third the dose of conventional antibodies. Aggregate deal value across the 15 assets exceeds **$25B in biobucks** from at least 12 big pharma licensees, with **~$2B+ in actual upfront payments received**. The nearest PD proof points are the Sunnybrook FUS+Cerezyme Phase I/II ([[exablate-neuro]]), Roche's preclinical BrainShuttle-anti-alpha-synuclein data ([[trontinemab]]), and Neurocrine/Voyager's GBA1 gene therapy IND ([[vcap-102]]). The central tension: pharma is paying billions for BBB delivery platforms, but the translation to PD clinical efficacy remains 2-5 years away. Everything hinges on whether improved brain exposure converts to measurable disease modification in alpha-synuclein, GBA1, or LRRK2 biology.

## 3. Receptor Target Analysis

### TfR1: The Incumbent

TfR1 (transferrin receptor 1) dominates the field with 6 of 8 antibody shuttle programs. Clinical proof: [[pabinafusp-alfa]] (approved, 64% CSF HS reduction, 5-year durability), [[tividenofusp-alfa]] (91% CSF HS reduction at 24 weeks), [[trontinemab]] (92% amyloid negativity at 28 weeks). The receptor is well-characterized, highly expressed on BBB endothelium, and the transcytosis mechanism is established. Key engineering insight: monovalent, moderate-affinity TfR1 binding (Denali, Roche) outperforms bivalent high-affinity binding (JCR) for brain penetration, because monovalent binding allows "bind-and-release" transcytosis instead of lysosomal trapping.

**The reticulocyte problem is real but manageable.** TfR1 is abundantly expressed on erythroid precursors. [[dnl919]] (Denali/Takeda) was discontinued because the highest dose caused moderate, reversible anemia -- the dose needed for TREM2 agonism overlapped with the hematologic toxicity threshold. [[trontinemab]] reports 10-18% mild anemia in Phase 1b/2a, all reversible. [[pabinafusp-alfa]] reports anemia as a TEAE but not dose-limiting over 5 years. BioArctic's [[ban2803|BrainTransporter]] explicitly claims no reticulocyte effects (70x brain exposure, PEGS 2024). Denali's Fc engineering (LALA mutations, effector silencing) and Roche's cross-Fab steric masking both mitigate but do not eliminate the risk. **Conclusion: TfR1 shuttles work clinically, and the reticulocyte issue constrains dosing but does not kill the approach.** For PD, where chronic dosing over years is required, the margin between therapeutic dose and hematologic toxicity is the rate-limiting safety parameter.

### IGF1R: The Challenger

[[grabody-b]] (ABL Bio) is the only clinically tested non-TfR1 shuttle. IGF1R's advantages are structural: (1) no hematologic effects because IGF1R is minimally expressed on reticulocytes; (2) 98% cross-species homology (vs. ~76% for TfR1), enabling direct preclinical translation; (3) stable expression with aging (TfR1 may decline in elderly). The platform has attracted $6.4B in cumulative biobucks from Sanofi, GSK, and Lilly -- the highest deal value of any BBB shuttle.

**The weakness: no human efficacy data.** Phase 1 of SAR446159 (anti-alpha-synuclein + Grabody-B) showed clean safety in 91 healthy volunteers but CSF penetration data have not been disclosed. Sanofi deprioritized the program in January 2026 before Phase 2. The GSK and Lilly deals signed after Phase 1 completion suggest the shuttle mechanism showed proof-of-principle, but the absence of published human CSF PK is the most informative negative signal.

### TfR1 + CD98hc: The Dual-Receptor Bet

[[alia-1758]] (AbbVie/ex-Aliada) uses the MODEL platform to target both TfR1 and CD98hc simultaneously. TfR1 drives rapid uptake; CD98hc provides slower, more sustained retention. Denali's August 2025 Cell Reports paper validates this dual-targeting concept: combined vehicles achieve significantly higher brain concentrations than either receptor alone. AbbVie paid $1.4B (all-cash acquisition) for a Phase 1 asset -- the highest price-per-stage in the shuttle space. Phase 1 completed July 2025; no data disclosed.

### ALPL: The Novel Receptor

[[vcap-102]] (Voyager) uses alkaline phosphatase (ALPL) on brain endothelium -- the only non-TfR, non-IGF1R molecularly characterized receptor in the field. Species conservation is >92% across mammals (vs. the PHP.eB/LY6A disaster where the mouse receptor was absent in primates). pH-dependent binding (KD ~20 nM at 7.4, rapid dissociation at 5.5) is biochemically consistent with efficient transcytosis. Second-generation capsids reach 98% substantia nigra DA neuron transduction at clinically relevant doses in NHP. **This is the most PD-relevant preclinical transduction metric from any platform.**

### Unknown/Novel

[[stac-bbb]] (Sangamo), [[sironax]] (BDM), and [[manifold-bio]] have undisclosed or uncharacterized receptor mechanisms. Manifold's AI platform is receptor-agnostic by design -- screening massive variant libraries in vivo to discover novel BBB-crossing molecules empirically. Sironax's BDM is described as "differentiated" (implying non-TfR1). These represent the next wave beyond the TfR1/IGF1R duopoly, but none have published preclinical efficacy data.

## 5. PD Translation Gap

**The central fact: no BBB-shuttled PD therapeutic has generated clinical efficacy data in PD patients.** The entire BBB delivery thesis for PD rests on an assumption -- that improving brain penetration of biologics from ~0.1% to 1-10%+ will convert marginal preclinical signals into clinical disease modification. This assumption is reasonable but unproven.

### The Logical Chain

For BBB delivery to matter for PD, the following must all be true:

1. **Brain penetration is the bottleneck** -- current biologics fail in PD because they cannot reach therapeutic concentrations in brain, not because the target biology is wrong
2. **A BBB shuttle platform works in humans** -- already partially validated by [[pabinafusp-alfa]], [[tividenofusp-alfa]], and [[trontinemab]], but only for non-PD indications
3. **The specific PD target responds to higher brain exposure** -- alpha-synuclein clearance, GCase restoration, or SNCA silencing produces measurable neuroprotection when drug concentrations increase 10-100x in brain
4. **Chronic dosing is safe** -- PD patients need years of treatment; the reticulocyte risk (TfR1), manufacturing complexity (AAV), and procedural burden (FUS) must be manageable over that timeframe

If any link in this chain breaks, the BBB delivery thesis for PD collapses -- but importantly, different links break for different modalities. AAV gene therapy ([[vcap-102]], [[stac-bbb]]) is a one-shot approach that sidesteps chronic dosing but faces immunogenicity risks. FUS ([[exablate-neuro]], [[sonocloud-9]]) is drug-agnostic but adds procedural burden. Antibody shuttles ([[grabody-b]], [[trontinemab]]) enable chronic dosing but face receptor-specific safety ceilings.

## 7. Key Risks

### Reticulocyte Toxicity (TfR1 class-wide)

The single most important cross-cutting safety risk. TfR1 is expressed on erythroid precursors; engagement causes dose-dependent reticulocyte depletion. [[dnl919]] was killed by it. [[trontinemab]] shows 10-18% mild anemia. [[pabinafusp-alfa]] has 5-year pediatric safety data but the MPS II population is young and small. **For PD patients (aged 60-80, many with comorbid anemia of chronic disease), even "mild" additional anemia may be clinically significant.** The field has developed engineering mitigations (monovalent binding, Fc silencing, affinity tuning), but none completely eliminate the risk. This is the rate-limiting parameter for TfR1 shuttle dose selection in PD.

### Receptor Saturation

All receptor-mediated transcytosis approaches face a fundamental ceiling: the number of receptors on brain endothelium limits transport capacity. TfR1 has ~100,000 copies per endothelial cell, but if multiple TfR1-targeted therapeutics are co-administered (or if endogenous transferrin competes), brain delivery efficiency drops. For PD combination regimens (e.g., anti-synuclein + GCase enzyme), receptor saturation becomes a practical constraint. IGF1R and ALPL face the same theoretical limitation, though clinical data are lacking.

### Manufacturing Complexity

Different modalities carry different manufacturing challenges:
- **Bispecific antibodies** ([[trontinemab]], [[grabody-b]], [[alia-1758]]): More complex than standard mAbs; require asymmetric chain pairing, lower yields, additional quality control. Manageable with current platform technology.
- **AAV capsids** ([[vcap-102]], [[stac-bbb]]): Notoriously difficult to manufacture at scale. Dose requirements for IV CNS delivery (1e13-3e13 vg/kg) are enormous. COGS are a major barrier to commercial viability outside ultra-rare diseases.
- **FUS devices** ([[exablate-neuro]], [[sonocloud-9]]): Capital equipment + disposable microbubbles + MRI time (Exablate) or surgical implantation (SonoCloud). Scalability is procedure-limited, not manufacturing-limited.
- **LNPs** ([[mk16-blnp]]): Potentially the most scalable, leveraging COVID vaccine manufacturing infrastructure. But no NHP data and academic-stage.
- **Exosomes** ([[ab126]]): Field-wide manufacturing challenge. GMP-grade, reproducible exosome production at clinical scale remains unproven.

### Regulatory Path Uncertainty

No BBB-shuttled PD drug has been filed for regulatory approval. The surrogate endpoint question is critical: will FDA accept CSF alpha-synuclein, GCase activity, or SNCA mRNA reduction as accelerated approval surrogates for PD, as it accepted CSF HS for Hunter syndrome? Tividenofusp's approval would set a precedent for CSF biomarker-based accelerated approval of BBB-shuttle biologics, but the translation from monogenic lysosomal storage disease to polygenic neurodegeneration is not automatic.

### Competitive Dynamics

The field is paradoxically both overcrowded and underpopulated. Overcrowded at the platform level: 8+ antibody shuttles, 3+ AAV capsids, 2 ultrasound devices, all competing for pharma partnerships. Underpopulated at the PD product level: only SAR446159 has entered clinical trials in PD (and was deprioritized). If multiple platforms generate positive human data simultaneously, the market fragments and no single approach achieves the scale needed for commercial viability. If none generate PD data, the field remains in a holding pattern.

### Going-Concern Risk (Company-Specific)

[[stac-bbb]] (Sangamo) has $21M cash with ~$35M quarterly burn and a departing CFO. The platform's 700x NHP transduction claim is best-in-class, but the company may not survive to see its own capsid enter the clinic. The IP is increasingly captured by partners (Roche, Lilly, Astellas) rather than Sangamo shareholders. Carthera ([[sonocloud-9]]) has EUR 42M -- thin for a 560-patient Phase 3 across 40 international sites. Both companies likely need additional financing or partnerships before their pivotal programs complete.
