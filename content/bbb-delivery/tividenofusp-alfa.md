# Tividenofusp Alfa (ETV:IDS)

## Summary

Tividenofusp alfa (DNL310) is an intravenous, brain-penetrant enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) composed of the iduronate-2-sulfatase (IDS) enzyme fused to Denali Therapeutics' proprietary Enzyme Transport Vehicle (ETV) platform. It binds the transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) monovalently using an engineered Fc domain, enabling receptor-mediated transcytosis across the blood-brain barrier (BBB). The drug addresses both the peripheral and neurological manifestations of Hunter syndrome (mucopolysaccharidosis type II, MPS II), a rare X-linked lysosomal storage disease affecting approximately 1 in 100,000-170,000 male births. Conventional ERT (idursulfase/Elaprase) does not cross the BBB and therefore fails to treat the CNS disease that affects roughly two-thirds of patients.

A BLA was filed under the FDA's accelerated approval pathway in Q1-Q2 2025, accepted with Priority Review in July 2025, and carries a PDUFA target date of **April 5, 2026**. If approved, tividenofusp alfa would be the **first BBB-shuttle biologic approved in the United States** and would validate Denali's entire Transport Vehicle platform -- the same platform powering [[dnl422]] (OTV:SNCA for Parkinson's), [[dnl111]] (ETV:GCase for PD/Gaucher), and [[biib122]] (LRRK2 inhibitor developed with the Biogen collaboration that also includes TV-enabled programs).

## Notes

### Science

**Transport Vehicle (TV) Platform Architecture**

Denali's TV platform uses Fc engineering (directed evolution of the CH3 domain) to create TfR1-binding domains without amino acid insertions, deletions, or unnatural appendages. The key architectural insight is that **monovalent, moderate-affinity TfR1 binding** is superior to bivalent, high-affinity approaches for brain delivery. Monovalent binding avoids interference with intracellular TfR trafficking and prevents receptor crosslinking that can cause sequestration at the endothelium rather than transcytosis into the parenchyma. Denali uses knob-into-hole (KiH) Fc technology to ensure only one arm of the molecule binds TfR1, leaving both Fab arms free for therapeutic payload or, in the case of ETV, the enzyme cargo (Source: Ullman et al., J Exp Med 2022; PMID 35226042).

**ETV:IDS Specific Design**

The IDS enzyme is fused directly to the engineered Fc domain. After transcytosis across the BBB, ETV:IDS is taken up by parenchymal cells via the mannose-6-phosphate receptor pathway for delivery to lysosomes, where IDS degrades accumulated heparan sulfate (HS) and dermatan sulfate. This dual delivery -- to the brain via TfR1, into cells via M6P receptor -- enables both CNS substrate reduction and peripheral clearance with a single IV infusion (Source: Denali R&D Day, October 2020; Ullman et al. 2022).

**Platform Variants**

Denali has diversified its TV technology into multiple modalities, each using the same core Fc-engineered TfR1 binding but carrying different therapeutic cargoes:

| Variant | Cargo | Lead Program | Indication |
|---------|-------|-------------|------------|
| **ETV** (Enzyme Transport Vehicle) | Lysosomal enzymes | DNL310 (tividenofusp alfa, IDS) | MPS II (Hunter) |
| **ETV** | Lysosomal enzymes | DNL126 (ETV:SGSH) | Sanfilippo Type A |
| **ETV** | Lysosomal enzymes | [[dnl111]] (ETV:GCase) | PD / Gaucher |
| **ETV** | Lysosomal enzymes | DNL952 (ETV:GAA) | Pompe disease |
| **OTV** (Oligonucleotide Transport Vehicle) | Antisense oligonucleotides | [[dnl422]] (OTV:SNCA) | Parkinson's disease |
| **OTV** | ASOs | DNL628 (OTV:MAPT) | Alzheimer's disease |
| **ATV** (Antibody Transport Vehicle) | Therapeutic antibodies | DNL921 (ATV:Abeta) | Alzheimer's disease |
| **PTV** (Protein Transport Vehicle) | Therapeutic proteins | DNL593 (PTV:PGRN) | FTD-GRN |

(Source: Denali 2026 Milestones Press Release, January 6, 2026)

**Dual TV -- Next-Generation Shuttle**

In a Cell Reports publication (August 2025), Denali disclosed a next-generation "Dual TV" platform that simultaneously targets both TfR1 and CD98hc (heavy chain of the large neutral amino acid transporter). TfR-mediated delivery drives rapid, high brain uptake, while CD98hc-mediated delivery provides slower uptake but more prolonged brain exposure. By tuning the relative affinities for each receptor, the Dual TV achieves significantly higher brain concentrations than either single-target TV alone, and can modulate the kinetics of brain exposure. This is a potential next-gen improvement for future PD-relevant programs (Source: Bhatt et al., Cell Reports 2025; PMID 40705600).

**Contrast with JCR Pabinafusp Alfa (IZCARGO)**

JCR Pharmaceuticals' pabinafusp alfa (IZCARGO), approved in Japan March 2021, also delivers IDS across the BBB via TfR-mediated transcytosis, but uses a fundamentally different architecture: a bivalent, high-affinity anti-TfR antibody fused to IDS. The key pharmacokinetic tradeoff is that high-affinity bivalent TfR binding provides rapid brain penetration but shorter plasma half-life, whereas Denali's moderate-affinity monovalent ETV design affords extended plasma exposure and potentially more sustained brain delivery. Both approaches validated the core concept that TfR-mediated transcytosis can deliver functional enzyme to the CNS (Source: JCR Pharmaceuticals press release, March 23, 2021; Ullman et al. 2022).

### Clinical

**Phase 1/2 Study (NCT04251026)**

- **Design:** Open-label, single-arm, dose-escalation/expansion in pediatric MPS II patients
- **Enrollment:** N=47 (15 ERT-naive, 32 previously treated with idursulfase)
- **Age range:** 0.3-13 years (median 5 years)
- **Severity:** Less severe to severe disease variants
- **Dosing:** Weekly IV infusions
- **Duration:** Up to 153 weeks of follow-up (approximately 3 years)

**Key Efficacy Results (NEJM, January 1, 2026; DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2508681):**

| Endpoint | Week 24 | Week 153 |
|----------|---------|----------|
| CSF HS reduction from baseline | 91% (95% CI: 90-92%; N=44) | 92% (95% CI: 90-93%; N=16) |
| CSF HS normalization rate | 93% of participants | Maintained |
| Urine HS reduction | 88% (95% CI: 85-90%; N=40) | 91% (95% CI: 87-94%; N=10) |
| Urine HS normalization rate | 58% | Maintained |
| Serum NfL reduction | 21% at Wk 49 (95% CI: 5-35%; N=34) | 76% (95% CI: 68-82%; N=13) |
| NfL normalization | -- | 85% reached normal range |
| Liver volume | Normalized by Week 24 | Maintained |
| Hearing | Improvement across tested frequencies | Maintained |
| Adaptive behavior/cognition | Skill gains in most participants | Maintained/improved |

The progressive deepening of NfL reduction from 21% at Week 49 to 76% at Week 153 is particularly notable, suggesting ongoing neuroprotective effects and not just substrate clearance.

**Phase 2/3 COMPASS Study (NCT05371613)**

- **Design:** Multiregional, double-blind, randomized, active-controlled (vs. idursulfase)
- **Target enrollment:** 54 participants
- **Cohort A:** Ages 2 to <6 years (neuronopathic); enrollment completed December 2025
- **Cohort B:** Ages 6 to <26 years
- **Purpose:** Confirmatory study to support full approval and global regulatory submissions

**Extension Study (NCT06075537)**

Open-label long-term extension evaluating safety, tolerability, and efficacy in participants rolling over from the Phase 1/2 and Phase 2/3 studies.

**Safety Profile**

- Most common treatment-related adverse events (TEAEs): infusion-related reactions (IRRs), anemia, vomiting, pyrexia, respiratory infections, rash
- IRRs decreased in incidence with continued dosing
- Most TEAEs were mild to moderate in severity
- Serious TEAEs in 3 participants (6.4%), all manageable with resolution or stabilization on continued treatment
- Long-term safety data (median follow-up >2 years, up to >4 years) demonstrated generally well-tolerated profile

**TfR1 Class Effect -- Hematological Considerations**

Because TfR1 is highly expressed on erythroid precursors (reticulocytes and erythroblasts), anti-TfR-based therapeutics carry an inherent risk of on-target hematological effects. Preclinical studies across the field have shown acute, reversible reticulocyte depletion with anti-TfR antibodies, attributed to effector functions (ADCC/CDC) and TfR-mediated sequestration. Denali's Fc engineering minimizes effector function, and the monovalent binding design reduces receptor crosslinking, mitigating but not eliminating this risk. The discontinued DNL919 (ATV:TREM2) program showed moderate, reversible hematologic effects at the highest dose tested in Phase 1 healthy volunteers, contributing to its discontinuation due to a "narrow therapeutic window" (Source: Denali Q2 2023 earnings, August 8, 2023; Fierceibiotech, August 2023). For tividenofusp alfa in the pediatric MPS II population, anemia was reported as a TEAE but did not appear dose-limiting. However, any future PD-relevant TV-enabled programs ([[dnl422]], [[dnl111]]) will need to carefully monitor reticulocyte counts and hemoglobin as standard practice.

### Financial

**Denali Cash Position**

As of September 30, 2025, Denali held approximately $872.9 million in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities. In December 2025, the company raised an additional ~$200 million through equity financing (Source: Denali Q3 2025 10-Q; January 2026 milestones press release).

**Royalty Pharma Deal (December 4, 2025)**

Royalty Pharma entered a $275 million synthetic royalty funding agreement for tividenofusp alfa:
- Initial payment: $200 million at closing (contingent on FDA accelerated approval)
- Additional payment: $75 million upon EMA approval by December 31, 2029
- Royalty rate: 9.25% on worldwide net sales
- Royalty cap: payments cease at 3.0x return (or 2.5x if reached by Q1 2039)

This deal structure implies Royalty Pharma expects cumulative worldwide net sales sufficient to generate at least $825 million in royalty payments (3.0x on $275M) over the royalty term. At a 9.25% royalty rate, that implies cumulative net sales of approximately $8.9 billion -- a strong vote of confidence in the commercial opportunity (Source: Royalty Pharma and Denali press releases, December 4, 2025).

**Biogen Collaboration (August 2020) -- PD Relevance**

The Biogen-Denali collaboration, while focused on LRRK2 inhibitors ([[biib122]]/DNL151) for Parkinson's disease, is worth up to $2.125 billion total:
- $560 million upfront payment
- $465 million equity investment (13.3M shares at ~$34.94/share)
- Up to $1.125 billion in development and commercial milestones
- Cost-sharing: 60/40 (Biogen/Denali) for global development; 50/50 US commercialization
- Biogen also received exclusive options to license two preclinical TV-platform programs (including ATV:Abeta)

This deal directly links tividenofusp alfa's platform validation to PD: the same Transport Vehicle engineering underlies both the Hunter syndrome ERT and the TV-enabled programs optioned to Biogen (Source: Biogen/Denali press release, August 6, 2020; BioSpace, August 2020).

**Market Context**

Hunter syndrome is ultra-rare (~1:100,000-170,000 male births). Existing standard-of-care (idursulfase/Elaprase, Takeda) generates ~$600-700M/year globally but does not address CNS disease. Tividenofusp alfa targets the ~two-thirds of patients with neuronopathic disease who derive incomplete benefit from conventional ERT. Analyst estimates for peak sales vary; one GlobalData estimate of $34M by 2039 appears conservative given the orphan drug pricing environment and unmet medical need. The Royalty Pharma deal math implies significantly higher commercial expectations.

## Analysis

### Platform Validation Thesis for PD

The approval of tividenofusp alfa would represent a watershed moment for the BBB delivery field with direct implications for Parkinson's disease therapeutics:

1. **Proof that Fc-engineered TfR1 shuttles work in humans.** The Phase 1/2 data showing 91% CSF HS reduction (a direct measure of CNS enzyme activity) is the strongest human evidence to date that engineered Fc-TfR1 binding achieves therapeutically meaningful brain exposure of a large biologic. This validates the core mechanism underlying [[dnl422]] (OTV:SNCA, an ASO targeting alpha-synuclein for PD) and [[dnl111]] (ETV:GCase, enzyme replacement for GBA-PD and Gaucher).

2. **Safety precedent for chronic TfR1 engagement.** Patients in the Phase 1/2 study have been on weekly IV tividenofusp alfa for up to 4+ years. The manageable safety profile -- including the hematological signal being non-dose-limiting in this population -- provides critical long-term safety data for the TfR1 shuttle mechanism that de-risks future PD programs. However, the DNL919 discontinuation is a cautionary signal: at higher doses needed for adult neurodegenerative diseases, the therapeutic window may narrow.

3. **Manufacturing and commercial infrastructure.** If Denali successfully launches tividenofusp alfa, the company builds manufacturing capability, supply chain, and commercial infrastructure for large-molecule BBB-penetrant biologics. This infrastructure directly supports future TV-enabled PD programs.

4. **Regulatory precedent.** An FDA approval under the accelerated pathway using CSF biomarker reduction as a surrogate endpoint establishes a template that could potentially be adapted for PD programs (e.g., using CSF alpha-synuclein or GCase activity as surrogate endpoints for TV-enabled PD therapies).

### Key Risks

- **PDUFA uncertainty:** The 3-month extension (clinical pharmacology data request) is procedural, not a safety/efficacy red flag, but any additional delay could impact the commercial launch timeline.
- **Hematological class effect:** The TfR1-targeting mechanism inherently risks reticulocyte effects. The DNL919 failure at high doses suggests this becomes more problematic in adult populations requiring higher exposures -- directly relevant to PD programs.
- **Small market for MPS II:** The commercial success of tividenofusp alfa in Hunter syndrome (ultra-rare) may not fully demonstrate the platform's potential for common neurodegenerative diseases where larger patient populations create different manufacturing and pricing dynamics.
- **Accelerated approval risk:** Post-marketing confirmatory study (COMPASS Phase 2/3) must demonstrate clinical benefit or approval could be withdrawn.

### PD-Relevant Pipeline Programs to Track

- **[[dnl422]] (OTV:SNCA):** IND-enabling stage. ASO targeting SNCA mRNA conjugated to OTV for brain-wide alpha-synuclein knockdown. Would be the first IV-administered, BBB-crossing ASO for PD.
- **[[dnl111]] (ETV:GCase):** IND-enabling stage. ETV-delivered glucocerebrosidase enzyme for GBA-PD and Gaucher disease. Same ETV architecture as tividenofusp alfa but with GCase payload instead of IDS.
- **[[biib122]] (BIIB122/DNL151):** Phase 2b LUMA study data expected H1 2026. LRRK2 inhibitor (small molecule, not TV-enabled) but part of the broader Denali-Biogen PD collaboration.
