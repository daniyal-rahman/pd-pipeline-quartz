# MTX325

## Summary

MTX325 is the first-in-class USP30 inhibitor to enter clinical trials for Parkinson's disease, developed by Mission Therapeutics (private, Cambridge UK). Phase 1a SAD/MAD studies in healthy volunteers completed with clean safety, good PK, confirmed CNS penetration via CSF sampling, and PET imaging showing distribution into brain parenchyma -- clearing the critical hurdle of demonstrating that this oral small molecule reaches its CNS target. Mission raised $13.3M in October 2025 specifically to fund a Phase 1b proof-of-mechanism study in PD patients (starting H1 2026, data expected H2 2027). If the Phase 1b shows target engagement and mitophagy enhancement in PD patients, it validates USP30 inhibition as a druggable node in the PINK1/Parkin pathway and positions Mission for a substantial partnership or Phase 2. If proof-of-mechanism fails, it raises questions about whether enhancing mitophagy downstream of PINK1/Parkin is sufficient, and capital shifts toward direct [[progenra-pink1|PINK1 activation]] (Progenra, AbbVie/ABBV-1088) or away from the mitophagy thesis entirely.

## Notes

### Science
- USP30 is a mitochondrial outer membrane deubiquitinase that counteracts PINK1/Parkin-mediated ubiquitylation of damaged mitochondria -- effectively acting as a brake on mitophagy. Inhibiting USP30 releases this brake, promoting clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria
- **Genetic validation is strong**: PINK1 and PRKN (Parkin) are the two most established autosomal recessive PD genes. Loss-of-function mutations in either cause early-onset PD by impairing mitophagy. USP30 inhibition is a pharmacological bypass -- it enhances mitophagy even in the context of partial PINK1/Parkin pathway dysfunction
- Key preclinical paper (Nature Communications, Nov 2023): USP30 knockout mice showed increased mitophagy, decreased phospho-S129 alpha-synuclein, and attenuation of substantia nigra dopaminergic neuron loss in an alpha-synuclein overexpression model. MTX115325 (the pharmacological tool compound/preclinical analog) recapitulated these findings dose-dependently
- Critically, USP30 inhibition addresses **multiple PD pathologies simultaneously**: mitochondrial dysfunction, alpha-synuclein accumulation, and neuroinflammation -- all demonstrated in the Nature Communications study
- A 2025 bioRxiv preprint showed USP30 inhibition improves mitochondrial health through both PINK1-dependent and PINK1-independent mechanisms, broadening the potential patient population beyond familial PD to idiopathic PD
- Key distinction vs. [[progenra-pink1|PINK1 activators]] (Progenra, AbbVie/ABBV-1088): USP30 inhibition works downstream of PINK1, meaning it can enhance mitophagy even when PINK1 is partially functional. PINK1 activators require residual PINK1 protein to stabilize. The two approaches are complementary rather than directly competitive
- Open question: whether enhancing mitophagy alone is sufficient to modify PD progression, or whether it needs to be combined with approaches targeting other pathogenic cascades (alpha-synuclein aggregation, neuroinflammation)

### Clinical

**MTX325-101 Phase 1a (SAD/MAD)** | ISRCTN20898392 | N=up to 158 (total program, all parts) | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, brain penetration
- **Key results:** Clean safety profile; CSF sampling confirmed high CNS penetration; PET imaging confirmed distribution into brain parenchyma; elderly cohort included
- **Status:** Completed (healthy volunteer cohorts)
- **Interpretation:** The demonstration of brain penetration via both CSF and PET is critical for a mitophagy-targeting CNS drug. Clears the major pharmacological hurdle for oral small molecule neurodegeneration programs

**MTX325-101 Phase 1b (Proof-of-Mechanism)** | ISRCTN20898392 (same adaptive study) | N=~50 | Mild-to-moderate PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Clinical proof-of-mechanism in PD patients; additional safety and tolerability
- **Key secondary:** Proof-of-mechanism biomarker and imaging data
- **Dosing:** 28-day treatment period
- **Status:** Not yet started; expected H1 2026, funded by $13.3M raise (Oct 2025) and $5.2M MJFF/Parkinson's UK grant
- **Interpretation:** This is the pivotal proof-of-concept gate. 28-day dosing in PD patients is short, so the endpoints will likely be mechanistic biomarkers (mitophagy markers, potentially pS65-ubiquitin) rather than clinical efficacy measures. Success here would be demonstrating target engagement and downstream pathway activation in PD patients

- MTX325 is currently the **only** USP30 inhibitor in clinical development for PD. The DUB inhibitor field is nascent with no direct clinical competitors
- The broader mitophagy enhancement competitive set includes [[progenra-pink1|Progenra's PINK1 activator program]] (preclinical, targeting 2027 FIH) and AbbVie's ABBV-1088 (PINK1 activator acquired via Mitokinin for $110M; Phase 1 SAD completed Feb 2025, MAD enrolled Aug 2025). ABBV-1088 is the most advanced competitor in the mitophagy space and backed by big pharma resources
- USP30 inhibition and PINK1 activation are mechanistically complementary (different nodes on the same pathway), meaning success of one does not invalidate the other. In fact, ABBV-1088 clinical validation of the mitophagy thesis would be a positive read-through for MTX325
- The [[biib122|LRRK2 inhibitor]] field (Denali/Biogen, Phase 2b) also intersects with mitochondrial biology -- LRRK2 regulates Rab GTPases that affect mitochondrial trafficking and lysosomal function. A positive LUMA readout (March 2026) would broadly validate genetic PD pathways and support the mitophagy thesis
- If [[biib122|BIIB122]] LUMA fails, capital may flow toward mitophagy as the next testable genetic PD hypothesis, potentially benefiting MTX325 positioning

## Analysis

MTX325 occupies an attractive position as the first-in-class USP30 inhibitor with clinical proof of brain penetration in a pathway with strong genetic validation. The PINK1/Parkin mitophagy axis is one of the most genetically validated pathways in PD -- second only to alpha-synuclein itself -- and USP30 sits at a pharmacologically tractable node. The Nature Communications data showing simultaneous effects on mitochondrial dysfunction, alpha-synuclein pathology, and neuroinflammation is notably broader than single-target approaches.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 1b proof-of-mechanism success probability: 55-60%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: Phase 1 to Phase 2 transition in CNS diseases ~60-65%
- Adjustments upward: strong preclinical efficacy in multiple models (+5%), confirmed brain penetration clears major risk (+5%), clean Phase 1a safety (+5%), multi-mechanism preclinical effects suggest robust biology (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: proof-of-mechanism in 28 days is a high bar for a neurodegenerative disease (-10%), biomarker readouts in PD patients may have high variability (-5%), first-in-class target with no clinical precedent (-10%)
- Net: ~55-60%

**Signal analysis:** The investor syndicate composition is highly informative. Roche Venture Fund and Pfizer Venture Investments are not typical biotech VC investors -- they are strategic arms of companies with neurodegeneration franchises. Their continued participation across GBP 25.2M (March 2024) and $13.3M (October 2025) rounds suggests internal scientific diligence at both pharma companies has endorsed the USP30 mechanism. The MJFF's sustained support across three grant cycles (2017, 2021, 2024) further validates the science through independent expert review. However, this remains a private startup-stage company with no clinical efficacy data yet. The 28-day proof-of-mechanism study will be read on biomarkers, not clinical endpoints -- and translating preclinical mitophagy enhancement into measurable PD patient biomarker changes in 28 days is not guaranteed.

The decision tree hinges on the H2 2027 readout. If proof-of-mechanism is positive: Mission becomes an attractive acquisition or licensing target for Roche, Pfizer, or AbbVie (all of whom have existing relationships or parallel mitophagy interest). A Phase 2 efficacy study would likely require partnership capital. If proof-of-mechanism is negative: the USP30 target loses clinical credibility, though the question would be whether the failure was target-related or pharmacological (dose, duration, biomarker selection). Either way, the mitophagy thesis still has ABBV-1088 and [[progenra-pink1|Progenra]] testing PINK1 activation at a different pathway node.
