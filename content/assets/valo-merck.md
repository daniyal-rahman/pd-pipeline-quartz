# Valo-Merck PD Program

## Summary

Valo Health (startup, Flagship Pioneering portfolio) and Merck KGaA (big pharma, via EMD Serono) announced a $3B+ biobucks collaboration in November 2025 to discover and develop novel PD therapeutics using Valo's AI-enabled human causal biology platform. The program is anchored by work on the NOD2 pathway -- a GWAS-validated innate immunity target at the intersection of neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration -- building on a September 2025 MJFF Targets to Therapies grant. This is a discovery-stage platform deal with no named compounds; if target validation succeeds and candidates emerge, the neuroinflammation thesis gains a credible pharmaceutical backer distinct from the dominant alpha-synuclein ([[prasinezumab]]) and genetic PD ([[biib122|LRRK2]], [[pariceract|GBA1]]) axes. If the AI platform fails to produce viable candidates, it joins the growing list of AI-discovery deals that generated headlines but not drugs.

## Notes

### Science
- NOD2 (nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain containing 2) is an innate immune receptor that detects bacterial peptidoglycan and activates NF-kB signaling, triggering inflammatory cascades
- GWAS meta-analyses have identified NOD2 as a PD risk gene; it is one of six targets prioritized by recent computational analyses as both genetically validated and druggable in PD
- NOD2 shares overlapping inflammatory pathway biology with LRRK2 -- both are linked to Crohn's disease risk and innate immune dysregulation, suggesting a shared gut-brain-immune axis in PD pathogenesis
- Valo's platform leverages 17M+ de-identified patient records (including ~160,000 PD patients and ~900,000 with neurodegenerative conditions), combined with genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data to build causal models linking molecular changes to clinical outcomes
- The "closed-loop chemistry platform" integrates AI modeling with iterative experimental validation to generate and optimize small-molecule candidates against validated targets
- 3D engineered human tissue models provide target validation using tissues mimicking mature human physiology -- a differentiated approach vs. standard cell-line screening
- MJFF Targets to Therapies grant (September 2025) specifically funds Valo to clarify causal relationships between NOD2 and related pathways across CNS and immune cells, using genetics, multi-omics, and network biology analytics
- Key open question: whether peripheral NOD2 modulation (more druggable) can meaningfully impact central neuroinflammation, or whether CNS-penetrant compounds are required

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. Program is at discovery/target-validation stage. The MJFF-funded work on NOD2 causal biology and the Merck KGaA collaboration for preclinical candidate generation represent the earliest stages of the drug discovery pipeline. IND-enabling studies are likely several years away.

### Financial
- **Deal structure:** >$3B total value including upfront payment (undisclosed), R&D funding, milestone payments, and royalties on any resulting products
- **Comparable Valo deals:** Novo Nordisk expanded collaboration (January 2025) -- $190M in near-term payments, up to $4.6B in milestones across 20 programs in cardiometabolic disease; original Novo deal (September 2023) was $2.7B across 11 programs. The Merck KGaA deal ($3B+) falls between these in total value
- **Valo corporate:** Private company founded by Flagship Pioneering (2019); total funding ~$450-595M from investors including Koch Disruptive Technologies, PSP Investments, HBM Healthcare Investments, Hercules Capital, and others
- **Context:** Merck KGaA acquired SpringWorks Therapeutics for $3.4B in 2025, signaling aggressive external innovation strategy. The Valo deal extends this approach into neuroscience
- **MJFF grant:** Awarded September 2025 as part of the Targets to Therapies Initiative; grant size not disclosed but part of a $10M initiative across multiple targets

- No other PD programs are currently known to target NOD2 specifically -- this is a first-mover in an emerging target class
- The broader neuroinflammation thesis competes with [[biib122|LRRK2 inhibitors]] (which share immune pathway biology with NOD2) and anti-inflammatory approaches from [[dnl111|Denali]] and others
- NOD2's genetic overlap with LRRK2 (both implicated in Crohn's disease and innate immunity) raises a key question: does NOD2 modulation offer differentiated biology or is it downstream of the same pathway that LRRK2 inhibitors already address?
- As an AI discovery platform deal, this competes in the "AI-for-neurodegeneration" space alongside Recursion, Insilico Medicine, and others -- but Valo's differentiation is its massive longitudinal patient dataset (17M+ records) enabling causal inference rather than purely computational target prediction
- If NOD2 modulation validates, it opens a new therapeutic axis in PD beyond alpha-synuclein and genetic PD targets -- potentially applicable to sporadic PD patients who lack LRRK2/GBA1 mutations

## Analysis

This deal is best understood as a validation signal for two things simultaneously: the neuroinflammation hypothesis in PD, and the AI-enabled drug discovery model. Merck KGaA's willingness to commit $3B+ in biobucks to a discovery-stage partnership -- with no named compounds and a target (NOD2) that has never been drugged in neurology -- signals their conviction in Valo's platform rather than any specific molecule. The deal structure (heavy on milestones, undisclosed upfront) is typical of platform partnerships where the pharma partner is buying optionality across multiple potential candidates.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of producing a clinical PD candidate: 15-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Base rate for discovery-stage AI partnerships producing clinical candidates is roughly 10-15% per target. Adjustments upward: NOD2 has genuine GWAS validation (+5%), Valo's platform has produced candidates that advanced (Novo Nordisk acquired 3 preclinical assets for $60M) (+5%), MJFF grant provides independent scientific validation (+3%). Adjustments downward: no proof-of-concept data in any neurodegenerative indication (-5%), CNS penetration challenges for NOD2-targeting compounds are unknown (-3%), the "AI drug discovery" space has produced many deals but few approved drugs (-5%). Net: ~15-25%.

The NOD2 biology itself is scientifically interesting. The genetic link between NOD2, LRRK2, and inflammatory bowel disease points to a gut-brain-immune axis that could be relevant to sporadic PD -- the ~90% of PD patients without known genetic mutations who are currently underserved by the genetic-PD pipeline ([[biib122|LRRK2 inhibitors]], [[pariceract|GBA1 activators]]). If Valo's causal biology platform can identify the specific NOD2 pathway nodes that drive neurodegeneration vs. peripheral inflammation, this could produce genuinely differentiated targets. However, the field has repeatedly struggled to translate neuroinflammation hypotheses into clinical benefit -- the anti-TNF, anti-IL-1, and other anti-inflammatory approaches in neurodegeneration have a poor track record.

The decision tree is straightforward but long-dated: if Valo nominates a target and generates a preclinical candidate that shows CNS engagement and disease-relevant pharmacology, Merck KGaA will fund development through IND and into clinical trials. If the platform fails to identify druggable NOD2-related targets, the deal quietly winds down -- as most discovery partnerships do. The key interim signal to watch is whether Merck KGaA exercises options on specific programs, which would indicate internal confidence in the target biology.
