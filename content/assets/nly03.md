# NLY03

## Summary

NLY03 is an early-stage parthanatos pathway inhibitor being developed by Neuraly (biotech, subsidiary of D&D Pharmatech, KOSDAQ: 347850) for stroke and Parkinson's disease, currently in preclinical lead optimization with no disclosed compound structure or in vivo efficacy data. The program builds on foundational work from the Ted and Valina Dawson lab at Johns Hopkins -- the same group behind [[nly01|NLY01]] (GLP-1R agonist, Phase 2) and [[nly02|NLY02]] (RIPK2 inhibitor, IND-enabling) -- which established that pathologic alpha-synuclein kills dopaminergic neurons through PARP-1 hyperactivation and the parthanatos cell death cascade (Kam et al., Science 2018), and identified PAANIB-1 as a brain-penetrant PAAN/MIF nuclease inhibitor that prevents neurodegeneration in multiple PD mouse models (Park et al., Cell 2022). If NLY03 advances to IND with a differentiated compound showing brain penetrance and on-target PARP-1 or PAAN/MIF inhibition, it would represent the first clinical program directly targeting parthanatos in PD -- a cell death mechanism with strong genetic and biochemical validation. If the program stalls in preclinical development or D&D Pharmatech deprioritizes it behind [[nly01|NLY01]] (AD, MS indications) and DD01 (MASH), it remains an academic proof-of-concept without near-term clinical impact.

## Notes

### Science
- **Parthanatos** (from Greek "thanatos" = death) is a PARP-1-dependent, caspase-independent programmed cell death pathway distinct from apoptosis, necrosis, and necroptosis. The cascade proceeds: DNA damage or oxidative stress --> PARP-1 hyperactivation --> excessive PAR (poly ADP-ribose) polymer accumulation --> PAR binds AIF (apoptosis-inducing factor) on mitochondrial outer membrane --> AIF translocates to cytoplasm and forms complex with PAAN/MIF (macrophage migration inhibitory factor) --> AIF/MIF complex translocates to nucleus --> MIF nuclease activity cleaves genomic DNA into large fragments --> cell death
- **Alpha-synuclein-PARP-1 feed-forward loop**: Kam et al. (Science, 2018) demonstrated that pathologic alpha-synuclein activates PARP-1, and PAR polymer in turn accelerates alpha-synuclein aggregation into a more toxic strain, creating a vicious cycle. PARP-1 knockout or PARP inhibitors blocked pathologic alpha-synuclein toxicity in mouse models. PAR levels were elevated in CSF and brains of PD patients, providing human translational evidence
- **PAANIB-1 proof-of-concept**: Park et al. (Cell, 2022) identified PAANIB-1, a brain-penetrant PAAN/MIF nuclease inhibitor, through compound screening. PAANIB-1 prevented dopaminergic neuron loss, restored striatal dopamine, and improved motor function across three PD models (alpha-syn PFF injection, AAV-alpha-syn overexpression, MPTP intoxication). Critically, PAANIB-1 was specific to parthanatos -- it had no effect on apoptosis or necroptosis, demonstrating pathway selectivity
- NLY03 is described on the Neuraly pipeline as targeting "parthanatos cascade targets" in lead optimization -- the undisclosed compound may be PAANIB-1 or a derivative optimized for drug-like properties (solubility, metabolic stability, toxicology)
- **Key differentiator vs. [[nly01|NLY01]] and [[nly02|NLY02]]**: NLY03 targets the cell death executioner pathway downstream of both neuroinflammation (which [[nly01|NLY01]] and [[nly02|NLY02]] target) and alpha-synuclein aggregation itself. In theory, a parthanatos inhibitor could be neuroprotective regardless of upstream pathology, making it potentially applicable across PD subtypes and even non-synucleinopathy neurodegeneration
- **Dual indication rationale (stroke + PD)**: Parthanatos is a major contributor to neuronal death in ischemic stroke (via excitotoxic PARP-1 activation) and in chronic neurodegeneration (via alpha-synuclein-driven PARP-1 activation). The stroke indication may offer a faster clinical path due to acute endpoints and shorter trial timelines
- **Open questions**: (1) Is the lead compound PAANIB-1 or a novel scaffold? (2) What is the specific target node -- PARP-1 inhibition (competing with established oncology PARP inhibitors like olaparib) or PAAN/MIF nuclease inhibition (novel target)? (3) Can sufficient brain exposure be achieved for chronic oral dosing? (4) What are the off-target effects of chronic PARP-1 inhibition on DNA repair, hematopoiesis, and cancer risk?

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. NLY03 is in preclinical lead optimization / discovery stage.

Key preclinical foundation (from Dawson lab, not NLY03-specific):
- **PARP-1 genetic validation**: PARP-1 knockout mice are protected against alpha-synuclein PFF-induced dopaminergic neuron loss (Kam et al., Science 2018)
- **PAANIB-1 pharmacological validation**: Brain-penetrant MIF nuclease inhibitor prevents neurodegeneration in alpha-syn PFF, AAV-alpha-syn, and MPTP models (Park et al., Cell 2022)
- **PAR as PD biomarker**: Elevated PAR levels in CSF and post-mortem brains of PD patients provide human translational evidence for pathway activation (Kam et al., Science 2018)
- No IND-enabling studies or NHP data have been disclosed for NLY03 specifically

### Competitive

| File | stage | status | developer | modality |

- NLY03 is the **only known PD-directed program targeting the parthanatos pathway** -- there are no direct competitors targeting PARP-1/PAR/AIF/MIF in PD specifically
- Oncology PARP inhibitors (olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib, talazoparib) exist as approved drugs but target PARP-1 for synthetic lethality in BRCA-mutant cancers, not for neuroprotection. Chronic PARP inhibition for neuroprotection raises distinct safety questions (DNA repair impairment, myelosuppression) that would not apply to PAAN/MIF-targeted approaches
- As a cell death pathway inhibitor, NLY03 competes conceptually with other neuroprotective strategies: [[nly01|NLY01]] and [[nly02|NLY02]] (anti-neuroinflammatory, same company), [[dapansutrile]] and [[selnoflast]] (NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors), and genetic PD targets like [[biib122|LRRK2 inhibitors]] and [[pariceract|GBA1 activators]] that also aim to prevent neuron loss
- The dual stroke/PD indication is strategically interesting -- if NLY03 demonstrates neuroprotection in acute stroke (faster, smaller trials), it could de-risk the chronic PD indication. No other PD pipeline asset has this dual-indication structure targeting a shared cell death mechanism
- The Dawson lab's academic track record (>30,000 citations, Science 2018, Cell 2022) provides scientific credibility, but academic pedigree does not predict clinical translation success -- [[nly01|NLY01]]'s Phase 2 miss despite strong preclinical data from the same lab illustrates this gap

## Analysis

NLY03 is the most scientifically novel but least clinically advanced asset in Neuraly's pipeline. The parthanatos pathway has unusually strong validation for a preclinical PD target: genetic proof (PARP-1 knockout is neuroprotective), pharmacological proof (PAANIB-1 protects across three distinct PD models), and human translational evidence (elevated PAR in PD CSF and brains). The 2018 Science paper establishing the alpha-synuclein/PARP-1 feed-forward loop and the 2022 Cell paper identifying PAANIB-1 as a brain-penetrant parthanatos inhibitor constitute a rare combination of mechanistic clarity and therapeutic tractability for a neurodegenerative disease target. This is the kind of foundational science that attracts pharmaceutical interest.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of NLY03 reaching Phase 1 within 5 years: 15-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for discovery/lead-optimization neuroscience assets reaching Phase 1 is approximately 20-30%; adjustment upward for strong target validation across multiple models (+5%), brain-penetrant tool compound already exists (+5%), and Johns Hopkins IP with founder-led company alignment (+5%); adjustment downward for apparent deprioritization within D&D Pharmatech behind NLY01 and NLY02 (-10%), no disclosed lead compound structure or IND-enabling data (-5%), chronic PARP pathway inhibition safety unknowns (-5%), and the cautionary example of [[nly01|NLY01]]'s preclinical-to-clinical translation failure (-5%).

The critical strategic question is whether D&D Pharmatech views NLY03 as a real development candidate or a long-term option they maintain at minimal cost. The company's behavior -- no public NLY03 data presentations, no partnership announcements, and active prioritization of NLY01 in non-PD indications -- suggests the latter. However, two scenarios could accelerate NLY03: (1) a licensing deal where an external partner funds development, attracted by the Cell 2022 data package; or (2) D&D Pharmatech's MASH or AD programs generate enough revenue/capital to fund the earlier-stage PD pipeline. The stroke indication could also be opportunistic -- acute neuroprotection trials are shorter and cheaper than chronic PD trials, and a positive stroke signal would generate substantial interest in the PD application. Watch for any Dawson lab publications on optimized PAANIB analogs or NLY03-specific compounds as the leading indicator of program advancement.
