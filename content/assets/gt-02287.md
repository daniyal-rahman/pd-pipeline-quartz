# GT-02287

## Summary

GT-02287 is the first GCase modulator to demonstrate CSF substrate reduction (glucosylsphingosine) in Parkinson's disease patients, with an 81% mean decrease in participants with elevated baseline GluSph after 90 days of oral dosing. Gain Therapeutics (biotech, GANX) is a micro-cap running a 21-patient open-label Phase 1b in Australia (NCT06732180), with 15 patients continuing into a 9-month extension expected to complete September 2026. MDS-UPDRS Part III motor scores improved by a mean 3.8 points at Day 90 -- directionally encouraging but uncontrolled and underpowered. A larger double-blind Phase 2 trial (100-200 patients) is planned for 2026. If the extension confirms durable motor benefit and substrate reduction, GT-02287 becomes the leading GCase activator with differentiated biomarker proof and a potential partnering or acquisition candidate. If Phase 2 fails to replicate the open-label signals, the mechanism question remains open alongside [[pariceract]] (BIAL's allosteric activator in the ACTIVATE trial) and [[pr001|PR001]] (Lilly's GBA1 gene therapy), and Gain's limited cash runway becomes critical.

## Notes

### Science
- GT-02287 is a structurally targeted allosteric regulator (STAR) discovered using Gain's proprietary SEE-Tx (Site-directed Enzyme Enhancement Therapy) computational platform, which identifies novel allosteric binding sites on misfolded enzymes via AI-supported structural biology and physics-based modeling
- Binds an allosteric site on GCase (distinct from the active site), stabilizing the enzyme and chaperoning it to the lysosome where it performs its function -- this restores GCase activity in both GBA1-mutant and wildtype enzyme
- Key mechanistic distinction vs. [[pariceract]]: both are allosteric GCase activators, but GT-02287 was designed de novo via computational platform while pariceract emerged from traditional medicinal chemistry at Lysosomal Therapeutics / BIAL. GT-02287 claims broader applicability to both GBA1-mutant and age-related GCase dysfunction in idiopathic PD
- Key distinction vs. substrate reduction therapy (venglustat, Sanofi): venglustat inhibited glucosylceramide synthase upstream to reduce toxic substrate but failed Phase 2 in GBA1-PD (no benefit vs. placebo). GT-02287 takes the opposite approach -- restoring enzyme function downstream rather than blocking substrate production
- Key distinction vs. [[pr001|PR001]] gene therapy: PR001 delivers a functional GBA1 gene via AAV9 intracisternal injection (one-time, invasive), while GT-02287 is an oral daily small molecule (chronic, non-invasive). Gene therapy addresses the genetic root cause; GT-02287 addresses the protein-level dysfunction
- GBA1 mutations are the most common genetic risk factor for PD (~7-10% of PD patients carry a mutation, and GCase activity declines with age even in non-carriers), providing strong genetic validation for the target
- In preclinical models, GT-02287 restored GCase enzymatic function, reduced ER stress, lysosomal and mitochondrial pathology, aggregated alpha-synuclein, neuroinflammation, neuronal death, and plasma NfL levels -- a broad disease modification profile
- Open question: whether restoring GCase activity in idiopathic PD patients (without GBA1 mutations) provides meaningful clinical benefit. The inclusion of both GBA1-mutant and idiopathic PD patients in Phase 1b is designed to address this

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (Healthy Volunteers)** | NCT TBD | N=~40 (SAD/MAD) | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability --> Safe and well tolerated at single (2.4-15.0 mg/kg) and multiple (4.8-13.5 mg/kg) doses; no SAEs or Grade 3 AEs; nausea and headache most common
- **Key secondary:** PK linear across dose range; plasma exposures at >=7.7 mg/kg within projected therapeutic range; CSF levels consistent with effective rodent doses; GCase activity in dried blood spots increased ~30% vs. placebo at clinically relevant doses, continuing to rise at 12h post-dose on Day 14
- **Status:** Completed (2024)
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety profile with confirmed CNS penetration and target engagement established the PK/PD foundation for Phase 1b in patients

**Phase 1b (PD Patients)** | NCT06732180 | N=21 | PD patients with or without GBA1 mutation, <7 years since diagnosis; 7 sites in Australia
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability after 90 days --> Generally well tolerated at plasma exposures within projected therapeutic range
- **Key exploratory (pre-specified):** CSF GluSph reduction --> In participants with elevated baseline CSF GluSph, mean 81% decrease after 90 days, normalizing toward healthy levels. First demonstration of CSF substrate reduction by a GCase modulator in PD
- **Motor function:** Baseline MDS-UPDRS scores: Part I = 5.8, Part II = 7.4, Part III = 24.7. At Day 90, Part II decreased by mean 0.8 points, Part III decreased by mean 3.8 points. Improvement was not observed at Day 30, suggesting gradual disease-slowing effect rather than acute symptomatic relief. Part I unchanged
- **Enrollment:** 21 enrolled, 19 completed 90-day dosing, 15 (79%) entered 9-month extension (Part 2)
- **Status:** Part 1 completed December 2025; Part 2 (extension) ongoing, anticipated completion September 2026
- **Interpretation:** The CSF GluSph data is the headline finding -- first proof that an oral small molecule can reduce GCase substrate in the CNS of PD patients. Motor improvements are encouraging but open-label, small N, and uncontrolled. The high extension enrollment rate (79%) is a positive signal. Longer follow-up data expected at AD/PD conference March 2026

**Phase 2 (Planned)** | NCT TBD | N=100-200 | PD patients (design TBD)
- **Primary endpoint:** TBD (likely motor + biomarker endpoints)
- **Status:** Planned for early 2026 initiation; double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Interpretation:** The critical de-risking step. Must replicate biomarker signals in a controlled setting and demonstrate clinical benefit on MDS-UPDRS to advance toward registration

- [[pariceract]] (BIAL) is the most direct competitor -- also an oral allosteric GCase activator in clinical trials for GBA-PD (ACTIVATE Phase 2, NCT05819359). Pariceract has a larger trial (double-blind) and bigger sponsor but has not yet reported CSF substrate reduction data comparable to GT-02287's GluSph findings
- [[pr001|PR001]] (Prevail/Lilly) addresses the same target via AAV9 gene therapy -- fundamentally different modality (one-time invasive vs. chronic oral), targeting severe GBA1-mutant patients. Not directly competitive for the broader GBA-PD population
- Venglustat (Sanofi) -- substrate reduction therapy that failed Phase 2 in GBA1-PD. Its failure validated the concept that simply reducing upstream substrate is insufficient; restoring enzyme function (GT-02287's approach) may be required
- Ambroxol -- repurposed mucolytic acting as a GCase pharmacological chaperone, in the GREAT trial (Europe) for early GBA-PD. Low-cost, well-characterized safety, but limited potency and uncertain brain penetration at achievable doses
- If GT-02287 Phase 2 succeeds with both biomarker and motor endpoints, it would be the first oral GCase modulator with proof of CNS target engagement AND clinical benefit in PD -- a transformative result for the GBA-PD field that could attract big pharma partnering
- If GT-02287 fails, the GCase activator approach narrows to [[pariceract]] and ambroxol, and the broader GBA-PD thesis faces headwinds alongside venglustat's failure

## Analysis

GT-02287's Phase 1b CSF GluSph data is a genuine first-in-class milestone. No other GCase-targeting small molecule has demonstrated measurable CSF substrate reduction in PD patients. This positions Gain Therapeutics to claim a pharmacological proof-of-concept that has eluded the field. The 81% GluSph reduction in patients with elevated baseline levels is large in magnitude and biologically coherent with the mechanism. Combined with the ~30% GCase activity increase in healthy volunteers (Phase 1), the pharmacology chain from target engagement to downstream substrate reduction appears intact.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 2 success probability: 25-30%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: Phase 1b-to-approval success rates for CNS small molecules are ~10-15%
- Adjustments upward: first-in-class CSF biomarker proof (+10%), strong genetic validation for GBA1 target (+5%), oral brain-penetrant modality favorable for chronic dosing (+5%), both GBA1-mutant and idiopathic PD inclusion broadens addressable population (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: open-label, N=21, no control arm (-10%), micro-cap with tight cash runway and no partner (-5%), venglustat GBA-PD failure creates field skepticism (-5%), MDS-UPDRS improvements at 90 days could be placebo effect in unblinded trial (-5%)
- Net: ~25-30%

**Signal analysis:** The company's behavior reveals both promise and fragility. The decision to include idiopathic PD patients (not just GBA1 carriers) is strategically important -- it expands the addressable market from ~7-10% to potentially all PD patients with age-related GCase decline, but it also introduces biological heterogeneity that could dilute signals in Phase 2. The $7M public offering at $1.55/share in 2025 signals a company stretching its cash to reach a value-inflection catalyst (Phase 1b data), which it achieved. The absence of a pharma partner is both a risk (limited resources for Phase 2) and an opportunity (clean asset for acquisition/licensing). A partnering announcement in 2026, leveraging the CSF biomarker data, would be a strong validation signal. The planned Phase 2 enrollment of 100-200 patients is ambitious for a company of this size and will almost certainly require either a partnership or substantial capital raise.

The decision tree is clear: if the Phase 1b extension (September 2026) shows durable motor benefit at 12 months alongside sustained biomarker improvement, and the Phase 2 initiates with adequate funding, GT-02287 becomes the leading clinical-stage GCase activator with differentiated data. If the extension data shows regression to baseline or the company cannot fund Phase 2, the asset becomes a potential acquisition target at distressed valuations -- the biomarker data package alone has value to a larger company ([[pariceract|BIAL]], Lilly) seeking to build a GCase portfolio.
