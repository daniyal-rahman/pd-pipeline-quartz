# RNDP-001

## Summary

RNDP-001 is an off-the-shelf, allogeneic iPSC-derived dopamine progenitor cell therapy from Kenai Therapeutics (startup, private) that dosed its first patient in the Phase 1 REPLACE trial in December 2025. Kenai raised $82M in Series A (Feb 2024) and secured FDA Fast Track designation and an $8M CIRM grant (Jan 2026), bringing total non-dilutive + equity funding to ~$93M. Initial safety, tolerability, and brain imaging data from all 12 patients are expected in 2026. RNDP-001 enters a competitive cell therapy landscape where [[raguneprocel]] (Sumitomo/CiRA, iPSC allogeneic) has filed for approval in Japan and [[bemdaneprocel]] (BlueRock/Bayer, ESC-derived) is in Phase 3 registrational trials. If REPLACE demonstrates clean safety and imaging evidence of graft survival, Kenai becomes a credible third entrant in the neuron replacement race with a differentiated iPSC manufacturing platform. If safety signals emerge or grafts fail to engraft, the allogeneic iPSC modality faces further scrutiny alongside autologous approaches like Aspen Neuroscience's ANPD001.

## Notes

### Science
- RNDP-001 consists of cryopreserved, donor-derived iPSC-differentiated dopamine progenitor cells designed to replace the A9 midbrain dopaminergic neurons progressively lost in PD
- Mechanism: cells are surgically delivered to targeted brain areas where they are intended to survive, integrate into host circuitry, form synapses, and produce dopamine — functionally restoring the nigrostriatal pathway
- Built on Nobel Prize-winning iPSC technology (Yamanaka factors); the allogeneic ("off-the-shelf") approach uses a single donor iPSC line to manufacture standardized doses, unlike autologous approaches (e.g., Aspen Neuroscience) that require individualized production from each patient's cells
- Manufactured through an exclusive partnership with FUJIFILM Cellular Dynamics, Inc. (FCDI), leveraging FCDI's industrial-scale iPSC differentiation and cryopreservation capabilities — a key differentiator for reproducibility and scale-up
- Co-founded by Jeffrey Kordower (ASU-Banner Neurodegenerative Disease Research Center), one of the field's leading experts on DA neuron transplantation with decades of work on fetal cell transplants and iPSC-derived neuron optimization; his 2022 npj Regenerative Medicine paper established dose-response and maturity parameters for iPSC-DA progenitor grafts that likely inform RNDP-001's dosing
- Key scientific questions: (1) Will allogeneic cells survive long-term without heavy immunosuppression? (2) Will transplanted progenitors mature into functional A9-type DA neurons rather than other DA subtypes? (3) Will host-to-graft alpha-synuclein transmission eventually compromise grafts, as observed in fetal transplant studies at >10 years post-grafting?
- Distinction vs. [[bemdaneprocel]]: RNDP-001 uses iPSC-derived cells while bemdaneprocel uses ESC-derived cells. iPSC manufacturing avoids embryonic tissue sourcing, but ESC lines have longer clinical track records. Both are allogeneic and cryopreserved
- Distinction vs. [[raguneprocel]]: both are iPSC-derived allogeneic DA progenitors, but RNDP-001 uses a different iPSC line (FCDI-manufactured) vs. raguneprocel's CiRA Foundation-derived cells, potentially differing in cell identity, maturity at transplant, and manufacturing process

### Clinical

**REPLACE (Phase 1)** | NCT07106021 | N=12 | Moderate to moderate-severe idiopathic PD, ages 45-75
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of RNDP-001
- **Key secondary:** Brain imaging evidence of cell survival and engraftment
- **Design:** Open-label, multi-center, two dose-level cohort study (lower dose cohort followed by higher dose cohort) at three US sites: University of Southern California, University of Arizona / Banner University Medical Center (Tucson), and Ohio State University
- **Status:** First patient dosed December 15, 2025; initial data from all 12 patients expected 2026
- **Regulatory:** IND cleared by FDA; Fast Track designation granted December 2025
- **Interpretation:** Standard first-in-human design for a surgically delivered cell therapy. N=12 is typical for Phase 1 neurosurgical cell therapy trials (comparable to bemdaneprocel Phase 1 N=12). The two-dose-level design will establish safety at both doses and provide preliminary engraftment signals via imaging. Fast Track designation enables rolling submission and accelerated review pathway if efficacy signals emerge.

- **Investor signal:** Alaska Permanent Fund Corporation (sovereign wealth-style investor) co-leading a Series A is unusual for a preclinical-stage biotech and signals institutional confidence in the platform

Kenai's $82M Series A at the IND stage suggests the market prices iPSC PD cell therapy platforms in the hundreds-of-millions range for later-stage deals
- **Burn rate consideration:** Neurosurgical cell therapy trials are expensive (per-patient costs for stereotactic surgery, imaging, long-term follow-up). $93M should fund through Phase 1 completion but a Phase 2 expansion will likely require Series B financing

- [[bemdaneprocel]] (BlueRock/Bayer, Phase 3) uses ESC-derived cells rather than iPSC and is the most advanced cell therapy in the West; 36-month Phase 1 OLE data showed mean 17.9-point improvement on MDS-UPDRS Part III in the high-dose cohort — a strong engraftment signal that sets the bar for RNDP-001
- Aspen Neuroscience (ANPD001, Phase 1/2a) takes the autologous iPSC approach — personalized from patient's own skin cells, eliminating immunosuppression requirement but creating manufacturing complexity and cost challenges; 6-month ASPIRO data reported in May 2025
- Kyoto University investigator-initiated trial (N=7, Phase I/II) published in Nature 2025 confirmed iPSC-derived DA progenitors survived, produced dopamine, and did not form tumors — foundational proof-of-concept for all iPSC-based approaches including RNDP-001
- Key competitive distinction for Kenai: FCDI manufacturing partnership may enable faster scale-up and more consistent product than academic-origin cell lines, positioning RNDP-001 for commercial manufacturing if efficacy is demonstrated
- If [[raguneprocel]] is approved in Japan (MHLW decision expected H2 2026): validates iPSC DA replacement, increases investor interest in Kenai, and may accelerate FDA regulatory pathway via international precedent
- If [[bemdaneprocel]] Phase 3 succeeds: ESC-derived cells become the standard, and RNDP-001 would need to demonstrate iPSC-specific advantages (manufacturing, cell identity, long-term outcomes) to compete

## Analysis

RNDP-001 enters clinical development at a favorable inflection point for the cell therapy modality in PD. The Kyoto University Phase I/II data published in Nature (2025) demonstrated that iPSC-derived DA progenitors can survive, produce dopamine, and avoid tumor formation in human brains — answering the foundational safety question that has loomed over the field since the fetal transplant era. BlueRock's bemdaneprocel 36-month data showing sustained motor improvements further validates the neuron replacement thesis. Kenai benefits from this rising tide of clinical evidence without having generated any of it.

**Analytical estimate — Phase 1 success probability (safety + engraftment signal): 65-70%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Base rate for Phase 1 cell therapy safety trials in PD is high (~80%) given that multiple programs (bemdaneprocel, raguneprocel, ANPD001) have demonstrated clean safety profiles with similar approaches. Adjustment upward: Kordower's preclinical dose-optimization work de-risks cell maturity and dose selection (+5%). Adjustment upward: FCDI's manufacturing consistency reduces batch-to-batch variability risk (+5%). Adjustment downward: RNDP-001's specific iPSC line and differentiation protocol are untested in humans — line-specific risks remain (-10%). Adjustment downward: immunosuppression protocol details are undisclosed, introducing uncertainty around graft rejection (-10%). Net: 65-70%.

The strategic question for Kenai is differentiation. With [[raguneprocel]] potentially approved in Japan and [[bemdaneprocel]] in Phase 3 registrational trials, RNDP-001 is a third entrant in a modality where first-mover advantage matters for regulatory and commercial positioning. Kenai's path to value creation likely depends on one of three scenarios: (1) demonstrating a best-in-class engraftment or durability profile that justifies a second-generation product thesis, (2) leveraging FCDI's manufacturing platform to achieve cost-of-goods and scale advantages that matter at the commercial stage, or (3) being acquired by a larger player seeking iPSC cell therapy capabilities (the BlueRock/Bayer playbook). The $82M Series A with sovereign-wealth-class investors and the Cure Ventures board chair suggest the investors are underwriting scenario (3) — a platform acquisition — rather than an independent commercialization path.

Company behavior signals are informative. Kenai's choice to target moderate-to-severe PD (rather than early PD) aligns with the regulatory path of least resistance — these patients have the most to gain and the clearest unmet need, making risk-benefit calculations favorable for a neurosurgical intervention. The two-dose-level design in Phase 1 is standard but positions them to move quickly into dose expansion if safety is clean. The CIRM grant ($8M) provides runway extension without dilution and signals California's regenerative medicine ecosystem investment in the program.
