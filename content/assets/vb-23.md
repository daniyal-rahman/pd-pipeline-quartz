# VB-23

## Summary

Vincere Biosciences (private, Boston) is advancing VB-23, an oral, brain-penetrant USP30 inhibitor through IND-enabling studies toward a Phase 1 trial planned for 2026. The program has received three separate MJFF grants (2019, 2022, $5M in November 2025), representing unusual multi-grant validation from the field's most rigorous nonprofit funder. VB-23 competes directly with Mission Therapeutics' MTX325, which completed Phase 1a in healthy volunteers and is entering Phase 1b in PD patients in H1 2026 -- making Vincere approximately 12-18 months behind in clinical development. If VB-23 reaches Phase 1 with clean safety and brain penetration data, it validates a second entrant in the USP30 space and increases confidence that the target itself is druggable. If IND-enabling studies reveal toxicity or inadequate CNS exposure, the setback would be contained to Vincere but would not invalidate USP30 given MTX325's existing Phase 1a data. The broader mitophagy thesis connects VB-23 to [[progenra-pink1|Progenra's PINK1 activators]] (upstream in the same pathway) and [[stealth-bio|Stealth BioTherapeutics]] (mitochondrial membrane stabilization).

## Notes

### Science
- USP30 is a mitochondrial deubiquitinase (DUB) that sits on the outer mitochondrial membrane and removes ubiquitin tags from damaged mitochondria -- effectively acting as a "brake" on mitophagy by opposing the PINK1/Parkin ubiquitination cascade
- Inhibiting USP30 releases this brake, lowering the threshold for mitophagy and promoting clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria without requiring PINK1 or Parkin to be fully functional -- a theoretical advantage over [[progenra-pink1|PINK1 activators]] in PINK1/Parkin-null patients
- Genetic validation is strong: loss-of-function mutations in PINK1 (PARK6) and Parkin (PARK2) cause autosomal recessive early-onset PD; USP30 knockout in a PD mouse model protected dopaminergic neurons (Rusilowicz-Jones et al., Nature Communications 2023)
- Vincere synthesized and characterized over 800 USP30 inhibitor molecules across two series: one optimized for CNS pharmacokinetic profile (PD indication) and one for peripheral/cardiorenal applications, demonstrating measurable mitophagy increase in the brain in preclinical models
- The company's AI platform initially prioritized USP30 as a target in 2018, predating the broader pharma interest -- per Mission Therapeutics' Nature Reviews Drug Discovery article (Jan 2025), each of the top 10 pharma companies now has some level of mitophagy R&D ongoing
- Key distinction vs. Mission Therapeutics' MTX325: both are oral, brain-penetrant USP30 inhibitors but emerge from independent chemical series; Vincere's dual CNS/cardiorenal optimization may enable lifecycle extension beyond neurology
- Open question: whether USP30 inhibition alone provides sufficient mitophagy enhancement in patients with complete PINK1/Parkin loss-of-function, or whether residual pathway activity is required for meaningful benefit

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. VB-23 is in IND-enabling studies as of February 2026.

- **IND-enabling studies (ongoing):** Pharmacology, toxicology, and regulatory workstreams funded by $5M MJFF Therapeutics Pipeline Program grant (November 2025). Biomarker development for target engagement evaluation is included.
- **Phase 1 planned:** Company targets clinical trial initiation in 2026, pending successful IND submission to FDA
- **Preclinical context:** Vincere has demonstrated mitophagy increase in the brain in animal models; over 800 molecules characterized to optimize potency, selectivity, and CNS penetration
- **Competitive clinical benchmark:** Mission Therapeutics completed Phase 1a SAD/MAD of MTX325 in healthy volunteers with clean safety, CSF sampling confirmed CNS penetration, and PET imaging confirmed brain parenchyma distribution. Mission's Phase 1b in PD patients begins H1 2026, with proof-of-mechanism data expected H2 2027.

- VB-23 is the second most advanced pure USP30 inhibitor after Mission's MTX325, approximately 12-18 months behind in clinical timelines
- [[progenra-pink1|Progenra's PINK1 activators]] work upstream: they restore PINK1 kinase activity to initiate the ubiquitination cascade that USP30 inhibitors seek to amplify -- mechanistically complementary, not directly competitive
- [[nrg5051|NRG5051]] (mPTP inhibitor) targets a different node of mitochondrial dysfunction (preventing pathological pore opening vs. promoting clearance of damaged mitochondria) -- parallel validation of the mitochondrial thesis
- [[stealth-bio|Stealth BioTherapeutics]] stabilizes mitochondrial membranes (cardiolipin targeting) -- orthogonal mechanism within the same organelle
- If MTX325 Phase 1b shows proof-of-mechanism in PD patients (H2 2027): USP30 is validated as a clinical target, dramatically de-risking VB-23 and making it an attractive licensing or acquisition target for pharma companies wanting a second USP30 asset
- If MTX325 fails on safety or efficacy: the failure mode matters -- if toxicity is mechanism-based (USP30-related), VB-23 faces a class-wide problem; if MTX325-specific (chemical series issue), VB-23's independent chemical series becomes the surviving asset in the space

## Analysis

VB-23 occupies an interesting position as the second-mover in a two-player USP30 inhibitor race. The three MJFF grants (2019, 2022, 2025) are a meaningful validation signal -- MJFF's Therapeutics Pipeline Program is selective, and repeat funding to the same target/company implies satisfactory milestone achievement across multiple independent reviews. The $5M November 2025 grant specifically for IND-enabling studies suggests MJFF's internal scientific review committee considers the program ready for clinical translation.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 1 initiation probability: 70-75%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for IND-enabling programs reaching Phase 1: ~65%
- Adjustments upward: triple MJFF validation (+10%), >800 molecules characterized suggesting robust SAR (+5%), Mission's Phase 1a safety data de-risks the USP30 target class (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: startup with limited disclosed capital (-5%), no disclosed GLP tox data yet (-5%)
- Net: ~70-75%

**Signal analysis:**
- The Beiersdorf strategic investment is unusual and revealing. A skincare company investing in a PD-focused biotech signals that Vincere's USP30 platform has demonstrated enough preclinical mitochondrial rejuvenation data to attract non-pharma interest. This creates optionality -- cardiorenal and dermatology applications could generate non-dilutive revenue that subsidizes the PD program, a rare capital structure advantage for a startup.
- Vincere's investor base (Draper Associates, Daewoong, HanAll) includes both generalist VC and Korean pharma -- the Korean pharma angle may indicate interest in ex-US rights or manufacturing partnerships, relevant given Korea's growing cell therapy and neurodegeneration ecosystem.
- The $16.3M total equity raise plus $8M+ in grants gives Vincere roughly $24M in total capital deployed. This is thin for IND-enabling through Phase 1 completion -- a Series A/B raise in 2026 is virtually certain. The timing of that raise will be heavily influenced by Mission's Phase 1b data: positive MTX325 data would make Vincere significantly easier to fund.
- The competitive dynamic with Mission is the central strategic question. Vincere is behind but benefits from Mission's path-clearing: every piece of MTX325 clinical data (safety, PK, brain penetration, target engagement) de-risks VB-23 without Vincere spending the capital. This is a classic fast-follower advantage in a novel target class.
