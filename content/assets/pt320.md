# PT320

## Summary

PT320 is Peptron's (biotech, KOSDAQ: 087010) sustained-release exendin-4 formulation using their proprietary SmartDepot polymer platform, designed to achieve steadier plasma levels and ~2% CSF/blood penetration ratio -- a claimed pharmacokinetic advantage over standard [[exenatide]] (Bydureon). The Phase 2a trial (NCT04269642, N=99, 48 weeks + 12-week washout) in South Korea missed its primary endpoint decisively (MDS-UPDRS Part III, p=0.7355 vs. placebo), announced December 2022. A subgroup of patients with more severe baseline symptoms showed a statistically significant signal (p=0.0110), and Peptron has stated it plans to redesign the trial with enriched enrollment and improved drug delivery (prefilled syringes for compliance). The asset sits in a crowded GLP-1 agonist field where [[exenatide]] has definitively failed Phase 3 (Lancet 2025), [[nly01|NLY01]] (also exendin-4-based) missed Phase 2 primary, [[lixisenatide]] showed a positive Phase 2 signal, and [[semaglutide]] trials are ongoing. PT320's path forward is unclear: no new trial has been registered since the Phase 2a failure, and the company's attention appears split between PD and Alzheimer's indications.

## Notes

### Science
- PT320 is a sustained-release formulation of exendin-4 (synthetic exenatide) using Peptron's SmartDepot platform -- a biodegradable polymer microsphere system (98% polymer, 2% peptide) that provides sustained drug release over ~20 days from a single subcutaneous injection
- The pharmacological thesis is formulation-driven, not mechanistically novel: same GLP-1 receptor agonism as [[exenatide]], [[nly01|NLY01]], and [[lixisenatide]], but the sustained-release profile is claimed to achieve higher and more consistent BBB penetration than pulsatile dosing
- In nonhuman primate PK studies (Vaughan et al., Scientific Reports 2019), PT320 achieved steady-state plasma exenatide levels from day 10-28 without the sub-therapeutic trough seen with Bydureon (which dips before reaching steady-state at day 35-49). CSF levels for the sustained-release formulation were 18.3-30 pg/mL vs. <6.9 pg/mL for immediate-release exendin-4 BID -- a ~3-4x improvement in brain exposure
- Preclinical efficacy demonstrated in MitoPark PD mice: PT320 delayed dopamine neuron degeneration, improved spontaneous locomotor activity, and mitigated L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia in 6-OHDA rat models (Frontiers Neurosci 2020, ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci 2021)
- Peptron holds an exclusive NIH license (2014, expanded 2018) covering the use of sustained-release exenatide for neurodegenerative diseases, developed under a CRADA with NIH investigators including the Greig laboratory
- Key distinction from [[nly01|NLY01]]: NLY01 is PEGylated exendin-4 (chemical modification for half-life extension); PT320 is unmodified exendin-4 in a sustained-release polymer depot (formulation modification). Different engineering approaches to the same pharmacological problem
- Key distinction from [[exenatide]]: the UCL program used off-the-shelf Bydureon (AstraZeneca's commercial weekly formulation); PT320 is a proprietary biweekly formulation specifically designed for CNS applications with claimed superior brain penetration

### Clinical

**PT320 Phase 2a** | NCT04269642 | N=99 | Early PD
- **Primary endpoint:** MDS-UPDRS Part III (OFF) change from baseline at 48 weeks → No statistical significance vs. placebo (**p=0.7355**)
- **Key secondary:** 12-week washout period to assess disease modification vs. symptomatic effect; exosome biomarker analysis (results not publicly disclosed in detail)
- **Subgroup analysis:** Patients with more severe baseline symptoms showed statistically significant improvement (p=0.0110) -- not pre-specified, likely post-hoc
- **Safety:** No serious adverse drug reactions reported; clean safety profile consistent with GLP-1 class
- **Study design:** 3-arm (placebo vs. PT320 2.0 mg QW vs. PT320 2.5 mg Q2W), 5 Korean hospitals (Seoul National University Hospital, SNU Bundang Hospital, Seoul Metropolitan Boramae Hospital, Asan Medical Center, Samsung Medical Center)
- **Timeline:** IND approved January 2015; protocol amendment approved January 2020; first patient dosed March 2020; enrollment completed mid-2021; 48-week dosing completed end-2021; results announced December 2022
- **Status:** Completed (failed)
- **Interpretation:** The p=0.7355 primary miss is decisive -- not a borderline signal diluted by heterogeneity but a near-null result. The severe subgroup signal (p=0.0110) is post-hoc and in a small subset, making it unreliable for prospective planning. Company-cited explanations -- small sample size, self-injection compliance issues, and heterogeneous early PD population -- are plausible contributing factors but do not rescue the result. The Phase 2a failure places PT320 in the same category as [[exenatide]] (Phase 3 fail) and [[nly01|NLY01]] (Phase 2 fail) as exendin-4-based formulations that have not demonstrated efficacy in PD.

- **Funding model:** Phase 2a was company-funded with NIH collaboration (CRADA partner); no disclosed external pharma funding for the PD program
- **NIH license:** Exclusive license for sustained-release exenatide in neurodegenerative diseases acquired 2014, expanded 2018 -- gives Peptron sole commercial rights to this formulation approach in PD/AD
- **Key investors:** M Venture Investment, DAOL Investment, Aju IB Investment, Innopolis Partners (most recent)
- **Pipeline diversification:** Peptron's SmartDepot platform also supports products in oncology (prostate cancer) and endocrinology; PT320/PT330 for Alzheimer's is also in development, suggesting the company may pivot CNS investment toward AD if PD path remains blocked
- **No disclosed partnerships with pharma for PD:** Unlike [[lixisenatide]] (Sanofi originator) or [[nly01|NLY01]] (Neuraly/D&D Pharmatech), Peptron has not secured a pharma partner for the PD indication -- a notable absence given the Phase 2a failure
- **Estimated trial cost:** Small Korean academic-adjacent trial (N=99, 5 sites, 48 weeks) likely cost $5-10M

- PT320 competes directly with four other GLP-1 agonists in PD, all targeting the same receptor through different molecular engineering: [[exenatide]] (Bydureon, Phase 3 failed), [[nly01|NLY01]] (PEGylated exendin-4, Phase 2 failed primary), [[lixisenatide]] (structurally distinct GLP-1 agonist, Phase 2 positive), and [[semaglutide]] (Phase 2 ongoing). [[liraglutide]] is also being explored in PD
- The formulation-differentiation thesis -- that PT320's sustained-release profile provides better brain penetration than [[exenatide]] -- was not validated clinically. The Phase 2a miss (p=0.7355) was worse than [[exenatide]]'s Phase 2 signal (p=0.0318) and [[nly01|NLY01]]'s Phase 2 miss (p=0.77). Only [[lixisenatide]] has shown a positive signal in this class
- Within the Korean GLP-1/PD ecosystem, PT320 competes with [[nly01|NLY01]] (Neuraly/D&D Pharmatech, also KOSDAQ-listed) -- both are Korean biotech exendin-4 variants that have failed Phase 2 primaries, both found post-hoc subgroup signals, and both face unclear paths forward
- The field-wide pattern is stark: 3 of 4 exendin-4-based programs that have reached clinical endpoints in PD have failed (PT320, [[exenatide]], [[nly01|NLY01]]); only the structurally distinct [[lixisenatide]] succeeded. This may indicate that the exendin-4 backbone specifically is insufficient for CNS efficacy regardless of formulation engineering

## Analysis

PT320's Phase 2a failure is the third clinical miss for an exendin-4-based GLP-1 agonist in PD, following [[exenatide]]'s Phase 3 failure (Lancet 2025) and [[nly01|NLY01]]'s Phase 2 miss (Lancet Neurol 2023). The formulation thesis -- that Peptron's SmartDepot polymer system would solve exenatide's brain penetration problem -- was not supported by clinical evidence. While the nonhuman primate PK data showed ~3-4x higher CSF levels vs. immediate-release exendin-4, this apparently did not translate to clinical benefit in a 99-patient trial. The decisive primary miss (p=0.7355) leaves little room for the "underpowered but real signal" interpretation that sustained [[exenatide]]'s academic development for 15 years.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of PT320 achieving Phase 3 success in PD: <5%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for GLP-1 agonists in PD Phase 3 is now anchored by [[exenatide]]'s failure (~5%); adjustment downward for PT320's own Phase 2a miss (-5%), absence of any biomarker confirmation (-3%), no pharma partner (-2%), and the post-hoc nature of the severe subgroup signal (-3%); marginal adjustment upward for the formulation-based PK advantage (+3%) and the clean safety profile (+1%). Net: <5%. The only scenario in which PT320 advances meaningfully is if Peptron redesigns the trial with a much larger, severity-enriched population and prefilled syringes -- but this would require significant capital investment with no pharma partner in a field where [[lixisenatide]] and [[semaglutide]] are drawing the commercial interest.

**Signal analysis:** Peptron's public statements after the Phase 2a failure emphasized process failures (small N, compliance issues, heterogeneous population) rather than questioning the pharmacological thesis. This framing is typical of companies that plan to continue development. However, the absence of a new registered trial as of early 2026, combined with the company's pivot toward PT330 (Alzheimer's indication), suggests the PD program may be de facto deprioritized even if not formally terminated. The NIH exclusive license gives Peptron blocking rights on sustained-release exenatide for neurodegeneration, which has strategic value even if PT320 in PD does not advance -- it prevents competitors from using the same formulation approach. For the broader GLP-1 PD landscape, the emerging pattern strongly favors [[lixisenatide]] (structurally distinct, positive Phase 2) and [[semaglutide]] (different backbone, better intrinsic BBB penetration) over any exendin-4-based approach.
