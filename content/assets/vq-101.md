# VQ-101

## Summary

VQ-101 is the first small molecule to demonstrate >75% lysosomal GCase activation in healthy volunteers and >70% activation in GBA-PD patients, with full CNS penetration (CSF:unbound plasma ratio >=1) and a clean safety profile through Phase 1b. Vanqua Bio (private startup, Northwestern spinout, ~$150M raised at $333M valuation) is running an open-label extension in PD patients with data expected early-to-mid 2026. The key differentiation vs. [[pariceract]] is the degree of enzyme activation (>75% vs. ~60% reported preclinically for pariceract) and the broader patient population strategy (GBA1 carriers and non-carriers). If the OLE data confirm sustained GCase activation and early biomarker signals in patients, VQ-101 becomes a strong Phase 2 candidate and Vanqua Bio a potential acquisition target; if the data are underwhelming or safety signals emerge, [[pariceract]]'s ACTIVATE Phase 2b (mid-2026 readout) remains the de facto GCase activator bet.

## Notes

### Science
- Allosteric GCase activator: binds a site distinct from the catalytic pocket to stabilize the enzyme in its active conformation, increasing lysosomal GCase activity and restoring lipid homeostasis
- Based on research by Dimitri Krainc (Northwestern University, Feinberg School of Medicine), who identified that GBA1 mutations cause lysosomal dysfunction, enabling toxic alpha-synuclein accumulation via impaired autophagy-lysosome pathway
- GBA1 mutations are the most common genetic risk factor for PD, present in ~5-10% of all PD patients worldwide; heterozygous carriers have ~5-30x increased lifetime PD risk depending on the specific mutation
- Key mechanistic distinction: activates the enzyme directly (restoring function) vs. substrate reduction inhibitors like [[venglustat]] (which reduce glucosylceramide synthesis but failed in MOVES-PD) vs. chaperone approaches like ambroxol (which stabilize protein folding but have limited potency)
- In patient-derived neurons, 50% GCase activity increase was sufficient to significantly reduce insoluble alpha-synuclein accumulation, providing the translational bridge from enzyme engagement to disease-relevant pathology
- Critical open question: whether GCase activation in non-GBA1-mutation PD patients (idiopathic PD with age-related GCase decline) provides clinical benefit — this would dramatically expand the addressable population beyond the ~10% genetic subgroup
- Full CNS penetrance with CSF:unbound plasma ratio >=1 is a meaningful differentiator; many small molecules targeting lysosomal enzymes struggle to achieve brain-relevant concentrations

### Clinical

**VQ-101 Phase 1a (SAD/MAD in Healthy Volunteers)** | NCT TBD | N=88 (80 completed) | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety profile; no dose-limiting or serious adverse events; all TEAEs mild or moderate
- **Key secondary:** PK/PD — single doses (20-800 mg) achieved >75% lysosomal GCase activation; multiple daily doses (50-600 mg, 14-28 days) showed sustained activation at >75% above baseline; CSF:unbound plasma ratios >=1 confirming full brain penetration
- **Status:** Completed (results announced October 2024)
- **Interpretation:** Best-in-class GCase activation magnitude and full CNS penetrance support once-daily oral dosing; PK/PD profile enables dose selection for patient studies

**VQ-101 Phase 1b (MAD in PD Patients)** | NCT TBD | N=~28 planned | PD patients with and without GBA1 mutations
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → No dose-limiting or serious adverse events; all TEAEs mild or moderate; zero discontinuations due to adverse events
- **Key secondary:** After 28 days of dosing, VQ-101 exceeded the target engagement goal of 50% lysosomal GCase activation at all doses tested, restoring GCase activity to healthy volunteer levels; fully CNS penetrant with mean CSF:unbound plasma ratios >=1
- **Status:** Ongoing; OLE portion (up to 6 months) active; positive interim data presented at MDS Congress (October 2025)
- **Interpretation:** First demonstration of robust GCase activation in actual PD patients (>70%). The inclusion of both GBA1 mutation carriers and non-carriers is strategically important — if activation shows biomarker effects in non-carriers, the addressable market expands dramatically. OLE data in early 2026 are the next key readout.
- **Trial site:** Centre for Human Drug Research (CHDR), Netherlands
- **First patient dosed:** April 2024

- [[pariceract]] (BIAL) is the direct competitor and most advanced GCase activator — Phase 2b ACTIVATE trial (N=273, mid-2026 readout). Pariceract's precedent preclinical activation was ~60%, whereas VQ-101 has demonstrated >75% in humans, but ACTIVATE will deliver the first clinical efficacy signal for any GCase activator
- Venglustat (Sanofi) — substrate reduction inhibitor (blocks glucosylceramide synthesis rather than activating GCase) — **failed** MOVES-PD Phase 2 in 2021. This failure validated the enzyme activation thesis over substrate reduction but damaged overall GBA1 pathway confidence
- GT-02287 (Gain Therapeutics) — allosteric GCase modulator also in Phase 1b (N=21, OLE initiated 2025). Gain positions GT-02287 for broader PD (with or without GBA1 mutations), similar to VQ-101's strategy. Direct competitor at the same stage
- Ambroxol — repurposed molecular chaperone for GCase; academic-led studies (AiM-PD) showed modest GCase activity increase but limited potency; not expected to be commercially competitive
- Gene therapy approaches: PR001/Prevail (Lilly) and [[cavgene|CAP-003]] (Capsida) target GBA1 via AAV-delivered GCase gene replacement — different risk/benefit profile (one-time treatment but invasive intracranial delivery, irreversible, manufacturing complexity)
- If [[pariceract]] ACTIVATE succeeds in mid-2026: validates the entire GCase activator mechanism, de-risks VQ-101's path to Phase 2, and likely triggers partnering/acquisition interest across the class. VQ-101's higher activation data could command a premium
- If [[pariceract]] ACTIVATE fails: serious setback for the mechanism — third GCase-pathway clinical failure after venglustat and ambroxol, though the activation vs. inhibition vs. chaperone distinction may preserve a path for VQ-101 if failure mode is pariceract-specific

## Analysis

VQ-101 occupies a distinctive position in the GCase activator space: best-in-class pharmacology (highest reported GCase activation in humans, full CNS penetration) in a company too early to have clinical efficacy proof. The Phase 1b data are genuinely impressive on the PK/PD front — >75% activation in healthy volunteers and >70% in PD patients, with CSF:unbound plasma >=1, exceeding what has been reported for any competing GCase approach. The safety profile is clean across ~100 subjects dosed. These are exactly the Phase 1 characteristics you want to see before investing in a large Phase 2 program.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of advancing to Phase 2 with positive OLE data: ~70%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Phase 1 PK/PD data are strong (high activation, CNS penetrance), safety is clean, and the OLE is designed to confirm durability rather than discover new risks. The main risk is that 6-month dosing reveals tolerability issues not seen in 28-day dosing or that GCase activation does not translate to downstream biomarker changes in patients.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of eventual Phase 2/3 success (clinical efficacy in PD): ~20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for Phase 1 neurology assets reaching approval is ~8-12%. Adjustments upward: strong genetic validation for GBA1 target (+10%), best-in-class PK/PD (+5%), clean safety enabling chronic dosing (+5%), broader population strategy could help if GCase decline is relevant beyond mutation carriers (+5%). Adjustments downward: no clinical efficacy data yet (-10%), GBA1 pathway has two prior clinical failures (venglustat, ambroxol) though different mechanisms (-5%), PD disease modification trials have near-100% historical failure rate (-5%). Net: ~20-25%.

The strategic signal from Eli Lilly's Series B participation is worth noting. Lilly acquired Prevail Therapeutics and its PR001 gene therapy for GBA-PD, yet also invested in VQ-101 — a competing oral small molecule. This suggests Lilly views the GCase pathway as high-conviction but uncertain on modality. If VQ-101 progresses well, Lilly has inside knowledge and a natural path to acquisition or licensing. The $333M private valuation at Phase 1 is aggressive but reflects the scarcity premium on differentiated GBA1 assets after venglustat's failure.

The critical near-term dynamic is the [[pariceract]] ACTIVATE readout in mid-2026. If ACTIVATE succeeds, every GCase activator including VQ-101 gets a massive de-risk event. If ACTIVATE fails, VQ-101's superior activation data become either the last hope for the mechanism or insufficient to overcome pathway skepticism. Either way, Vanqua Bio should have OLE data and a Phase 2 design ready by mid-2026 to capitalize on or respond to the ACTIVATE result.
