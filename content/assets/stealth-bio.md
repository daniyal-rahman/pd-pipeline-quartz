# Bevemipretide (SBT-272)

## Summary

Bevemipretide (SBT-272) is Stealth BioTherapeutics' next-generation mitochondria-targeted peptidomimetic with demonstrated BBB penetrance and neuroprotective activity in preclinical PD models. The company is NOT a zombie -- it completed a go-private transaction with Morningside Ventures in November 2022 (delisted from Nasdaq as MITO), won FDA accelerated approval for its lead compound elamipretide (FORZINITY) in Barth syndrome in September 2025, and is now a commercial-stage private biotech. However, the PD program specifically remains early-stage: positive preclinical data in alpha-synuclein mouse models (MDS Congress 2021), completed Phase 1 SAD/MAD in healthy volunteers (subcutaneous), and an MJFF-funded biomarker collaboration with Duke University. The company's 2026 priorities focus on FORZINITY launch, dry AMD Phase 3, and ALS/rare neuro indications for bevemipretide -- PD is listed as a preclinical-stage indication with "next development steps under evaluation." If mitochondrial dysfunction biomarkers from the MJFF study identify a targetable PD subpopulation, this could accelerate into clinical development; if not, PD remains deprioritized behind ALS and ophthalmology programs. Compare to [[abbv-1088]] for an alternative mitophagy approach.

## Notes

### Science
- Bevemipretide is a novel peptidomimetic that selectively binds **cardiolipin**, an anionic phospholipid almost exclusively localized on the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) essential for cristae structure and electron transport chain function
- Mechanism: stabilizes cardiolipin against peroxidation, preserving mitochondrial cristae integrity, reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and maintaining ATP synthesis -- functionally restoring bioenergetics in dysfunctional mitochondria
- Key differentiator vs. parent compound elamipretide (SS-31): **higher mitochondrial uptake, greater brain concentrations, and improved oral bioavailability** -- designed specifically for CNS indications
- The combination of hydrophobic (Dmt, Phe) and electrostatic (D-Arg, Lys) residues enables selective mitochondrial accumulation via the large negative membrane potential of mitochondria
- Biological rationale for PD: mitochondrial dysfunction is a hallmark of PD pathology; misfolded alpha-synuclein directly interacts with cardiolipin on the IMM, creating a vicious cycle of mitochondrial damage and protein aggregation
- This is a **downstream neuroprotective** approach -- it does not target alpha-synuclein directly but instead protects mitochondria from the damage caused by alpha-synuclein and other insults
- Genetic validation: PINK1 and Parkin mutations (causes of genetic PD) operate in the mitophagy pathway; LRRK2 mutations also impair mitochondrial function; cardiolipin abnormalities documented in PD patient tissues
- Open question: is mitochondrial protection sufficient for disease modification in PD, or is it a symptomatic neuroprotective effect that delays but does not halt progression? No clinical data exist to answer this in PD
- SBT-272 has shown broad neuroprotective effects across preclinical models of alpha-synucleinopathy, ALS, frontotemporal dementia, Huntington's disease, and ischemic stroke -- this breadth is both a strength (platform potential) and a concern (lack of PD-specific optimization)

### Clinical

**SBT-272 Phase 1 SAD/MAD (Phase 1)** | ISRCTN12414966 | N=up to 64 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of single and multiple ascending SC doses
- **Key secondary:** PK profiling; dose range identification for Phase 2
- **Status:** Completed (~2020-2022); results described as "supporting further clinical development" but no detailed publication available
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety enables Phase 2 planning, but no efficacy signal possible in healthy volunteers. The company has not yet initiated any Phase 2 study in any neurological indication.

**Preclinical PD Study (A53T alpha-synuclein mouse model)**
- Viral A53T alpha-synuclein injection in substantia nigra
- Systemic daily SBT-272 at two doses: both doses **significantly protected dopaminergic neurons** from loss and **reduced pathological alpha-synuclein aggregation**
- Higher dose **significantly reduced two neuroinflammation markers**
- Presented at MDS Virtual Congress 2021
- Interpretation: Standard preclinical PD proof-of-concept; the A53T model is well-established but many compounds showing preclinical efficacy in this model have failed clinically

**MJFF-Funded Biomarker Study (Preclinical)**
- Two-year collaboration with Laurie Sanders, Ph.D. (Duke University)
- Goal: identify biomarkers of mitochondrial dysfunction that could inform PD clinical development and patient selection
- Awarded 2023; expected completion ~2025
- Interpretation: This is the critical gating step for PD-specific clinical development -- without validated biomarkers for patient selection, a Phase 2 PD trial would face the same heterogeneity problem that plagues the field

## Analysis

Stealth BioTherapeutics is emphatically not a zombie company. The initial premise of this research query assumed a dead/shell entity, but the reality is a privately held, now-commercial biotech with an FDA-approved product, two Phase 3 programs, and an active pipeline. The Morningside go-private in 2022 rescued the company from Nasdaq delisting and potential bankruptcy, and the FORZINITY approval in September 2025 validated both the elamipretide platform and the cardiolipin-targeting mechanism.

However, the PD-specific program within Stealth IS low-priority and early-stage. Bevemipretide (SBT-272) completed Phase 1 in healthy volunteers and has solid preclinical PD data, but the company has explicitly listed ALS as its lead neuro indication, not PD. The MJFF-funded biomarker study with Duke is the key gating item -- if it identifies a mitochondrial dysfunction biomarker that can stratify PD patients, a precision-medicine Phase 2 becomes viable. Without that biomarker, a PD trial would face the same patient heterogeneity problem that undermines most PD trials.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of SBT-272 entering a PD-specific Phase 2 by 2028: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base: company has competing priorities (FORZINITY launch, dry AMD Phase 3, nPMD Phase 3, ALS development) that consume capital and management attention
- Upward adjustments: MJFF biomarker study could provide patient selection strategy (+5%); FORZINITY revenue provides financial runway (+3%); platform validation across multiple neuro indications builds PD case (+2%); Morningside as deep-pocketed backer enables pipeline breadth (+2%)
- Downward adjustments: ALS is explicitly prioritized over PD (-5%); no PD KOL champion visible in company leadership (-3%); private status limits capital for multiple indications (-2%); preclinical-to-Phase 2 transition failure rate in PD is >80% (-5%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:** The company's 2026 strategic priorities press release (January 2026) mentions systemic bevemipretide for Parkinson's only as having "recently demonstrated neuroprotective effects" in preclinical studies, with "next development steps under evaluation." This is corporate language for "we have not committed resources to this." The FORZINITY commercial launch and dry AMD Phase 3 readout (2027) are the overwhelming priorities. PD would require either (a) the MJFF biomarker study producing a clear patient selection strategy, (b) a partner willing to fund a PD trial, or (c) FORZINITY revenue exceeding expectations and freeing capital for pipeline expansion.

For the mitophagy thesis cluster, Stealth represents a validated platform (FDA-approved in one indication) with unvalidated PD application. The mechanism is scientifically sound but the commercial and strategic barriers to a PD-specific program are substantial. This is a "watch" asset, not an "act" asset.
