# BIIB094

## Summary

BIIB094 (ION859) was an intrathecally delivered LRRK2-targeting antisense oligonucleotide developed by Ionis Pharmaceuticals (biotech, IONS) and partnered with Biogen (big pharma, BIIB). The Phase 1 REASON trial demonstrated robust target engagement — CSF LRRK2 reduced up to 59% and pRab10 up to 50% — but Biogen discontinued the program in February 2025 as part of a broader PD pipeline reprioritization that also terminated the [[biib122|BIIB122]] LIGHTHOUSE Phase 3 trial. The discontinuation was not driven by safety concerns but by strategic portfolio decisions: intrathecal delivery is a significant commercial liability for a chronic neurodegenerative disease, and Biogen chose to consolidate its LRRK2 investment behind [[biib122|BIIB122]] (oral small molecule kinase inhibitor). The program's biomarker data remains scientifically valuable as the first human demonstration of LRRK2 ASO-mediated pathway modulation in CSF. ION859 remains listed on the Ionis pipeline, suggesting potential re-partnering or internal advancement, though no new development has been announced.

## Notes

### Science
- Antisense oligonucleotide that binds LRRK2 mRNA and triggers RNase H-mediated degradation, reducing LRRK2 protein levels in the CNS
- LRRK2 gain-of-function mutations (most commonly G2019S) are the most frequent genetic cause of inherited PD, accounting for ~5% of familial and ~1-2% of sporadic cases
- Elevated LRRK2 kinase activity drives pathology through impaired vesicle trafficking, lysosomal dysfunction, and neuroinflammation — all upstream of alpha-synuclein aggregation
- Key mechanistic distinction vs. [[biib122|BIIB122]] (small molecule kinase inhibitor): ASO reduces total LRRK2 protein (kinase-dependent AND kinase-independent functions) whereas kinase inhibitors only block enzymatic activity. This is a double-edged sword — broader knockdown may capture more pathology but also eliminates potential protective scaffolding functions
- Preclinical data showed intracerebral LRRK2 ASO injection reduced alpha-synuclein fibril formation, protected dopaminergic neurons, and improved grip strength in mouse models — with brain-specific effects (no systemic LRRK2 reduction in kidneys/lungs)
- Intrathecal delivery bypasses the blood-brain barrier entirely, achieving direct CNS exposure — but requires lumbar puncture every 4 weeks, a major patient burden for a chronic disease requiring decades of treatment
- Open question: does reducing LRRK2 protein in sporadic PD (without LRRK2 mutations) provide benefit? The REASON trial enrolled both LRRK2-mutant and wild-type patients, and CSF biomarker reductions were observed independent of mutation status, but clinical efficacy was never tested

### Clinical

**REASON Phase 1 (Part A — Single Ascending Dose)** | NCT03976349 | N=40 | PD patients with/without LRRK2 mutations
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → No major safety issues identified
- **Key secondary:** Pharmacokinetics of BIIB094 in blood; dose range 10-150 mg intrathecal
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Established acceptable safety profile for intrathecal ASO delivery in PD patients; enabled multi-dose evaluation

**REASON Phase 1 (Part B — Multiple Ascending Dose)** | NCT03976349 | N=42 | PD patients stratified by LRRK2 variant status
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety profile maintained with repeated dosing (4 doses every 4 weeks)
- **Key secondary:** CSF LRRK2 protein reduced up to **59%**; CSF pRab10 (direct LRRK2 kinase substrate) reduced up to **50%** — both dose-dependent and independent of LRRK2 mutation status. Suppression maintained at 3-month follow-up
- **Exploratory:** Cathepsin lysosomal proteins (B, L, S) reduced up to 25% with return to baseline after treatment discontinuation, suggesting reversible modulation of the LRRK2-endolysosomal network
- **Status:** Completed (August 2024)
- **Interpretation:** First demonstration of LRRK2-ASO modulatory effects on CSF biomarkers in human PD. Dose range 40-120 mg. Robust target engagement confirmed, but program was discontinued before efficacy could be assessed

### Financial
- BIIB094 was developed under the broader 2018 Biogen-Ionis neurology collaboration, which included a $1 billion total payment ($375M upfront + $625M for Ionis equity at $54.34/share) for a 10-year collaboration across multiple neurological disease targets
- Specific milestone and royalty terms for BIIB094 within the collaboration were not publicly disclosed
- Biogen's discontinuation of BIIB094 occurred alongside termination of the BIIB122 LIGHTHOUSE Phase 3 trial and other pipeline cuts, suggesting a broader cost-reduction or prioritization strategy rather than asset-specific failure
- ION859 remains on the Ionis pipeline, implying Ionis may retain rights to advance or re-partner the asset — potential value if another company is willing to pursue LRRK2 ASO with a better delivery strategy
- The intrathecal delivery requirement was likely the primary commercial concern: monthly lumbar punctures severely limit the addressable patient population and make reimbursement challenging vs. oral [[biib122|BIIB122]]

- [[biib122|BIIB122]] (Biogen/Denali) is the dominant LRRK2 competitor — oral small molecule kinase inhibitor with better delivery profile. LIGHTHOUSE Phase 3 was terminated but LUMA Phase 2 continues, now enrolling LRRK2-mutant patients alongside idiopathic PD
- Key modality distinction: ASO (total protein knockdown) vs. kinase inhibitor (enzymatic blockade only). The ASO approach captures kinase-independent LRRK2 functions but at the cost of intrathecal delivery
- [[ion464|ION464]] (Ionis/Biogen) was an intrathecal alpha-synuclein ASO that was also discontinued — pattern suggests Biogen has systematically deprioritized the intrathecal ASO modality in neurodegeneration, not just the LRRK2 target
- If oral LRRK2 inhibitors ([[biib122|BIIB122]]) succeed in LUMA, the ASO approach becomes redundant — the same pathway can be modulated without lumbar punctures
- If oral LRRK2 inhibitors fail on efficacy, the ASO approach could theoretically be revisited under the hypothesis that total protein knockdown (not just kinase inhibition) is needed — but this would require solving the delivery problem (e.g., subcutaneous depot ASO, or AAV-delivered shRNA)

## Analysis

BIIB094's discontinuation illustrates a recurring pattern in neurodegeneration drug development: strong target engagement does not guarantee program survival when delivery logistics undermine the commercial thesis. The REASON trial produced textbook Phase 1 results — dose-dependent, reversible, mutation-agnostic biomarker modulation with clean safety — yet the program was killed before efficacy was ever tested. This was a delivery-driven decision, not a science-driven one.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of re-emergence: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Ionis retains the asset and has demonstrated willingness to re-partner discontinued programs. However, the intrathecal barrier remains unsolved, oral LRRK2 inhibitors are advancing through Phase 2, and no company has publicly expressed interest in licensing ION859. The scenario where this asset returns would require either (a) failure of all oral LRRK2 inhibitors, creating demand for alternative modalities, or (b) a delivery technology breakthrough enabling subcutaneous or depot ASO administration to the CNS.

**Signal analysis:** Biogen's simultaneous discontinuation of BIIB094 and the LIGHTHOUSE trial — while preserving the LUMA trial for [[biib122|BIIB122]] — reveals a clear strategic calculus: consolidate LRRK2 investment into the asset with the best delivery profile and broadest patient population (idiopathic + LRRK2-mutant PD). The company explicitly stated these decisions were "not based on safety or efficacy data," confirming this was portfolio optimization under resource constraints. For the LRRK2 field more broadly, BIIB094's biomarker data actually strengthens the target validation case — 59% LRRK2 knockdown and 50% pRab10 reduction in human CSF is a compelling proof-of-mechanism that benefits [[biib122|BIIB122]] and other LRRK2-targeting programs.
