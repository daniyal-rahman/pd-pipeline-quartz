# CREXONT (IPX203)

## Summary

CREXONT is Amneal Pharmaceuticals' (biotech, AMRX) next-generation extended-release carbidopa/levodopa, FDA-approved August 2024 and launched September 2024 as the designated successor to [[rytary|Rytary]] (IPX066). The RISE-PD pivotal trial demonstrated statistically superior good ON time vs. IR carbidopa/levodopa with a TID vs. QID+ dosing advantage. Launch-quarter revenue was ~$3M (Q3 2024, confirmed); FY2024 total is ~$5-10M est. (not individually disclosed). 2025 guidance was ~$50M per Amneal's February 2025 press release; a January 2026 JPMorgan conference report suggests possible revision to $120M+ based on 2025 actuals — these two figures are not necessarily contradictory if guidance was revised upward mid-year. The commercial thesis is converting Rytary patients as Rytary loses exclusivity Q3 2025, plus capturing new starts. Key risk: payer coverage friction against yet another branded CD/LD formulation.

## Notes

### Science
- **Formulation technology:** Capsule contains mucoadhesive ER beads coated with bioadhesive polymers (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, polycarbophil) that adhere to duodenal/jejunal mucosa, extending drug residence time in the proximal small intestine — the primary levodopa absorption window
- **PK profile:** Rapid initial rise from IR component combined with longer sustained release (~6-7 hours effective absorption vs. ~4-5 hours for Rytary). Plasma fluctuation index lower than IR CD/LD and comparable to or better than Rytary
- **Dosing:** TID (three times daily) achieved in RISE-PD vs. Rytary's typical QID; fewer daily administrations reduce patient burden and pill count
- **Distinction from [[rytary|Rytary]] (IPX066):** Rytary uses coated beads without mucoadhesive properties — dissolution rate alone controls release. CREXONT's bioadhesive layer slows intestinal transit through physical mucosa adhesion, achieving a longer effective absorption window that does not depend solely on bead dissolution kinetics
- **vs. device-based alternatives:** CREXONT is oral — no pump, no cannula, no infusion site reactions. Targets the large moderate-to-advanced PD population optimizing oral therapy before device-based escalation (VYALEV, Duopa, ND0612)

### Clinical

**RISE-PD (Phase 3 Pivotal)** | NCT03670992 | N=616 | PD with motor fluctuations on stable levodopa
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in good ON time (ON without troublesome dyskinesia) at Week 13 → IPX203 statistically superior to IR carbidopa/levodopa (LS mean difference +0.49 hours; p=0.022)
- **Key secondary:** OFF time reduction statistically significant; fewer daily doses (TID vs. QID+ for IR); MDS-UPDRS Part II+III improvement consistent with clinical benefit
- **Safety:** Generally well tolerated; most common AEs consistent with the levodopa class (nausea, dizziness, dyskinesia). No new signals vs. IR carbidopa/levodopa or Rytary
- **Status:** Completed; basis for NDA and FDA approval August 2024
- **Interpretation:** Active comparator design (vs. IR CD/LD, not placebo) makes the result clinically relevant. Key limitation: no head-to-head vs. Rytary, so clinical superiority over the prior-generation ER formulation is inferential (PK modeling, indirect comparison) rather than demonstrated in a randomized trial

**Regulatory history:**
- NDA submitted: H1 2024
- FDA approved: August 2024 (PDUFA met on schedule)
- Commercial launch: September 2024

- **vs. [[rytary|Rytary]]:** CREXONT is the designated successor. Amneal is actively transitioning its own sales force. TID vs. QID dosing advantage is real; RISE-PD superiority vs. IR CD/LD was demonstrated, but head-to-head vs. Rytary was not conducted
- **vs. generic IR carbidopa/levodopa:** Price gap (~$6,000-8,000/yr CREXONT vs. ~$120-200/yr generic) means CREXONT requires payer coverage approvals and step-through requirements. Most patients start generics; CREXONT targets the subgroup with motor fluctuations failing generic optimization
- **vs. [[duopa|Duopa]]/[[vyalev|VYALEV]]:** Advanced infusion therapies for severe motor fluctuations. CREXONT is a step below: oral convenience for moderate fluctuators. Patient flow: generic IR → branded ER (Rytary → CREXONT) → device-based (VYALEV/Duopa/ND0612) → DBS
- **vs. [[nd0612|ND0612]] (if approved):** SC levodopa/carbidopa pump targeting a partially overlapping population. Oral vs. pump preference is the key differentiator — CREXONT retains the large majority who prefer pills

## Analysis

CREXONT's commercial case rests on Amneal's ability to execute the Rytary-to-CREXONT franchise transition. The clinical differentiation from Rytary is real but modest — TID vs. QID dosing and incrementally better PK modeling. Without a direct head-to-head superiority trial, the case rests on PK modeling and dosing convenience rather than a clean clinical win vs. the predecessor. Payers who already fought over Rytary will negotiate similarly for CREXONT.

The Rytary LOE in Q3 2025 is the key commercial catalyst. Amneal's field force will redirect from Rytary to CREXONT, and existing Rytary patients face a choice between generic Rytary (cheaper) and CREXONT (patented, potentially better dosing profile). If patients and physicians see CREXONT as a genuine upgrade — not just a lifecycle management play — the transition can maintain franchise revenue. If generics dominate the switching behavior, the PD franchise contracts.

**Analytical estimate — CREXONT peak annual revenue: $150-250M.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Rytary peaked at ~$200M before LOE pressure. CREXONT has fresh IP (patent protection into the 2030s) and a cleaner clinical story vs. the prior-gen formulation. If Amneal achieves $120M in 2025 (per JPM guidance revision scenario) and grows modestly through 2028 before generic ER competition stabilizes, $200M+ peak is achievable. The $50M 2025 guidance scenario implies a slower ramp with lower peak (~$150M). The wide range reflects uncertainty over payer acceptance and actual Rytary-switching dynamics.
