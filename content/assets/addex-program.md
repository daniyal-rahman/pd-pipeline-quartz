# Dipraglurant (Addex Therapeutics)

## Summary

Dipraglurant is an oral mGluR5 negative allosteric modulator developed by Addex Therapeutics (biotech, ADXN) for levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID) in Parkinson's disease. A Phase 2a trial (NCT01336088, N=76) showed statistically significant anti-dyskinetic effects on days 1 and 14 but the signal faded by day 28. Addex initiated a pivotal Phase 2b/3 trial in June 2021 but terminated it in June 2022 due to slow recruitment (COVID-19 impact). The company has since repositioned dipraglurant toward brain injury recovery (post-stroke/TBI) and spun out its preclinical allosteric modulator platform into Neurosterix ($63M, April 2024). The PD-LID indication is retained on paper but not actively developed. If a partner emerges for PD-LID, the program could restart with the existing Phase 2a data and FDA orphan drug designation. If not, dipraglurant's PD relevance is effectively over, and the LID space is left to [[mesdopetam]] (D3 antagonism) and generic amantadine.

## Notes

### Science
- mGluR5 is a Group I metabotropic glutamate receptor highly expressed in the striatum; in PD patients on chronic levodopa, mGluR5 signaling becomes dysregulated, contributing to the development of dyskinesia through excessive glutamatergic drive on the indirect pathway
- Negative allosteric modulation of mGluR5 reduces this glutamatergic overactivity without fully blocking the receptor, theoretically preserving normal glutamate signaling while dampening the pathological hyperstimulation that produces dyskinesia
- A PET receptor occupancy study in healthy volunteers demonstrated dose-proportional mGluR5 binding: 27% occupancy at 100 mg, 44% at 200 mg, 54% at 300 mg -- confirming CNS target engagement
- Key distinction from NMDA antagonism (amantadine): mGluR5 NAMs act upstream of the ionotropic glutamate receptor, modulating metabotropic signaling rather than blocking ion channel-mediated excitation. This should produce a different side-effect profile and potentially complementary efficacy
- Open scientific question: the Phase 2a signal faded at day 28 (no significance vs. placebo), raising the possibility of receptor adaptation or tolerance with chronic dosing -- a fundamental concern for any LID therapy requiring long-term use
- Received FDA orphan drug designation for PD-LID in January 2016
- The mGluR5 target is shared with the discontinued Prexton/Lundbeck program foliglurax (PXT002331), an mGluR4 PAM -- a related but distinct glutamate receptor approach that also failed to advance

### Clinical

**Phase 2a (ADX48621-201)** | NCT01336088 | N=76 | PD patients with moderate-to-severe LID
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability -- met; clean safety profile
- **Key secondary:** Modified Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (mAIMS) -- dipraglurant significantly reduced peak-dose dyskinesia on day 1 (50 mg, 20% reduction, p=0.04) and day 14 (100 mg, 32% reduction, p=0.04). Three-hour post-dose mAIMS also significant on day 14 (p=0.04)
- **Day 28 result:** No statistically significant improvement vs. placebo, attributed to increased placebo response or possible tolerance
- **Additional findings:** ON time without dyskinesia increased by +1.25 to +2.3 hours across weeks 1-4; OFF time reduced by 50 minutes at week 4; no worsening of parkinsonism (stable UPDRS-III)
- **Safety:** Two discontinuations due to AEs; most common AEs: dyskinesia, dizziness, nausea, fatigue
- **Status:** Completed (2012; published 2016, Movement Disorders)
- **Interpretation:** Encouraging short-term anti-dyskinetic signal but the loss of significance at day 28 is a serious concern. The dose-escalation design (50 mg to 100 mg TID) complicates interpretation. Publication in Movement Disorders and subsequent FDA orphan designation confirmed the signal was considered real by regulators.

**Phase 2b/3 Pivotal (Study 301)** | NCT TBD | N=140 (planned) | PD patients with LID
- **Primary endpoint:** UDysRS at week 12 vs. baseline
- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled; dipraglurant 150 mg/day x 1 week then 300 mg/day (100 mg TID) vs. placebo; 12-month open-label extension planned
- **Status:** Initiated June 2021; **terminated June 2022** due to slow recruitment attributed to COVID-19 patient hesitancy and site staffing shortages
- **Interpretation:** The termination was operational, not safety- or efficacy-related. However, the inability to enroll 140 patients across ~50 US sites over 12 months raises questions about the practicality of the PD-LID indication and the competitiveness of the trial design.

### Financial
- **Addex market cap:** Micro-cap (~$10-20M range); listed on Nasdaq and SIX Swiss Exchange (ADXN)
- **Neurosterix spin-off (April 2024):** Addex divested its preclinical allosteric modulator platform to Neurosterix, backed by $63M from Perceptive Xontogeny Venture Fund II, Perceptive Life Sciences, and Acorn Bioventures. Addex retained a 20% equity interest in Neurosterix and kept clinical-stage assets including dipraglurant
- **Current focus:** Dipraglurant repositioned toward brain injury recovery (post-stroke/TBI); the PD-LID indication is retained but not actively funded
- **MJFF support:** The Michael J. Fox Foundation previously provided grants for dipraglurant pre-Phase 2b studies, signaling external validation of the PD-LID concept
- **Orphan drug designation:** Grants 7 years US market exclusivity from potential launch plus reduced development costs -- a meaningful commercial incentive if the program ever restarts
- **Cash position:** Limited; 2024 net loss from continuing operations was CHF 4.9M. The company cannot fund a PD-LID Phase 2b/3 independently

- [[mesdopetam]] is the most directly competitive LID program: oral D3 receptor antagonist, Phase 3-ready with FDA/EMA alignment, but also unpartnered and underfunded. Both programs share the same fundamental challenge -- LID is a real unmet need but a difficult commercial market against generic amantadine
- Amantadine ER (Gocovri/ADS-5102, now generic) is the only approved pharmacological LID treatment; its availability as generic creates significant pricing pressure for any new entrant
- The mGluR5 NAM mechanism overlaps conceptually with the discontinued mGluR4 PAM approach (foliglurax/PXT002331, Prexton/Lundbeck) -- both target glutamate modulation in the basal ganglia but through different receptor subtypes. Foliglurax's failure in Phase 2 weakened enthusiasm for glutamate receptor approaches to LID broadly
- If dipraglurant PD-LID is ever restarted and succeeds: first mGluR5-based anti-dyskinetic therapy, potentially complementary to amantadine (different mechanism), orphan drug exclusivity provides commercial protection
- If it remains deprioritized: the glutamate receptor modulation approach to LID loses its last active clinical program, and the field defaults to [[mesdopetam]] (D3 antagonism) or non-pharmacological interventions (DBS, LCIG)

## Analysis

Dipraglurant's PD-LID program is effectively in limbo. The Phase 2a data showed a real but fragile anti-dyskinetic signal -- statistically significant at early timepoints but not at day 28. The Phase 2b/3 termination due to enrollment failure (not drug failure) means the efficacy question remains unanswered. Addex's strategic pivot toward brain injury recovery and the Neurosterix spin-off suggest the company is deprioritizing PD-LID in favor of indications with clearer paths to value creation.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of PD-LID program restarting: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: micro-cap biotechs successfully restarting terminated programs in competitive indications ~5-10%
- Adjustments upward: orphan drug designation provides commercial incentive (+5%), existing Phase 2a data reduces de novo development cost (+5%), mGluR5 mechanism is scientifically differentiated from amantadine (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: company has explicitly pivoted toward brain injury (-10%), cash position is insufficient for independent development (-5%), Phase 2a day 28 tolerance signal raises fundamental efficacy concerns (-5%), generic amantadine limits commercial attractiveness for potential partners (-5%)
- Net: ~10-15% that PD-LID is ever re-prosecuted

**Signal analysis:** The sequence of events tells a clear story -- Phase 2a showed promise (2012), orphan designation validated the concept (2016), but the program stalled for years before the Phase 2b/3 attempt (2021), which failed operationally within 12 months (2022). The subsequent Neurosterix spin-off and brain injury pivot (2024) confirm that Addex management sees more value elsewhere. The PD-LID indication is retained as an option but not as a priority. For anyone tracking the LID space, [[mesdopetam]] is the active program to watch, not dipraglurant.
