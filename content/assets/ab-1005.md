# AB-1005 (AAV2-GDNF)

## Summary

AB-1005 is AskBio's (biotech, wholly owned by Bayer) one-time GDNF gene therapy delivered directly into the putamen via MRI-guided convection-enhanced delivery, currently in the Phase 2 REGENERATE-PD trial (NCT06285643) enrolling ~87 patients across the US, UK, Germany, and Poland. The Phase 1b trial (NCT04167540, N=11) met its primary safety endpoint with no product-related serious adverse events through 36 months and showed trends toward motor improvement in the moderate PD cohort on MDS-UPDRS and motor diaries, with reductions in levodopa-equivalent daily dose. AB-1005 has accumulated an unusually strong regulatory designation portfolio -- FDA RMAT, FDA Fast Track, UK MHRA Innovation Passport, and Japan SAKIGAKE (Pioneering Regenerative Medical Product) -- signaling regulatory confidence across three major markets. If REGENERATE-PD demonstrates disease modification in the randomized controlled setting, this would be the first neurotrophic factor gene therapy to succeed in PD after decades of GDNF protein delivery failures, validating the hypothesis that continuous local expression via gene therapy overcomes the pharmacokinetic limitations that sank earlier approaches. If it fails, the neurotrophic factor thesis in PD takes another major hit, and the gene therapy modality in PD narrows to enzyme replacement approaches like AADC gene therapy.

## Notes

### Science
- GDNF is the most potent known survival factor for dopaminergic neurons, identified in 1993 based on its ability to promote embryonic dopamine neuron survival in vitro. It signals through the RET receptor tyrosine kinase, triggering intracellular cascades that support neuronal health, axonal sprouting, and dopamine synthesis
- AB-1005 uses AAV serotype 2 (AAV2), which has exclusive neuronal tropism when delivered directly to the brain, ensuring the transgene is expressed only in neurons rather than glia or other cell types
- Delivery is via one-time bilateral MRI-guided convection-enhanced delivery (CED) to the putamen, achieving 63% (+/-2%) target putamen coverage in Phase 1b -- a critical advance over earlier GDNF protein infusion studies where inadequate distribution was blamed for clinical failures
- The gene therapy approach solves the fundamental pharmacokinetic problem that plagued GDNF protein delivery: recombinant GDNF protein has poor tissue penetration and requires continuous infusion hardware, while AAV2-GDNF produces stable, continuous local expression from a single administration. Post-mortem analysis in one patient showed persistent GDNF expression 45 months after infusion
- Historical context matters: GDNF protein delivery trials (Amgen, 2004-2006) and neurturin gene therapy (CERE-120/Ceregene) both failed in randomized trials. The failures were attributed to (a) patient selection (too advanced), (b) inadequate target coverage, and (c) for protein delivery, inability to achieve sustained therapeutic concentrations. AB-1005 addresses all three: moderate (not advanced) patients, CED with MRI monitoring for coverage, and gene therapy for sustained expression
- Key distinction vs. [[cavgene|CavGene (CGT-102/103)]]: AB-1005 aims to rescue dopaminergic neurons via neurotrophic support, while CavGene uses AAV to silence calcium channels for dyskinesia. Different targets, different patient populations, same delivery modality
- Open question: whether GDNF-driven dopaminergic neuron rescue can meaningfully slow disease in moderate-stage PD where substantial neuronal loss has already occurred. The Phase 1b signal favoring moderate over mild cohort is counterintuitive but may reflect a floor effect in mild patients

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (NCT01621581)** | N=13 (of intended 24) | Advanced PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability of bilateral intraputaminal AAV2-GDNF at 4 escalating dose levels (9x10^10 to 3x10^12 vg)
- **Key findings:** Well tolerated, no cerebral parenchyma toxicity on serial MRI up to 5 years post-infusion; increased 18F-DOPA PET uptake at infusion sites suggesting GDNF bioactivity
- **Status:** Completed (initiated 2013, NIH/NINDS-sponsored)
- **Interpretation:** Proof-of-concept for safety and putaminal GDNF expression; conducted in advanced PD patients who may be beyond the therapeutic window for neurotrophic rescue

**Phase 1b (NCT04167540)** | N=11 | Mild (n=6) and Moderate (n=5) PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability at 18 months --> MET. No product-related serious adverse events through 36 months of follow-up
- **Key secondary:** MDS-UPDRS motor scores showed numerical stability (mild cohort) and improvement trends (moderate cohort) at 18 and 36 months. Motor diary self-assessments improved in moderate cohort. Trends toward LEDD reduction in moderate cohort. DaTSCAN assessed dopaminergic network integrity
- **Putaminal coverage:** 63% (+/-2%), surpassing the >50% target
- **Status:** Completed; follow-up ongoing to 5 years
- **Interpretation:** Small, open-label, uncontrolled -- standard caveats apply. The moderate > mild differential response is notable and shaped the Phase 2 population selection. Safety profile is clean, which is critical for a neurosurgical gene therapy. RMAT designation was granted on these data, indicating FDA sees the signal as meaningful

**REGENERATE-PD (Phase 2)** | NCT06285643 | N=~87 | Moderate PD (ages 45-75)
- **Primary endpoint:** Efficacy and safety (specific primary endpoint measure not publicly disclosed)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, surgery-controlled (sham surgery comparator)
- **Sites:** US, UK, Germany, Poland
- **Status:** US enrollment initiated January 2025; first European participants randomized September 2025; enrollment ongoing
- **Interpretation:** Surgery-controlled design is the gold standard for neurosurgical gene therapy trials -- critical after earlier open-label GDNF studies were criticized for placebo effects from the surgical procedure itself. The ~87 patient enrollment is appropriately powered for a Phase 2 signal-detection study. Results expected 2027-2028

**REGENERATE MSA-101 (Phase 1)** | NCT TBD | N=11 | Multiple System Atrophy-Parkinsonian type (MSA-P)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety of AB-1005 in MSA-P
- **Status:** Enrollment completed September 2025; 3-year follow-up period; results after all participants complete 1 year of blinded assessments
- **Interpretation:** MSA-P expansion represents optionality beyond PD. MSA involves striatonigral degeneration -- if GDNF can rescue striatal neurons, the mechanism applies. This is a higher unmet need population with no approved disease-modifying treatments

- **Acquisition context:** Bayer acquired AskBio in Q4 2020 for ~$2B upfront + ~$2B in milestone-based contingent payments (~$4B total). AB-1005 was one of several programs in the AskBio portfolio, not the sole driver of the acquisition price
- **Bayer is not publicly traded on US exchanges** but trades as BAYRY (ADR) and BAYN on the Frankfurt exchange. AskBio operates as a wholly owned subsidiary
- **Investment signal:** Bayer's continued investment through Phase 2 across two indications (PD and MSA-P), plus REGENERATE-PD's multi-country expansion to European sites, signals sustained commitment post-acquisition. Bayer's broader gene therapy portfolio has faced setbacks (e.g., hemophilia), making PD program success increasingly important for justifying the AskBio acquisition
- **Regulatory designations as value drivers:** RMAT (Feb 2025), Fast Track, MHRA Innovation Passport, and SAKIGAKE (Dec 2025) collectively position AB-1005 for accelerated review pathways in the US, UK, and Japan -- potentially shortening time-to-market by 1-2 years
- **No peak sales estimates available** from public sources. For context, a disease-modifying PD gene therapy with one-time administration would command premium pricing ($300K-$1M+ per treatment based on gene therapy precedents like Zolgensma), but the neurosurgical delivery limits the addressable population to patients willing and able to undergo brain surgery

- AB-1005 is currently the only GDNF-targeted gene therapy in active clinical development for PD. The earlier CERE-120 (AAV2-neurturin, Ceregene) used the related neurotrophic factor neurturin and failed two Phase 2 trials -- AB-1005 must demonstrate that GDNF is the superior ligand and that improved delivery technique (CED with MRI monitoring) overcomes the coverage problems that plagued CERE-120
- VY-AADC (Voyager/Neurocrine) represented a different gene therapy approach in PD -- delivering the AADC enzyme to convert levodopa to dopamine more efficiently. That program was discontinued in 2021 after an FDA clinical hold, removing a direct gene therapy competitor but also casting a shadow on the modality
- The broader competitive frame is disease modification vs. symptom management: AB-1005 competes not just with other gene therapies but with [[prasinezumab]] (antibody), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (siRNA), [[biib122|BIIB122]] (small molecule LRRK2), and cell therapy approaches. The neurosurgical delivery requirement is a significant competitive disadvantage for scalability but a potential advantage for direct CNS target engagement
- If REGENERATE-PD succeeds, it validates neurotrophic factor gene therapy as a modality and could revive interest in other neurotrophic targets. The one-time administration and potential for sustained benefit differentiate it from chronic dosing approaches like [[prasinezumab]] (monthly IV) or [[aro-snca]] (quarterly SC)
- Japan SAKIGAKE designation creates a potential fast-follower regulatory path -- Japan could approve AB-1005 before the US/EU if an early efficacy signal emerges, providing real-world evidence to support broader approval

## Analysis

The AB-1005 program is best understood as the latest -- and potentially most sophisticated -- attempt to make the GDNF hypothesis work in PD after 30 years of failures. The target biology is strong: GDNF is unambiguously neuroprotective for dopaminergic neurons in every preclinical model tested. The historical failures were not failures of biology but of delivery -- recombinant protein could not achieve sustained, targeted concentrations in the putamen. AB-1005's gene therapy approach directly addresses this by enabling continuous local production of GDNF from a single neurosurgical procedure. The Phase 1b evidence of persistent expression at 45 months in post-mortem tissue and 63% putaminal coverage validates the delivery hypothesis.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 2 success probability: 20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: neurotrophic factor therapies in PD are 0 for ~6 in controlled trials (GDNF protein infusion, neurturin gene therapy) --> starting point ~5-10%
- Adjustments upward: gene therapy delivery solves the historical pharmacokinetic problem (+10%), moderate rather than advanced patient selection addresses prior trial design errors (+5%), surgery-controlled design with MRI-monitored delivery is methodologically superior to all prior attempts (+5%), 36-month Phase 1b safety/efficacy trends in moderate cohort (+5%), unprecedented regulatory designation accumulation (RMAT + SAKIGAKE) suggests regulators see meaningful signal (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: open-label Phase 1b with N=11 is very thin evidence (-5%), neurosurgical delivery introduces placebo/sham surgery effects that are notoriously large in PD (-5%), GDNF protein failed even in well-powered randomized trials with adequate coverage per imaging (-5%)
- Net: ~20-25%

**Signal analysis:** Bayer's behavior post-acquisition is the most informative signal. They paid $2B upfront for the entire AskBio platform, not just AB-1005, but their continued investment through multi-country Phase 2 expansion and pursuit of regulatory designations in three major markets indicates internal conviction. The SAKIGAKE designation in Japan (December 2025) is particularly telling -- pursuing this requires active engagement with PMDA and commitment to a Japanese development program, which Bayer would not undertake for a low-conviction asset. The MSA-P expansion (Phase 1, enrollment complete) suggests Bayer sees neurotrophic gene therapy as a platform play across synucleinopathies, not a single-indication bet.

The key decision tree: if REGENERATE-PD shows statistically significant efficacy in the surgery-controlled design, this transforms into a registration-enabling asset with accelerated pathways in three markets. Given the one-time administration model and premium gene therapy pricing, even a moderate-sized PD subpopulation (moderate stage, surgical candidates) could support a multi-billion dollar franchise. If it fails, the neurotrophic factor hypothesis in PD is likely permanently shelved, and Bayer's AskBio acquisition thesis takes a significant hit -- though the broader AskBio platform (CDMO, other indications) retains independent value.
