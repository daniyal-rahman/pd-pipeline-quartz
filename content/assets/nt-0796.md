# NT-0796

## Summary

NodThera (UK biotech, private) completed a Phase 1b/2a biomarker study showing NT-0796 reversed NLRP3-mediated neuroinflammation in PD patients over 28 days -- CSF pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, CCL2) dropped to levels approximating healthy elderly controls, with a 13% reduction in CSF NfL after one week. This is the first clinical demonstration that an oral NLRP3 inhibitor can engage its target in the CNS of PD patients and move neurodegeneration biomarkers. NodThera is now pivoting its PD program to NT-0150, a next-generation compound optimized for brain penetration, with SAD/MAD studies initiated H2 2025 and a Phase 2 trial planned. If NT-0150 Phase 2 shows clinical motor benefit beyond biomarker changes, it validates the neuroinflammation thesis for PD disease modification and opens a new target class alongside [[prasinezumab|alpha-synuclein]] and [[biib122|LRRK2]] approaches. If biomarker changes fail to translate to clinical endpoints, NLRP3 inhibition joins the long list of mechanism-positive, outcome-negative PD programs.

## Notes

### Science
- NLRP3 inflammasomes are multiprotein cytosolic complexes in microglia and peripheral immune cells that activate in response to pathological stress (misfolded alpha-synuclein, mitochondrial damage, oxidative stress), triggering release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1beta and IL-18
- NT-0796 is an isopropyl ester prodrug that converts to its active carboxylic acid form in human immune cells; potent inhibition with IC50 of 6.8 nM in human blood assays
- Brain penetration is a key differentiator: blood-to-brain ratio of 0.79 in mice; in the PD trial, CSF drug levels were 2-3x higher than in elderly healthy volunteers at equivalent plasma concentrations, suggesting disease-state-dependent enhanced CNS exposure
- Genetic validation: NLRP3-driven neuroinflammation is implicated in multiple PD pathways -- alpha-synuclein aggregates directly activate NLRP3 in microglia, creating a feedforward loop of inflammation and protein aggregation. LRRK2 mutations (the most common genetic PD cause) also activate NLRP3 signaling
- Key scientific question: NLRP3 inhibition addresses a downstream consequence (neuroinflammation) rather than a proximal cause (alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1). Whether dampening inflammation alone is sufficient for disease modification -- or whether it must be combined with targeting the upstream trigger -- remains unknown
- NodThera has pivoted PD development to NT-0150, a next-generation compound with a novel pyridazinone chemotype optimized for exceptional CNS exposure; NT-0796 itself is being repositioned toward cardiometabolic indications (obesity, cardiovascular risk)

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (First-in-Human)** | ACTRN12621001082897 | N=76 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability --> Clean safety profile; SADs 1-300 mg and MADs well tolerated
- **Key secondary:** Dose-proportional pharmacokinetics; reduced inflammatory cytokines in ex vivo blood assays confirming target engagement
- **Status:** Completed (2021-2022)
- **Interpretation:** Established PK/PD relationship and safety; enabled disease-population studies

**Phase 1b/2a (PD Biomarker Study)** | Not registered on ClinicalTrials.gov | N=not disclosed | PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Effect on inflammatory and disease-specific biomarkers in CSF and blood over 28 days at 150 mg BID
- **CSF results:** Mean reductions in IL-1beta, IL-6, CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL8 to levels approximating healthy elderly controls; 13% reduction in NfL after 1 week; reduction in sTREM2 (microglial activation marker)
- **Peripheral results:** In subjects with elevated CRP and fibrinogen at baseline, significant reductions consistent with peripheral anti-inflammatory effect
- **Safety:** Safe and well tolerated; AEs mainly mild and transient; no SAEs observed
- **Status:** Completed; results published in Movement Disorders (Aug 2025)
- **Interpretation:** First clinical proof that an oral NLRP3 inhibitor achieves CNS target engagement in PD patients and moves neurodegenerative biomarkers (NfL, sTREM2). The 28-day duration and biomarker-only endpoints are a limitation -- no clinical motor outcomes were measured. The NfL reduction in 1 week is surprisingly rapid and may reflect dampening of active neuroinflammatory damage rather than structural neuroprotection.

**Phase 1b/2a (Obesity/CV Risk)** | NCT TBD | Obese subjects with cardiovascular risk
- **Primary endpoint:** CRP reduction --> Met primary endpoint; highly statistically significant CRP reduction vs. placebo; >75% of NT-0796 subjects achieved CRP <=2 mg/L vs. <25% placebo at day 28
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Validates peripheral anti-inflammatory activity; this indication is now NT-0796's primary commercial pathway

- **Roche/Inflazome (inzomelid/emlenoflast):** Roche acquired Inflazome for EUR 380M (2020) for brain-penetrant NLRP3 inhibitor inzomelid, but withdrew the Phase 1 PD trial before it started, citing "business reasons." Roche pivoted to a related compound selnoflast. This withdrawal creates an opening for NodThera as potentially the only company with positive PD clinical biomarker data for NLRP3 inhibition
- **Novartis/IFM Tre (IFM-2427):** Acquired for $310M+ in 2019; IFM-2427 is a systemic NLRP3 antagonist focused on inflammatory/fibrotic diseases, not CNS-penetrant. Preclinical CNS molecules remain undisclosed. NodThera's brain-penetrant profile is a clear differentiator
- **Key distinction:** NT-0796/NT-0150 are differentiated by demonstrated brain penetration with clinical CSF biomarker proof. Most competing NLRP3 programs (Novartis, others) target peripheral inflammation and are not designed to cross the BBB
- NLRP3 inhibition in PD is mechanistically complementary to -- not competitive with -- alpha-synuclein targeting ([[prasinezumab]]), [[biib122|LRRK2 inhibition]], and [[pariceract|GBA1 activation]]. Future PD combination regimens could pair NLRP3 inhibition with these upstream approaches
- If NodThera's NLRP3 program succeeds clinically in PD, it validates neuroinflammation as a tractable PD target and could attract partnership interest from the big pharma companies already invested in the space (Roche, Novartis)

## Analysis

NT-0796 represents the leading clinical evidence that NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition can reverse neuroinflammation in PD patients. The biomarker data is internally consistent: CSF cytokine reductions confirm CNS target engagement, NfL reduction suggests reduced neuronal damage, and sTREM2 reduction confirms dampened microglial activation. The fact that CSF drug levels exceeded predictions (2-3x higher than in healthy elderly at equivalent plasma levels) raises intriguing questions about disease-state-dependent BBB permeability that could favor drug exposure in PD brains.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of PD disease modification success (any NLRP3 inhibitor reaching Phase 3 with positive motor outcomes): 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: neuroinflammation-targeting therapies in neurodegeneration have a poor track record; anti-TNF, anti-IL-1, and other anti-inflammatory approaches have not succeeded in PD or AD clinical trials --> starting point ~5-10%
- Adjustments upward: NLRP3 is more mechanistically specific than broad anti-inflammatory approaches (+5%); genetic validation linking NLRP3 to PD pathways (alpha-synuclein activation, LRRK2 signaling) (+5%); first-ever CSF biomarker proof of target engagement in PD patients (+5%); NfL reduction in 1 week suggests active process being modified (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: biomarker changes may not translate to clinical outcomes (-5%); 28-day study too short to assess durability (-3%); NodThera pivoting to NT-0150 introduces compound-switch risk (-3%); no clinical motor endpoint data yet (-2%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:**
- NodThera's decision to pivot PD to NT-0150 while repositioning NT-0796 toward cardiometabolic disease is revealing. It suggests NT-0796's CNS properties, while sufficient for proof-of-concept, were not optimal for chronic PD dosing. NT-0150's pyridazinone chemotype with "exceptional CNS exposure" implies meaningfully better brain penetration -- this compound switch is rational but adds 2+ years to the PD development timeline.
- The April 2025 Series D (GBP 38.5M) came shortly after the PD biomarker data disclosure, suggesting investors are backing the neuroinflammation thesis. The investor syndicate (Novo, Sofinnova, 5AM) is high-quality life sciences venture.
- Roche's withdrawal of inzomelid from PD trials despite a EUR 380M acquisition is a negative signal for the NLRP3-in-PD thesis, but may reflect Roche-specific portfolio priorities rather than target invalidation. NodThera's positive biomarker data post-dates Roche's withdrawal.
- The combination therapy opportunity is the long-term strategic value: if [[prasinezumab]] or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] demonstrate alpha-synuclein-driven disease modification, adding NLRP3 inhibition to address the inflammatory cascade could become the standard combination approach -- analogous to statin + anti-inflammatory in cardiovascular disease.
