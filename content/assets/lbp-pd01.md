# LB-P4

## Summary

LB-P4 is an oral live biotherapeutic product (LBP) being developed by LISCure Biosciences (Korean biotech, private) in partnership with Celltrion (KOSPI: 068270) to target gut-brain axis dysfunction in Parkinson's disease. The program is in preclinical/discovery stage with no public clinical timeline. LISCure's website lists LB-P4 at "preclinical PoC" alongside two Celltrion-partnered candidates (LC-PC01 and LC-PC02) also in preclinical PoC for PD. The underlying science -- that gut dysbiosis drives neuroinflammation and alpha-synuclein propagation from gut to brain -- is increasingly validated by academic research, but no microbiome-based therapeutic has yet reached clinical proof of concept in PD. If LB-P4 advances to Phase 1 and shows biomarker engagement, it would be a first-mover in a genuinely novel modality for PD; if the program stalls, it reflects the broader difficulty of translating microbiome science into drug development.

## Notes

### Science
- LISCure's LMT (LISCure Microbiology-based Technology) platform screens and optimizes microbial candidates for therapeutic use, combining genomics-based screening, microbial optimization, and a proprietary microbiome database
- The core mechanism targets gut dysbiosis in PD -- studies show >30% of gut microbial species are altered in PD patients, with reduced anti-inflammatory taxa (Lachnospiraceae, Prevotellaceae) and increased pro-inflammatory species (Verrucomicrobiaceae)
- Scientific rationale: alpha-synuclein aggregates may originate in the enteric nervous system and propagate to the brain via the vagal nerve (the "body-first" PD hypothesis). Braak staging supports this -- alpha-synuclein pathology appears in the gut up to 20 years before motor diagnosis
- LISCure separately develops ExoPN-101, a BBB-penetrating bacterial exosome shuttle platform that can deliver ASOs, siRNAs, and small molecules into the brain. Non-human primate data confirms BBB penetration with minimal immunogenicity. Four MTAs signed with global pharma partners as of late 2025
- LB-P4 appears to be a standalone LBP (live bacteria), while LC-PC01 and LC-PC02 (Celltrion-partnered) may incorporate the Exo-Pass N brain shuttle technology -- but this distinction is not publicly clarified
- Key open questions: (1) which specific bacterial strains or consortia compose LB-P4; (2) whether the mechanism is direct (bacterial exosomes crossing BBB) or indirect (modulating gut inflammation to reduce alpha-synuclein seeding); (3) what the preclinical PoC endpoints are
- The fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) literature provides partial validation: the GUT-PARFECT Phase 2 trial showed mild but long-lasting motor benefit from a single FMT in early PD patients, and multiple RCTs show MDS-UPDRS improvement in FMT groups. However, LBPs are a more targeted and reproducible approach than FMT

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. The program remains at preclinical proof-of-concept stage as of February 2026.

- LISCure anticipated bringing LB-P4 into Phase 1 in late 2023 per early reporting, but no IND filing or trial registration has been publicly disclosed
- LISCure's most advanced clinical program is LB-P8 (Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis), which received FDA IND clearance and Fast Track designation and began Phase 2 dosing in the US in November 2025 -- demonstrating the company can execute clinical development
- The Celltrion-partnered PD candidates (LC-PC01, LC-PC02) are listed at preclinical PoC on LISCure's website with no public timeline

### Financial
- **LISCure total funding:** ~$47.5M across seed ($5M, 2019), Series B ($21M, 2021), and Series C ($13.64M, January 2025)
- **Celltrion deal terms:** Undisclosed. LISCure receives research funding with additional milestone payments as the program progresses. Deal structure suggests Celltrion has clinical development and commercialization rights
- **Celltrion context:** Major Korean biosimilar manufacturer (KOSPI: 068270, ~KRW 46-47T market cap). H1 2025 revenue $1.31B (+11.9% YoY). Expanding beyond biosimilars into novel therapeutics -- the LISCure partnership is part of this pivot
- **Comparable microbiome deals:** Seres Therapeutics' VOWST (fecal microbiota product) was approved by FDA in 2023 for C. difficile, validating the LBP regulatory pathway. Vedanta Biosciences raised >$100M before discontinuing operations in 2024, illustrating microbiome sector risk
- **PD market context:** Global PD treatment market valued at ~$3.5B (2019), projected to reach $11.5B by 2029 (12.6% CAGR). A disease-modifying microbiome therapy would address a completely unmet segment

- No direct competitors in the microbiome-for-PD therapeutic space have reached clinical stage. This is a genuinely novel modality in the PD pipeline, which is both an opportunity (first-mover) and a risk (no clinical validation path established)
- Indirect competition from FMT trials: multiple academic groups are running FMT studies in PD (GUT-PARFECT at Ghent, NCT03808389, and others), but FMT is a crude intervention vs. defined LBP products
- [[lixisenatide|Lixisenatide]] (GLP-1 agonist) represents a different approach to gut-brain axis modulation -- the LY3154207 LIXIPARK trial showed motor benefit, possibly through gut-mediated mechanisms in addition to direct CNS effects
- The alpha-synuclein gut-to-brain propagation hypothesis, if validated by programs like [[prasinezumab]] or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], would strengthen the rationale for microbiome interventions that reduce enteric alpha-synuclein seeding
- Neuroinflammation-focused competitors like [[muna-therapeutics|Muna Therapeutics]] or NLRP3 inhibitor programs target overlapping biology (inflammation driving neurodegeneration) via different mechanisms

## Analysis

LB-P4 sits at the frontier of PD therapeutics -- scientifically compelling but clinically unproven. The gut-brain axis hypothesis in PD has accumulated substantial evidence: Braak staging, epidemiological data (vagotomy reducing PD risk), metagenomics studies showing consistent dysbiosis signatures, and the GUT-PARFECT FMT trial demonstrating motor benefit. The question is whether a defined microbial product can replicate and improve upon these signals.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching Phase 2: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for preclinical microbiome programs reaching Phase 2 in CNS: very low, <10% historically given the sector's high attrition (Vedanta, Finch Therapeutics both failed)
- Adjustments upward: LISCure has demonstrated clinical execution with LB-P8 reaching Phase 2 (+5%), Celltrion partnership provides regulatory and manufacturing expertise (+5%), the FMT clinical data provides partial target validation (+5%), FDA has established a regulatory pathway for LBPs (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: no public preclinical data for LB-P4 (-5%), missed anticipated 2023 Phase 1 timeline suggests development challenges (-5%), PD is a harder indication than liver or GI disease for microbiome approaches (-3%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:**
- Celltrion's involvement signals strategic seriousness -- as a ~$35B market cap company, the partnership is small relative to their biosimilar franchise but represents a deliberate bet on novel modalities. However, undisclosed deal terms and lack of public milestones suggest this remains early-stage exploration rather than high-conviction investment.
- LISCure's focus appears to be shifting toward ExoPN-101 (the BBB shuttle platform, with four pharma MTAs) and LB-P8 (PSC, in Phase 2). The PD program may be deprioritized relative to these more advanced assets.
- The most important near-term signal would be IND filing or disclosure of preclinical PoC data. Without this, the program remains speculative. The broader microbiome-PD field needs a clinical proof-of-concept win -- if an academic FMT trial delivers robust Phase 2 data, it would catalyze investment into defined LBP products like LB-P4.
