# KM-819

## Summary

KM-819 is a first-in-class oral FAF1 (Fas-associated factor 1) inhibitor developed by FAScinate Therapeutics (startup, San Diego subsidiary of Kainos Medicine, KOSDAQ: 284620) targeting disease modification in PD through a dual mechanism: restoring autophagy to clear alpha-synuclein and blocking FAF1-mediated dopaminergic cell death. Phase 1 in healthy volunteers (N=88, Korea) showed acceptable safety and PK; Phase 2 Part 1a (NCT05670782) in healthy elderly confirmed safety at doses up to 800 mg. However, a parallel Kainos-sponsored Phase 2 trial in MSA (NCT05695378) was terminated due to drug-induced liver injury (DILI) characterized as autoimmune-like hepatitis — a major red flag that hangs over the PD program. The PD Phase 2 Part 1b in PD patients is ongoing but no longer recruiting, with the critical Part 2 efficacy component (24-month, placebo-controlled) still to come. If Part 1b clears the DILI concern and Part 2 shows motor slowing, this validates a novel anti-apoptotic/pro-autophagy mechanism distinct from direct alpha-synuclein targeting. If the liver signal recurs in PD patients, the program is likely dead — and there is no obvious backup asset from FAScinate.

## Notes

### Science
- FAF1 is a pro-apoptotic protein normally degraded by parkin (an E3 ubiquitin ligase via K48-linked ubiquitination). In PD, loss of parkin function — from PARK2 mutations or from oxidative stress — leads to FAF1 accumulation, which triggers caspase activation, ROS generation, JNK activation, and dopaminergic neuron death
- Elevated FAF1 also activates mTOR, suppressing autophagosome formation and thereby blocking autophagy-mediated clearance of alpha-synuclein. The result is a vicious cycle: FAF1 accumulation causes both direct neuronal death AND alpha-synuclein buildup (Lewy body formation)
- KM-819 breaks this cycle by inhibiting FAF1, restoring autophagic flux for alpha-synuclein degradation while simultaneously blocking the apoptotic cascade
- In MPTP mouse models, KM-819 showed neurorestorative effects: [18F]FE-PE2I PET imaging showed striatal dopamine transporter BPND improved from -48% to -29% vs. controls, and tyrosine hydroxylase expression was significantly increased. However, midbrain recovery was not observed, suggesting parkin protein availability may be critical for full efficacy
- FAF1 colocalizes with alpha-synuclein in post-mortem PD brains, and FAF1 levels are significantly elevated in PD vs. healthy controls — providing direct human pathological validation
- Key distinction from other alpha-synuclein approaches: KM-819 promotes intracellular clearance through autophagy restoration, whereas [[prasinezumab]] targets extracellular aggregates and [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] reduces production at the mRNA level. These are complementary rather than competing mechanisms
- FAScinate is also testing KM-819 for multiple system atrophy (MSA), another synucleinopathy, though that program has been halted (see Clinical section)
- Open question: whether the DILI signal from the MSA trial is dose-dependent, population-specific, or intrinsic to FAF1 inhibition. This is the single most important scientific/safety question for the program

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (SAD/MAD)** | NCT03022799 | N=88 (66 KM-819, 22 placebo) | Healthy volunteers (Korea)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Favorable; no serious adverse events
- **SAD doses:** 10, 30, 100, 200, 400 mg single dose
- **MAD doses:** 30, 100, 200, 400 mg once daily for 7 days
- **PK findings:** SAD showed less-than-dose-proportional exposure; MAD showed approximately dose-proportional exposure across 30-400 mg range. Half-life: 1.8-4.8 hours at lower doses, ~9 hours at higher doses. Elderly subjects showed 191% higher Cmax and 102% higher AUC at steady state (200 mg)
- **PD findings:** No clear treatment-related pharmacodynamic effects on cognitive/mood assessments or biomarkers (alpha-synuclein, tau) — lacked dose-response pattern
- **Status:** Completed (January 2019)
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety in short-duration healthy volunteer studies but notably no PD biomarker signal, and the elderly PK differences warranted further investigation

**Phase 2 Part 1a (MAD in healthy elderly)** | NCT05670782 (Part 1a) | N=TBD | Healthy adults 40+
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability of 400, 600, 800 mg in healthy older adults → Favorable safety profile at all doses including 800 mg
- **Status:** Completed (January 2023)
- **Interpretation:** Extended dose range beyond Phase 1 without safety signals in healthy subjects. However, short-duration studies may not capture the autoimmune-like hepatitis seen in longer MSA dosing

**Phase 2 Part 1b (MAD in PD patients)** | NCT05670782 (Part 1b) | N=TBD | PD patients (Hoehn & Yahr ≤4)
- **Doses:** 200, 400, 600 mg vs. placebo
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, PK in PD patients
- **Status:** In progress (no longer recruiting); sites: UCSD Medical Center, Parexel (Glendale, CA), Quest Research Institute (Royal Oak, MI)
- **Interpretation:** Critical safety bridge — whether the DILI signal from the MSA trial manifests in PD patients will determine whether Part 2 proceeds

**Phase 2 Part 2 (efficacy)** | NCT05670782 (Part 2) | N=~314 total study | Early to mid-stage PD (including GBA mutation carriers)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 24-month treatment, 2:1 drug:placebo, 2 dose cohorts
- **Primary endpoint:** Expected to include UPDRS Part 2 (activities of daily living) and motor/non-motor PD symptoms
- **Status:** Not yet initiated; dose selection pending Part 1b results
- **Estimated study completion:** November 2025 per ClinicalTrials.gov (likely delayed)
- **Interpretation:** The 24-month disease-modification trial is the true value-creating event. GBA mutation carrier inclusion is strategically smart — this population has faster progression AND impaired autophagy, making them theoretically more responsive to autophagy restoration

**MSA Phase 2** | NCT05695378 | N=small (6 with PET, 15 with UPDRS data) | MSA-P and MSA-C subtypes (Korea)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 36 weeks, 400 mg daily
- **PET imaging:** Placebo group showed significant decline in striatal [18F]FP-CIT BPND; KM-819 group did not decline. KM-819 patients' binding was comparable to healthy controls
- **Clinical:** UPDRS III scores showed trend toward benefit (p=0.07 for differences before/after KM-819 withdrawal)
- **Safety:** **TERMINATED due to drug-induced liver injury (DILI)** — characterized as drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis of acute onset
- **Status:** Terminated
- **Interpretation:** The efficacy signals (PET imaging stabilization, clinical trend) are genuinely encouraging for the mechanism. But the DILI is a program-threatening risk. The autoimmune-like pattern suggests idiosyncratic hepatotoxicity rather than predictable dose-dependent toxicity, which is harder to mitigate

### Financial
- **FAScinate Therapeutics:** Private company, San Diego-based subsidiary of Kainos Medicine. Total raised ~$15M. Funded primarily by Kainos Medicine, with Wells Investment and CONNECT (Accelerator) among investors
- **Kainos Medicine:** Publicly traded on KOSDAQ (ticker: 284620). Founded 2007, IPO via SPAC merger 2020. Raised ~$9.3M externally. Investors include Axis Investment, Magna Investment (Seoul), DreamStone Private Equity, NH Investment & Securities, Wells Investment
- **Licensing structure:** FAScinate licensed KM-819 from Kainos Medicine for US clinical development and commercialization. Terms not publicly disclosed
- **Financial risk:** Very limited capital base ($15M raised) for a Phase 2 program that requires a 24-month efficacy trial in 200+ PD patients. The MSA trial termination likely complicates fundraising. Will need significant additional capital or a partner to execute Part 2
- **No known pharma partner or MJFF grant** — unusual for a PD disease-modification program at this stage. The SAB includes Carrolee Barlow, MD, PhD (former CEO of Parkinson's Institute, current CMO at ESCAPE Bio), which adds some credibility but hasn't translated to partnership traction
- **Kainos also develops KM-023** (non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor for HIV, Phase 3) — providing some revenue diversification for the parent, but the PD pipeline is entirely dependent on KM-819

- KM-819's mechanism (autophagy restoration for intracellular alpha-synuclein clearance) is distinct from direct alpha-synuclein targeting. The closest mechanistic comparisons are:
  - [[minzasolmin]] (terminated) — also a small molecule targeting alpha-synuclein but via direct binding to prevent toxic pore formation, not through autophagy. ORCHESTRA Phase 2a failure in December 2024 killed this approach
  - [[pariceract]] and [[ambroxol]] — GBA1/GCase activators that also restore lysosomal function and reduce alpha-synuclein burden, representing a parallel pro-clearance strategy through a different molecular target
- KM-819's oral bioavailability and small molecule format are advantages vs. antibody ([[prasinezumab]]) and siRNA ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]) approaches in terms of patient convenience and manufacturing cost
- The GBA mutation carrier strategy in Part 2 creates a potential niche-within-a-niche: if KM-819 works specifically in patients with autophagy impairment (GBA carriers, PARK2 carriers), it could become a genetically-stratified precision therapy even if it fails in all-comers PD
- If the DILI issue kills KM-819, the FAF1 target concept is not necessarily dead — a different chemical scaffold might avoid the hepatotoxicity. But FAScinate/Kainos would need a backup molecule, which there is no evidence of

## Analysis

KM-819 presents a scientifically compelling but commercially precarious story. The FAF1 target has strong biological rationale — genetic validation through the parkin-FAF1 axis, direct pathological evidence from human PD brains, and a clean preclinical package showing both neuroprotection and alpha-synuclein clearance. The dual mechanism (anti-apoptotic + pro-autophagy) is differentiated from everything else in the PD pipeline, which clusters around direct alpha-synuclein targeting, LRRK2 inhibition, or GBA1 activation. The MSA PET data showing dopamine transporter stabilization is the kind of objective biomarker evidence that Phase 2 programs rarely generate.

The DILI from the Korean MSA trial is the elephant in the room. Drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis is an idiosyncratic toxicity — it cannot be easily predicted from dose, duration, or patient characteristics, and it cannot be reliably managed with dose reduction. The fact that Phase 1 and Phase 2 Part 1a in healthy subjects showed clean safety does not mitigate this concern: autoimmune hepatitis typically emerges with longer exposure or in patients with underlying immune dysregulation (MSA and PD patients have altered immune profiles). That FAScinate is continuing the PD trial suggests either (a) they believe the MSA DILI was population-specific, (b) the US FDA accepted their risk mitigation plan with enhanced liver monitoring, or (c) the data cutoff for Part 1a completion predated the MSA DILI signal. The answer matters enormously.

**Analytical estimate — probability of success (Phase 2 to approval): 5-8%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for Phase 2 neurodegenerative disease-modification trials: ~10-15%
- Adjustments upward: novel validated target with genetic/pathological evidence (+5%), clean Phase 1/2a safety in healthy subjects (+3%), encouraging MSA PET biomarker data supporting mechanism (+5%), oral small molecule with good PK (+2%), GBA carrier enrichment strategy could reduce heterogeneity (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: MSA trial terminated for DILI (-15%), very limited capital base for 24-month trial (-5%), no pharma partner (-3%), no PD biomarker signal in Phase 1 PD analysis (-3%), startup with single-asset risk (-2%)
- Net: ~5-8%

**Signal analysis:** The absence of a pharma partner at this stage is telling. Companies like Kainos/FAScinate have been developing KM-819 since 2016 with publications in reputable journals (Drug Metabolism and Disposition, Frontiers in Pharmacology) and clean Phase 1 data — yet no big pharma or large biotech has licensed or co-developed the asset. This could reflect (a) the DILI concern making partners wary, (b) the target being too novel for risk committees, or (c) the company not actively seeking a partner. The DILI signal from the MSA trial almost certainly narrows the partnership window further. The leadership — Kisub Lee (serial entrepreneur, semiconductor background) and Carrolee Barlow (SAB, neurodegeneration expert) — is credible but thin for executing a Phase 2b disease-modification trial in PD.

**Decision tree:** If Part 1b shows clean liver safety in PD patients and Part 2 demonstrates motor slowing in the GBA-enriched subgroup, KM-819 validates a novel pro-autophagy mechanism and becomes an attractive licensing target for big pharma interested in genetically-stratified PD populations. If DILI recurs in PD patients, the program terminates and the FAF1 target goes back to the lab for a new chemical entity with a different hepatic safety profile — this would set back FAF1-based PD therapy by 5-7 years minimum.
