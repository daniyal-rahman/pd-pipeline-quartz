# ABL301

## Summary

ABL301 is a bispecific antibody pairing an anti-alpha-synuclein aggregate-binding arm with ABL Bio's (Korean biotech, ABL.KS) Grabody-B IGF1R-based blood-brain barrier shuttle — the first clinical test of whether enhanced brain penetration can rescue the alpha-synuclein antibody modality after its 0-for-6 record on primary endpoints. Phase 1 in 91 healthy adults showed clean safety (no SAEs, only Grade 1-2 AEs) but zero published human CSF or brain PK data, and Sanofi deprioritized the program in January 2026 before Phase 2. The Grabody-B platform itself carries $5.4B in cumulative pharma deals (Sanofi $1.06B, Lilly $2.6B, GSK $2.8B), creating a paradox: the delivery platform is heavily validated while the first clinical payload stalled. If [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 succeeds and Sanofi's Phase 1b biomarker study demonstrates target engagement, ABL301 could re-enter active development with a differentiated BBB-crossing mechanism. If [[prasinezumab]] fails and ABL301's human CSF data remain absent, the alpha-synuclein antibody modality is likely exhausted and capital shifts to production inhibition via [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]].

## Notes

### Science
- Bispecific antibody format: one arm binds C-terminal region of aggregated alpha-synuclein with high selectivity for aggregates over monomers; the other arm binds IGF1R on brain endothelial cells to trigger receptor-mediated transcytosis across the BBB
- Grabody-B platform exploits IGF1R's 98% cross-species homology (vs. 76% for transferrin receptor) enabling strong preclinical-to-clinical translation, and critically does not interfere with endogenous IGF1R signaling — a key safety feature for chronic dosing
- Preclinical data in rats and monkeys showed "significantly higher brain and CSF penetration" vs. monospecific anti-alpha-synuclein antibody, and prevented alpha-synuclein seeding capacity in vitro and in vivo
- Key differentiator vs. [[prasinezumab]]: standard mAbs achieve only 0.1-0.2% brain penetration; Grabody-B aims to increase CNS exposure by an order of magnitude, testing whether the 0-for-6 antibody failure record reflects insufficient brain drug levels rather than target invalidity
- IGF1R shuttle has a safety advantage over TfR-based shuttles (Roche Brain Shuttle, BioArctic BrainTransporter) — TfR targeting causes reticulocyte depletion and anemia risk at high doses, while IGF1R has neuroprotective signaling properties (anti-inflammatory effects on astrocytes/microglia)
- Core scientific question: does delivering more antibody across the BBB matter if alpha-synuclein pathology is predominantly intracellular? The prion-like spreading hypothesis says clearing extracellular aggregates blocks cell-to-cell transmission — but 0-for-6 antibody failures have not confirmed this clinically
- Nature publication (January 2025) confirmed ABL301 binds the C-terminal portion of alpha-synuclein with high aggregate selectivity — same epitope region as [[prasinezumab]], which showed the strongest signal among alpha-syn antibodies

### Clinical

**ABL301 Phase 1 (SAD/MAD)** | NCT05756920 | N=91 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety profile. No deaths, no SAEs
- **Key secondary:** SAD (N=56): 7.1% treatment-related AEs, half in placebo group, all Grade 1-2. MAD (N=35): 5.7% treatment-related AEs, all Grade 1-2. 69.6% had mild TEAEs overall
- **Status:** Completed (December 2022 – April 2025)
- **Interpretation:** Safety is validated for the Grabody-B platform. The critical gap is the absence of any published human CSF concentrations, brain PK data, or target engagement biomarkers. If the shuttle delivered impressive brain penetration, Sanofi would have shown the data — the silence suggests either underwhelming PK, embargoed data pending Phase 1b, or both

**ABL301 Phase 1b (biomarker study)** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | PD patients (planned)
- **Primary endpoint:** Alpha-synuclein target engagement via CSF analysis and imaging (alpha-synuclein PET, approach modeled on Roche's [[prasinezumab]] biomarker strategy)
- **Status:** Announced by Sanofi; not yet registered. Timeline TBD pending "dedicated measurement equipment" development
- **Interpretation:** Sanofi is demanding proof of target engagement before committing to Phase 2. This is rational de-risking but cedes first-mover advantage to [[prasinezumab]], which is already in Phase 3

### Financial
- **Sanofi deal (January 2022):** $75M upfront, up to $985M in milestones (~$1.06B total). First Grabody-B partnership. Conviction ratio 7.1% — moderate upfront relative to total, but deprioritization in January 2026 means milestone trajectory stalled
- **GSK deal (April 2025):** $50M upfront, up to $2.8B in milestones. Platform license for non-amyloid, non-tau targets in neurodegenerative diseases including PD. GSK can pair Grabody-B with LRRK2 antibodies, GCase enzyme replacement, SNCA ASOs, neuroinflammation payloads
- **Lilly deal (December 2024):** $40M upfront + $15M equity, up to $2.6B milestones. Multi-program CNS license extending beyond neurodegeneration into obesity/muscle disease applications
- **Cumulative Grabody-B platform value:** $5.4B across three pharma partners, but only ~$165M in actual upfront payments — pharma is paying for cheap options, not expressing deep conviction
- **ABL Bio stock:** Dropped 19.5% on Sanofi deprioritization news (January 2026). Market cap ~$2.4B as of June 2025
- **Deal comparison:** GSK's $50M upfront is comparable to its $570M Vesalius deal and Alector partnership — part of a broader BBB portfolio strategy, not a singular bet. Lilly signed AFTER seeing Phase 1 data and still wanted the platform, which is a mild positive signal
- **Sanofi also deprioritized eclitasertib (RIPK1 inhibitor, Denali partnership) in the same Q4 2025 earnings call and acquired Vigil (TREM2 agonist), Blueprint, and Vicebio — this is portfolio churn, not necessarily ABL301-specific failure**

- ABL301 is the only alpha-synuclein antibody with a BBB shuttle — if [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 succeeds, it validates the extracellular alpha-syn target and gives ABL301 a differentiated next-gen positioning (enhanced brain penetration + same mechanism). If [[prasinezumab]] fails, no amount of BBB enhancement saves the antibody modality
- The competitive threat from [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] ($2.2B Novartis deal) is orthogonal: siRNA targets mRNA production (upstream), while ABL301 targets extracellular aggregates (downstream). Both could theoretically succeed independently, but the field momentum is shifting toward production inhibition
- Sanofi's deprioritization partly reflects competitive timing — Roche has a 5-year head start with [[prasinezumab]] and biomarker evidence of target engagement. Sanofi may be rationally waiting for the Phase 3 readout (2027-2028) before committing Phase 2 resources
- Within BBB platforms, Grabody-B (IGF1R) competes against Roche Brain Shuttle (TfR1, Phase 2a trontinemab data showing 91% amyloid clearance in Alzheimer's), Denali Transport Vehicle (TfR1/CD98hc, BLA submitted for Hunter syndrome), and BioArctic BrainTransporter (TfR, 3 pharma deals). Grabody-B has the most pharma deal value ($5.4B) but the least clinical validation — an inverse relationship that is the central paradox of this platform

## Analysis

The ABL301 story is fundamentally about the distinction between platform value and product value. The Grabody-B BBB shuttle platform is validated by $5.4B in cumulative deals from three major pharma partners, yet the first clinical application was deprioritized before Phase 2 generated any efficacy data. The most parsimonious explanation combines three factors: alpha-synuclein antibodies have a 0-for-6 track record on primary endpoints (target risk), Sanofi lacks target engagement biomarkers to prove the drug is hitting its target in the human brain (biomarker gap), and Sanofi's portfolio shifted toward assets with clearer proof-of-concept after acquiring Vigil's TREM2 agonist (capital allocation).

**Analytical estimate — ABL301 clinical success probability: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: 0/6 alpha-synuclein antibodies have met primary endpoints, and ABL301 was deprioritized before Phase 2 → starting point <10%
- Adjustments upward: BBB shuttle could meaningfully increase brain drug levels (+10%), clean Phase 1 safety profile enables regulatory flexibility (+3%), if [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 succeeds it validates the target and gives ABL301 a clear path (+5%), IGF1R safety advantage enables chronic dosing (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: zero published human CSF data after 3 years of clinical work (-5%), Sanofi deprioritization is a negative behavioral signal (-5%), alpha-synuclein pathology is primarily intracellular while antibodies work extracellularly (-3%)
- Net: ~15-20%, conditional on the program resuming active development

**Signal analysis:** The silence on human CSF PK data is the most informative signal. If Phase 1 had demonstrated robust brain penetration via the IGF1R shuttle, Sanofi would be advancing to Phase 2, not demanding new biomarker infrastructure. GSK and Lilly licensing the platform after seeing Phase 1 data is a partial counterpoint — but they are licensing Grabody-B for different payloads (LRRK2, GCase, neuroinflammation), not for alpha-synuclein. They may have concluded the shuttle works but the first payload is on thin ice.

The decision tree is asymmetric: if [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 (2027-2028) succeeds and Sanofi's Phase 1b shows target engagement, ABL301 becomes a compelling next-generation asset with differentiated brain penetration — potentially worth restarting Phase 2. If [[prasinezumab]] fails, the entire extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance thesis collapses and ABL301 is permanently shelved regardless of what the BBB shuttle achieves. The Grabody-B platform itself survives either scenario — GSK and Lilly can pair it with non-alpha-synuclein payloads — but the ABL301 product does not.
