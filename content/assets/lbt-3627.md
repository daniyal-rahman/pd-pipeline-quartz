# LBT-3627

## Summary

LBT-3627 is a synthetic VPAC2 receptor agonist peptide developed by Longevity Biotech (startup, Philadelphia) using their proprietary Hybridtide platform, currently in a Phase 1a/1b SAD/MAD trial in Melbourne, Australia (NCT06466525). Preclinical data from UNMC showed up to 80% protection of dopaminergic neurons in MPTP mouse models and 43-53% TH+ neuron survival in rat 6-OHDA and alpha-synuclein overexpression models, driven by Treg activation and microglial phenotype conversion. The MJFF has funded LBT-3627 development across multiple grants since 2015, reflecting the foundation's thesis that neuroinflammation modulation represents a viable disease-modification strategy. If Phase 1 safety is clean and Treg biomarker engagement is demonstrated, Longevity Biotech would need a pharma partner to fund a Phase 2 efficacy study -- the company has raised minimal capital (~$170K per Crunchbase, supplemented by grants). If safety or PD signals fail, the VPAC2 approach for PD is likely shelved, though the Hybridtide platform has other indications (MS, ALS).

## Notes

### Science
- LBT-3627 is a Hybridtide -- a synthetic peptide backbone incorporating non-natural amino acid residues that confer ~24-fold increased metabolic stability vs. native vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), overcoming the rapid degradation that made VIP itself clinically impractical
- Mechanism: selectively agonizes VPAC2 (vasoactive intestinal peptide receptor type 2), which shifts adaptive immune responses from Th1/Th17 (pro-inflammatory) toward Th2/Treg (anti-inflammatory/neuroprotective). This peripheral immune rebalancing reduces neuroinflammation centrally
- Key distinction from native VIP: VIP non-selectively activates both VPAC1 and VPAC2, causing cardiovascular side effects; LBT-3627's VPAC2 selectivity is designed to maintain immune modulation while minimizing cardiovascular liability
- The Treg-mediated neuroprotection hypothesis: in PD, peripheral Treg dysfunction permits neurotoxic Teff cells and activated microglia to accelerate dopaminergic neuron death. Restoring Treg function peripherally can suppress central neuroinflammation without requiring BBB penetration of the drug itself
- Scientific validation: the same UNMC group (Gendelman, Mosley) demonstrated that sargramostim (GM-CSF), another Treg-expanding agent, improved motor function and enhanced Treg suppressive activity in a Phase 1 PD trial (NCT01882010), establishing clinical proof-of-concept for the Treg modulation approach
- Open question: whether Treg expansion alone is sufficient for disease modification, or whether it must be combined with therapies targeting alpha-synuclein pathology or other upstream drivers
- The Hybridtide platform is protected by US Patent No. 9,782,454 (granted October 2017), originally developed by Prof. Sam Gellman at the University of Wisconsin and exclusively licensed to Longevity Biotech

### Clinical

**Neuroinflammation Biomarker Study (Observational)** | NCT03633513 | N=~40 | PD patients vs. healthy controls at VA Medical Center (PADRECC), Philadelphia
- **Primary endpoint:** Identify immune/inflammatory blood-based biomarkers correlating with PD diagnosis and severity
- **Key secondary:** Ex-vivo evaluation of LBT-3627 on patient-derived immune cells; UPDRS correlation with inflammatory markers
- **Status:** Completed (~2018-2020)
- **Interpretation:** This observational study established that approximately half of PD patients display measurable peripheral immune imbalance comparable to preclinical models, validating patient stratification for the interventional trial. Results informed the Phase 1a/1b design

**LBT-3627-101 (Phase 1a/1b SAD/MAD)** | NCT06466525 | N=64 (estimated) | Healthy volunteers + PD patients, Melbourne, Australia
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of single ascending doses (Phase 1a) and multiple ascending doses (Phase 1b)
- **Key secondary:** PK/PD (pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics), immunological biomarkers (Treg function, inflammatory cytokine panels), MDS-UPDRS exploratory
- **Phase 1a initiated:** July 2024 at The Alfred Hospital, Melbourne
- **Phase 1b initiated:** November 2024 at The Alfred Hospital, Melbourne
- **Dosing:** Once daily for up to 14 days (MAD portion); 2-3 night hospitalization per dosing period; total participation ~28 days
- **Eligibility:** PD diagnosis within past 10 years; medication-naive or stable on levodopa for 3+ months; ages 30-89
- **Status:** Actively recruiting
- **Interpretation:** Running the trial in Australia (via subsidiary Longevity Biotech Australia Pty Ltd) is typical for small companies seeking faster regulatory pathways and CRO cost advantages. The 64-patient enrollment target is standard for SAD/MAD. No data readouts reported as of February 2026

- LBT-3627 belongs to the broader neuroinflammation/immunomodulation class in PD, which includes diverse mechanisms: NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors ([[dapansutrile]], [[selnoflast]], [[nt-0796]]), GLP-1 agonists with anti-inflammatory properties ([[exenatide]], [[lixisenatide]], [[semaglutide]]), and anti-inflammatory biologics ([[nly01]], [[nly02]])
- The closest mechanistic comparator is sargramostim (GM-CSF/Leukine), developed by the same UNMC team (Gendelman/Mosley) via Partner Therapeutics -- also targets Treg expansion but through GM-CSF signaling rather than VPAC2. Sargramostim has Phase 1 safety/biomarker data in PD showing Treg enhancement and motor improvement signals, but requires daily SC injection and has broader immune activation (not Treg-selective)
- LBT-3627's theoretical advantage over sargramostim is VPAC2 selectivity, which should provide more targeted Treg modulation with fewer off-target immune effects and potential for oral dosing via the Hybridtide platform
- The neuroinflammation thesis faces a fundamental question across all assets: is neuroinflammation a cause or consequence of neurodegeneration? If it is primarily downstream of alpha-synuclein pathology, anti-inflammatory approaches may provide symptomatic benefit without true disease modification
- If GLP-1 agonists ([[exenatide]], [[lixisenatide]]) demonstrate disease modification in ongoing Phase 3 trials, their established safety profiles and oral availability would overshadow early-stage immunomodulators like LBT-3627

## Analysis

LBT-3627 represents one of the most mechanistically novel approaches in the PD pipeline -- rather than targeting alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1, or dopaminergic replacement, it aims to restore peripheral immune homeostasis through selective VPAC2 agonism. The scientific rationale is credible: the Gendelman/Mosley group at UNMC has published two decades of work on immune transformation in PD, and their sargramostim Phase 1 trial provided clinical proof-of-concept that Treg expansion can produce measurable motor improvements. LBT-3627 refines this approach with VPAC2 selectivity and the metabolically stable Hybridtide backbone.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching Phase 2: 50-60%. Probability of eventual approval: <5%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for Phase 1 to Phase 2 progression in neurology: ~65%
- Adjustments upward: clean preclinical safety in beagle cardiovascular studies (+5%), validated mechanism via sargramostim Phase 1 data (+5%), MJFF continued funding signals expert confidence (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: tiny company with minimal capital constraining ability to fund Phase 2 (-10%), no pharma partner (-5%), neuroinflammation approaches in PD have no Phase 3 success precedent (-5%)
- Phase 1-to-2 net: ~55%
- For overall approval: multiply by historically low Phase 2/3 success rates in PD disease modification (~10-15%), yielding <5%

The key signal to watch is the board appointment of Dr. Raymond Sanchez (October 2024), a seasoned neuroscience executive with Biogen and Sage Therapeutics experience. This suggests the company is positioning for partnering or fundraising based on Phase 1 data. The choice to run the trial in Australia through a local subsidiary (Longevity Biotech Australia Pty Ltd) reflects the financial constraints of a grant-funded startup -- Australian TGA pathways and clinical trial infrastructure offer cost advantages that matter when every dollar is grant money.

The critical decision point will arrive with Phase 1a/1b data (expected mid-2026 to early 2027). If safety is clean and Treg biomarker engagement is demonstrated (increased Treg suppressive function, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines), the company becomes a potential acquisition target or licensing partner for pharma companies interested in neuroinflammation. If the immune biomarker data are equivocal, the program likely stalls given funding constraints. The asset's value is as much about validating the Treg modulation thesis for PD as about LBT-3627 itself -- positive biomarker data would boost the entire neuroinflammation cluster, while negative data would dampen enthusiasm for immune-based PD approaches broadly.
