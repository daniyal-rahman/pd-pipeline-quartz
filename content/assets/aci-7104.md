# ACI-7104.056

## Summary

AC Immune (biotech, ACIU) is running the VacSYn Phase 2 adaptive trial of ACI-7104.056, an active anti-alpha-synuclein vaccine that achieved 100% immunogenicity responder rate with antibody titers >500-fold over placebo and early biomarker signals of disease stabilization (CSF alpha-synuclein p=0.018, NfL stabilization, MDS-UPDRS Part III stabilization vs. 2-3 point worsening in placebo). The December 2025 interim readout on 20 participants at 18 months is promising but underpowered — the Part 1 final data in mid-2026 and potential Part 2 expansion to 150 patients will determine whether this translates into a registrational signal. If VacSYn confirms disease modification, ACI-7104.056 becomes the most scalable and cost-effective approach to alpha-synuclein targeting — an infrequently dosed vaccine vs. [[prasinezumab]]'s monthly IV infusions or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s quarterly subcutaneous injections. If it fails, the active immunotherapy modality for alpha-synuclein loses credibility and capital flows toward passive antibodies and gene silencing approaches.

## Notes

### Science
- Active immunotherapy vaccine using a **synthetic 8-amino-acid peptide mimotope** of the alpha-synuclein C-terminal region, conjugated to keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH) carrier protein with aluminum hydroxide adjuvant
- The mimotope has a **different amino acid sequence** from native alpha-synuclein but elicits antibodies that preferentially bind pathological aggregates over native monomers — this selectivity is key to avoiding autoimmune depletion of physiological alpha-synuclein
- Mechanism bypasses auto-reactive T cell mobilization, reducing risk of harmful neuroinflammatory responses — a critical safety advantage over approaches that could trigger T cell-mediated encephalitis
- Generates endogenous polyclonal antibody response targeting multiple epitopes on aggregated alpha-synuclein, theoretically broader coverage than monoclonal antibodies like [[prasinezumab]] (single epitope)
- CSF antibody penetration confirmed at ~0.1% of serum levels with strong serum-CSF correlation (r=0.92, p<0.05 at week 24) — comparable to standard antibody CNS penetration
- Predecessor PD01A showed 51% reduction in CSF alpha-synuclein oligomers in post-hoc Phase 1 analysis (N=24, 3.5-year follow-up)
- The "boostability" signal — repeated immunizations amplify the antibody response — is essential for chronic PD treatment where sustained target engagement over decades is needed
- Open question: whether vaccine-induced polyclonal antibodies achieve sufficient CNS titers and target specificity to meaningfully clear brain alpha-synuclein aggregates, given the 0.1% BBB penetration ratio

### Clinical

**PD01A (Phase 1)** | NCT02216188 | N=32 | Early idiopathic PD (age 45-65)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety; no brain inflammation on MRI
- **Key secondary:** Significant IgG titers in 23 of 24 completers; antibodies detected in CSF of high responders; 51% reduction in CSF alpha-synuclein oligomers (post-hoc)
- **Status:** Completed (3.5-year follow-up); 21 of 24 participants completed; no withdrawals due to AEs
- **Interpretation:** Established proof-of-concept that an active vaccine can generate sustained anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies with CNS penetration. The CSF oligomer reduction is hypothesis-generating but post-hoc.

**VacSYn Part 1 (Phase 2)** | NCT06015841 | N=34 | Early PD, randomized 3:1 (active:placebo)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, MRI changes, antibody levels → All immunogenicity targets met; 100% responder rate
- **Key secondary (April 2025 interim, ~12 months):** Anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies 20-fold higher than placebo after 4 immunizations; positive responses by week 6 after 2 immunizations; boostability confirmed
- **Key secondary (December 2025 interim, 18 months, N=20):** Serum/CSF antibody titers >500-fold over placebo at week 76; CSF alpha-synuclein stabilized vs. decreased in placebo (p=0.018); NfL stable in active arm vs. increased in placebo; plasma GFAP and DaT-SPECT trends toward stabilization; MDS-UPDRS Part III stabilized vs. 2-3 point worsening in placebo
- **Safety:** No serious drug-related AEs; injection site reactions (56%), headache (15%), fatigue (12%)
- **Status:** Active; final Part 1 data expected mid-2026
- **Interpretation:** The immunogenicity is exceptionally strong (100% responder rate, 500-fold titers). The biomarker concordance across CSF alpha-synuclein, NfL, GFAP, and DaT-SPECT is encouraging but the sample size (N=20 at 18 months) is far too small for definitive conclusions. The p=0.018 for CSF alpha-synuclein stabilization is notable in such a small cohort.

**VacSYn Part 2 (Phase 2, planned)** | NCT06015841 | N=up to 150 | Early PD, randomized 2:1
- **Primary endpoint:** Expected exploratory efficacy endpoints including motor/non-motor progression, digital imaging, fluid biomarkers
- **Status:** Pending Part 1 final data; adaptive design allows expansion
- **Interpretation:** Part 2 enrollment and design will signal whether AC Immune and regulators see enough in Part 1 to pursue registrational endpoints. The adaptive design is smart for a small biotech — allows rapid pivotal study transition if data support it.

- Key competitive distinction is **modality**: active vaccines (endogenous polyclonal antibodies from infrequent dosing) vs. passive monoclonal antibodies ([[prasinezumab]] — monthly IV) vs. gene silencing ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] — quarterly SC, [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] — intrathecal)
- Active vaccine advantages: polyclonal response covering multiple epitopes, infrequent dosing (quarterly or less), low COGS, no infusion center required, potential for sustained response even if boosters are delayed
- Active vaccine disadvantages: cannot precisely control antibody levels (dose-response less tunable than mAbs), slower onset of action (weeks to mount immune response vs. immediate with passive antibodies), patient-to-patient variability in immune response, irreversibility concerns if autoimmune complications emerge
- [[prasinezumab]] is furthest ahead (Phase 3 PARAISO) — if it validates extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance, ACI-7104.056 becomes the scalable follow-on; if PARAISO fails, the entire antibody-mediated alpha-synuclein clearance thesis is challenged, though ACI-7104.056's polyclonal response targeting multiple epitopes could differentiate
- UB-312 (Vaxxinity) is the direct competitor — also a synthetic peptide alpha-synuclein vaccine but targeting a different epitope; less advanced clinically
- If ACI-7104.056 succeeds: could become the preferred first-line alpha-synuclein therapy due to COGS and dosing convenience, particularly for global/emerging market access where infusion infrastructure is limited

## Analysis

The VacSYn interim data represent the most encouraging active immunotherapy signal in the alpha-synuclein field to date. The 100% immunogenicity responder rate, >500-fold antibody titers over placebo, and concordant biomarker stabilization across CSF alpha-synuclein, NfL, GFAP, and DaT-SPECT create a coherent mechanistic narrative. The CSF alpha-synuclein stabilization (p=0.018) in only 20 patients is statistically notable, though far from definitive.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of eventual approval: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: active vaccines in neurodegeneration have a ~5% historical success rate — no alpha-synuclein vaccine has reached Phase 3
- Adjustments upward: 100% immunogenicity responder rate (+5%), concordant biomarker package across multiple modalities (+5%), CSF penetration confirmed with strong serum-CSF correlation (+3%), clean safety profile enabling long-term dosing (+3%), COGS/convenience advantages could lower regulatory bar if efficacy is marginal (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: N=20 interim data is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory (-5%), no Phase 3 trial initiated or partnered (-3%), AC Immune's cash runway (Q3 2027) creates financing risk before Phase 3 data could mature (-3%), the fundamental question of whether 0.1% CNS antibody penetration is sufficient remains unanswered (-2%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:**
- AC Immune's decision to restructure and focus on three clinical-stage vaccine programs — cutting 30% of workforce while protecting ACI-7104.056 — signals management conviction that the vaccine platform is the company's most valuable franchise. This is a resource-constrained biotech making a bet.
- The wholly-owned status of the alpha-synuclein program is a double-edged sword: full economics if successful, but no big pharma validation or funding de-risk. The absence of a partner at the Phase 2 stage, despite AC Immune's track record of major pharma deals (Roche, Lilly, Janssen on other programs), may indicate partner caution about the modality or may reflect AC Immune's strategic choice to retain full ownership of their most promising asset.
- The Strungmann family (BioNTech lead investors) participating in the 2021 financing is a notable signal — these are sophisticated vaccine-thesis investors with deep domain expertise.
- The decision tree is clear: if mid-2026 final Part 1 data confirm biomarker signals in the full 34-patient cohort, AC Immune will need to either expand to Part 2 (150 patients) or seek a partner to fund registrational development. Cash runway through Q3 2027 means a partnership announcement in 2026-2027 is likely if data hold up. If data disappoint, the company still has its Alzheimer's vaccine (ACI-24.060) and NLRP3 programs, but ACIU stock is heavily weighted toward the PD vaccine thesis.
