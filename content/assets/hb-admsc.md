# HB-adMSCs

## Summary

Hope Biosciences Research Foundation (HBRF, nonprofit) reported positive Phase 2 topline results (Dec 2025) for HB-adMSCs, an allogeneic adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cell therapy delivered via IV infusion in early-to-moderate PD. The trial (NCT04995081, N=60) met its clinician-rated primary endpoint on MDS-UPDRS Part III (placebo-adjusted -9.32 points, p=0.0023), but showed divergent results on patient-reported MDS-UPDRS Part II -- a significant discrepancy that Fierce Biotech flagged and that the FDA will need to reconcile before Phase 3. This is a fundamentally different approach from the dopaminergic neuron replacement programs ([[bemdaneprocel]], [[anpd001]], [[raguneprocel]]) -- HB-adMSCs do not replace lost neurons but instead modulate neuroinflammation and provide trophic support via paracrine signaling from IV-infused MSCs that never reach the brain parenchyma. If Phase 3 confirms the Part III signal and resolves the Part II divergence, this would be a paradigm-shifting result for the MSC field; if the Part II/III discrepancy persists or Part III attenuates in a larger trial, the program likely stalls and reinforces long-standing skepticism about IV MSCs in neurodegeneration.

## Notes

### Science
- HB-adMSCs are allogeneic (donor-derived) mesenchymal stem cells isolated from adipose tissue and expanded using Hope Biosciences' proprietary HB-101 culture media
- Mechanism is paracrine-mediated immunomodulation, not cell replacement: IV-infused MSCs lodge primarily in the lungs and reticuloendothelial system and exert systemic effects through secretion of anti-inflammatory cytokines, neurotrophic factors, and extracellular vesicles
- Key proposed pathways: suppression of Th17/Treg imbalance in PD patients, reduction of peripheral inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-23), upregulation of LIF secretion, and indirect neuroprotection via dampened neuroinflammation
- Critical biological question: IV-infused MSCs have minimal blood-brain barrier penetration -- the therapeutic hypothesis relies on peripheral immunomodulation being sufficient to slow central neurodegeneration, which is a contested mechanism across the MSC field
- Distinction from [[bemdaneprocel]] and [[anpd001]]: those programs transplant dopaminergic neuron precursors directly into the putamen to replace lost neurons. HB-adMSCs are IV-infused and do not differentiate into neurons in vivo
- Distinction from [[brainstorm-nurown|NurOwn]]: NurOwn uses autologous bone marrow-derived MSCs differentiated to secrete neurotrophic factors via intrathecal delivery. HB-adMSCs use allogeneic adipose-derived MSCs via IV with no directed differentiation
- The allogeneic approach has a scalability advantage over autologous MSC therapies -- donor cells can be banked and manufactured in bulk, avoiding per-patient cell processing
- MSCs are considered immunologically privileged (low MHC-I, absent MHC-II), enabling allogeneic use without immunosuppression

### Clinical

**Expanded Access Program (Autologous)** | N=10 | Elderly PD patients (ages 76-95)
- **Design:** Open-label, intermediate-size expanded access; six IV infusions of 200M autologous HB-adMSCs over 18 weeks; end of study at week 26
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → 46 AEs reported (37 mild); 5 SAEs, none drug-related; no deaths. Safe and well-tolerated
- **Key secondary:** Modest improvements (effect size >0.3) in MDS-UPDRS Parts I and III, PDQ-39, and PHQ-9, but no statistical significance (N=10, no comparator)
- **Status:** Completed; published in Cytotherapy (Oct 2024, PMID 39425736)
- **Interpretation:** Established safety in elderly PD patients and provided preliminary efficacy signals to support the Phase 2 trial design

**Phase 2 Allogeneic Trial** | NCT04995081 | N=60 | Early to moderate PD
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-center (Sugar Land, TX); 30 treatment, 30 placebo; six IV infusions of 200M allogeneic HB-adMSCs over 32 weeks; end of study at 52 weeks
- **Co-primary endpoint (MDS-UPDRS Part III, clinician-rated):** Treatment group -9.82 points vs. placebo -0.50 points; RMA adjusted mean difference -9.32 (95% CI [-15.11, -3.54]; **p=0.0023**). Exceeded MCID of -3.25. Effect sizes grew with successive infusions (Cohen's d: Infusion 4 = 0.34, Infusion 5 = 0.40, Infusion 6 = 0.87)
- **Co-primary endpoint (MDS-UPDRS Part II, patient-reported):** Divergent results -- did not show the same robust treatment effect as Part III. Specific Part II data not disclosed in press release
- **End of study (week 52):** Some decline from peak infusion 6 timepoint, suggesting effect may wane after treatment cessation
- **Safety:** Safe and tolerable in both groups; no specific AEs highlighted
- **Status:** Completed December 2025
- **Interpretation:** The 9.32-point Part III improvement is clinically meaningful and statistically significant, but the divergence with Part II is a material concern. Clinician-rated motor improvement without patient-perceived benefit raises questions about whether the effect is a true functional improvement or a measurement artifact. The single-center design amplifies rater bias risk. The FDA will need to see Part II/III reconciliation before greenlighting Phase 3

**Phase 2 Autologous Trial** | NCT04928287 | Early to moderate PD
- **Design:** Phase 2 trial of autologous HB-adMSCs (patient's own cells) running in parallel with the allogeneic trial
- **Status:** Completed; results not yet reported publicly
- **Interpretation:** Running both autologous and allogeneic trials simultaneously is an unusual but informative strategy -- a head-to-head comparison, if published, would be valuable for the MSC field

**Phase 3 (Planned)** | NCT TBD | Population TBD
- **Status:** HBRF planning FDA close-out meetings to determine Phase 3 design; key question is how to balance clinician-rated and patient-reported outcomes
- **Interpretation:** Phase 3 design will be critical -- FDA will likely require Part II concordance or addition of more objective endpoints

### Financial
- **Organization type:** Hope Biosciences Research Foundation (HBRF) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 2020 by Donna Chang; Hope Biosciences LLC (the parent commercial entity) is a private biopharmaceutical company founded in Sugar Land, TX in 2016
- **Funding model:** HBRF conducts clinical trials; Hope Biosciences LLC operates a stem cell banking business (autologous cell storage for adults and newborns) and manufactures cells using proprietary HB-101 media
- **Revenue streams:** Stem cell banking fees and expanded access program participation (patient-funded); not venture-backed in the traditional biotech sense
- **No disclosed VC funding, no disclosed valuation, no institutional investors** -- this is fundamentally different from venture-backed cell therapy companies like Aspen Neuroscience ($115M Series C) or BlueRock ($1B+ Bayer acquisition)
- **FDA RMAT designation** (Aug 2025) for HB-adMSCs in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis -- the first RMAT for MS. This is the strongest regulatory validation of the platform
- **Phase 3 funding is the key question:** A multi-center Phase 3 PD trial would cost $30-100M+ depending on design. How a nonprofit without institutional investors funds this is unclear. Options include: NIH/MJFF grants, patient-funded expanded access revenue, philanthropic donations, or licensing the PD program to a pharma partner
- **No deal comparables** -- nonprofit-sponsored cell therapy trials are rare; no direct precedent for HBRF's model in PD

- HB-adMSCs occupy a unique niche in the PD cell therapy landscape: IV-delivered immunomodulatory MSCs, as opposed to the intracranially delivered neuron replacement approaches that dominate the field
- [[bemdaneprocel]] (BlueRock/Bayer, Phase 3), [[raguneprocel]] (Sumitomo, NDA filed in Japan), and [[anpd001]] (Aspen Neuroscience, Phase 1/2) all transplant dopaminergic neuron precursors directly into the putamen -- a fundamentally different therapeutic hypothesis (replace lost neurons vs. modulate inflammation)
- If HB-adMSCs show durable Phase 3 efficacy, it would validate peripheral immunomodulation as a PD disease modification strategy -- a result that would have implications far beyond cell therapy, potentially validating neuroinflammation-targeting approaches broadly
- If HB-adMSCs fail, it reinforces the consensus that IV MSCs cannot meaningfully impact central neurodegeneration, and the cell therapy field consolidates around direct neuron replacement
- [[brainstorm-nurown|NurOwn]] (BrainStorm Cell Therapeutics) is the closest comparator in concept -- MSC-based, paracrine mechanism -- but is functionally dead (zombie company, no PD clinical program, $5K cash)
- The IV delivery route is HB-adMSCs' key practical advantage: no neurosurgery, no immunosuppression, repeatable dosing. If efficacy holds, the scalability and accessibility advantages over intracranial approaches are enormous

## Analysis

The HB-adMSCs Phase 2 data presents an unusual profile: a strong clinician-rated motor signal (p=0.0023, Cohen's d=0.87 at final infusion) but divergent patient-reported outcomes. This discrepancy is the central interpretive challenge. In PD trials, MDS-UPDRS Part III is clinician-assessed and can be influenced by rater expectation, particularly in single-center studies with a small N. Part II is patient-reported and captures how motor symptoms affect daily life. When the two diverge, regulators and the field typically weigh patient-reported outcomes more heavily, because they reflect functional impact rather than examination-room performance.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 3 success probability: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: IV MSC therapies have a poor track record in neurodegeneration trials globally; no IV MSC therapy has ever been approved for any neurodegenerative indication → starting point ~5%
- Adjustments upward: Phase 2 Part III p-value was robust (+5%), growing effect size across infusions suggests dose-response relationship (+3%), clean safety profile enables long-term dosing (+2%), RMAT designation in MS validates platform-level FDA engagement (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: Part II/III divergence is a material red flag (-5%), single-center design with N=60 limits generalizability (-3%), nonprofit without clear Phase 3 funding path (-3%), effect waned after treatment cessation (-2%), IV MSCs have minimal BBB penetration and the central mechanism remains unvalidated (-2%)
- Net: ~10-15%

**Signal analysis:** The nonprofit structure is both a strength and a weakness. On one hand, HBRF has no stock price to manage, no quarterly earnings calls, and no incentive to hype data -- the press release's acknowledgment of "divergent results" is more transparent than many biotech press releases. On the other hand, the lack of institutional investors means no sophisticated due diligence has vetted the program, and the single-center design at the sponsor's own facility raises the usual conflicts of interest. The parallel autologous trial (NCT04928287) is scientifically interesting -- if both datasets are published, a direct autologous vs. allogeneic comparison in the same indication from the same sponsor would be uniquely informative for the MSC field.

The FDA RMAT designation for MS (Aug 2025) is the strongest external validation. RMAT requires "preliminary clinical evidence indicating that the therapy has the potential to address unmet medical needs for a serious condition" -- the FDA reviewed Hope Biosciences' MS Phase 2 data and granted an expedited pathway, which signals the agency takes the platform seriously. Whether this extends to PD depends entirely on the Phase 3 design and whether the Part II/III discrepancy can be resolved.

The biggest open question is funding. A Phase 3 PD trial at multiple centers would require $30-100M+. HBRF's current revenue model (stem cell banking, expanded access) cannot support this scale. The most likely paths are: (1) a licensing deal with pharma (but the data package is thin for a major deal), (2) MJFF or NIH funding (possible but competitive), or (3) a novel patient-funded model. Without a clear funding path, Phase 3 may not materialize regardless of the Phase 2 signal.
