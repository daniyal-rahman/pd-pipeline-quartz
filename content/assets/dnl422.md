# DNL422

## Summary

DNL422 (OTV:SNCA) is Denali Therapeutics' (biotech, DNLI) IV-delivered antisense oligonucleotide designed to silence alpha-synuclein production in the CNS using the proprietary Oligonucleotide Transport Vehicle (OTV) platform, currently in IND-enabling studies. The OTV platform, validated in NHP with >1,000-fold greater brain exposure versus naked ASOs and published in Science Translational Medicine (August 2024), is the key differentiator -- it could solve the CNS delivery problem that forces competitors like [[ion464|ION464]] and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] to rely on intrathecal dosing. Denali's lead OTV program, DNL628 (OTV:MAPT for Alzheimer's), is entering Phase 1b and will serve as the clinical proof-of-concept for the platform itself. If DNL628 demonstrates safe, meaningful target knockdown via IV dosing, DNL422 becomes a high-conviction IND with a differentiated delivery profile against [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (siRNA, SC) and the intrathecal approaches; if DNL628 fails on CNS penetration or safety, DNL422's timeline and viability are directly impaired.

## Notes

### Science
- DNL422 is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) conjugated to Denali's OTV platform -- an engineered Fc domain that binds transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) and CD98 heavy chain amino acid transporter at the blood-brain barrier, triggering receptor-mediated transcytosis to deliver the ASO into the CNS following IV administration
- The OTV platform demonstrated >1,000-fold greater brain ASO exposure in nonhuman primates compared to systemically delivered unconjugated ASOs (Science Translational Medicine, Aug 2024)
- In NHP studies using a MALAT1-targeting OTV tool compound, IV administration achieved uniform biodistribution across multiple CNS regions and knockdown in all major cell types: endothelial cells, neurons, astrocytes, microglia, and oligodendrocytes
- Critically, IV OTV delivery produced more uniform CNS biodistribution and target knockdown than intrathecal delivery of the same unconjugated ASO -- directly addressing the gradient problem (high spinal/surface, low deep brain) that limits conventional intrathecal approaches
- SNCA mRNA knockdown reduces all forms of alpha-synuclein protein (monomeric, oligomeric, aggregated), unlike antibody approaches ([[prasinezumab]], [[cinpanemab]]) that target only extracellular aggregated protein
- Preclinical ASO literature supports the mechanism: ~50% SNCA mRNA reduction in NHP with sustained duration; prevention and clearance of alpha-synuclein aggregates in rodent preformed fibril (PFF) models
- Genetic validation: SNCA gene duplication/triplication causes autosomal dominant PD with dose-dependent severity; SNCA downregulation is genetically validated as protective
- Key open question: whether the OTV conjugation alters ASO pharmacokinetics, duration of knockdown, or introduces immunogenicity from the engineered Fc domain in chronic dosing
- Additional open question: optimal level of alpha-synuclein reduction -- too much knockdown could impair normal synaptic function (alpha-synuclein has physiological roles in vesicle trafficking)

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated for DNL422. The program is in IND-enabling studies as of January 2026.

Key platform-level clinical readouts that will inform DNL422:
- **DNL628 (OTV:MAPT) Phase 1b** -- CTA approved, study start-up underway (2025-2026). This is the first clinical test of the OTV platform and will establish safety, PK, and target knockdown of the OTV approach in humans. Results directly de-risk or de-rail DNL422.
- Denali expects to advance 1-2 additional programs to clinic per year over the next three years across TV-enabled franchises, suggesting DNL422 IND filing could occur in 2027.

### Financial
- Denali Therapeutics (DNLI) market cap ~$2.5B as of early 2026; 52-week range $10.57-$24.34
- Cash position: ~$872.9M as of September 30, 2025, supplemented by a $200M equity offering and up to $275M Royalty Pharma agreement (December 2025) -- well-funded runway for IND-enabling and early clinical work
- DNL422 is internally developed -- no external partnership or licensing deal announced for this specific asset
- Denali's major partnership is with Biogen ($560M upfront + $465M equity investment in 2020) for the LRRK2 program ([[biib122|BIIB122]]), not for OTV programs. Biogen had options on TV-enabled programs but walked away from the Alzheimer's collaboration, potentially leaving OTV programs unpartnered
- The OTV platform value is primarily de-risked through DNL628 (tau/AD); DNL422 represents incremental pipeline value contingent on platform validation
- No peak sales estimates available for DNL422 at this stage; the alpha-synuclein gene silencing market is being sized by the [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]/Novartis $2.2B deal as the reference transaction

- DNL422's primary differentiator is **IV delivery with broad CNS biodistribution** -- no other alpha-synuclein gene silencing program offers this. [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (Alnylam/Novartis) uses subcutaneous siRNA with hepatocyte tropism, requiring the liver to produce the silencing effect; [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] (Lilly) and [[ion464|ION464]] (Ionis/Biogen, discontinued) rely on intrathecal delivery with its inherent gradient problem and patient burden
- [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] is furthest ahead among gene silencing approaches and backed by a $2.2B Novartis deal; it is the primary competitive benchmark. However, ARO-SNCA's mechanism of action (hepatic siRNA reducing peripheral alpha-synuclein) raises questions about whether peripheral knockdown translates to sufficient CNS effect
- [[ion464|ION464]] (Biogen/Ionis) was discontinued in February 2025 despite acceptable safety data in the HORIZON trial -- the intrathecal delivery burden and Biogen's strategic retreat from the alpha-synuclein space are cautionary signals for that route of administration, and potentially validating for DNL422's IV approach
- Antibody-based competitors ([[prasinezumab]], [[cinpanemab]]) target extracellular aggregated protein rather than reducing production; DNL422 addresses the upstream biology by reducing total alpha-synuclein, which is mechanistically orthogonal and potentially complementary
- The competitive risk is timeline: DNL422 is years behind [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] and [[prasinezumab]] in clinical development. If gene silencing is validated by earlier movers, DNL422 must demonstrate a meaningful delivery advantage to justify a later entry

## Analysis

DNL422's value proposition is inseparable from the OTV platform. The >1,000-fold brain exposure improvement in primates is a striking preclinical finding, and the head-to-head superiority over intrathecal delivery on uniformity is directly relevant to the clinical challenge -- but these are NHP data with a tool compound (MALAT1-targeting), not the actual DNL422 molecule. The translation gap from platform validation to specific therapeutic candidate is real. The DNL628 Phase 1b readout will be the single most important data point for DNL422's future: if OTV achieves safe, measurable MAPT knockdown in human CSF via IV dosing, the platform is clinically validated and DNL422 becomes a high-priority IND.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching Phase 1: 65-70%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Denali has a strong track record of advancing TV-enabled programs through IND (tividenofusp alfa for Hunter syndrome is under BLA review); the OTV platform has robust NHP data; the company is well-capitalized ($1.1B+ in available resources). Downward adjustments: the program is early and competes internally with DNL628 and DNL111 for OTV manufacturing and regulatory bandwidth; Denali may deprioritize if the DNL628 Phase 1b disappoints; the alpha-synuclein gene silencing space is crowding and may face a "first adequate therapy" dynamic that reduces the commercial rationale for a later entrant.

**Signal analysis:** Denali's decision to publicly highlight DNL422 in its 2026 milestones press release -- alongside the far more advanced BIIB122 and tividenofusp alfa -- signals internal confidence in the OTV platform's readiness. The company is simultaneously running multiple TV-enabled programs across enzyme (ETV), antibody (ATV), and oligonucleotide (OTV) modalities, suggesting the transport vehicle technology is robust enough to support a franchise strategy. However, the absence of a partner for DNL422 (in contrast to the Biogen partnership for [[biib122|BIIB122]]) indicates this is still a platform-risk-stage asset, not yet at the point where external validation through a licensing deal would be expected.

The decision tree is clear: if DNL628 Phase 1b shows safe CNS target engagement via IV OTV (expected readout 2026-2027), DNL422 advances rapidly toward IND with a differentiated delivery profile. If DNL628 fails, the entire OTV franchise -- including DNL422 -- is significantly impaired, and Denali's PD strategy contracts to the [[biib122|BIIB122]] LRRK2 program and the ETV:GCase program (DNL111). Regardless of DNL628 outcome, the [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] Phase 2 data and [[prasinezumab]] PARAISO Phase 3 readout will shape whether alpha-synuclein gene silencing remains a viable therapeutic thesis by the time DNL422 could reach the clinic.
