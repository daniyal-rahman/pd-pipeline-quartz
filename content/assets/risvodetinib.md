# Risvodetinib

## Summary

Risvodetinib is the most advanced selective, brain-penetrant c-Abl kinase inhibitor in PD, now being developed by ABLi Therapeutics (private startup) after founder Milton Werner spun it out from Inhibikase Therapeutics (NASDAQ: IKT) in May 2025. The Phase 2 201 Trial (NCT05424276, N=126) met its primary safety endpoint with a placebo-like adverse event profile and showed exploratory signals: nominal significance on MDS-UPDRS Part 2 (100 mg), SEADL (50 mg), ~5-fold fall reduction vs. placebo, and alpha-synuclein aggregate reduction in skin biopsy at all doses — the first experimental treatment shown to reduce synuclein pathology in PD patients. ABLi completed an End of Phase 2 meeting with FDA (May 2025) and is planning two 2026 trials: the ABILITY-PD longitudinal biomarker study (re-enrolling 120 201 Trial participants) and the CAMPD Phase 2b/3 registrational trial (~450 participants). The competitive c-Abl field is littered with failures — nilotinib (NILO-PD) showed no benefit and vodobatinib (PROSEEK) was terminated for futility with concerning NfL elevations — making risvodetinib's clean safety profile and biomarker signal a genuine differentiator. If CAMPD succeeds, this becomes the first oral disease-modifying therapy for PD and validates c-Abl as a druggable target. If it fails, the c-Abl hypothesis in PD is likely dead.

## Notes

### Science
- **c-Abl activation cascade:** Misfolded alpha-synuclein internalized by neurons activates c-Abl tyrosine kinase, which phosphorylates alpha-synuclein at Y39, creating a toxic, aggregation-prone form that triggers downstream neurodegeneration including dopaminergic neuron loss, neuroinflammation, and impaired autophagy
- Risvodetinib is a chemical derivative of imatinib (Gleevec) with an IC50 of 33 nM for c-Abl — approximately 20-fold more potent than imatinib — specifically engineered for BBB penetration via the proprietary RAMP (Re-engineering Approach with Metabolism Preserved) drug innovation platform
- In preclinical models (Science Translational Medicine, Jan 2023), IkT-148009 suppressed c-Abl activation to baseline, substantially protected dopaminergic neurons, restored motor function within 8 weeks, and reduced alpha-synuclein pathology in both heritable and sporadic PD mouse models when dosed orally once daily
- Key differentiator vs. repurposed oncology c-Abl inhibitors (nilotinib, bosutinib, dasatinib): purpose-built for brain penetration with favorable toxicology — no cardiotoxicity, no myelosuppression, no GI toxicity signals seen in oncology c-Abl inhibitors
- Also targets GI alpha-synuclein pathology — PD GI complications (constipation, gastroparesis) driven by same c-Abl/synuclein pathway in enteric nervous system; dual brain+gut targeting is unique
- FDA Orphan Drug Designation granted for Multiple System Atrophy (October 2023), reflecting broader c-Abl/synucleinopathy applicability
- Open scientific question: does c-Abl inhibition address the root cause of synuclein misfolding, or only one amplification loop? The vodobatinib PROSEEK failure (with NfL elevations) raises concern about whether c-Abl inhibition per se is neuroprotective, or whether selectivity and brain exposure are the critical variables

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (101 Trial)** | NCT TBD | N=108 | Healthy volunteers (55-70 yr) + PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Favorable profile across SAD (single ascending dose) and MAD (multiple ascending dose) studies
- **Key secondary:** Linear PK between 12.5-200 mg for Cmax and AUC; exposures high relative to other kinase inhibitors; no clinically significant adverse events; only 11 possibly treatment-related AEs across 108 participants, none of clinical significance
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Clean Phase 1 establishing PK/PD, dose proportionality, and safety; published in Journal of Parkinson's Disease (Jan 2024)

**201 Trial (Phase 2)** | NCT05424276 | N=126 | Untreated PD, ~14 months post-diagnosis
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability at 50 mg, 100 mg, 200 mg vs. placebo (1:1:1:1) over 12 weeks → **MET.** AE frequency and severity similar to placebo at all doses. 95% completion rate, 99% dosing compliance. No severe treatment-related AEs. No nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, edema, or cardiovascular AEs above placebo
- **Key secondary (functional):** MDS-UPDRS Part 2 reached nominal significance at 100 mg (p<0.05); SEADL reached nominal significance at 50 mg (p<0.05); fall frequency reduced ~5-fold vs. placebo at all doses
- **Exploratory (biomarker):** Alpha-synuclein aggregate pathology reduced in skin biopsy at all doses — first experimental treatment to demonstrate synuclein pathology clearance in PD patients. Blood biomarkers confirmed mechanistic c-Abl target engagement
- **Safety detail:** Vision monitoring simplified by FDA to 6-month intervals after no risvodetinib-related vision impact observed (relevant because other c-Abl inhibitors cause ocular toxicity)
- **Status:** Completed (Q4 2024)
- **Interpretation:** The trial was not powered for efficacy, so the functional signals (MDS-UPDRS Part 2, SEADL) are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. However, the combination of clean safety, biomarker evidence of synuclein clearance, and directionally positive functional data across multiple scales provides a coherent package. The 12-week duration was short for a disease modification claim; the ABILITY-PD 12-month extension will be critical

**ABILITY-PD (Phase 2a extension)** | NCT TBD | N=120 | Re-enrolled 201 Trial participants
- **Primary endpoint:** 12-month longitudinal biomarker assessment — brain (alpha-synuclein PET via 18F-FD4), blood, and tissue (skin biopsy) biomarkers
- **Key secondary:** Motor, non-motor, and dopamine transporter functional data
- **Status:** Planned 2026 initiation
- **Interpretation:** Bridges the 12-week 201 Trial data to longer-term evidence; the alpha-synuclein PET imaging (via SynuSight collaboration) will provide the first in-brain measurement of synuclein clearance by risvodetinib

**CAMPD — c-Abl inhibitor Modification of Parkinson's Disease (Phase 2b/3)** | NCT TBD | N=~450 | Untreated PD within 3 years of diagnosis
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in rate of clinical worsening (disease progression measure); FDA endorsed MDS-UPDRS Part 2 as primary outcome
- **Key secondary:** Activities of Daily Living; GI function; time to levodopa initiation (novel endpoint — FDA agreed this is a direct measure of disease modification, requested follow-up meeting on clinical standards for levodopa initiation timing); alpha-synuclein PET and skin biopsy
- **Design:** Two-dose, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Status:** Planned 2026 initiation; End of Phase 2 meeting with FDA completed May 2025
- **Interpretation:** This is the registrational trial. The "time to levodopa" secondary endpoint is strategically important — if risvodetinib delays need for dopamine replacement, it is among the most clinically meaningful disease modification signals possible. Trial size (~450) is substantial for a private startup; financing remains the key execution risk

**202 Trial (Phase 2/3 in MSA)** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | Multiple System Atrophy
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, PK, and efficacy on disease progression
- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled; up to 35 sites in EU and U.S.
- **Status:** Planned
- **Interpretation:** MSA represents an orphan indication (7-year exclusivity) with higher unmet need and faster disease progression than PD, potentially enabling a shorter trial with clearer signal. Dual PD+MSA development is a smart portfolio strategy

### Financial
- ABLi Therapeutics launched May 2025 as a private company; specific funding amounts not disclosed
- Company stated "strong insider support" but declined to disclose fundraising details — typical of early private rounds where insider-led financing signals founder conviction but limited institutional validation
- Licensed risvodetinib and the RAMP platform from Inhibikase Therapeutics (NASDAQ: IKT); licensing terms undisclosed
- Inhibikase context: IKT put risvodetinib on hold in January 2025 after 201 Trial missed pre-specified efficacy bars; pivoted to IkT-001Pro in pulmonary arterial hypertension (closed $275M financing for PAH in October 2024). Werner departed Inhibikase in February 2025 after 16 years as founder/CEO
- Patent protection for risvodetinib extends beyond 2036
- Key execution risk: financing a ~450-patient Phase 2b/3 trial (CAMPD) plus a biomarker extension (ABILITY-PD) plus MSA Phase 2/3 (202) as a private startup is extremely capital-intensive. Analytical estimate — CAMPD alone likely requires $80-150M to execute. Without disclosed institutional investors or pharma partnership, financing visibility is low
- Leadership team is experienced: Karl Kieburtz (Interim CMO) is a prominent PD clinical trialist from University of Rochester; board includes Dr. Anastasia Daifotis (ex-J&J CSO North America) and Jeffrey Young (biotech CFO veteran)

- **Risvodetinib is the last c-Abl inhibitor standing in PD.** The two closest competitors both failed:
  - **Nilotinib** (repurposed CML drug): Georgetown NILO-PD Phase 2a (NCT03205488, N=76) showed no symptomatic benefit, low CSF exposure, and failed to change dopamine metabolites. MJFF-funded investigators concluded "findings do not warrant further testing." Key issue: not designed for brain penetration
  - **Vodobatinib/K0706** (Sun Pharma/SPARC): PROSEEK Phase 2 (NCT03655236, N=506) terminated for futility in April 2024. Not only failed primary endpoint (MDS-UPDRS Part 3) but showed dose-dependent NfL elevations suggesting potential neuronal harm. Devastating outcome for c-Abl hypothesis broadly
- Risvodetinib's competitive advantage: purpose-built for brain penetration (vs. repurposed oncology agents), clean safety without cardiotoxicity or myelosuppression, and the only c-Abl inhibitor to show synuclein clearance in human tissue
- Broader disease modification landscape: competes for same "slow PD progression" positioning as [[prasinezumab]] (anti-synuclein mAb, Phase 3), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (alpha-syn siRNA), and [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 inhibitor) — all targeting different nodes of the same disease biology
- The alpha-synuclein PET collaboration (SynuSight/XingImaging, January 2026) is strategically important — if CAMPD can show in-brain synuclein reduction alongside clinical improvement, it would provide the most compelling disease modification evidence package in PD

## Analysis

The risvodetinib story is a rare case of a founder rescuing an asset from corporate abandonment. Inhibikase deprioritized risvodetinib after a Phase 2 that actually produced a coherent biomarker+functional signal package — unusual for a "failed" program. The fact that Milton Werner left his own company of 16 years to create ABLi and re-license the asset he developed is an exceptionally strong insider conviction signal. This is not a speculative acquirer; this is the scientist-founder betting his career on data he believes was underappreciated.

**Analytical estimate — Phase 2b/3 CAMPD success probability: 20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: c-Abl inhibitors in PD are 0/2 on efficacy endpoints (nilotinib, vodobatinib) → starting point ~10%
- Adjustments upward: risvodetinib is the only purpose-built brain-penetrant c-Abl inhibitor (+10%); clean safety profile distinct from failed competitors (+5%); alpha-synuclein skin biopsy clearance is a first-in-class biomarker result (+5%); nominal functional significance in an underpowered 12-week trial (+5%); FDA engagement on endpoint design (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: vodobatinib PROSEEK failure with NfL harm signal casts doubt on entire c-Abl hypothesis (-8%); 201 Trial was only 12 weeks in 126 patients — extremely thin efficacy basis for a registrational program (-5%); private startup execution risk for ~450-patient trial (-5%); nominal significance in small trial frequently does not replicate (-5%)
- Net: ~20-25%

**Signal analysis:**
- Werner's founder-led spinout is the strongest positive signal. This is a scientist with 16+ years of data on risvodetinib who chose to personally stake his next company on it. Similar to a management buyback, founder re-licensing signals deep knowledge-based conviction
- The SynuSight alpha-synuclein PET collaboration (January 2026) is strategically important. If CAMPD incorporates in-brain synuclein PET alongside clinical endpoints, a positive result would provide the most mechanistically complete disease modification package in PD — clinical benefit + peripheral synuclein clearance + brain synuclein clearance. This is what Sanofi demanded (and could not get) before proceeding with [[abl301|ABL301]]
- The "time to levodopa initiation" endpoint proposed for CAMPD and endorsed by FDA is novel and high-impact. If risvodetinib meaningfully delays when patients need dopamine replacement, it is intuitively compelling to patients, payers, and regulators
- Key risk: financing. A ~450-patient registrational trial at a private startup with undisclosed funding is an execution red flag. The "strong insider support" language without naming institutional investors suggests the fundraising story is still incomplete. If ABLi cannot close a substantial Series A or pharma partnership in 2026, CAMPD could be delayed or downsized, diminishing statistical power

**Decision tree:**
- If CAMPD positive → first oral disease-modifying PD therapy; validates c-Abl as druggable target; massive pharma acquisition target (>$2B potential); second-generation RAMP compounds gain platform value; MSA program accelerates
- If CAMPD negative → c-Abl hypothesis in PD is likely dead across all compounds; field capital shifts decisively toward [[prasinezumab|extracellular synuclein clearance]], [[aro-snca|gene silencing]], and [[biib122|genetic PD targets]]
