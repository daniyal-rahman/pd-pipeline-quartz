# NT-0150

## Summary

NodThera (UK biotech, private) is advancing NT-0150, its next-generation oral NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor built on a novel pyridazinone chemotype optimized for exceptional CNS exposure, into Phase 1 SAD/MAD studies initiated H2 2025. NT-0150 inherits the clinical proof-of-concept established by [[nt-0796|NT-0796]], which demonstrated reversal of neuroinflammation in PD patients over 28 days (CSF IL-1beta, IL-6, CCL2 reductions to healthy control levels; 13% NfL reduction in one week). NodThera has explicitly pivoted its PD program from NT-0796 to NT-0150, repositioning NT-0796 toward cardiometabolic indications -- suggesting NT-0796's CNS properties, while sufficient for proof-of-concept, were suboptimal for chronic PD dosing. If NT-0150 SAD/MAD confirms safety and enhanced brain penetration, Phase 2 in PD follows; if Phase 2 then shows clinical motor benefit beyond biomarkers, it validates NLRP3-mediated neuroinflammation as a tractable PD disease modification target alongside [[prasinezumab|alpha-synuclein]] and [[biib122|LRRK2]] approaches. If the compound switch introduces unexpected liabilities or biomarker changes fail to translate clinically, the program joins the long list of mechanism-positive, outcome-negative neuroinflammation efforts.

## Notes

### Science
- NLRP3 inflammasomes are multiprotein cytosolic complexes in microglia and peripheral immune cells activated by pathological stress signals (misfolded alpha-synuclein, mitochondrial damage, oxidative stress), triggering caspase-1-mediated release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1beta and IL-18
- NT-0150 is a novel pyridazinone chemotype distinct from NT-0796 (isopropyl ester prodrug) and NT-0527 (spirocyclic isoquinoline tool compound halted due to CYP 2C19 time-dependent inhibition). NT-0150 was specifically designed for exceptional CNS exposure, addressing the limitations of earlier NodThera compounds
- Preclinical efficacy demonstrated across multiple animal models of inflammatory disease, with rapid and robust reductions in both brain and peripheral pro-inflammatory cytokines as well as neurological markers
- Genetic validation: alpha-synuclein aggregates directly activate NLRP3 in microglia, creating a feedforward loop of inflammation and protein aggregation; LRRK2 mutations (the most common genetic PD cause) also activate NLRP3 signaling -- placing NLRP3 at a convergence node of multiple PD pathways
- Key scientific question inherited from [[nt-0796|NT-0796]]: NLRP3 inhibition addresses a downstream consequence (neuroinflammation) rather than a proximal cause (alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1). Whether dampening inflammation alone is sufficient for disease modification -- or whether it must be combined with upstream targeting -- remains unknown
- The compound switch from NT-0796 to NT-0150 introduces a separate scientific question: whether the pyridazinone chemotype achieves meaningfully better CNS target engagement than NT-0796's prodrug approach, and whether this translates to greater efficacy

### Clinical

**Phase 1 SAD/MAD** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, PK
- **Key secondary:** Expected to include PD assessment of CNS penetration and target engagement biomarkers
- **Status:** Initiated H2 2025; ongoing
- **Interpretation:** First-in-human study for the pyridazinone chemotype. The critical question is whether NT-0150 achieves superior brain penetration vs. NT-0796 (which had blood-to-brain ratio of 0.79 in mice and CSF drug levels 2-3x higher than healthy volunteers in PD patients). If safety and PK are clean, Phase 2 in PD patients follows.

*Note: The clinical proof-of-concept for NLRP3 inhibition in PD was established with [[nt-0796|NT-0796]] (Phase 1b/2a biomarker study). See [[nt-0796]] for detailed trial results. NT-0150 is designed to improve upon NT-0796's CNS properties for chronic PD dosing.*

## Analysis

NT-0150 is best understood as a compound-optimized bet on a thesis that [[nt-0796|NT-0796]] has already partially validated. The NT-0796 PD biomarker data -- CSF cytokine normalization, NfL reduction, sTREM2 reduction -- provides the strongest clinical evidence to date that oral NLRP3 inhibition can reverse active neuroinflammation in PD patients. NT-0150 exists because NodThera concluded that NT-0796's CNS properties, while sufficient for a 28-day proof-of-concept, were not optimal for chronic dosing in a neurodegenerative population. The pyridazinone chemotype is designed to solve this problem with "exceptional CNS exposure."

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of NT-0150 reaching Phase 3 with positive motor outcomes in PD: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: neuroinflammation-targeting therapies in neurodegeneration have a poor track record; anti-TNF, anti-IL-1, and other anti-inflammatory approaches have not succeeded in PD or AD clinical trials --> starting point ~5-10%
- Adjustments upward: NT-0796 provided first clinical proof of CNS NLRP3 target engagement in PD (+5%); genetic validation linking NLRP3 to multiple PD pathways (alpha-synuclein, LRRK2) (+3%); NfL reduction in 1 week with NT-0796 suggests active neuroinflammatory process being modified (+2%); purpose-built CNS optimization may outperform NT-0796 (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: compound switch introduces new molecule risk (safety, PK, tolerability unknown) (-3%); no clinical motor endpoint data yet for any NLRP3 inhibitor in PD (-3%); biomarker-to-clinical translation risk is high in neurodegeneration (-3%); 2+ year timeline delay from compound switch (-2%)
- Net: ~10-15%

**Signal analysis:**
- NodThera's decision to create a purpose-built CNS compound rather than advancing NT-0796 directly into Phase 2 PD is informative. It suggests the company saw meaningful room for improvement in brain exposure -- enough to justify the cost and delay of developing a new clinical candidate. This is rational drug development, not sunk-cost behavior.
- The April 2025 Series D (GBP 38.5M) came after the PD biomarker data disclosure, indicating investors are backing both the neuroinflammation thesis and the compound switch to NT-0150. The investor syndicate (Novo, Sofinnova, 5AM, F-Prime) represents high-quality life sciences venture capital.
- Roche's withdrawal of inzomelid from PD trials and pivot to the peripheral compound selnoflast is a mixed signal. It could reflect Roche-specific portfolio decisions, or it could suggest their internal data raised concerns about CNS NLRP3 inhibition in PD. NodThera's positive biomarker data post-dates Roche's withdrawal, partially mitigating this concern.
- The combination therapy opportunity remains the strategic long-term value: if [[prasinezumab]] or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] demonstrate disease modification via alpha-synuclein targeting, adding NLRP3 inhibition to address the downstream inflammatory cascade could become standard -- analogous to combination approaches in oncology. An acquirer with an alpha-synuclein or LRRK2 program would find NodThera's CNS NLRP3 platform strategically complementary.
