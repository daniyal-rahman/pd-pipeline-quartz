# IRL757

## Summary

IRL757 is an oral small molecule from IRLAB Therapeutics (biotech, IRLAB-A.ST, Nasdaq Stockholm) targeting apathy in Parkinson's disease -- a non-motor symptom affecting 20-70% of PD patients with no approved pharmacological treatment. Discovered via IRLAB's proprietary ISP (Integrative Screening Process) platform, the same system-pharmacology/ML pipeline that generated [[mesdopetam]], IRL757 activates frontal-subcortical circuit signaling to reverse the motivational deficits underlying apathy. Phase 1 SAD/MAD studies in healthy volunteers (including a 65+ elderly cohort) completed in H1 2025 with clean safety, good oral absorption, and favorable tolerability. A Phase 1b signal-finding study in 75 PD patients with apathy across four European countries received regulatory approval in December 2025, fully funded by development partner MSRD (Otsuka group). If the Phase 1b shows an efficacy signal in apathy endpoints, IRL757 would be first-in-class for PD-associated apathy with a clear path to Phase 2 proof-of-concept; if no signal emerges, the program faces the fundamental challenge of whether pharmacological reversal of apathy is achievable, a question no drug has yet answered.

## Notes

### Science
- IRL757 was designed to specifically activate nerve signaling and gene expression involved in synaptic activity and plasticity, strengthening weakened nerve connections in the frontal cortex
- Targets frontal-subcortical circuits, especially those linking the ventromedial prefrontal cortex to related basal ganglia regions -- dysfunction in these circuits is the hypothesized neurobiological substrate of apathy across neurodegenerative diseases
- Preclinical models demonstrated efficacy in cognitive function paradigms with signals of improved motivation
- Discovered using IRLAB's ISP platform, which combines systems pharmacology with machine learning to link chemical entity phenotypic profiles to predicted human clinical effects -- the same platform that produced [[mesdopetam]] (IRL790, D3 antagonist) and IRL942 (cognitive impairment)
- Designed as a once-daily oral tablet, a practical advantage for a chronic non-motor symptom
- The compound is described as "first in a completely new class" -- there is no established drug class for PD-associated apathy, making target validation a key open question
- Apathy in PD is distinct from depression (different neural substrates, does not respond to antidepressants), underscoring the need for a mechanism-specific approach rather than repurposing existing CNS drugs
- Broader indication potential: Alzheimer's disease (43-59% prevalence of apathy) and frontotemporal dementia, though the PD indication is leading

**Phase 1b (Signal-Finding)** | NCT TBD | N=75 | PD patients with apathy

The "signal-finding" design suggests exploratory efficacy endpoints (likely apathy rating scales such as the Apathy Evaluation Scale or the Lille Apathy Rating Scale). A positive signal here would be the first clinical evidence that pharmacological modulation of frontal-subcortical circuits can reverse PD-associated apathy. Fully funded by MSRD.

- IRL757 is effectively uncontested in PD-associated apathy -- no other drug in active clinical development specifically targets this indication with a novel mechanism
- Current clinical management of PD apathy relies on off-label use of cholinesterase inhibitors (rivastigmine), dopamine agonists, and stimulants -- none with robust evidence or regulatory approval for this specific indication
- Within IRLAB's own portfolio, IRL757 is complementary to [[mesdopetam]] (LID) and IRL942 (cognitive impairment, preclinical), collectively addressing motor complications, non-motor symptoms, and cognition -- the [[otsuka-program|Otsuka]] connection via MSRD spans both IRL757 and IRL942
- The non-motor PD symptom space is nascent: [[tavapadon]] and other dopaminergic agents address motor symptoms but do not specifically target motivational circuits
- If IRL757 Phase 1b succeeds: first clinical validation that PD apathy is pharmacologically tractable, potentially creating an entirely new treatment category with significant first-mover advantage
- If IRL757 Phase 1b fails: raises the question of whether apathy, as a circuit-level phenomenon, can be addressed with oral small molecules -- may redirect interest toward neuromodulation approaches (e.g., TMS, DBS targeting medial prefrontal cortex)

## Analysis

IRL757 occupies a rare position in PD drug development: a first-in-class compound targeting a highly prevalent, clinically debilitating symptom with zero approved treatments. The scientific rationale -- restoring frontal-subcortical circuit function to reverse apathy -- is mechanistically coherent with what is known about the neurobiology of motivation, but remains clinically unproven. The key question is not whether the drug is safe (Phase 1 data is clean) but whether pharmacological activation of cortical synaptic plasticity can produce a measurable, clinically meaningful improvement in a construct as diffuse as apathy.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 1b efficacy signal probability: 25-35%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: novel mechanism in an untreated indication with no precedent for pharmacological success in PD apathy --> starting point ~15-20%
- Adjustments upward: MSRD/Otsuka fully funding the study signals internal conviction based on data review (+5%), ISP platform has produced at least one clinical-stage asset with consistent pharmacological signal in [[mesdopetam]] (+5%), clean Phase 1 safety/PK enabling optimal dosing in the Phase 1b (+5%), well-defined patient population (PD + apathy) reducing heterogeneity (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: apathy is notoriously difficult to measure with validated scales, introducing endpoint noise (-5%), no prior clinical precedent for any drug succeeding in PD apathy (-5%), frontal circuit mechanisms are poorly understood relative to motor/dopaminergic targets (-5%)
- Net: ~25-35%

**Signal analysis:** MSRD's involvement is the most informative external signal. The McQuade Center is closely linked to the Otsuka pharmaceutical group, which has deep CNS expertise (aripiprazole, brexpiprazole). Their decision to fully fund the Phase 1b through proof-of-concept -- and their progressive escalation of payments beyond the original deal structure -- suggests internal pharmacological or preclinical data review that supports the mechanism. The MJFF grant is also a meaningful endorsement: MJFF's therapeutics pipeline program specifically targets assets that "prevent, stop, or delay disease progression or reduce the burden of daily symptoms," and their due diligence process provides independent scientific validation.

The commercial thesis is compelling but distant. If IRL757 demonstrates efficacy, the absence of competition means IRLAB/MSRD would define the treatment category. The 1.1-4.0 million addressable PD patients and the Alzheimer's extension (4.9-6.7 million patients) create a substantial market opportunity. But the path from Phase 1b signal to registration is long, and the deal structure -- capped at ~$8.5M through proof-of-concept with an option to expand -- reflects appropriate early-stage risk management by MSRD rather than conviction-level commitment. The decision tree is clear: a positive Phase 1b signal triggers MSRD option exercise and likely a substantially expanded deal; a negative signal either terminates the program or forces a rethink of endpoint selection and dose optimization.
