# NRG5051

## Summary

NRG5051 is a first-in-class oral mPTP inhibitor from NRG Therapeutics (private, UK) that dosed its first healthy volunteers in January 2026 in a Phase 1 SAD/MAD trial (EUCT 2025-523872-22-00). Backed by a well-subscribed GBP 50M Series B led by SV Health Investors' Dementia Discovery Fund with participation from M Ventures, Novartis Venture Fund, and the British Business Bank, plus three MJFF grants and seed investment from Parkinson's UK, NRG is targeting both PD and ALS/MND with a shared mitochondrial protection mechanism. If Phase 1 produces clean safety and dose-proportional PK, the company plans a Phase 1b in PD patients and proof-of-concept in ALS/MND -- PD data would position NRG5051 as a mechanistically differentiated disease-modifying candidate targeting upstream mitochondrial dysfunction rather than protein aggregation. If safety signals emerge or CNS penetration is insufficient at tolerable doses, the program likely stalls given the company's single-asset clinical portfolio.

## Notes

### Science
- The mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) is a non-selective channel in the inner mitochondrial membrane; pathological opening causes mitochondrial swelling, membrane depolarization, cytochrome c release, and cell death
- In PD, alpha-synuclein oligomers disrupt calcium homeostasis and trigger mPTP opening, creating bioenergetic deficits, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation in substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons
- In ALS/MND, misfolded TDP-43 translocates to the cytoplasm and triggers mPTP opening, causing aberrant mitochondrial DNA release that activates the cGAS/STING innate immune pathway, driving neuroinflammation and motor neuron death
- Classical "first-generation" mPTP inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporin A) work by inhibiting cyclophilin D (CypD) but fail in the CNS due to poor BBB penetration, immunosuppression, and nephrotoxicity
- NRG5051 acts through a novel, undisclosed mitochondrially-localized "protein A" that is essential for pore opening -- mechanistically distinct from CypD inhibition and designed for oral bioavailability with CNS penetration
- NRG discovered its mPTP inhibitor series via phenotypic screening in isolated mitochondria, identifying multiple chemically differentiated series that act independently of CypD
- Preclinical data shows neuroprotection against alpha-synuclein toxicity in PD models and inhibition of innate immune gene expression in ALS models, with improvement in motor function and reduction in NfL (a translatable neurodegeneration biomarker) across both disease models
- Key scientific question: mPTP is implicated in many forms of cell death across tissues -- achieving therapeutic selectivity for CNS neurons without systemic effects on mitochondrial function in heart, liver, and muscle will be critical
- Genetic validation: MPTP (the parkinsonian neurotoxin) kills dopaminergic neurons via mPTP opening; Ppif-knockout mice (lacking CypD) are protected from MPTP neurotoxicity, providing strong genetic evidence for the target in PD

### Clinical

**NRG5051-101 (Phase 1 SAD/MAD)** | EUCT 2025-523872-22-00 | N=TBD | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetic parameters
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, combined single ascending dose (SAD) and multiple ascending dose (MAD)
- **Site:** Centre for Human Drug Research (CHDR), Leiden, The Netherlands
- **Status:** First participants dosed January 2026; readout expected end of 2026
- **Interpretation:** Standard Phase 1 first-in-human design. Key questions beyond safety: does NRG5051 achieve CNS-relevant concentrations at tolerable doses, and can PK support once-daily oral dosing? Results will inform dose selection for Phase 1b in PD and ALS/MND patients.

No PD patient trials initiated yet. Company has stated that Phase 1b in PD patients and proof-of-concept studies in ALS/MND will follow successful Phase 1 completion.

- NRG5051 targets mPTP directly (preventing pathological pore opening), while [[progenra-pink1|Progenra's PINK1 activators]] and [[stealth-bio|Stealth BioTherapeutics]] work upstream on mitophagy -- different nodes of the same mitochondrial dysfunction cascade
- If mPTP inhibition shows clinical benefit in PD, it would validate mitochondrial protection as a therapeutic strategy and boost the broader mitophagy thesis, benefiting [[progenra-pink1|PINK1 activators]] and related approaches
- NRG5051's dual PD/ALS indication strategy is both an advantage (larger market, more shots on goal) and a risk (resources split, regulatory complexity)
- The oral, CNS-penetrant small molecule modality is a major practical advantage over gene therapy or cell therapy approaches to mitochondrial dysfunction -- if efficacious, it would be scalable and accessible
- No other clinical-stage mPTP inhibitors exist for neurodegenerative indications, making NRG5051 genuinely first-in-class in the clinic
- Indirect competitors include any disease-modifying PD approach: [[prasinezumab]] (alpha-synuclein antibody, Phase 3), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (alpha-synuclein siRNA, Phase 2), [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 inhibitor, Phase 3), [[pariceract]] (GBA1 activator, Phase 2) -- all target different nodes of PD pathology

## Analysis

NRG5051 represents a mechanistically novel approach to PD disease modification that sidesteps the crowded alpha-synuclein and genetic-PD landscapes by targeting mitochondrial dysfunction directly. The scientific rationale is sound: mPTP opening is a well-established driver of dopaminergic neuron death, with genetic validation from CypD-knockout studies and decades of literature linking mitochondrial dysfunction to PD pathogenesis. The key innovation is NRG's discovery of a novel mitochondrial protein target ("protein A") that enables CNS-penetrant small molecule inhibition -- something first-generation CypD-targeted approaches like cyclosporin A could never achieve.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 2 entry probability (i.e., clean Phase 1 enabling progression): 60-65%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for Phase 1 safety success in small molecules is ~70%; adjustments upward for oral route and well-characterized target biology (+5%); adjustments downward for novel undisclosed target (risk of unexpected on-target toxicity in non-CNS tissues where mitochondria are essential, -10%). Net: ~60-65%. The bigger uncertainty is whether NRG5051 achieves sufficient CNS exposure at safe doses -- this is the make-or-break question that Phase 1 PK data will answer.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of ultimately demonstrating disease modification in PD: 8-12%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for neurodegenerative disease modification success is extremely low (~5%); adjustments upward for strong genetic validation of target (+3%), novel mechanism avoiding alpha-synuclein field failures (+3%), clean preclinical NfL signal (+2%); adjustments downward for the fundamental challenge that PD is likely multifactorial and mPTP may be a downstream effect rather than a causal driver (-3%), no clinical efficacy data yet (-3%). Net: ~8-12%.

**Signal analysis:** The investor syndicate is strong and strategically relevant. SV Health's DDF specializes in neurodegenerative disease -- they perform deep scientific diligence and their lead reflects conviction in the mechanism. Novartis Venture Fund's participation signals pharma-level interest and potential future licensing or acquisition optionality. Parkinson's UK's ongoing involvement from seed through Series B provides domain validation. The MJFF's escalating grant commitment ($500K to $500K to $5M) shows increasing confidence as the program matured through discovery into IND-enabling studies. The company's decision to pursue both PD and ALS/MND with the same molecule is efficient (shared Phase 1, potential for accelerated ALS pathway) but will force prioritization decisions post-Phase 1. ALS may move faster clinically given the disease severity and regulatory flexibility, potentially generating proof-of-mechanism data that de-risks the PD program.
