# Semaglutide

## Summary

Semaglutide (Novo Nordisk, big pharma, NVO) is an oral GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for type 2 diabetes and obesity, now being tested as a repurposed disease-modifying therapy in PD. The MOST-ABLE Phase 2 trial in Japan (N=99, Osaka University) is evaluating oral semaglutide 7 mg and 14 mg vs. placebo over 36 weeks with a 12-week washout, with completion expected March 2026. A separate Novo Nordisk-sponsored Phase 2 trial (NCT03659682, N=120) using injectable semaglutide was registered in 2018 but has not recruited. The GLP-1 class is validated by lixisenatide's positive LixiPark Phase 2 (p=0.0068 on MDS-UPDRS III) but undermined by exenatide's clean Phase 3 miss (Exenatide-PD3, Lancet 2025) and semaglutide's own EVOKE/EVOKE+ Phase 3 failure in Alzheimer's. If MOST-ABLE shows a clear motor signal with disease-modification evidence at washout, it would reinvigorate the GLP-1 class in PD and likely trigger a larger Novo Nordisk-sponsored Phase 3. If it misses, combined with the exenatide Phase 3 failure, the GLP-1 repurposing thesis in PD is substantially weakened.

## Notes

### Science
- GLP-1 receptors are expressed on neurons and microglia in the substantia nigra and other brain regions; agonism triggers downstream anti-inflammatory and pro-survival signaling via cAMP/PKA/CREB pathways
- In the chronic MPTP mouse model, semaglutide reduced alpha-synuclein accumulation, rescued tyrosine hydroxylase (TH+) neuron loss, improved motor impairments, and attenuated chronic neuroinflammation — outperforming liraglutide in most parameters
- Semaglutide also showed neuroprotection in 6-OHDA models through enhanced autophagy and reduced oxidative stress
- The oral formulation (Rybelsus) uses SNAC absorption enhancer for gastric absorption; whether sufficient CNS penetration occurs via oral route is debated — injectable semaglutide may achieve higher brain exposure
- Key distinction from failed exenatide: semaglutide has a longer half-life (~1 week vs. exenatide's daily/weekly dosing), higher potency at the GLP-1 receptor, and potentially better CNS penetration — the "not all GLP-1 agonists are equal" hypothesis
- Epidemiological evidence: diabetic patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists show ~40-60% reduced PD incidence vs. other antidiabetic medications (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.24-0.66), though confounders are substantial
- Open question: is the GLP-1 effect in PD genuinely disease-modifying (slowing neurodegeneration) or symptomatic (improving dopaminergic signaling)? The lixisenatide washout data partially addressed this — motor benefit persisted after washout, favoring disease modification — but exenatide Phase 3 found no benefit at all

### Clinical

**MOST-ABLE (Phase 2)** | U1111-1271-3794 (Japan Registry) | N=99 | Early PD, Hoehn & Yahr 1-2.5
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in MDS-UPDRS Part III in practically defined off-medication state from baseline to 48 weeks
- **Design:** 3-arm (oral semaglutide 7 mg, 14 mg, placebo), 36-week treatment with dose titration + 12-week washout
- **Key secondary:** Japanese MDS-UPDRS, PDQ-39, MoCA, Symbol Digit Modalities Test, dopamine transporter imaging
- **Sponsor:** Osaka University Hospital (PI: Y. Kimura); funded by Japan Agency for Medical Research and Development (AMED)
- **Status:** Recruiting began November 2023; completion planned March 2026
- **Interpretation:** The 12-week washout is critical — if motor benefit persists post-washout, it supports disease modification over symptomatic effect (same logic as LixiPark). However, N=99 across three arms (~33/arm) is underpowered for definitive conclusions; this is a signal-finding study.

**GLP1R in Parkinson's Disease (Phase 2)** | NCT03659682 | N=120 (planned) | Newly diagnosed PD
- **Design:** 1 mg injectable semaglutide weekly vs. placebo for 2 years, with 2-year open-label extension
- **Sponsor:** University of Oslo (Haukeland University Hospital)
- **Status:** Registered September 2018; has NOT begun recruiting as of 2026
- **Interpretation:** This trial would have been more informative (larger N, longer duration, injectable route) but appears stalled. If it eventually recruits, the 4-year design would provide robust disease-modification data.

**EVOKE / EVOKE+ (Phase 3, Alzheimer's)** | NCT04777409 / NCT05804006 | N=3,808 total | Early AD
- **Primary endpoint:** CDR-Sum of Boxes change → **FAILED** (no superiority over placebo, November 2025)
- **Interpretation:** Not a PD trial, but highly relevant — semaglutide's failure in AD despite biomarker improvements raises fundamental questions about whether GLP-1 receptor agonism translates to meaningful clinical benefit in neurodegeneration. Novo Nordisk discontinued extension periods.

### Financial
- Semaglutide is already one of the highest-revenue drugs in history: ~$29.3B total semaglutide revenue in 2024 (across Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus); Rybelsus oral specifically generated ~DKK 23.3B (~$3.4B) in 2024
- PD repurposing represents negligible incremental revenue for Novo Nordisk given existing franchise, but could expand label and extend exclusivity
- The MOST-ABLE trial is investigator-initiated and AMED-funded — Novo Nordisk is not bearing trial costs, reflecting low commercial commitment to PD indication specifically
- Novo Nordisk's EVOKE failure in AD (~$500M+ investment) is a negative signal for the company's appetite to pursue further neurodegenerative indications
- No dedicated Novo Nordisk PD development program has been announced despite the epidemiological signal; the company's neuroscience investment is largely written off post-EVOKE
- For investors: semaglutide PD data is immaterial to NVO stock (~$300B+ market cap); the thesis matters for the broader GLP-1-in-PD field rather than for Novo Nordisk specifically

- The GLP-1 class in PD includes three agents with clinical data: **lixisenatide** (LixiPark Phase 2 positive, p=0.0068 on MDS-UPDRS III; being advanced to Phase 3 by Sanofi/Cure Parkinson's), **exenatide** (Phase 3 failure, Lancet 2025, N=194, no benefit over placebo), and semaglutide (Phase 2 ongoing)
- Lixisenatide's positive Phase 2 is the strongest clinical signal in the class, but the exenatide Phase 3 failure casts doubt on whether the class effect is real or whether lixisenatide was a false positive
- Semaglutide's potential advantage is oral formulation (vs. subcutaneous injection for lixisenatide/exenatide) and higher GLP-1R potency, but its EVOKE failure in AD weakens the neurodegeneration thesis
- The competitive landscape for disease modification in PD is crowded — semaglutide competes with fundamentally different approaches: [[prasinezumab]] (anti-alpha-synuclein antibody, Phase 3), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (alpha-synuclein siRNA), [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 inhibitor), [[pariceract]] (GBA1 activator) — all targeting specific PD biology rather than broad neuroprotection
- If the GLP-1 class ultimately validates in PD, the commercial winner will likely be whichever agent has the best-tolerated oral formulation — giving semaglutide (Rybelsus) a structural advantage over injectable competitors. GI tolerability (nausea rates ~46% with lixisenatide) remains a class-wide concern

## Analysis

The semaglutide-in-PD story sits at a precarious inflection point. The epidemiological signal (40-60% PD risk reduction in GLP-1 users) and lixisenatide's positive Phase 2 generated significant enthusiasm, but two subsequent events have substantially dampened it: exenatide's clean Phase 3 miss in PD (Lancet, February 2025) and semaglutide's own EVOKE failure in AD (November 2025). The field must now grapple with whether lixisenatide's result was a true signal or a Phase 2 false positive — a question the underpowered MOST-ABLE trial (N=99 across 3 arms) may not definitively answer.

**Analytical estimate — probability of MOST-ABLE showing a meaningful signal: 25-30%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: GLP-1 agonists in PD Phase 2 — 1/2 positive (lixisenatide yes, exenatide Phase 2 mixed) → ~50% starting point
- Adjustments upward: semaglutide higher potency than exenatide (+5%), preclinical data showing superiority to liraglutide (+5%), washout design can demonstrate disease modification (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: exenatide Phase 3 failure suggests class effect may not be real (-15%), EVOKE AD failure undermines broad neuroprotection thesis (-10%), small sample size increases noise risk (-10%), oral route may have inferior CNS penetration (-5%)
- Net: ~25-30%

**Signal analysis:**
- Novo Nordisk's behavior is telling: they invested ~$500M+ in EVOKE for AD but have NOT launched their own PD trial (NCT03659682 never recruited). The MOST-ABLE trial is investigator-initiated and government-funded. This signals that Novo Nordisk's internal assessment of GLP-1 in neurodegeneration is cautious at best, and post-EVOKE, likely negative.
- The decision tree is straightforward: if MOST-ABLE shows a clear motor signal that persists through washout, it reignites the GLP-1-in-PD thesis and likely triggers industry-sponsored Phase 3 trials (Novo Nordisk or Sanofi/lixisenatide). If it misses, combined with exenatide Phase 3 failure, the repurposing thesis is effectively dead and the field moves on to purpose-built disease-modifying approaches like [[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], and [[pariceract]].
- The broader strategic question is whether PD needs a targeted intervention (alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1) or whether broad neuroprotection via metabolic pathways could be sufficient. The GLP-1 class represents the latter hypothesis. The emerging pattern — positive Phase 2s that fail to replicate in Phase 3 — is consistent with broad neuroprotection providing marginal benefit that disappears with larger, more rigorous trials.
