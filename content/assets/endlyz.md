# ATP13A2 Modulators (Endlyz Therapeutics)

## Summary

Endlyz Therapeutics (startup, private) emerged from stealth in March 2025 after four years of incubation by SV Health Investors' Dementia Discovery Fund (DDF), developing first-in-class small molecule potentiators of ATP13A2 (PARK9) — a lysosomal transporter whose loss-of-function causes Kufor-Rakeb syndrome and is genetically validated in PD. The program sits in the broader lysosomal dysfunction thesis alongside [[pariceract|GBA1 modulators]] and [[ambroxol]], but targets a distinct enzyme with NHP validation published in 2024. ATP13A2 was selected as one of five priority targets by MJFF's Target to Therapies Initiative, providing non-dilutive validation. If the IND-enabling studies succeed and Series A closes, Endlyz enters a still-uncrowded lysosomal niche with first-mover advantage on PARK9; if preclinical pharmacology does not translate, the program lacks the clinical de-risking that more advanced lysosomal assets like [[pariceract]] already have.

## Notes

### Science
- ATP13A2 is a lysosomal P5B-type ATPase that transports polyamines (spermine, spermidine) across lysosomal membranes; recently characterized as a lysosomal H+/K+-ATPase contributing to lysosomal acidification and alpha-synuclein degradation
- Loss-of-function mutations in ATP13A2 (PARK9 locus) cause **Kufor-Rakeb syndrome** — autosomal recessive, juvenile-onset parkinsonism with brain iron accumulation, supranuclear gaze palsy, and cognitive decline (OMIM #606693)
- ATP13A2 deficiency leads to impaired lysosomal acidification, decreased proteolytic processing of lysosomal enzymes, reduced substrate degradation, and diminished autophagosome clearance — a cascade that directly promotes alpha-synuclein accumulation
- A 2024 NHP pilot study demonstrated that nigral ATP13A2 depletion induces PD-related neurodegeneration, providing critical large-animal validation beyond rodent models
- ATP13A2 interacts with GBA1 in a digenic manner: partial loss of GBA1 in glia combined with ATP13A2 loss in neurons synergistically disrupts lysosomal pH and glucosylceramide homeostasis — suggesting combination potential with [[pariceract|GBA1 activators]]
- Endlyz's approach is to potentiate (activate) the remaining ATP13A2 function with small molecules, analogous to how [[pariceract]] activates GCase — this requires residual enzyme activity and may not rescue complete loss-of-function alleles
- Key distinction from GBA1 programs: ATP13A2 modulation targets the polyamine transport axis of lysosomal health rather than the lipid/glucocerebrosidase axis, potentially addressing a complementary pathological mechanism
- Open question: whether potentiating ATP13A2 in sporadic PD (where ATP13A2 is not mutated but may be downregulated by aging/pathology) provides meaningful therapeutic benefit — the genetic validation is clear for PARK9 carriers but the sporadic PD case is less proven
- Second pipeline program targets **ATP10B**, a lipid transporter that works in concert with GBA1 to prevent lysosomal glucosylceramide accumulation — also a PD candidate risk gene

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. The program is in preclinical stage.

- IND-enabling studies planned contingent on Series A financing (targeted 2025-2026)
- Initial clinical proof-of-concept will target genetic subsets of PD (PARK9 mutation carriers), with planned expansion to sporadic PD
- MJFF $2.2M grant supports biomarker discovery to bridge preclinical-to-clinical translation
- The NHP study (npj Parkinson's Disease, 2024) demonstrating ATP13A2 depletion-induced nigral neurodegeneration is the most advanced in vivo validation
- ATP13A2 knockout rat model phenotypic characterization published in 2025, providing additional rodent pharmacology tools
- No IND filed; no NCT numbers

- **First-mover on PARK9:** No other company is publicly developing ATP13A2 modulators — Endlyz has an exclusive collaboration with Peter Vangheluwe (KU Leuven), the leading academic expert on this transporter
- The closest competitive frame is the broader **lysosomal dysfunction thesis**: [[pariceract]] (GBA1 activator, Phase 2b) and [[ambroxol]] (GBA1 chaperone, Phase 2 academic) target the same organelle through a different enzyme — GCase rather than ATP13A2
- ATP13A2 and GBA1 are mechanistically complementary rather than competitive: digenic interactions suggest combination therapy could be rational, not zero-sum
- If [[pariceract]] or [[ambroxol]] demonstrate clinical proof-of-concept for the lysosomal PD thesis, it validates the organelle-level strategy and could de-risk Endlyz by analogy
- If GBA1 programs fail, it raises questions about whether lysosomal restoration in general is sufficient to modify PD progression — this would be a negative read-across for ATP13A2 modulation
- SandboxAQ collaboration on ATP10B drug discovery introduces an AI-enabled discovery angle to the second pipeline program
- The PARK9 patient population is very small (rare mutations), making initial PoC trials feasible but commercial scale dependent on sporadic PD expansion

## Analysis

Endlyz represents a high-risk, first-in-class bet on a genetically validated but therapeutically unexplored PD target. The ATP13A2/PARK9 locus has strong Mendelian validation (Kufor-Rakeb syndrome) and growing functional biology — the 2024 NHP study and MJFF's inclusion of ATP13A2 among five priority targets provide meaningful non-clinical endorsement. However, no small molecule modulator of ATP13A2 has entered clinical development anywhere, making this a true discovery-stage investment thesis despite the $16M already deployed.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of reaching Phase 2 clinical data: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for preclinical-to-Phase-2 transition in neurodegeneration is approximately 15-20%; adjustments upward for strong genetic validation (+5%), first-mover exclusivity on target (+3%), DDF/AbbVie syndicate quality (+2%); adjustments downward for no disclosed lead compound or potency data (-5%), no clinical safety/PK data (-5%), small molecule CNS penetration unknowns (-3%), dependence on Series A close (-2%). Net: ~10-15%.

The four-year DDF incubation is a revealing signal. DDF, managed by SV Health Investors, typically incubates programs until they reach a defined preclinical milestone before launching publicly. The fact that they chose to emerge in March 2025 — rather than earlier — suggests the team reached a go/no-go inflection, likely compound identification with acceptable in vitro potency and selectivity. The simultaneous MJFF grant for biomarker development further suggests they have a molecule and now need translational tools to design the first-in-human study.

The investor syndicate is strategically constructed. AbbVie Ventures brings neuroscience commercial capabilities and potential future acquirer optionality. Oxford Science Enterprises connects to the Wade-Martins lab (iPSC disease modeling). CD3 at KU Leuven provides the Vangheluwe structural biology and medicinal chemistry pipeline. Parkinson's UK provides patient community access and regulatory advocacy. This is not random — it is a deliberate build for a program that needs academic depth, translational biomarkers, and eventual pharma partnership.

The key decision tree: if the Series A closes and IND-enabling studies show clean tox/PK, Endlyz becomes a compelling genetic-PD platform with two lysosomal targets (ATP13A2 + ATP10B) and potential combination rationale with [[pariceract|GBA1 programs]]. If preclinical pharmacology disappoints — particularly if ATP13A2 potentiation in wild-type animals fails to show phenotypic rescue — the program stalls at the most capital-efficient failure point, which is the correct design for a target-risk investment.
