# CT1812

## Summary

CT1812 (zervimesine) is a sigma-2 receptor antagonist from Cognition Therapeutics (biotech, CGTX) that is primarily an Alzheimer's and DLB drug with an exploratory PD preclinical program funded by an MJFF Therapeutic Pipeline Program Grant (2022). Preclinical data published in 2021 showed sigma-2 antagonists rescue neurons from alpha-synuclein oligomer-induced lysosomal trafficking defects, establishing biological rationale for PD. The Phase 2 SHIMMER trial in dementia with Lewy bodies (a synucleinopathy sharing pathology with PD) met its primary endpoint and showed improvement across motor, cognitive, and behavioral measures including MDS-UPDRS Part III -- the closest clinical evidence to PD relevance. However, there are no PD-specific clinical trials registered, and the company's stated pipeline priorities are AD (Phase 3 planning) and DLB, not PD. If the MJFF-funded preclinical studies in PD animal models produce strong efficacy signals, this could advance toward a PD IND; if not, it remains a DLB/AD asset with indirect PD relevance through shared alpha-synuclein pathology.

## Notes

### Science
- CT1812 is an orally bioavailable, brain-penetrant small molecule that selectively antagonizes the sigma-2 receptor (encoded by TMEM97), blocking the ability of toxic oligomers (both amyloid-beta and alpha-synuclein) to bind neuronal synapses
- Mechanism in PD context: alpha-synuclein oligomers disrupt autophagy and intracellular trafficking, causing protein accumulation and neuronal death. Sigma-2 receptor antagonism rescues these lysosomal trafficking defects in preclinical models
- Key 2021 publication (Limegrover et al., J Neurosci Res) screened compounds against alpha-synuclein oligomer-induced neuronal deficits and found sigma-2 antagonists were the most effective class at rescuing function
- The sigma-2 receptor is a distinct target from the direct alpha-synuclein clearance approach used by [[prasinezumab]] or the production-inhibition approach of [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] -- it intervenes at the receptor level to block oligomer toxicity rather than reducing alpha-synuclein protein levels
- Open question: whether blocking oligomer-receptor interaction is sufficient for disease modification, or whether intracellular aggregation (Lewy body formation) continues independently of extracellular oligomer signaling
- The DLB and PD connection is biologically meaningful -- both are alpha-synucleinopathies, and the Lewy body pathology is shared, but motor neuron vulnerability patterns differ

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated in Parkinson's disease specifically.

**MJFF-Funded Preclinical Program** | No NCT | Preclinical | PD animal models
- **Objective:** Test CT1812 and CT2168 (second-generation sigma-2 modulator) in animal models of Parkinson's disease for impact on biomarkers of disease progression
- **PI:** Mary E. Hamby, Ph.D. (VP of Research, Cognition Therapeutics)
- **Funding:** MJFF Therapeutic Pipeline Program Grant (Fall 2021 RFP, announced January 2022)
- **Status:** Active (preclinical)
- **Interpretation:** This is exploratory proof-of-concept work. If CT1812 shows efficacy, it could advance to Phase 2 in PD given existing human safety data. If CT2168 proves superior, it would require separate IND-enabling studies.

**SHIMMER (Phase 2, DLB -- adjacent indication)** | NCT04735536 | N=130 | Mild-to-moderate dementia with Lewy bodies
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability --> Met
- **Key secondary:** 82% slowing of neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPI); 91% reduction in cognitive fluctuation decline; improvement in parkinsonian motor symptoms (MDS-UPDRS Part III); improvements in episodic memory, MoCA, and activities of daily living
- **Doses:** 100 mg and 300 mg oral daily vs. placebo for 6 months
- **Funding:** ~$30M NIH/NIA grant
- **Status:** Completed (November 2024); expanded access program launched June 2025
- **Interpretation:** DLB shares alpha-synuclein pathology with PD, making these results directionally relevant. The motor symptom improvement on MDS-UPDRS Part III is the most PD-relevant endpoint. However, DLB patient populations differ from PD populations, and formal statistical analysis has not been fully published as of early 2026.

## Analysis

CT1812 is a speculative PD asset at this stage. The biological rationale is sound -- sigma-2 receptor antagonism rescuing neurons from alpha-synuclein oligomer toxicity is a differentiated mechanism with published preclinical evidence. The DLB SHIMMER data showing motor symptom improvement on MDS-UPDRS Part III in a synucleinopathy population is directionally encouraging. But the gap between "MJFF-funded preclinical program" and "clinical PD candidate" is substantial, and Cognition Therapeutics has neither the capital nor the stated strategic priority to bridge it quickly.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of CT1812 entering a PD-specific clinical trial within 3 years: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base: Company has existing human safety data and oral formulation (removes IND timeline risk) (+15%)
- MJFF preclinical data could provide proof-of-concept for direct advancement (+10%)
- Adjustments downward: Limited cash runway and AD/DLB strategic priority means PD competes for resources (-15%), small biotech with ~$130M market cap lacks capital for multi-indication development (-10%), no registered PD trial or disclosed PD development timeline (-5%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:** The MJFF grant signals external scientific validation of the PD rationale -- MJFF's Therapeutic Pipeline Program is competitive and disease-specific. The company's choice to pursue the grant while focusing clinical resources on AD/DLB suggests PD is an optionality play rather than a committed program. The most likely path to a PD clinical trial would be: (1) positive preclinical data from MJFF studies, (2) successful AD Phase 3 funding/partnership that frees bandwidth, and (3) potential repositioning if the DLB expanded access program generates compelling motor data. The CT2168 backup compound adds optionality -- if it shows PD-specific advantages over CT1812, it could become the PD candidate while CT1812 remains focused on AD/DLB.

For the PD pipeline tracking purposes, this asset belongs in a "watch list" category -- real science, credible company, but no clinical PD commitment. The DLB SHIMMER motor data and MJFF preclinical readout are the two signals to monitor.
