# Seal Rock Therapeutics LASC Inhibitors

## Summary

A preclinical, first-in-class dual LRRK2/ASK1 inhibitor program from Seal Rock Therapeutics (startup, private), a Seattle-based company that built its ASK1 expertise in liver disease (SRT-015, clinical-stage for NASH/ACLF) and is now extending to neurodegeneration. Joined the MJFF LITE program in August 2025, gaining access to the 30+ academic center consortium and PPMI/GP2 resources. The key differentiator vs. selective LRRK2 inhibitors like [[biib122|BIIB122]] is the claim that co-inhibiting ASK1 alongside LRRK2 eliminates the reversible type II pneumocyte and kidney toxicity seen as a class effect with LRRK2-only inhibitors -- if validated, this would be a meaningful safety advantage for chronic dosing in a neurodegenerative population. PD is a secondary therapeutic extension; liver disease (SRT-015) is the company's primary franchise and revenue driver. If [[biib122|BIIB122]] LUMA succeeds, dual LASC inhibition becomes a potential best-in-class follow-on; if LUMA fails and invalidates the LRRK2 kinase hypothesis entirely, this program loses its rationale alongside the class.

## Notes

### Science
- LASC (LRRK2/ASK1 Signaling Complex) inhibitors are oral, brain-penetrant small molecules that block both LRRK2 kinase activity and ASK1 (Apoptosis Signal-regulating Kinase 1) simultaneously
- The core claim: dual inhibition is more neuroprotective than targeting either LRRK2 or ASK1 alone, based on preclinical models (details unpublished as of February 2026)
- ASK1 is a MAP kinase kinase kinase (MAP3K5) that activates JNK/p38 stress pathways; implicated in neuronal apoptosis, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress -- biologically plausible as a neurodegeneration target independent of LRRK2
- The safety proposition: selective LRRK2 kinase inhibitors cause reversible accumulation of lamellar bodies in type II pneumocytes (lung) in NHP studies -- a class effect documented with GNE-7915, GNE-0877, MLi-2, PFE-360, and [[biib122|BIIB122]]; Seal Rock claims LASC inhibition eliminates this lung toxicity as well as kidney findings, though the mechanism for this improvement is not yet published
- Seal Rock's ASK1 expertise derives from their lead program SRT-015, a first-in-class, liver-selective ASK1 inhibitor that completed Phase 1 (69 healthy adults, single and multiple ascending dose) with clean safety and favorable PK supporting once- or twice-daily dosing
- The company was founded by Takeda veterans (Neil McDonnell, Artur Plonowski, Kathleen Elias) in 2016, likely building on Takeda's internal ASK1 preclinical work
- Open scientific questions: (1) Is the LASC complex a validated drug target distinct from inhibiting LRRK2 and ASK1 separately? (2) What is the mechanistic basis for the claimed elimination of LRRK2-class lung toxicity? (3) Does brain penetrance of the LASC inhibitor achieve sufficient target engagement in CNS to affect PD pathology? (4) Can a dual-target inhibitor achieve selectivity adequate for chronic dosing without off-target liabilities?

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated for the LASC inhibitor program. The PD/neurodegenerative indication is preclinical.

Note: Seal Rock's lead clinical program is SRT-015 (ASK1 inhibitor for liver disease), which has completed Phase 1 and was out-licensed to GENFIT for injectable ACLF formulation. This clinical experience with ASK1 inhibition informs the LASC program but is a different compound for a different indication.

### Financial
- **Total raised:** ~$8.6M (Crunchbase data as of 2025), primarily from NuFund Venture Group, Hanawa Group, K2X, and USAMRAA (US Army Medical Research) -- modest funding for a company with a clinical-stage asset and preclinical CNS program
- **GENFIT deal (May 2023):** Out-licensed injectable SRT-015 for ACLF; up to EUR 100M in milestones plus tiered royalties -- this validates the ASK1 platform and provides non-dilutive capital
- **MJFF LITE membership (August 2025):** Non-dilutive support through access to preclinical/clinical resources, biomarkers (PPMI, GP2), and consortium expertise across 30+ centers; does not appear to include direct grant funding based on available disclosures
- **Valuation:** Unknown; the modest fundraise and private status make this a very early-stage investment profile
- **Comparable deals:** No direct comparable for a preclinical dual LRRK2/ASK1 program; the Biogen-Denali LRRK2 deal ($2.15B total, $1.025B upfront for [[biib122|BIIB122]]) sets the ceiling for validated LRRK2 assets but is not comparable given the stage difference

## Analysis

Seal Rock's LASC program is a scientifically interesting but very early-stage entry into the LRRK2 space, and PD is clearly secondary to the company's core liver disease franchise. The founding team's Takeda ASK1 pedigree and the completed Phase 1 for SRT-015 demonstrate genuine ASK1 pharmacology expertise, and the GENFIT deal provides commercial validation of the platform. The extension to a brain-penetrant dual LRRK2/ASK1 molecule is a logical adjacency, but the program lacks published preclinical data, a named development candidate, or a disclosed IND timeline as of February 2026.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching Phase 1 in PD: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for preclinical programs reaching Phase 1: ~30%
- Adjustments upward: validated ASK1 platform (SRT-015 Phase 1 done) (+5%), MJFF LITE access to biomarkers and consortium (+5%), novel mechanism with potential safety advantage (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: PD is secondary indication, company may deprioritize (-10%), modest capitalization for CNS development (-10%), no published preclinical PD data (-5%), dual-target selectivity risk (-5%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:**
- MJFF LITE membership is a positive signal -- MJFF is selective about LITE participants and brings meaningful preclinical resources, but it is not a large capital injection. It suggests MJFF scientific advisors found the dual-inhibition hypothesis credible enough to include.
- The liver-first, neuro-second strategy is common for small companies managing capital constraints. The GENFIT deal provides milestone revenue that could fund early LASC development without additional equity raises.
- The company's total raise of ~$8.6M is very low for a clinical-stage company, suggesting either extreme capital efficiency, significant non-dilutive funding (USAMRAA, GENFIT milestones), or limited ambition for the CNS program. This is a key watchpoint: will Seal Rock raise a dedicated CNS-focused round?
- The decision tree is dependent on external events: a positive [[biib122|BIIB122]] LUMA readout would validate the LRRK2 target and create a clear path for LASC as a best-in-class safety play; a negative LUMA readout would make it nearly impossible to fund a preclinical dual LRRK2 program.
