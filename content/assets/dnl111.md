# DNL111

## Summary

DNL111 is Denali Therapeutics' (biotech, DNLI) application of its Enzyme Transport Vehicle (ETV) platform to deliver recombinant GCase enzyme across the blood-brain barrier for GBA1-associated Parkinson's disease and Gaucher disease. The asset is in IND-enabling studies with no clinical data yet. Denali's TV platform has been validated in humans through tividenofusp alfa (ETV:IDS) for Hunter syndrome, which completed BLA submission in 2025 with a PDUFA date of April 5, 2026 -- if approved, it de-risks the core platform technology underlying DNL111. The competitive landscape for GBA1-targeted PD therapy is crowded: oral small molecule activators ([[pariceract]], [[gt-02287|GT-02287]]) are further along clinically, and AAV gene therapies (Lilly/Prevail PR001) address the same genetic target with a different modality. If [[pariceract]]'s ACTIVATE Phase 2b succeeds (mid-2026), the oral route may dominate GBA-PD and narrow DNL111's addressable population to patients requiring enzyme replacement-level correction. If ACTIVATE and GT-02287 both fail, DNL111's direct enzyme delivery approach becomes one of the few remaining paths to restoring GCase in the CNS.

## Notes

### Science
- GBA1 mutations are the most common genetic risk factor for PD (5-10% of all PD patients, 20-30x increased risk), encoding the lysosomal enzyme glucocerebrosidase (GCase). Reduced GCase activity leads to accumulation of glucosylceramide and glucosylsphingosine, lysosomal dysfunction, and alpha-synuclein aggregation
- DNL111 fuses recombinant GCase enzyme to Denali's proprietary Enzyme Transport Vehicle -- an engineered Fc fragment that binds the transferrin receptor (TfR1) on brain endothelial cells, enabling receptor-mediated transcytosis across the BBB after IV administration
- In preclinical models, ETV-fused enzymes achieve 10-30x greater brain exposure than unfused enzymes, substantially correcting downstream pathologies including lysosomal lipid accumulation, neuroinflammation, and neuroaxonal damage
- The platform has been validated clinically with ETV:IDS (tividenofusp alfa / DNL310) for Hunter syndrome, which demonstrated CNS biomarker proof-of-concept in Phase 1/2 and is now under FDA BLA review -- the first TV-enabled therapeutic to reach regulatory submission
- Key distinction vs. small molecule GCase activators ([[pariceract]], [[gt-02287|GT-02287]]): enzyme replacement delivers exogenous functional GCase directly, which could achieve higher absolute enzyme activity restoration than allosteric activation of misfolded endogenous enzyme. However, IV infusion is a significant burden vs. oral dosing
- Key distinction vs. AAV gene therapy (Lilly/Prevail PR001): ETV:GCase is a chronic IV therapy (repeat dosing), whereas AAV gene therapy is a one-time intracisternal injection delivering a permanent GBA1 gene copy. AAV carries immunogenicity and manufacturing scalability risks; ETV carries chronic dosing burden
- Open question: whether peripheral IV delivery of ETV:GCase achieves sufficient enzyme activity in deep brain structures (substantia nigra, basal ganglia) where PD pathology is concentrated, vs. more direct delivery routes

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. DNL111 is in IND-enabling studies as of early 2026. Denali has stated it expects to advance 1-2 additional programs to the clinic per year over the next three years, suggesting DNL111 could enter Phase 1 in the 2027 timeframe.

Key preclinical milestones to watch:
- NHP pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic data demonstrating brain GCase activity restoration
- GLP toxicology studies supporting IND submission
- Manufacturing scale-up of the ETV:GCase fusion protein

### Financial
- **Denali financial position:** ~$872.9M cash as of Q3 2025, supplemented by ~$200M equity raise (Dec 2025) and up to $275M royalty deal with Royalty Pharma tied to tividenofusp alfa -- total liquidity ~$1.3B, providing multi-year runway
- **Market cap:** ~$2.57B as of January 2026
- **Biogen partnership:** $2.15B total deal signed August 2020 ($560M upfront + $465M equity + up to $1.125B milestones). The collaboration primarily covers LRRK2 ([[biib122|BIIB122/DNL151]]) but includes option rights on TV platform programs -- Biogen's continued involvement provides potential co-development/co-commercialization path for DNL111 though specific deal terms for this asset are not disclosed
- **Platform economics:** Tividenofusp alfa's regulatory outcome (PDUFA April 5, 2026) is a binary event for the entire ETV platform valuation. Approval would validate the manufacturing and clinical pathway for all ETV-fused enzymes including DNL111
- **No standalone deal or valuation for DNL111** -- it is an internal program within Denali's broader TV platform portfolio. Value is embedded in DNLI equity, alongside multiple other programs (DNL422/OTV:SNCA, DNL921/ATV:Abeta, DNL628/OTV:MAPT)

- [[pariceract]] (BIAL, Phase 2b) is the most advanced GBA1-targeted oral small molecule -- allosteric activator of endogenous GCase enzyme. ACTIVATE Phase 2b readout mid-2026 is the key competitive inflection point. If positive, oral dosing convenience likely dominates over IV enzyme replacement for GBA-PD patients with residual enzyme activity
- [[gt-02287|GT-02287]] (Gain Therapeutics, Phase 1b) is a second oral GCase activator showing 81% CSF GluSph reduction in patients with elevated baseline levels. Phase 2 planned for early 2026 with 100-200 patients
- Lilly/Prevail PR001 (LY3884961) is an AAV9 gene therapy delivering functional GBA1 gene via intracisternal injection -- one-time treatment but invasive route. PROPEL Phase 1/2 ongoing in GBA-PD patients
- Sanofi's venglustat (substrate reduction therapy) was terminated after MOVES-PD showed worsening motor symptoms despite achieving target pharmacology -- a cautionary precedent that biochemical target engagement in the GBA1 pathway does not guarantee clinical benefit
- DNL111's niche may be patients with null GBA1 mutations (no residual enzyme to activate) where small molecule activators have limited utility, or patients requiring supraphysiological enzyme correction -- though this is a small subset
- Denali's own [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 inhibitor) addresses PD via a complementary genetic pathway. LRRK2 and GBA1 pathways converge on lysosomal dysfunction, raising the possibility of combination approaches

## Analysis

DNL111 is an early-stage platform extension play, not a standalone asset bet. Its value proposition depends on three sequential de-risking events: (1) tividenofusp alfa FDA approval validating the ETV platform, (2) generation of NHP or clinical PK/PD data showing meaningful brain GCase restoration, and (3) competitive landscape evolution -- specifically, whether oral GCase activators succeed and capture most of the GBA-PD market before DNL111 reaches the clinic.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of reaching Phase 2: 50-60%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for IND-enabling to Phase 2 in rare/genetic disease: ~65%
- Adjustments upward: platform clinically validated with tividenofusp alfa (+10%), strong financial runway (+5%), transferrin receptor transcytosis mechanism proven in humans (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: crowded GBA1 competitive landscape with oral assets 3-5 years ahead (-15%), IV chronic dosing burden for a chronic disease (-10%), no published DNL111-specific preclinical data (-5%)
- Net: ~50-60% probability of reaching Phase 2

The strategic logic for Denali is portfolio optionality rather than DNL111 as a lead program. The ETV platform is being applied across multiple lysosomal enzymes (IDS for Hunter, IDUA for MPS I, GAA for Pompe, GCase for PD/Gaucher). Each approved indication amortizes the platform development cost and regulatory precedent. DNL111 benefits from this "platform conveyor belt" -- every ETV approval strengthens the regulatory and manufacturing pathway for the next. The Gaucher disease indication (non-CNS) could also serve as a faster registration path than PD, generating proof-of-concept data that supports the PD indication.

The decision tree is less about DNL111 in isolation and more about the GBA1 pathway: if [[pariceract]] ACTIVATE succeeds in mid-2026, oral therapy captures the addressable GBA-PD market and DNL111's PD path narrows to null-mutation patients or combination therapy. If ACTIVATE fails and GT-02287 Phase 2 also disappoints, DNL111 and PR001 gene therapy become the remaining viable approaches to GCase restoration in the CNS, significantly increasing the strategic value of the ETV:GCase program.
