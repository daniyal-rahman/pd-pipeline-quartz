# Spark PD Gene Therapy Program

## Summary

Spark Therapeutics (wholly owned by Roche since the $4.8B acquisition in December 2019) has been flagged in multiple pipeline databases as having a preclinical gene therapy program for Parkinson's disease, but no specific target, transgene, or program code (SPK-xxxx) has been publicly disclosed. Spark's website was blocked during our research (January 2026), and the company underwent a major restructuring in 2024-2025 that resulted in a $2.4B goodwill impairment, the shelving of SPK-8011 (hemophilia A), and full integration into Roche's Pharmaceuticals Division. Within the Roche ecosystem, Spark has access to Dyno Therapeutics' AI-designed CNS-targeted capsids ($1.8B+ total collaboration) and Neurochase's convection-enhanced delivery technology (January 2023 collaboration), but whether either platform is being applied to a PD-specific program is unknown. If Spark discloses a PD program with a credible target and differentiated delivery platform, it enters a crowded gene therapy field competing with [[aav-gad]], [[pr001]], [[ab-1005]], [[cap-003]], and [[gba1-voyager]]. If the program has been deprioritized or shelved during restructuring, this is a non-event -- Roche's PD strategy is anchored by prasinezumab (Phase 3), not gene therapy.

## Notes

### Science
- Spark's core platform is adeno-associated virus (AAV) gene therapy, with approved product Luxturna (AAV2, retinal) and pipeline spanning liver-directed (hemophilia) and CNS (Huntington's disease, Batten disease) indications
- No publicly disclosed PD-specific target. Plausible candidates based on Roche's broader neuroscience strategy and the PD gene therapy field include: GBA1/GCase restoration (the most active PD gene therapy target, pursued by [[pr001|Lilly/Prevail]], [[cap-003|AbbVie/Capsida]], [[gba1-voyager|Neurocrine/Voyager]]), GDNF/neurotrophic factor delivery (pursued by [[ab-1005|AskBio/Bayer]]), or AADC enzyme replacement
- The Pfizer/Spark AAV-GDNF collaboration for PD was previously referenced in our research as having been discontinued, suggesting an earlier PD gene therapy effort that did not advance
- Roche's October 2024 deal with Dyno Therapeutics ($50M upfront, $1B+ milestones) specifically targets engineered AAV capsids for neurological diseases. Dyno's CapsidMap AI platform has generated capsids with 280x CNS transduction efficiency vs. AAV9 -- if applied to PD, this could enable systemic IV delivery similar to [[cap-003]]'s approach
- Spark's January 2023 Neurochase collaboration provides access to convection-enhanced delivery (CED) technology for CNS gene therapy, which could enable more precise intraparenchymal delivery to the putamen or substantia nigra
- The most advanced Spark CNS program is RG6662 (SPK-10001) for Huntington's disease, which dosed its first patient in 2025 -- this confirms Spark's operational capability in CNS gene therapy
- Key scientific question: what target and delivery route would Spark pursue that is not already covered by competitors within the Roche ecosystem? Roche already partners with 4D Molecular Therapeutics and Dyno for capsid engineering

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. No IND-enabling studies have been publicly disclosed for a PD-specific program.

- Spark's CNS clinical experience is limited to the RG6662 (Huntington's) Phase 1 trial and earlier SPK-TPP1 (Batten disease) work
- No ClinicalTrials.gov registrations for Spark + Parkinson's disease
- The January 2026 research flagged "Spark Therapeutics gene therapy" under "Still Needs Manual Verification," indicating this program's existence was noted in pipeline aggregators but could not be confirmed through primary sources

### Financial
- Roche acquired Spark for $4.8B ($114.50/share) in December 2019, making Spark a wholly owned subsidiary
- Roche absorbed a **$2.4B goodwill impairment** on Spark in 2024-2025, with an additional $341M in restructuring costs -- total losses approaching $2.7B+
- Luxturna (Spark's only commercial product) generated $20.5M in 2024, down 59% year-over-year
- SPK-8011 (hemophilia A) shelved December 2024; SPK-8016 and SPK-3006 previously discontinued
- The impairment signals that Roche concluded Spark's future revenue pipeline cannot justify the acquisition price, raising questions about the depth of Spark's preclinical pipeline including any PD program
- If a PD gene therapy is advancing internally, it would be funded entirely from Roche's balance sheet -- no external deal economics or milestone structure to analyze
- For context, competing PD gene therapy deals: AbbVie/Capsida ($665M+), Lilly/Prevail ($1B+), Neurocrine/Voyager ($1.7B), Roche/Dyno ($1.8B+), MeiraGTx/Hologen ($430M)

- The PD gene therapy field is stratified by target: **GBA1/GCase** ([[pr001]], [[cap-003]], [[gba1-voyager]]), **GDNF** ([[ab-1005]]), **GAD** ([[aav-gad]]), and **AADC** (historical programs). A new Spark entrant would need to differentiate on either target or delivery platform
- [[aav-gad|AAV-GAD]] (MeiraGTx) is the most clinically advanced PD gene therapy, with 58 patients treated, RMAT designation, and Phase 3 (exPDite-2) initiated -- this is the nearest-term approval threat that any new entrant must contend with
- [[cap-003|CAP-003]] (Capsida/AbbVie) is the most relevant IV-delivery comparator: BBB-crossing capsid, GBA1 target, IND cleared June 2025, Phase 1/2 dosing underway. If Spark's approach also uses an engineered IV-deliverable capsid (via Dyno), they would compete directly on capsid performance
- [[pr001|PR001]] (Lilly/Prevail) is the most advanced GBA1 gene therapy (Phase 1/2, PROPEL trial), using intracisternal delivery of AAV9. If Spark targets GBA1, this is the direct benchmark
- [[gba1-voyager|Voyager GBA1]] (Neurocrine) is at IND-enabling stage with a TRACER-derived capsid for IV delivery -- similar stage to wherever Spark may be
- [[ab-1005|AB-1005]] (AskBio/Bayer) is the only active GDNF gene therapy, in Phase 2 (REGENERATE-PD) -- if Spark revisits the GDNF approach that Pfizer/Spark previously abandoned, they carry the burden of explaining what has changed
- Given the $2.4B impairment and pipeline attrition, Spark may be consolidating around Huntington's (RG6662) as the lead CNS indication and deprioritizing PD

## Analysis

This is a low-confidence entry. The Spark PD gene therapy program was flagged in pipeline aggregator databases and our internal company tracking lists but has no publicly confirmed target, mechanism, program code, or development timeline. Multiple attempts to access Spark's pipeline page (spark-therapeutics.com/en/pipeline) returned connection errors, and no press releases, SEC filings, or conference presentations have disclosed a PD-specific program.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of meaningful PD program progression: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for undisclosed preclinical programs advancing to IND: ~10-15% (most preclinical candidates never reach IND)
- Adjustments upward: Roche has strategic commitment to PD (prasinezumab Phase 3 validates disease area interest) (+5%), Spark has genuine AAV platform capability (+5%), access to Dyno's AI-designed capsids is a technological differentiator (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: $2.4B goodwill impairment signals pipeline value destruction (-5%), three programs shelved/discontinued in 2024 (-5%), no public disclosure after years as a Roche subsidiary (-5%), Roche may prefer external partnerships (Dyno, 4D Molecular) over internal Spark development for PD (-5%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:**
- The absence of any public disclosure is itself a signal. Companies advancing meaningful programs toward IND typically begin laying groundwork via conference posters, patent filings, or press releases 12-24 months before IND filing. The silence suggests either very early preclinical work, deprioritization, or that the "program" exists only as a platform capability (AAV + CNS delivery) rather than a nominated PD candidate.
- The Roche restructuring of Spark is the dominant signal. A $2.4B impairment, $341M restructuring cost, 59% Luxturna revenue decline, and multiple pipeline terminations indicate a company in strategic retrenchment, not expansion. New PD indication programs are unlikely to be a priority in this context.
- The decision tree is simple: if Spark discloses a PD program with a specific target and differentiated delivery approach, reassess based on the data package. If no disclosure emerges by end of 2026, this entry should be reclassified as "Deprioritized" or removed from active tracking.
- Roche's broader PD commitment (prasinezumab Phase 3) does not directly support a Spark gene therapy program -- these are different modalities addressing different patient populations. However, it does confirm Roche views PD as a strategic disease area worth large investment.
