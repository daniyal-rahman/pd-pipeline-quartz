# ARV-102

## Summary

The first LRRK2 PROTAC degrader in the clinic, achieving >90% LRRK2 protein reduction in PBMCs and >50% in CSF at well-tolerated oral doses — a mechanistic step beyond kinase inhibitors like [[biib122|BIIB122]] because it eliminates the entire LRRK2 protein, including non-kinase scaffolding functions implicated in neurodegeneration. Arvinas (biotech, ARVN) presented positive Phase 1 data at MDS 2025 showing dose-dependent brain penetration, clean safety, and CSF proteomic changes in lysosomal and neuroinflammatory pathways after just 14 days. Multiple-dose PD patient data from ARV-102-103 is expected in 2026 and will determine whether degradation depth in actual patients matches healthy volunteers. If positive, ARV-102 establishes a differentiated position in the LRRK2 space by addressing the scaffolding biology that kinase inhibitors leave untouched; if LRRK2 degradation shows no advantage over inhibition — particularly if [[biib122|BIIB122]]'s LUMA readout validates kinase inhibition alone — the incremental benefit of degradation becomes unclear and ARV-102 may struggle to differentiate.

## Notes

### Science
- PROTAC (Proteolysis-Targeting Chimera): a bifunctional small molecule with one end binding LRRK2 and the other recruiting an E3 ubiquitin ligase, bringing them into proximity to tag LRRK2 for proteasomal degradation
- Eliminates the **entire LRRK2 protein**, not just its kinase activity — this is the core differentiator vs. kinase inhibitors like [[biib122|BIIB122]]. LRRK2 is a large, multidomain scaffolding kinase; increased LRRK2 kinase activity, scaffolding, and expression are all implicated in PD pathogenesis. Scientific data demonstrates that scaffolding function promotes neuronal death and reducing total LRRK2 rescues neuronal loss in PD models
- LRRK2 genetic validation is strong: G2019S gain-of-function mutations are the most common genetic cause of familial PD (1-2% of all PD; up to 40% in North African populations). Common LRRK2 variants are also GWAS hits for idiopathic (sporadic) PD, supporting a broader patient population beyond mutation carriers
- Known on-target concern with LRRK2 kinase inhibitors: type I inhibitors cause LRRK2 dephosphorylation, protein aggregation, and mislocalization to microtubules, with observed effects on lungs and kidneys in preclinical studies. Degradation may avoid these effects by removing the protein rather than trapping it in an abnormal conformation — though this remains to be confirmed clinically
- PROTACs act catalytically (sub-stoichiometric) — a single PROTAC molecule can degrade multiple LRRK2 proteins, potentially requiring lower drug exposure for equivalent target suppression
- Open questions: (1) Is >90% peripheral LRRK2 reduction in PBMCs a reliable proxy for brain tissue degradation, given that CSF reduction was >50%? (2) Does complete LRRK2 protein elimination produce safety liabilities not seen with partial kinase inhibition? (3) Will the scaffolding biology hypothesis hold up in disease-relevant endpoints?

### Clinical

**ARV-102-101 (Phase 1, Healthy Volunteers)** | NCT TBD | N=47 (SAD) + MAD cohorts | Healthy male volunteers
- **Design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled SAD (10-200 mg) and MAD (10-80 mg QD x 14 days) study
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Well tolerated; no discontinuations due to AEs; no SAEs. Most common AEs: headache (17.1% vs 0% placebo), fatigue (8.6% vs 25% placebo)
- **Key PD results:** >90% LRRK2 reduction in PBMCs at single doses >=60 mg and repeated doses >=20 mg; >50% LRRK2 reduction in CSF at same doses; dose-dependent CSF exposure confirming BBB penetration
- **Biomarkers:** >50% decrease in pRab10 at single doses >=30 mg; >90% decrease in urinary BMP at single doses >=30 mg. CSF proteomics at 80 mg QD x 14 days showed significant decreases in lysosomal pathway markers and neuroinflammatory microglial markers elevated in LRRK2-PD patients
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety and deep target engagement — the CSF proteomic changes after only 14 days in healthy volunteers are noteworthy and suggest deeper pathway suppression than kinase inhibition alone achieves. First-in-human data presented at AD/PD 2025 (March) and expanded data at MDS 2025 (October).

**ARV-102-103 (Phase 1, PD Patients)** | EUCT 2024-516888-84-00 | N~19 (15 active, 4 placebo) | PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Only mild treatment-related AEs (headache, diarrhea, nausea); no SAEs
- **Key PD results (single-dose cohort):** Median 86% PBMC LRRK2 reduction at 50 mg; median 97% PBMC LRRK2 reduction at 200 mg
- **Status:** SAD cohort completed; MAD cohort enrolling. Multiple-dose data expected 2026
- **Interpretation:** LRRK2 degradation in PD patients appears to recapitulate healthy volunteer findings. The MAD cohort data in PD patients — including CSF and biomarker endpoints — is the key upcoming readout

**Phase 1b PSP Trial (Planned)** | NCT TBD | Patients with progressive supranuclear palsy
- **Status:** Planned for initiation in H1 2026, pending ARV-102-103 MAD data and IND clearance
- **Interpretation:** PSP expansion is scientifically rational — common LRRK2 variants have been linked with PSP. A dual-indication strategy (PD + PSP) broadens the commercial opportunity and the patient population for clinical development

- **No disclosed LRRK2-specific partnership** — ARV-102 is internally developed. Arvinas has existing partnering capacity (Pfizer partnership for vepdegestrant), so a neuroscience partnership or out-licensing deal is plausible if clinical data continues to mature

- [[biib122|BIIB122]] (Denali/Biogen) is the LRRK2 class leader at Phase 2b — but it is a **kinase inhibitor**, not a degrader. The core competitive question is whether eliminating the entire LRRK2 protein (ARV-102) provides clinical benefit beyond inhibiting kinase activity alone (BIIB122). If BIIB122's LUMA readout in March 2026 shows kinase inhibition is sufficient, the burden on ARV-102 increases to demonstrate degradation adds incremental value
- Type I kinase inhibitors (BIIB122 class) cause LRRK2 mislocalization and aggregation as an on-target effect — theoretically a safety liability that degradation avoids. If long-term BIIB122 data reveals organ toxicity from this mechanism, ARV-102's degradation approach becomes the preferred modality
- ARV-102 is currently the **only LRRK2 PROTAC in clinical development** — first-mover advantage in the degrader subclass, though the PROTAC modality has not yet been validated in any neurodegenerative indication
- If LUMA fails for idiopathic PD, the entire LRRK2 thesis is questioned — ARV-102 could argue that kinase inhibition was the wrong approach and full degradation is needed, but the bar for investor conviction will be high
- [[neu-723|NEU-723]] takes a different approach (precision patient selection for LRRK2 targets); if the field moves toward genetically defined populations, ARV-102's oral brain-penetrant profile positions it well for chronic dosing in LRRK2 mutation carriers

## Analysis

ARV-102 represents a genuinely novel modality application — the first PROTAC degrader to demonstrate brain penetration and CNS target engagement in humans. The >90% LRRK2 reduction in PBMCs and >50% in CSF after oral dosing is a pharmacological achievement, given that PROTACs are large molecules (typically >800 Da) that were historically considered poor candidates for CNS penetration. The CSF proteomic data showing changes in lysosomal and neuroinflammatory markers after just 14 days of dosing in healthy volunteers goes beyond simple target engagement and suggests downstream biological activity.

**Analytical estimate — Phase 2 success probability (if advanced): 20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: LRRK2 inhibition has never been validated in a disease-modifying PD trial; the PROTAC modality has never been validated in neurodegeneration → starting point ~10%
- Adjustments upward: >90% target degradation depth (+5%), demonstrated BBB penetration with CNS biomarker changes (+5%), strong genetic validation for LRRK2 in PD (+5%), differentiated mechanism vs. kinase inhibitors that may avoid on-target toxicity (+5%), catalytic mechanism enabling lower exposure (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: Phase 1 only (long road to efficacy proof) (-5%), PROTAC modality unvalidated in CNS diseases (-3%), CSF reduction only >50% vs >90% peripherally — brain engagement may be insufficient (-3%), unknown long-term safety of complete LRRK2 elimination (-2%)
- Net: ~20-25%

**Signal analysis:**
- Arvinas is primarily an oncology PROTAC company — ARV-102 demonstrates platform extensibility into neuroscience. The fact that they are investing in a PD/PSP program alongside a capital-intensive oncology portfolio suggests internal confidence in the data. However, the neuroscience program is not existential for Arvinas the way BIIB122 is for Denali, which means it could be deprioritized if oncology demands capital.
- The PSP expansion strategy is smart — it tests the LRRK2 thesis in a faster-progressing disease (shorter trials, clearer endpoints) while building a multi-indication package that attracts larger pharma partners.
- The March 2026 LUMA readout for [[biib122|BIIB122]] is the single most important external event for ARV-102. If LUMA validates LRRK2 kinase inhibition in idiopathic PD, ARV-102 becomes a next-generation molecule with a differentiation story (degradation > inhibition). If LUMA fails, ARV-102 is either the last hope for the LRRK2 thesis or collateral damage — the company will need to argue convincingly that degradation addresses what inhibition could not.
