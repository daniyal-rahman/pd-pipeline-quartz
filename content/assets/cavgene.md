# CGT-102 / CGT-103 (CavGene Therapeutics)

## Summary

CavGene Therapeutics (startup, private) is developing first-in-class AAV-delivered RNAi gene therapies (CGT-102 and CGT-103) that silence CaV1.3 L-type calcium channels in the striatum to prevent and reverse levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID) in Parkinson's disease. The program has strong preclinical validation including a landmark 2025 nonhuman primate study showing near-complete LID prevention and progressive reversal of parkinsonian motor deficits without pharmacotherapy. The company was founded in November 2022 as an MSU spinout and remains in preclinical development, seeking grant funding to develop a human-safe viral vector -- clinical trials are likely years away. If NHP results translate, this would be the first gene therapy specifically targeting LID and the first to demonstrate disease-modifying-like motor benefit via calcium channel silencing; if the vector safety profile proves problematic in IND-enabling toxicology, the program stalls at a stage where most academic gene therapy spinouts fail.

## Notes

### Science
- CaV1.3 is a voltage-gated L-type calcium channel (encoded by CACNA1D) expressed on medium spiny neurons (MSNs) in the striatum
- In PD, dopamine loss causes compensatory upregulation of CaV1.3, driving excessive calcium influx that damages dendritic spines on MSNs and disrupts normal motor circuit function
- Chronic levodopa treatment further exacerbates CaV1.3-mediated calcium overload, which is a key driver of levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- CaV1.3-selective oral antagonists exist but cannot achieve therapeutic brain concentrations without causing severe cardiovascular side effects (CaV1.3 is also expressed in cardiac tissue) -- hence the target is considered "undruggable" by conventional pharmacology
- CavGene's approach uses AAV to deliver short hairpin RNA (shRNA) directly into the putamen via MRI-guided stereotactic injection, achieving local gene silencing of CaV1.3 mRNA in the target brain region without systemic exposure
- This is an RNAi mechanism (post-transcriptional gene silencing), not gene replacement or gene editing -- conceptually analogous to [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] but using viral delivery to a focal brain region rather than systemic siRNA
- Two candidates in development: CGT-102 and CGT-103, both targeting CaV1.3 via AAV-RNAi, being evaluated in NHP models
- The 2025 NHP study demonstrated three distinct therapeutic effects: (1) near-complete prevention of LID induction despite dose-escalating levodopa, (2) progressive reversal of existing parkinsonian motor deficits without any pharmacotherapy, and (3) preservation of levodopa motor benefit (anti-dyskinetic effect without blunting therapeutic response)
- The motor deficit reversal without medication is unprecedented for a CaV1.3-targeting approach and suggests potential disease-modifying properties beyond pure symptomatic benefit
- Open question: which AAV serotype will be used for the clinical candidate, and whether the vector achieves sufficient transduction volume in the larger human putamen

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. The company is in preclinical development with two candidates (CGT-102 and CGT-103) being tested in nonhuman primate models.

Key preclinical milestones:
- **2019 rat study:** Demonstrated that AAV-CaV1.3-shRNA completely prevents LID induction and ameliorates pre-existing severe dyskinesia in parkinsonian rats (published in Movement Disorders)
- **2023 aged rat study:** Confirmed anti-dyskinetic efficacy in aged male and female parkinsonian rats, a more clinically relevant model (published in Neurobiology of Disease)
- **2025 NHP study:** MRI-guided intraputaminal AAV-CaV1.3-shRNA in aged (25-29 years) male and female parkinsonian macaques with 8+ months of moderate-to-severe motor deficits showed unprecedented multidimensional efficacy including LID prevention, motor deficit reversal, and restoration of postural stability (published in Molecular Therapy)

Next step is development of a human-safe viral vector, followed by IND-enabling studies. Clinical trials are described by the founders as "likely still years away."

### Financial
- Founded November 2022 in East Lansing, Michigan, as a spinout from Michigan State University via Spartan Innovation
- No disclosed venture funding or institutional investment rounds
- Seeking SBIR/STTR small business grant funding to support human-safe vector development
- Research has been supported by federal (NIH) grants to the founding lab at MSU
- Leadership team: Fredric Manfredsson, PhD (President, co-founder; Barrow Neurological Institute), Kathy Steece-Collier, PhD (CSO, co-founder; MSU), James M. Hamby, PhD (COO; former VP Business Development at Ash Stevens/CDMO, former Director at Parke-Davis/Pfizer)
- No disclosed valuation; typical for an academic spinout at this stage relying on grant funding rather than venture capital

### Competitive

*No results*

- CavGene occupies a unique niche: it is the only known gene therapy approach specifically targeting levodopa-induced dyskinesia rather than disease modification at the alpha-synuclein or genetic PD level
- Amantadine (Gocovri) is the only FDA-approved treatment for LID -- it is a small molecule with partial efficacy, tolerance development, and side effects including hallucinations
- Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is the current gold standard for refractory LID but is invasive, expensive, and not suitable for all patients
- The intracranial delivery route is shared with other PD gene therapies (e.g., Voyager Therapeutics' historical AAV programs) but the CaV1.3 target is entirely distinct from dopamine synthesis or neurotrophic factor approaches
- If the CaV1.3-silencing approach demonstrates disease-modifying properties (as suggested by the NHP motor reversal data), it would compete in a broader landscape against [[prasinezumab]], [[biib122|BIIB122]], and other disease-modifying candidates

## Analysis

CavGene represents an unconventional entry in the PD pipeline: rather than targeting alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1, or neuroinflammation, it addresses a downstream electrophysiological mechanism (calcium channel overload in striatal neurons) that drives the most debilitating side effect of the standard-of-care treatment. The 2025 NHP data is genuinely remarkable -- near-complete LID prevention plus progressive motor deficit reversal without medication in aged parkinsonian macaques is a preclinical result profile that few PD programs have achieved. Dr. Steece-Collier's statement that the results show "promise that I have not seen in my 30-year career" reflects the strength of the animal data, not marketing hyperbole.

The challenges are equally clear. CavGene is a very early-stage academic spinout with no disclosed venture funding, relying on SBIR/STTR grants to advance a program that requires expensive IND-enabling toxicology, GMP vector manufacturing, and ultimately a neurosurgical clinical trial. The gap between NHP proof-of-concept and a human-ready clinical candidate is where most academic gene therapy programs fail. The intracranial delivery route, while necessary for target specificity, limits the addressable patient population to those willing to undergo stereotactic brain surgery -- though this is comparable to the DBS population already accepting invasive procedures for dyskinesia.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of reaching Phase 1: 15-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: strong NHP efficacy data (upward adjustment from ~10% base rate for preclinical academic programs), experienced COO with pharma/CDMO background (upward), but no disclosed VC funding, grant-dependent financing model (downward), and the high cost/complexity of AAV gene therapy manufacturing and neurosurgical trial design (downward). The founders' track record in the field and the quality of the NHP publication in Molecular Therapy are positive signals, but translation from academic lab to funded clinical program requires either a licensing deal with a larger gene therapy company or a substantial Series A round that has not materialized as of early 2026.

If the NHP data continues to replicate and the human-safe vector is successfully developed, CavGene becomes a compelling licensing target for companies with AAV manufacturing and neurosurgical trial infrastructure (e.g., Neurocrine, AbbVie, or any company with DBS/intracranial delivery experience). If grant funding stalls or vector development encounters safety signals, the program remains an academic research project rather than a clinical-stage company.
