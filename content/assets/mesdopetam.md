# Mesdopetam

## Summary

Mesdopetam is an oral dopamine D3 receptor antagonist developed by IRLAB Therapeutics (biotech, IRLAB-A.ST, Nasdaq Stockholm) for levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID) in Parkinson's disease. The Phase 2b trial missed its primary endpoint of Good ON-time (change in ON time without troublesome dyskinesia) but showed statistically significant anti-dyskinetic effects on the pre-specified secondary endpoint UDysRS at the 2.5 mg and 7.5 mg doses, with a safety profile comparable to placebo. Both FDA (End-of-Phase 2 meeting, early 2024) and EMA (scientific advice, February 2025) have confirmed the data package supports progression to Phase 3, with two parallel studies of ~250-270 patients total using UDysRS as the primary endpoint. IRLAB regained full ownership from former partner Ipsen in August 2023 and is actively seeking a new partner to fund and co-develop Phase 3. If Phase 3 succeeds, mesdopetam becomes the first oral D3-selective anti-dyskinetic therapy approved for PD-LID, competing against generic amantadine ER. If it fails or partnering stalls, the program likely dies given IRLAB's limited cash runway.

## Notes

### Science
- Mesdopetam is a dopamine D2/D3 receptor antagonist with preferential selectivity for the D3 receptor subtype, representing a novel mechanism for treating levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- D3 receptors are upregulated in the striatum by chronic levodopa exposure; this ectopic D3 expression in the dorsal striatum is thought to contribute to the development of dyskinesia by sensitizing the direct pathway to dopaminergic stimulation
- By selectively antagonizing D3 receptors, mesdopetam reduces dyskinesia without blocking D1/D2-mediated motor function -- a critical distinction from older antipsychotics that worsen parkinsonism
- Preclinical models show mesdopetam has both anti-dyskinetic and anti-psychotic-like properties, suggesting potential dual utility in PD-LID and PD-psychosis (PD-P)
- Pharmacokinetics: rapid oral absorption, dose-linear PK, plasma half-life of ~7 hours, supporting twice-daily dosing
- Received INN (International Nonproprietary Name) from WHO in 2020, confirming recognition of its unique mechanism
- Key scientific question: whether D3-selective antagonism alone produces sufficient anti-dyskinetic efficacy in humans, given that the Phase 2b primary endpoint miss raises questions about effect size magnitude
- Substance patents provide market exclusivity into the 2040s; 10-year data exclusivity in Europe, 5-year NCE exclusivity in the US

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (First-in-Human)** | NCT03901950 | N=48 | Healthy male volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, and PK of escalating single and multiple doses
- **Key findings:** Safe and well-tolerated; rapid absorption; dose-linear PK; plasma half-life ~7 hours
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety profile and favorable PK supported progression to patient studies

**Phase 2a (IRL790C003)** | NCT03368170 | N=75 | PD patients with levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in ON time without troublesome dyskinesia (Good ON time) via Hauser diaries
- **Key findings:** 7.5 mg BID dose showed Good ON time increase of 5.6 hours/day vs. 1.0 hour for placebo. Clinically meaningful improvements on MDS-UPDRS
- **Safety:** AEs reported in 56.9% mesdopetam vs. 46.2% placebo; similar withdrawal rates
- **Status:** Completed (2019)
- **Interpretation:** Strong proof-of-concept signal; 7.5 mg emerged as the best-performing dose

**Phase 2b (IRL790C005)** | NCT04435431 | N=156 | PD patients with troublesome dyskinesia on stable levodopa
- **Primary endpoint:** Change from baseline to week 12 in Good ON time (diary) --> **NOT MET** (no significant difference vs. placebo across 2.5, 5.0, 7.5 mg BID doses)
- **Key secondary:** Modified UDysRS (Parts 1+3+4) --> **Statistically significant improvement** at 2.5 mg and 7.5 mg doses; clinically meaningful anti-dyskinetic effect
- **Additional:** MDS-UPDRS Part 4.2 showed improvement; OFF time showed dose-dependent decrease (highest at 7.5 mg); safety profile comparable to placebo
- **Status:** Completed (top-line January 2023; published Movement Disorders Clinical Practice, 2025)
- **Interpretation:** The Good ON time primary endpoint was problematic -- it combines anti-dyskinetic and anti-parkinsonian effects into a single composite that may dilute a pure anti-dyskinetic signal. The UDysRS secondary endpoint directly measures dyskinesia and showed clear efficacy. Both FDA and EMA agreed that UDysRS is the appropriate primary endpoint for Phase 3, effectively redesigning around the Phase 2b learnings.

**Meta-analysis of Phase 2a + Phase 2b** | MDS Congress presentation
- Combined analysis of both RCTs confirmed consistent anti-dyskinetic effect across studies
- Supported selection of 7.5 mg BID as the optimal dose for Phase 3

**Phase 3 (Planned)** | NCT TBD | N=~250-270 (two parallel studies) | PD patients with levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- **Primary endpoint:** UDysRS Parts 1+3+4 (regulatory-aligned with both FDA and EMA)
- **Secondary endpoints:** Elements of UDysRS, MDS-UPDRS, 24-hour patient diaries
- **Design:** Two parallel studies, 1:1 randomization (active vs. placebo), 3-month treatment duration
- **Status:** Phase 3-ready; awaiting partnership to fund execution. Target initiation 2025-2026 if partner secured.
- **Interpretation:** Regulatory alignment with both FDA and EMA is a significant de-risking milestone. The switch to UDysRS as primary endpoint addresses the Phase 2b miss directly. Relatively small trial size (~125-135 per study) suggests manageable cost and timeline.

- The LID treatment landscape is dominated by **amantadine** (NMDA receptor antagonist), available in extended-release formulations (Gocovri/ADS-5102, Osmolex ER). Amantadine is now generic in many markets, creating pricing pressure for any new LID entrant
- Mesdopetam's key differentiation is its novel mechanism (D3 antagonism vs. NMDA antagonism), potentially offering benefit in patients who do not respond to or tolerate amantadine
- Deep brain stimulation (GPi-DBS and STN-DBS) is the most effective intervention for severe dyskinesia but requires surgery -- mesdopetam targets the large population of moderate-severity LID patients not eligible for or refusing surgery
- Levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG/Duopa) reduces dyskinesia through continuous dopaminergic stimulation but requires a percutaneous jejunal tube -- mesdopetam's oral route is a major practical advantage
- PD-psychosis indication provides potential line extension: pimavanserin (Nuplazid, Acadia) is the only approved PD-psychosis drug but has a black-box mortality warning and limited efficacy, creating an opening for a better-tolerated alternative
- If mesdopetam Phase 3 succeeds: first-in-class oral D3 antagonist for LID, potentially applicable to PD-psychosis; validates D3 receptor as a therapeutic target
- If mesdopetam Phase 3 fails: D3 receptor antagonism thesis for LID is weakened; field remains reliant on amantadine and surgical options

## Analysis

Mesdopetam occupies an unusual position: a Phase 3-ready symptomatic PD asset with full regulatory alignment from both FDA and EMA, yet currently unpartnered and underfunded. The science is sound -- D3 receptor upregulation in dyskinesia is well-established, and the Phase 2 data consistently show anti-dyskinetic effects. The critical failure in Phase 2b was an endpoint selection problem (Good ON time), not a drug efficacy problem. Both regulators agreed with IRLAB's analysis that UDysRS is the appropriate primary endpoint, which is a meaningful validation.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 3 success probability: 35-45%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for Phase 3 success in CNS symptomatic programs: ~50-55%
- Adjustments upward: FDA/EMA alignment on redesigned primary endpoint (+10%), consistent UDysRS signal across Phase 2a and 2b (+5%), clean safety profile comparable to placebo (+5%), relatively small trial size reducing execution risk (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: Phase 2b primary endpoint miss raises questions about overall effect size (-10%), Ipsen walked away after completing Phase 3 prep (-10%), small biotech execution risk for a two-study Phase 3 (-5%), generic amantadine may limit commercial enthusiasm and thus partnership quality (-5%)
- Net: ~35-45%

**Signal analysis:** Ipsen's decision to return rights in August 2023 is the single most important negative signal. They invested $28M upfront, completed three Phase 1 studies and full CMC/manufacturing work, then walked away. This could reflect a portfolio reprioritization (Ipsen shifted neuroscience strategy), commercial skepticism about competing against generic amantadine, or doubts about the Phase 2b primary miss. Conversely, the subsequent FDA and EMA regulatory interactions both concluded positively, and the endpoint redesign directly addresses the Phase 2b issue. IRLAB's ability to secure a credible partner will be the strongest external validation signal. The micro-cap status (~$23M market cap) means the stock is essentially pricing in failure or prolonged limbo.

The decision tree is binary on partnering: if IRLAB secures a well-resourced partner (mid-to-large pharma) in 2025-2026, Phase 3 proceeds on a ~2027-2028 readout timeline with reasonable success probability. If no partner materializes by late 2026, the program may stall indefinitely despite being Phase 3-ready, as IRLAB cannot fund execution alone. The PD-psychosis indication provides a potential second swing that could interest partners focused on the broader opportunity beyond LID alone.
