# SER-252

## Summary

SER-252 is a polymer-conjugated extended-release apomorphine from Serina Therapeutics (biotech, SER) designed to deliver continuous dopaminergic stimulation via twice-weekly subcutaneous injection, replacing the cumbersome pumps and on-demand rescue injections that limit current apomorphine formulations. The FDA cleared the IND in January 2026 after resolving a clinical hold related to a formulation excipient (not the drug substance), and the Phase 1b registrational study (SER-252-1b) is enrolling in Australia with US sites expected in Q1 2026 under a 505(b)(2) NDA pathway. If Phase 1b PK data demonstrate sustained therapeutic apomorphine levels with acceptable tolerability, the 505(b)(2) pathway could enable a streamlined path to approval leveraging existing apomorphine efficacy data. If the polymer conjugate fails to maintain therapeutic levels or causes injection-site reactions, the POZ platform's applicability to neurology is called into question and the CDS space remains dominated by pump-based approaches like foslevodopa/foscarbidopa (Vyalev) and continuous apomorphine infusion.

## Notes

### Science
- Apomorphine is a potent, non-selective dopamine D1/D2 receptor agonist — the most efficacious dopamine agonist available, but severely limited by its short half-life (~30-45 minutes), requiring either continuous pump infusion or on-demand rescue dosing
- SER-252 uses Serina's proprietary POZ (poly(2-oxazoline)) platform to conjugate apomorphine to cleavable pendant groups on a synthetic, water-soluble, low-viscosity polymer backbone via click chemistry, enabling sustained drug release from a subcutaneous depot
- The POZ platform is engineered to provide precise drug loading and controlled release rates — designed to keep patients within apomorphine's narrow therapeutic window longer without the peaks (dyskinesia, nausea) and troughs (OFF episodes) of bolus dosing
- Continuous dopaminergic stimulation (CDS) is pharmacologically validated: the TOLEDO trial showed continuous apomorphine infusion significantly reduced OFF time vs. placebo, and LCIG/Duopa has demonstrated similar benefits — the question is whether a polymer conjugate can replicate pump-like PK with a simple injection
- Key advantage over PEG (polyethylene glycol): POZ is claimed to have lower immunogenicity and viscosity, potentially avoiding anti-PEG antibody concerns that affect PEGylated drugs
- Serina has partnered with Enable Injections for the enFuse wearable delivery platform to enhance patient convenience
- Open question: whether the polymer cleavage rate can be tuned precisely enough to maintain steady-state apomorphine levels in the narrow therapeutic window across diverse patient populations

### Clinical

**SER-252-1b (Phase 1b)** | NCT TBD | N=up to 88 | Advanced PD with motor fluctuations
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled with SAD (5 cohorts of 8; n=40) and MAD (up to 3 cohorts of 16; n=48) components
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics of subcutaneous SER-252 vs. placebo
- **Exploratory efficacy:** MDS-UPDRS motor scores, structured motor-state assessments (ON/OFF time)
- **Regulatory pathway:** 505(b)(2) NDA — leveraging prior knowledge of approved apomorphine products (APOKYN, Kynmobi) with PK bridging
- **Status:** IND cleared January 2026 after resolution of clinical hold (placed November 2025, related to formulation excipient, not active drug substance). Dosing initiated in Australia Q4 2025; US enrollment anticipated Q1 2026
- **Interpretation:** The 505(b)(2) pathway is the critical strategic element — if Serina can demonstrate bioequivalent apomorphine exposure with extended PK, they can reference the existing apomorphine safety/efficacy database rather than running large efficacy trials. The registrational framing of this Phase 1b is notable and suggests FDA alignment on a PK-bridging approval path.

- **Approved CDS competitors:** Continuous apomorphine infusion pumps (available in Europe >20 years; recently FDA-approved in US) deliver CDS but require 16+ hour/day pump wear, needle changes, and cause nodules/skin reactions. Foslevodopa/foscarbidopa (Vyalev, AbbVie) — approved Feb 2025 — is a subcutaneous levodopa infusion requiring a pump device
- **On-demand apomorphine:** APOKYN (SC injection, approved 2004) and Kynmobi (sublingual film, approved 2020 but subsequently withdrawn from market) are rescue therapies for acute OFF episodes, not CDS approaches
- **SER-252's niche:** If it works, it replaces the pump with a simple twice-weekly injection while achieving the same CDS pharmacology — a major convenience and compliance advantage. No other extended-release apomorphine formulation is in clinical development
- **Broader symptomatic landscape:** SER-252 competes in the advanced PD motor fluctuation space alongside DBS, LCIG (Duopa), and emerging approaches like subcutaneous levodopa. It does not compete with disease-modifying programs like [[prasinezumab]] or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]

## Analysis

SER-252 occupies a well-defined niche: take a proven drug (apomorphine) with a known efficacy profile and fix its delivery problem using polymer conjugation. The 505(b)(2) regulatory pathway is the lynchpin of the entire thesis — it means Serina does not need to prove apomorphine works (decades of data already establish that), only that their formulation delivers it in a sustained, predictable manner. This dramatically de-risks the clinical program relative to novel mechanism assets.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of regulatory success: 35-45%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Base rate for 505(b)(2) submissions is relatively high (~60-70% approval rate historically) because they leverage known drugs. Adjustments downward: the clinical hold over a formulation excipient raises manufacturing/formulation risk (-10%); micro-cap company with limited cash runway creates execution risk (-10%); polymer-drug conjugate PK may not achieve the flat apomorphine profile needed for CDS (-5%). Adjustments upward: FDA has explicitly supported the registrational design and 505(b)(2) pathway (+10%); apomorphine efficacy is well-established in CDS setting (+5%); no competing extended-release apomorphine in development (+5%). Net: ~35-45%.

**Signal analysis:** The company's financial position is precarious — $8.6M cash with a $15.8M annual burn rate. The $20M convertible note led by a board director, not an institutional investor, suggests difficulty attracting traditional biotech financing. However, the FDA's explicit support for the 505(b)(2) registrational pathway is a strong positive signal that the regulatory strategy is sound. The Pfizer POZ platform license (for LNP applications, not PD) provides external validation of the underlying polymer technology. The clinical hold resolution (excipient-related, not drug-related) is reassuring for the core pharmacology but highlights formulation challenges inherent to polymer-drug conjugates.

The decision tree is straightforward: if Phase 1b demonstrates sustained apomorphine levels within the therapeutic window with clean safety, the 505(b)(2) pathway could yield approval within 3-4 years at relatively low cost. If PK is erratic or injection-site reactions are problematic, the program likely stalls — and given the company's cash constraints, may not survive a reformulation cycle.
