# ION464

## Summary

Biogen discontinued ION464 on February 12, 2025 -- an intrathecally delivered ASO targeting SNCA mRNA -- despite a clean safety profile in the Phase 1 HORIZON trial (NCT04165486) in MSA patients. The discontinuation rationale was never publicly disclosed, making ION464's internal data the single most consequential unpublished dataset in the alpha-synuclein field: if Biogen saw adequate SNCA knockdown but no biomarker improvement, the entire production-inhibition thesis underpinning [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] ($2.2B Novartis deal) and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] (Lilly) collapses. The market's default interpretation -- intrathecal delivery failure, neutral to [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s subcutaneous platform -- is plausible but unconfirmed. ION464 was killed alongside BIIB094 (LRRK2 ASO) as part of Biogen's broader ASO modality retreat, while [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 small molecule) was retained.

## Notes

### Science
- ASO targeting SNCA mRNA via RNase H-mediated degradation -- reduces alpha-synuclein protein at the source (production inhibition) rather than clearing downstream aggregates like [[prasinezumab]] or [[cinpanemab]]
- Preclinical validation (Cole et al., JCI Insight 2021): in PFF mouse models, the ASO prevented pathology development, reduced pre-existing alpha-synuclein aggregates, and rescued neuronal loss. NHP data showed intrathecal delivery lowered alpha-synuclein protein across brain and spinal cord
- Boutros et al. (J Parkinsons Dis 2021): ASO successfully decreased alpha-synuclein and phospho-synuclein protein levels in PFF motor cortex model, but noted body weight decline and motor impairments alongside pathology reduction -- a potential safety signal for chronic dosing
- Key scientific limitation: intrathecal delivery distributes drug through CSF but may achieve insufficient concentration in deep brain structures (substantia nigra, basal ganglia) where PD/MSA pathology is concentrated. CSF-bathed cortical neurons receive highest exposure; dopaminergic neurons in the midbrain may receive subtherapeutic doses
- Mechanistic question shared with all production-inhibition approaches: sporadic PD may be a clearance disease (impaired autophagy/lysosomal degradation), not an overproduction disease. Reducing production does not fix clearance. Remaining 20-50% of protein can still aggregate if clearance pathways are impaired
- Alpha-synuclein knockout mice show subtle but real deficits: reduced dopaminergic neurons, cognitive impairment, increased neuroinflammation. Clinical programs target 50-80% knockdown (not ablation), but chronic partial reduction over decades in humans is untested

### Clinical

**HORIZON (Phase 1/2a)** | NCT04165486 | N=~40 (22 reported at MDS 2024) | MSA patients (MSA-P 45%, MSA-C 55%)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability → Well tolerated; no SAEs or deaths related to study drug
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled MAD (Part 1: intrathecal ascending doses over 12 weeks) + open-label extension (Part 2: set dose over 72 weeks). 10 sites in Austria, France, Germany, UK
- **Key secondary:** CSF total alpha-synuclein pharmacodynamics → Data not publicly disclosed before discontinuation
- **Baseline demographics:** Mean age 59, 36% female, 91% white; all required unassisted walking (>=10m) and DaTscan-confirmed striatal dopamine loss
- **Common AEs:** Falls (25 events), UTI (12), headache (6), contusion (5), neck pain (3) -- consistent with MSA disease burden and intrathecal procedure
- **Status:** Discontinued by Biogen, February 12, 2025. Expected completion had been September 2027
- **Interpretation:** Safety was clean but unremarkable for an intrathecal ASO. The critical pharmacodynamic data -- whether ION464 achieved meaningful CSF alpha-synuclein reduction -- was never disclosed. This is the key missing information. If knockdown was insufficient (delivery failure), it explains discontinuation without threatening the production-inhibition thesis. If knockdown was adequate but biomarkers did not improve, the target-engagement-without-benefit pattern seen in [[cinpanemab]] and [[minzasolmin]] repeats, devastating for the entire approach

### Financial
- ION464 was developed under Biogen's 2018 expanded neurology collaboration with Ionis: $375M upfront + $625M equity investment (~$1B total), covering a 10-year platform across multiple neurological targets. ION464 was one program among many; the per-asset economics are not disclosed
- Biogen bore all development costs (non-clinical studies, clinical development, manufacturing, commercialization) with Ionis responsible for target identification and ASO design
- Biogen discontinued ION464 alongside BIIB094 (LRRK2 ASO), BIIB113 (early Alzheimer's), and cemdomespib (diabetic neuropathy) -- a portfolio-wide cut coinciding with Q4 2024 earnings and "Fit for Growth" restructuring
- The simultaneous killing of both ASO programs (ION464 and BIIB094) while retaining [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 small molecule) signals an ASO modality retreat, not a target-level decision. Oral small molecules are commercially superior to intrathecal ASOs for chronic diseases
- No standalone market projections exist for ION464 given early discontinuation. MSA (prevalence ~50,000 in US/EU) is a much smaller commercial opportunity than PD (~1M US patients), which may have contributed to deprioritization

- ION464's discontinuation leaves two active production-inhibition programs: [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (Arrowhead/Novartis, subcutaneous siRNA, preclinical) and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] (Lilly, intrathecal siRNA, Phase 1). Both target the same biology (SNCA mRNA knockdown) but differ on delivery
- [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s TRiM platform enables subcutaneous dosing -- the critical competitive advantage over ION464's intrathecal route. Novartis paid $2.2B ($200M upfront) for this program 9 months after [[minzasolmin]]'s failure, implying they believe delivery, not target, was ION464's problem
- [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] shares ION464's intrathecal delivery limitation. ION464's fate is a direct warning for LY3962681: if intrathecal SNCA knockdown is commercially or biologically nonviable, Lilly's program inherits the same constraints
- [[prasinezumab]] (antibody, extracellular clearance) operates on a fundamentally different mechanism -- if PARAISO Phase 3 succeeds, it validates antibodies over production-inhibition and makes the ION464 failure moot. If PARAISO fails, production inhibition becomes the last remaining alpha-synuclein modality with rationale
- The vaccine approach ([[aci-7104-056|ACI-7104.056]], UB-312) is downstream of the same target but lacks the intracellular access advantage that production inhibition claims

## Analysis

ION464's discontinuation despite a clean safety profile is one of the most consequential events in the alpha-synuclein field, precisely because of what remains unknown. The undisclosed pharmacodynamic data -- specifically whether the ASO achieved meaningful CSF alpha-synuclein reduction -- determines how to interpret the entire production-inhibition wave.

**Analytical estimate -- probability that ION464 was discontinued for delivery/commercial reasons (benign for ARO-SNCA thesis): 60-65%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Biogen killed both ASO programs simultaneously (ION464 and BIIB094), suggesting a modality-level decision about intrathecal delivery viability, not a target-level conclusion about alpha-synuclein (+20%)
- Intrathecal dosing is commercially nonviable for chronic neurodegenerative diseases requiring decades of treatment. Lumbar punctures every 1-3 months create compliance, infection, and cost barriers that independently justify discontinuation (+15%)
- Biogen's "Fit for Growth" restructuring prioritized late-stage, high-ROI assets. MSA (50K patients) with intrathecal delivery in early Phase 1 is the opposite of that profile (+10%)
- However, Biogen had full access to the pharmacodynamic data and never disclosed it. Companies typically release positive biomarker data even from discontinued programs to support the scientific literature. The silence is concerning (-15%)
- The PFF mouse model showed body weight decline and motor impairments alongside pathology reduction (Boutros et al.), hinting at an unfavorable efficacy/toxicity ratio that could translate to humans (-10%)

**What ION464 teaches the field:**

First, intrathecal delivery is likely a dead end for chronic synucleinopathies regardless of target engagement. The convergent evidence -- ION464 discontinued, nusinersen (SMA) commercially displaced by oral risdiplam, tofersen (ALS) succeeding only in a monogenic orphan context -- establishes that intrathecal ASOs survive commercially only in rare, severe diseases with no oral alternative. PD does not meet this criterion.

Second, the market's favorable interpretation of ION464's failure has allowed [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] and the TRiM subcutaneous platform to absorb none of the alpha-synuclein target risk. If Biogen's data actually showed adequate SNCA knockdown without biomarker improvement, the $2.2B Novartis/Arrowhead deal is built on a false premise. Back-channel diligence on Biogen's internal ION464 dataset is the highest-value piece of intelligence in the alpha-synuclein space.

Third, ION464's discontinuation accelerates the field's bifurcation. Companies are either exiting alpha-synuclein entirely (Biogen, UCB after [[minzasolmin]]) or doubling down via modality pivot (Novartis with [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], Roche with [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3). The middle ground -- cautious exploration via intrathecal approaches -- is collapsing. [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] is the last intrathecal alpha-synuclein program standing and faces the same structural headwinds that killed ION464.
