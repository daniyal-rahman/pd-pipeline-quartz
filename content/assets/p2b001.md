# P2B001

## Summary

P2B001 is a once-daily, no-titration, fixed-dose combination capsule containing low-dose extended-release pramipexole (0.6 mg) and rasagiline (0.75 mg) -- doses lower than their respective marketed monotherapies. Pharma Two B (biotech, private, Israel-based) completed a pivotal Phase 3 trial (NCT03329508, N=544) that met both primary and key secondary endpoints: P2B001 was superior to each component alone on UPDRS II+III and comparable in efficacy to optimally titrated commercial pramipexole ER (1.5-4.5 mg), while producing significantly fewer dopaminergic side effects (somnolence, orthostatic hypotension, neuropsychiatric events). NDA submission via the 505(b)(2) pathway is planned for H1 2026. If approved, P2B001 becomes a convenient first-line symptomatic option for early PD that avoids titration; if rejected or delayed, the company faces financial pressure as a single-asset private biotech with $38M total raised and a failed reverse merger attempt with Hepion Pharmaceuticals (terminated December 2024).

## Notes

### Science
- P2B001 combines two well-established PD mechanisms in a single capsule: **pramipexole** (dopamine D2/D3 receptor agonist) and **rasagiline** (irreversible MAO-B inhibitor that prevents dopamine breakdown)
- The key innovation is **subtherapeutic dosing of each component** -- pramipexole at 0.6 mg (vs. 1.5-4.5 mg marketed dose) and rasagiline at 0.75 mg (vs. 1 mg marketed dose) -- achieving synergistic efficacy at lower individual doses, which reduces dopaminergic side effects
- Extended-release formulation enables once-daily dosing with no titration required, unlike standard pramipexole ER which requires weeks of dose titration
- Mechanistic rationale: dual-pathway dopamine enhancement (receptor agonism + degradation inhibition) provides complementary and potentially synergistic effects on the dopaminergic system
- This is a **symptomatic** therapy -- it does not address disease modification or slow neurodegeneration. It competes in the same space as generic pramipexole and rasagiline, needing the convenience/safety differentiation to justify premium pricing
- Regulatory pathway: 505(b)(2) NDA, which relies in part on the existing safety/efficacy data for the individual approved components, reducing development cost and timeline

### Clinical

**Phase 2b** | NCT01968460 | N=149 | Early PD, treatment-naive
- **Primary endpoint:** UPDRS change from baseline at 12 weeks --> Met; dose-dependent symptomatic improvement
- **Key secondary:** Both dose combinations met primary and secondary endpoints
- **Status:** Completed (2015)
- **Interpretation:** Proof of concept for the low-dose combination approach; established dose selection (0.6 mg pramipexole / 0.75 mg rasagiline) for the Phase 3 study

**Phase 3 (Pivotal)** | NCT03329508 | N=544 | Early PD, treatment-naive, 70 centers (US, Europe, Canada)
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in UPDRS Parts II+III from baseline to Week 12 --> P2B001 superior to pramipexole 0.6 mg component by 2.66 points (p=0.0018) and superior to rasagiline 0.75 mg component by 3.30 points (p=0.0001)
- **Key secondary:** Comparable efficacy to titrated commercial pramipexole ER (mean dose 3.2 mg) -- MET. Significantly less daytime sleepiness (ESS) vs. titrated pramipexole ER
- **Safety:** Well tolerated; >98% TEAEs mild/moderate. Fewer dopaminergic AEs vs. titrated pramipexole ER (45.7% vs. 66.2%), including lower somnolence, orthostatic hypotension, and neuropsychiatric side effects
- **Status:** Completed (topline results December 2021; published in Movement Disorders, November 2023)
- **Interpretation:** Clean positive trial on a well-powered superiority design vs. components and non-inferiority vs. commercial dose. The safety advantage over titrated pramipexole is the primary differentiator -- efficacy alone would not justify a branded product over generics

**Integrated Safety Database (Phase 2 + Phase 3)** | N=693
- >98% of TEAEs mild or moderate in severity
- Consistent safety profile across both studies
- No new safety signals identified

- **Key investors:** JK&B Capital, Generali, JVC Investment Partners
- **Failed reverse merger:** Announced July 2024 merger with Hepion Pharmaceuticals (HEPA) valued at ~$58.5M pro-forma implied equity; **terminated December 11, 2024** by mutual agreement with no termination fees
- **Concurrent private placement:** $11.5M concurrent financing was arranged alongside the Hepion merger; status unclear post-termination
- **Regulatory pathway:** 505(b)(2) NDA reduces development cost by leveraging existing safety/efficacy data for pramipexole and rasagiline
- **Commercial challenge:** Both pramipexole ER and rasagiline are available as generics. P2B001 must justify premium pricing based on convenience (once-daily, no titration) and safety profile (fewer dopaminergic AEs). The branded combination will compete directly against cheap generic co-prescription
- **Peak sales estimates:** No published third-party estimates identified. Market sizing is constrained by generic competition -- the value proposition is convenience and safety differentiation, not novel efficacy

- P2B001 competes primarily against **generic pramipexole ER and rasagiline**, not other pipeline assets. The commercial question is whether a branded fixed-dose combination can capture meaningful share from inexpensive generics
- The convenience argument (once-daily, no titration, single pill) has precedent in other therapeutic areas (e.g., HIV fixed-dose combinations, cardiovascular polypills) but PD has not yet seen a successful branded combination of generic components

## Analysis

P2B001 is a low-risk, incremental-innovation symptomatic asset. The clinical data package is clean -- positive Phase 3, both primary and secondary endpoints met, favorable safety profile -- and the 505(b)(2) regulatory pathway is well-established. The probability of FDA approval is relatively high given that both active ingredients are already approved and the combination showed superiority to components and comparable efficacy to marketed pramipexole ER.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of FDA approval: 65-75%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for 505(b)(2) NDAs with positive Phase 3 data using approved components is high (~80%); adjustment downward for company execution risk as a small private biotech with failed reverse merger and uncertain financing (-10 to -15%); no scientific or safety red flags to further discount.

The real question is commercial, not regulatory. Generic pramipexole ER costs ~$30-50/month and generic rasagiline ~$15-30/month. A physician can prescribe both generics today for under $80/month. P2B001 would need to price at a meaningful premium to justify the development investment, yet it offers no novel efficacy -- only convenience and a modestly better side-effect profile. The HIV combination model (where branded fixed-dose combinations command $1,000+/month despite generic components) succeeded partly because of adherence-critical viral suppression. In early PD, the consequences of imperfect adherence are less acute, weakening the convenience argument.

The failed Hepion reverse merger (terminated December 2024) raises financing concerns. Pharma Two B is a private company with $38M total raised, limited cash runway visibility, and no clear path to public markets. The NDA submission itself requires investment in CMC, regulatory consulting, and commercial preparation. Without a partner or new financing, the company may need to out-license P2B001 or find an alternative public listing vehicle before or shortly after NDA submission. This creates optionality for potential acquirers -- a small biotech with a near-NDA symptomatic PD asset and limited negotiating leverage could be an inexpensive acquisition target for a mid-size specialty pharma.
