# Vodobatinib (K0706)

## Summary

Vodobatinib was a third-generation c-Abl kinase inhibitor developed by Sun Pharma Advanced Research Company (SPARC, publicly traded on NSE/BSE India) for early Parkinson's disease. The PROSEEK Phase 2 trial (NCT03655236, N=513) was terminated in April 2024 after a pre-planned interim analysis of 442 patients showed no evidence of treatment benefit on MDS-UPDRS Part 3 vs. placebo. Compounding the failure, dose-dependent serum neurofilament light chain (NfL) elevations suggested potential neuronal harm rather than neuroprotection. The full results were published in npj Parkinson's Disease (Sarva et al., 2026), though the interpretation remains nuanced — some biomarker signals were reported but could not overcome the primary endpoint futility finding. This is the second c-Abl inhibitor failure in PD after nilotinib (NILO-PD), leaving [[risvodetinib]] as the only surviving c-Abl program and raising fundamental questions about whether c-Abl inhibition is neuroprotective in humans.

## Notes

### Science
- c-Abl (Abelson tyrosine kinase) is activated by misfolded alpha-synuclein and in turn phosphorylates alpha-synuclein at Y39, creating a toxic feedforward loop of aggregation, impaired autophagy, and dopaminergic neuron death — inhibiting c-Abl aims to break this amplification cycle
- Vodobatinib is a third-generation c-Abl inhibitor structurally related to dasatinib and ponatinib (oncology agents), with an IC50 of 0.9 nM for c-Abl — approximately 17-50x more potent than nilotinib (IC50 15-45 nM)
- Designed for CNS penetration: PK studies confirmed measurable CSF concentrations, and at achieved plasma levels, estimated brain c-Abl inhibition was substantially higher than nilotinib — addressing the key failure mode of earlier repurposed oncology agents
- Walsh et al. (Parkinsonism & Related Disorders, 2023) published the plasma and CSF PK data establishing brain penetration, differentiating vodobatinib from nilotinib which failed in part due to insufficient CNS exposure
- However, unlike [[risvodetinib]], vodobatinib was not purpose-built for neurological indications — it was originally developed as a BCR-ABL inhibitor for Philadelphia chromosome-positive leukemia (Phase 1/2 in CML still ongoing), then repurposed for PD
- Open scientific question post-failure: the dose-dependent NfL elevations in PROSEEK raise concern that potent c-Abl inhibition without sufficient selectivity may cause off-target neuronal damage — whether this is a vodobatinib-specific issue or a class effect for non-selective c-Abl inhibitors remains debated

### Clinical

**Phase 1 (Dose-Ranging)** | NCT02970019 | N=~78 (60 PD + 18 healthy) | PD patients + healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Well-tolerated across 6-384 mg daily dose range; no serious adverse events
- **Key secondary:** Dose-proportional PK; CSF penetration confirmed at 48-192-384 mg doses; blood/CSF levels guided PROSEEK Phase 2 dosing
- **Status:** Completed (2017-2019)
- **Interpretation:** Established PK/PD profile and confirmed brain exposure, supporting advancement to Phase 2 with 192 mg and 384 mg dose arms

**PROSEEK (Phase 2)** | NCT03655236 | N=513 | Early PD not on dopamine therapy (MAO-B inhibitor permitted), age 50+, symptom onset within 3 years
- **Primary endpoint:** Change from baseline in MDS-UPDRS Part 3 at 40 weeks → **FAILED.** Vodobatinib at neither 192 mg nor 384 mg demonstrated superiority over placebo
- **Pre-planned interim analysis (April 2024):** Based on 442 patients completing 40-week Part I; Independent Data Monitoring Committee recommended termination for futility
- **Biomarker finding:** Serum neurofilament light chain (NfL) increased significantly in both vodobatinib dose groups vs. placebo — a concerning signal suggesting potential neuronal injury rather than neuroprotection
- **Safety:** Generally tolerable; adverse events predominantly mild-to-moderate (GI disturbances, fatigue)
- **Sites:** 77 sites across U.S., Hungary, India, Poland, Slovakia, Spain
- **Status:** Terminated (April 2024)
- **Interpretation:** A clean negative result — no efficacy signal on the primary motor endpoint, combined with a biomarker (NfL) suggesting harm. This was the largest and most rigorous c-Abl inhibitor trial in PD (N=513 vs. nilotinib's N=76), making the futility finding highly informative for the field. SPARC stated vodobatinib "did not exhibit any evidence of treatment benefit"

**DLB Phase 2 (Georgetown)** | NCT TBD | N=29 (of 45 planned) | Dementia with Lewy Bodies
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/adverse events → Treatment was safe
- **Key secondary:** Only amyloid-beta 42/40 ratio showed significant biomarker change; no drug-placebo differences on exploratory clinical outcomes
- **Status:** Completed; results published 2025 (Pagan et al.)
- **Interpretation:** Small, short (12-week) trial confirming safety but no clinical or synuclein-related biomarker signal in DLB, further undermining the neuroprotection hypothesis for vodobatinib

### Financial
- SPARC is listed on India's National Stock Exchange (NSE: SPARC) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE: 532872); market cap ~INR 4,333 crore (~$520M USD) as of January 2026
- SPARC shares fell ~5% on the day PROSEEK termination was announced (April 10, 2024)
- SPARC is a clinical-stage company spun out from Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd in 2007 — PROSEEK was one of its flagship programs
- Vodobatinib continues in oncology development (Phase 1/2 for chronic myeloid leukemia), partially de-risking the investment from SPARC's perspective
- No pharma partnership or licensing deal for the PD indication — SPARC developed vodobatinib internally for PD, bearing full trial cost
- The PROSEEK trial enrolled 513 patients across 77 sites in 6 countries, representing a substantial investment (estimated $30-60M) that yielded no path forward in PD

- The vodobatinib NfL elevation finding is a significant competitive data point for [[risvodetinib]] — if risvodetinib's CAMPD Phase 2b/3 shows stable or declining NfL alongside clinical improvement, it would argue the vodobatinib failure was compound-specific, not target-specific
- Broader disease modification landscape: vodobatinib's failure shifts field capital and attention toward [[prasinezumab]] (anti-synuclein mAb, Phase 3), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (alpha-syn siRNA, Phase 2), and [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 kinase inhibitor, Phase 2b) — all pursuing different nodes of PD biology with more positive or pending clinical data

## Analysis

Vodobatinib's PROSEEK failure is a landmark negative result in the PD disease modification field. With 513 patients and a 40-week treatment duration, this was the most rigorous test of c-Abl inhibition in PD to date — and the null result was unambiguous. Unlike [[prasinezumab]] PADOVA, which generated a borderline miss (p=0.0657) with pre-specified subgroup signals, PROSEEK produced no evidence of benefit by any measure. The Independent Data Monitoring Committee did not recommend continuing to Part II, and SPARC's own characterization — "no evidence of treatment benefit" — leaves no room for optimistic re-interpretation.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of any future PD development: <5%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: SPARC terminated the program and removed PD from its pipeline. The company's own assessment was unequivocal. Vodobatinib continues in oncology (CML) but there is no scientific or commercial rationale to revisit PD. The NfL elevation finding makes it nearly impossible to justify further PD exposure.

**Signal analysis:**
- The NfL elevation is the most consequential finding from PROSEEK for the broader field. NfL is an established biomarker of neuro-axonal damage. Dose-dependent increases in a neuroprotection trial suggest the compound may be neurotoxic at therapeutic doses — the opposite of the intended effect. This finding reverberates beyond vodobatinib: it provides ammunition for critics of the c-Abl hypothesis and raises the bar for [[risvodetinib]] to demonstrate NfL safety in CAMPD
- SPARC's behavior post-failure is consistent with genuine futility (vs. financial constraints): the company continued other pipeline programs and did not attempt to re-frame PROSEEK data positively. CEO Anil Raghavan acknowledged the study's contribution to understanding "the role of c-Abl kinase in alpha synucleinopathies" — coded language for a failed mechanism-of-action test
- The 2026 npj Parkinson's Disease publication (Sarva et al.) reports some biomarker nuance, but the overall conclusion aligns with the termination decision. Peer-reviewed publication of negative results is valuable for the field, providing detailed data for meta-analyses and informing the design of future c-Abl programs

**Decision tree:** N/A — program terminated. The key remaining question is whether vodobatinib's failure is compound-specific (insufficient selectivity, off-target toxicity, oncology-derived pharmacology) or target-specific (c-Abl inhibition is not neuroprotective in humans). [[risvodetinib]] CAMPD will provide the definitive answer.
