# Booster Therapeutics Proteasome Activator Program

## Summary

Booster Therapeutics (startup, private, Berlin) is developing first-in-class small molecule 20S proteasome activators to clear misfolded alpha-synuclein and other toxic proteins in PD. The company launched in October 2024 with $15M seed financing from Apollo Health Ventures and Novo Holdings, and received a $5M grant from the Michael J. Fox Foundation in November 2025 to advance its lead program toward Phase 1. The mechanism is genuinely novel -- rather than tagging individual proteins for degradation (as PROTACs and molecular glues do), Booster's molecules directly activate the 20S proteasome to restore broad protein quality control. This is preclinical-stage with no disclosed lead candidate, making it high-risk but scientifically differentiated from existing alpha-synuclein approaches like [[prasinezumab]] (antibody) or [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (siRNA).

## Notes

### Science
- 20S proteasomes naturally recognize and degrade intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) including misfolded alpha-synuclein without requiring ubiquitin tagging
- Proteasome activity declines with age, leading to accumulation of toxic protein aggregates -- a hallmark of PD and other neurodegenerative diseases
- Booster's approach is fundamentally different from 26S proteasome-mediated degradation (used by PROTACs/molecular glues): 26S requires ubiquitin tagging and targets one protein at a time; 20S activation clears multiple misfolded proteins simultaneously
- DGRADX platform combines automated high-throughput screening, X-ray crystallography, and computational tools to discover proteasome-activating small molecules
- Key scientific challenge: achieving sufficient blood-brain barrier penetration for a proteasome activator -- the MJFF grant specifically funds BBB-crossing candidate development
- Open question: selectivity of 20S activation -- will boosting proteasome activity cause off-target degradation of normal proteins? Safety signal monitoring will be critical
- Founded on pioneering work by Prof. Darci Trader (UC Irvine), who developed the foundational proteasome activation chemistry
- Alpha-synuclein is the primary target protein for PD indication, but the mechanism could theoretically address multiple proteinopathies (Alzheimer's amyloid-beta, tau)

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. The program is in preclinical stage.

- MJFF $5M grant (November 2025) specifically funds: lead candidate identification, biomarker development for proteasome activity, testing in animal models of PD, and IND-enabling studies
- Project title: "Development of Small Molecule Proteasome Activators for the Treatment of Parkinson's Disease"
- IND filing estimated 2027-2028 based on typical preclinical-to-IND timelines and MJFF grant milestones
- No NCT numbers; no animal model data publicly disclosed

## Analysis

Booster Therapeutics represents a genuinely novel approach to PD therapeutics -- 20S proteasome activation has no direct competitors in clinical development. The scientific rationale is sound: proteasome dysfunction is well-documented in aging and neurodegeneration, alpha-synuclein is a validated 20S substrate, and restoring protein quality control addresses a root cause rather than a single protein. The MJFF grant is a meaningful validation signal; MJFF's Therapeutics Pipeline Program has historically been selective and its backing provides both funding and credibility for a preclinical startup.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of reaching Phase 1: ~40-50%. This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:** Base rate for preclinical programs reaching IND is approximately 30-40%. Adjustments upward: novel mechanism with strong academic foundation (Trader lab), well-funded for stage ($20M total), MJFF validation and oversight, Novo Holdings operational support, experienced board (Trojer has prior exit with Constellation). Adjustments downward: BBB penetration for a proteasome activator is unproven, no animal model data disclosed yet, no lead candidate publicly identified, small team in early build-out phase, and the fundamental question of whether broad proteasome activation can be made safe (off-target protein degradation risk). Net: modestly above base rate.

The investor syndicate is notable. Apollo Health Ventures is a longevity-focused venture creation firm -- Booster fits their thesis of addressing age-related cellular decline. Novo Holdings brings Novo Nordisk Foundation resources and the Seed Lab infrastructure. Board Chair Patrick Trojer has relevant exit experience (Constellation Pharmaceuticals acquired by MorphoSys for $1.7B). This is a well-constructed syndicate for a preclinical-stage startup. The decision tree: if MJFF-funded studies identify a BBB-penetrant lead with clean safety in animal models, Booster becomes an attractive acquisition target or Series A candidate in 2027-2028. If BBB penetration proves intractable or proteasome activation shows toxicity, the program stalls at preclinical.

Limited public information available on specific compound structures, in vivo data, or detailed timeline -- requires direct outreach for diligence.
