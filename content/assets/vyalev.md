# VYALEV (foslevodopa/foscarbidopa)

## Summary

VYALEV is the first subcutaneous 24-hour continuous levodopa infusion, FDA-approved October 2024 for motor fluctuations in advanced PD. It solves the core problem with Duopa (intrajejunal gel requiring PEG-J surgery) by delivering equivalent continuous dopaminergic stimulation through a wearable pump and SC cannula repositioned every 1-3 days. Phase 3 (M15-736) showed +2.72 hours ON time vs. +0.97 hours for oral LD/CD (p=0.0083). The main tolerability liability is infusion site reactions (nodules in ~25% of patients, cellulitis/infections in 28%), which drove a 22% discontinuation rate in trials. AbbVie projects >$2B peak sales; Q3 2025 revenue hit $138M, tracking toward rapid Duopa cannibalization. If [[nd0612]] (NeuroDerm/Mitsubishi Tanabe) wins FDA approval, the SC levodopa space becomes a two-product market; if not, VYALEV owns the category.

## Notes

### Science
- Foslevodopa and foscarbidopa are phosphate ester prodrugs of levodopa and carbidopa. The phosphate group dramatically increases aqueous solubility (from ~4 mg/mL to >1 g/mL), enabling a concentrated solution suitable for small-volume SC infusion
- Alkaline phosphatase in subcutaneous tissue cleaves the phosphate group, converting prodrugs to active levodopa and carbidopa in situ
- Carbidopa inhibits peripheral DOPA decarboxylase, preventing levodopa metabolism outside the CNS and maximizing brain delivery
- The continuous dopaminergic stimulation (CDS) hypothesis: in the dopamine-depleted striatum, intermittent oral LD doses cause pulsatile receptor stimulation that induces molecular changes in striatal medium spiny neurons, driving motor complications (wearing-off, dyskinesia). Continuous delivery restores more physiologic, tonic dopamine receptor occupancy
- PK advantage: steady-state levodopa achieved within 2 hours (with loading dose) vs. repeated peaks/troughs with oral formulations. Plasma levodopa fluctuation index is dramatically reduced compared to oral LD/CD, [[rytary]], or [[ipx203|IPX-203]]
- SC delivery achieves equivalent levodopa exposure to Duopa's intrajejunal route (bypassing gastric emptying variability), validated in head-to-head PK studies

### Clinical

**M15-736 (Phase 3 pivotal)** | NCT04380142 | N=130 | Advanced PD with motor fluctuations
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in ON time without troublesome dyskinesia (PD diary, 16-hour waking day) at Week 12 -> +2.72 hours VYALEV vs. +0.97 hours oral LD/CD (p=0.0083)
- **Key secondary:** OFF time reduction: -2.75 hours VYALEV vs. -0.96 hours oral LD/CD (p=0.0054). Improvements observed from Week 1 and sustained through Week 12
- **Safety:** Infusion site erythema, pain, cellulitis, edema, and nodules were the dominant AEs. Infusion site infections in 28% (vs. 3% placebo SC infusion). 8% withdrew due to infusion site reactions
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Clear superiority over oral LD/CD on the primary endpoint. The ~1.75-hour ON time advantage is clinically meaningful for advanced PD patients. Double-blind double-dummy design (SC infusion + oral capsules in both arms) is methodologically rigorous

**M15-741 (Phase 3, open-label, 52-week safety)** | NCT03781167 | N=~244 | Advanced PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Long-term safety and tolerability of continuous SC infusion
- **Key finding:** Generally well tolerated over 12 months; infusion site reactions remained the primary safety signal but were manageable with site rotation
- **Status:** Completed; supported NDA filing

**M15-741 OLE (Phase 3 extension)** | NCT04379050 | N=~130 | Rollover from M15-741
- **Primary endpoint:** Long-term safety, vital signs, ECGs, MDS-UPDRS Parts I-IV, quality of life
- **Status:** Active; expected completion April 2026
- **Interpretation:** Will provide multi-year real-world durability and tolerability data critical for the Duopa-switch narrative

**Regulatory history:**
- NDA submitted 2022
- First CRL (March 2023): FDA concerns about the infusion pump device, not the drug
- Second CRL (June 2024): FDA concerns about a third-party contract manufacturer's facility — not ABBV-951-specific
- FDA approval: October 17, 2024 — third submission succeeded
- EU: Produodopa approved via Decentralized Procedure in Q3 2022; VYAFUSER pump CE-marked November 2023; EU launch January 2024
- Global: approved in 35 countries as of late 2025; >15,000 patients treated worldwide

- **vs. Duopa (AbbVie):** Same parent company, so cannibalization is intentional. Duopa requires PEG-J tube placement (surgical procedure, risk of peritonitis, tube complications) limiting uptake to ~10,000 US patients. VYALEV eliminates the surgical barrier — the addressable population expands to any advanced PD patient willing to wear a pump with SC cannula. Duopa's 16-hour infusion window vs. VYALEV's 24-hour coverage is an additional advantage for nocturnal symptom management
- **vs. [[nd0612]] (NeuroDerm/Mitsubishi Tanabe):** Direct competitor — also SC continuous LD/CD, also delivered by wearable pump. Key difference: [[nd0612]] infuses levodopa/carbidopa directly (not prodrugs), which creates solubility challenges requiring a larger infusion volume. [[nd0612]] received a CRL in June 2024 (carbidopa safety data, manufacturing concerns); NDA resubmitted May 2025 with PDUFA Q4 2025. If approved, [[nd0612]] competes head-to-head with VYALEV on efficacy, infusion site tolerability, pump ergonomics, and price. VYALEV's first-mover advantage and AbbVie's neurology sales infrastructure are significant moats
- **vs. [[ipx203|IPX-203]] (Amneal):** Extended-release oral LD/CD formulation designed to reduce dosing frequency. Oral convenience advantage over any infusion, but cannot match the steady-state PK of continuous SC delivery. Targets a less advanced patient population — patients progress from oral ([[ipx203|IPX-203]], [[rytary]]) to infusion (VYALEV) as motor fluctuations worsen
- **vs. [[rytary]] (Amneal):** Extended-release oral LD/CD (IPX066). Same hierarchy as [[ipx203|IPX-203]] — oral convenience for moderate fluctuators, not a substitute for continuous infusion in advanced disease
- **vs. DBS (deep brain stimulation):** Surgical alternative for advanced motor fluctuations. Different patient selection (DBS requires intact cognition, excludes patients with dementia). VYALEV and DBS are complementary rather than substitutes in many cases; some patients use both

## Analysis

VYALEV is a lifecycle management success story. AbbVie took the validated Duopa concept (continuous levodopa delivery reduces motor complications in advanced PD) and removed the surgical barrier that capped Duopa's addressable market. The pharmacology is not novel — levodopa/carbidopa is a 50-year-old therapy — but the drug-device engineering (phosphate prodrugs enabling high-concentration SC solution + miniaturized pump) is genuinely innovative. The two FDA rejections (both manufacturing/device, not efficacy) delayed launch by 18 months but also validated that the clinical package was never in question.

The commercial trajectory is strong. $138M in Q3 2025 — roughly one year post-launch — puts VYALEV on a >$500M annual run rate, already approaching Duopa's peak. The key growth driver is expansion beyond the Duopa-eligible population: the ~10,000 US Duopa patients were self-selected as willing to undergo PEG-J surgery, but an estimated 50,000-100,000 advanced PD patients with inadequately controlled motor fluctuations declined Duopa because of the surgical requirement. VYALEV lowers the barrier to continuous infusion therapy for this much larger pool.

**Analytical estimate — VYALEV peak sales: $1.5-2.5B globally.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Duopa peaked at ~$500M with a surgically limited addressable market. VYALEV removes that limitation, plausibly expanding the treatable population 3-5x. At similar pricing (~$100K/year), 15,000-25,000 patients globally reaches $1.5-2.5B. Downside risk: [[nd0612]] approval splits the market; infusion site reaction burden limits real-world adherence below trial rates. Upside risk: 24-hour coverage drives earlier adoption in the disease course (moderate, not just advanced, fluctuators).

The infusion site reaction profile is the main commercial risk. Trial discontinuation of 22% is high. Nodules in 25%, cellulitis in 28% — these are not trivial for a chronic-use product. Real-world adherence will depend heavily on patient education, site rotation compliance, and AbbVie's nurse educator support infrastructure. If real-world discontinuation rates match trials, peak sales compress to the lower end of estimates. If AbbVie can bring discontinuation below 15% through improved cannula design or patient management protocols, the upper range is achievable.
