# HL192

## Summary

HL192 (ATH-399A) is the first Nurr1 transcription factor activator to enter clinical development for Parkinson's disease, completing a Phase 1 FIH study in 76 healthy volunteers (November 2024) with clean safety across 5 dose levels and PK supporting once-daily oral dosing. NurrOn Pharmaceuticals (startup, Boston) originated the compound from Kwang-Soo Kim's lab at Harvard/McLean Hospital, with co-development partners HanAll BioPharma (KRX: 009420) and Daewoong Pharmaceutical (KRX: 069620) providing funding and clinical infrastructure in Korea. The next milestone is initiating the first patient trial in PD, expected Q2 2026 -- this is the key proof-of-concept gate. If patient data shows target engagement and motor/non-motor improvement, it validates an entirely novel mechanism class (transcription factor modulation) for neurodegeneration. If it fails to show efficacy in patients, the Nurr1 thesis remains preclinical-only and the neuroprotective approach loses a key clinical data point.

## Notes

### Science
- Nurr1 (NR4A2) is an orphan nuclear receptor that functions as a master transcription factor for dopaminergic neuron development, differentiation, maintenance, and survival -- it controls expression of tyrosine hydroxylase (TH), dopamine transporter (DAT), VMAT2, and other dopaminergic genes
- Nurr1 expression is reduced in post-mortem PD brains and correlates with alpha-synuclein accumulation; genetic variants in NR4A2 have been linked to familial PD in rare pedigrees, providing modest genetic validation
- Dual mechanism: (1) neuroprotective -- enhances dopaminergic neuron survival and function; (2) anti-inflammatory -- suppresses NF-kB-mediated microglial neurotoxicity. This dual action is distinctive vs. single-mechanism approaches like [[prasinezumab|alpha-synuclein clearance]] or [[biib122|LRRK2 inhibition]]
- HL192 derives from the amodiaquine scaffold (4-amino-7-chloroquinoline); Kim lab screened >570 derivatives to optimize brain penetrance and Nurr1 binding. The optimized compound (4A7C-301/ATH-399A) shows robust neuroprotection in MPTP mouse models with improvement in both motor and non-motor (olfactory) deficits without dyskinesia
- Key scientific question: transcription factor targets are historically considered "undruggable" -- HL192 engages Nurr1's ligand-binding domain directly, which is unusual for a nuclear receptor classified as an orphan (no known endogenous ligand). Whether pharmacological activation in humans recapitulates the preclinical neuroprotection is entirely unproven
- MJFF grant ($1.7M) for Phase 1 signals external scientific validation from the most rigorous PD-focused funder

### Clinical

**Phase 1 FIH (SAD/MAD/Food Effect)** | NCT06088784 | N=76 | Healthy volunteers (ages 18-80)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability across 5 ascending dose groups --> Met; no serious treatment-related adverse events
- **Key secondary:** PK characterization --> Supports once-daily oral dosing; all dose levels well below pre-determined NOAEL
- **Safety detail:** TEAEs equivalent between treatment and placebo groups with no dose-dependent trends; no serious AEs
- **Status:** Completed (October 2023 first dose; November 2024 completion)
- **Interpretation:** Clean Phase 1 in healthy volunteers is necessary but insufficient -- the compound clears the safety bar. No biomarker or target engagement data has been disclosed, which is a gap. The critical question is whether Nurr1 activation can be measured in the CNS of living humans.

**Patient Trial (Phase 1b/2)** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | Parkinson's disease patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Expected safety/tolerability in PD patients, with exploratory efficacy endpoints
- **Status:** Planned; estimated initiation Q2 2026
- **Interpretation:** This will be the first test of Nurr1 activation in PD patients. Trial design details (endpoints, enrichment strategy, biomarkers) have not been disclosed.

- HL192 is currently the **only** clinical-stage Nurr1 activator globally -- there are no direct competitors targeting this transcription factor in PD trials
- The broader competitive context is the neuroprotective/disease-modifying PD landscape: [[prasinezumab]] (alpha-synuclein clearance), [[biib122|BIIB122]] (LRRK2 inhibition), [[pariceract]] (GBA1 activation), and [[minzasolmin]] (alpha-synuclein misfolding) all pursue disease modification through different mechanisms
- Nurr1 activation is mechanistically orthogonal to these approaches -- it targets the transcriptional program that keeps dopaminergic neurons alive rather than clearing toxic proteins or correcting single-gene defects. This means HL192 could theoretically be additive/combinatorial with other disease-modifying therapies
- The main competitive risk is not other Nurr1 compounds but rather: (1) if [[prasinezumab]] or [[biib122|BIIB122]] succeeds first in Phase 3, the urgency for novel mechanisms diminishes; (2) if multiple disease-modification approaches fail, the entire neuroprotective thesis loses investor appetite
- Academic competitors: multiple groups are pursuing Nurr1 gene therapy and Nurr1 agonist chemistry (e.g., amodiaquine repurposing), but none have entered clinical development

## Analysis

HL192 represents a genuinely novel mechanism class in PD therapeutics. Transcription factor targets have historically been considered undruggable, and Nurr1 is an orphan nuclear receptor -- meaning no endogenous ligand was known before the Kim lab's work identified the amodiaquine scaffold as a binding partner for the ligand-binding domain. The preclinical rationale is compelling: Nurr1 is downregulated in PD, its loss causes dopaminergic neuron dysfunction, and pharmacological activation rescues neurons in multiple animal models. The dual neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory mechanism is attractive because it addresses two pathological axes simultaneously.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of clinical success (approval): 5-10%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: first-in-class small molecules for neurodegenerative disease have ~5% Phase 1-to-approval success rates
- Adjustments upward: novel validated target with strong preclinical data (+3%), clean Phase 1 safety (+2%), oral once-daily dosing is commercially optimal (+1%), MJFF endorsement suggests rigorous scientific vetting (+1%)
- Adjustments downward: no human target engagement or biomarker data disclosed (-3%), transcription factor modulation is unprecedented in CNS (-3%), tiny startup with limited capital (-2%), no established clinical endpoint for neuroprotection trials (-2%)
- Net: ~5-10% -- heavily front-loaded risk given the mechanism novelty

The key signal to watch is the patient trial design. If the consortium includes Nurr1-specific biomarkers (e.g., CSF dopaminergic metabolites, DaT-SPECT, neuromelanin MRI), it suggests they have a plan to demonstrate target engagement -- which would be the single most de-risking data point. If the trial is safety-only in patients, the program remains a long-duration bet. The MJFF grant is a positive quality signal but at $1.7M is modest -- MJFF funds early bets broadly. The Korean pharma partnership provides clinical trial infrastructure and cost advantages but does not add drug development expertise in neurodegeneration specifically. HL192's most interesting strategic position is as a potential combination partner: if disease modification proves to require multi-target approaches (as in oncology), a Nurr1 activator layered on top of alpha-synuclein lowering or LRRK2 inhibition could be compelling. But that thesis requires at least one other approach to succeed first.
