# Troculeucel (SNK01)

## Summary

Troculeucel (SNK01) is an autologous, non-genetically modified, ex vivo-expanded natural killer (NK) cell therapy from NKGen Biotech (biotech, OTC: NKGN) that received FDA IND clearance in April 2024 for a Phase 1/2a trial in Parkinson's disease (N=30, placebo-controlled). The PD program leverages a neuroinflammation hypothesis -- NK cells cross the BBB via CXCR3, phagocytose alpha-synuclein aggregates, eliminate autoreactive T cells, and secrete immunoregulatory cytokines. However, the PD trial has not visibly commenced enrollment as of February 2026, while the company faces severe financial distress (delisted from Nasdaq March 2025, ~$22M market cap, raising small tranches of capital). The lead indication is Alzheimer's, where Phase 1 data showed cognitive stabilization in 90% of subjects and FDA Fast Track designation was granted (February 2025), but sample sizes are tiny (N=13 combined). If the PD trial initiates and shows safety plus neuroinflammatory biomarker reductions similar to the AD data, this would be the first NK cell therapy to demonstrate CNS activity in PD and would validate a novel immune modulation axis alongside [[bhv-8000|BHV-8000 (TYK2/JAK1)]] and [[hb-admsc|HB-adMSCs]]. If the company cannot fund the trial, the PD program is effectively shelved and the asset becomes a case study in capital constraints killing a biologically interesting hypothesis.

## Notes

### Science
- Troculeucel consists of the patient's own NK cells, collected via blood draw, expanded ex vivo to billions of cells with enhanced cytotoxic activity and increased activating receptor expression, then cryopreserved and re-infused IV
- Not genetically modified -- distinguished from CAR-NK or engineered approaches; the expansion process alone enhances NK function
- Three proposed mechanisms relevant to PD:
  1. **Protein clearance:** In vitro data shows NK cells can phagocytose and digest alpha-synuclein and amyloid aggregates, similar to microglial (HMC3) cells
  2. **Immunomodulation:** NK cells identify and eliminate autoreactive T cells contributing to neuroinflammation without disrupting resting T cells
  3. **Cytokine secretion:** Expanded NK cells secrete immunoregulatory cytokines that dampen neuroinflammatory cascades
- BBB penetration claimed via upregulation of CXCR3 chemokine receptor on expanded NK cells -- this is a critical and unproven claim; peripheral NK cells accessing CNS parenchyma in sufficient numbers to be therapeutic is not established in the literature
- The shared neuroinflammatory component between AD and PD is the scientific rationale for extending from AD to PD -- CEO Paul Song: "Both share a neuroinflammatory component, which led to our hypothesis that SNK01 may be beneficial in both indications"
- Key scientific question: does a peripheral IV infusion of NK cells produce meaningful target engagement in the brain? The mechanism is fundamentally different from [[bhv-8000|BHV-8000]] (brain-penetrant small molecule) or [[hb-admsc|HB-adMSCs]] (paracrine MSC effects without CNS entry) -- troculeucel requires actual cellular trafficking into the CNS
- NKGen's Parkinson's Foundation collaboration (initiated November 2022) provides some external validation of scientific rationale but is not a funding or co-development arrangement

### Clinical

**SNK01-PD Phase 1/2a** | NCT TBD | N=30 (planned: 20 SNK01, 10 placebo) | PD patients (details TBD)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability; exploratory efficacy
- **Status:** IND cleared April 29, 2024; first patient dosing originally anticipated H2 2024; no public confirmation of enrollment as of February 2026
- **Interpretation:** The 21-month gap between IND clearance and absence of enrollment updates is a major red flag. The company's financial constraints (delisting, sub-$25M market cap) are the most likely explanation. PD has not appeared on NKGen's clinical trials page as an active study.

**Compassionate Use (PD)** | No NCT | Single-patient expanded access
- **Status:** FDA compassionate use authorization granted February 2023; additional expanded access protocol (EAP) authorized July 2025 covering PD among other neurodegenerative conditions (up to 20 patients total across all indications)
- **Key finding:** CEO reported "real dramatic improvement" in early PD patients under compassionate use, but no published data, no peer review, and no patient numbers disclosed
- **Interpretation:** Anecdotal compassionate use reports are not evidence. The expanded access program may serve as a surrogate data-generation strategy while funding for the formal trial remains constrained.

**SNK01-AD01 Phase 1/2a (Alzheimer's -- cross-reference)** | NCT06189963 | N=13 (Phase 1 cohort) | Moderate AD
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → No drug-related adverse events across ~100 patients dosed (all indications combined)
- **Key findings (Phase 1, published Feb 2025):** 90% of subjects showed stable or improved cognition at 3 months; 2 patients completing 17 doses improved from moderate to mild AD within 3 months; dose-dependent decreases in pTau181 and GFAP; improvements in amyloid-beta 42/40 ratio and CSF alpha-synuclein levels
- **FDA Fast Track:** Granted February 2025 for moderate AD
- **Status:** Phase 2a cohort actively enrolling (sites in California, Florida, Ontario)
- **Interpretation:** The AD data is the only published clinical evidence for troculeucel's CNS mechanism. Sample sizes are extremely small (N=2 completers at 12 months). The biomarker signals (GFAP reduction, alpha-synuclein improvement) are directionally relevant to PD but far from conclusive.

### Financial
- **Market cap:** ~$22.5M as of mid-2025 (OTC: NKGN)
- **Delisted from Nasdaq:** March 5, 2025; trading transferred to OTC Markets
- **Recent funding:** $5M second lien convertible loan (April 2024); ~$4.75M in prior 2024 funding; $2M from Japanese strategic investor (July 2025, priced at $0.25/share); additional funding announced July 2025 to regain SEC reporting compliance
- **Financial distress:** The company is raising small, dilutive tranches of capital to maintain operations -- total funding appears to be in the low tens of millions, insufficient to fund a PD Phase 1/2a plus ongoing AD Phase 1/2a simultaneously
- **No partner:** Unlike [[bemdaneprocel]] (Bayer) or [[aro-snca]] (Novartis), troculeucel has no pharma partner to fund development -- this is a critical risk factor for a cell therapy that requires manufacturing infrastructure
- **Autologous manufacturing constraint:** Each patient requires individual cell collection, expansion, and cryopreservation -- COGS per patient are inherently high and do not scale like allogeneic approaches ([[bemdaneprocel]], [[hb-admsc]])

- SNK01 occupies a unique modality niche -- the only NK cell therapy in PD clinical development -- but competes for the broader neuroinflammation thesis with [[bhv-8000|BHV-8000]] (brain-penetrant TYK2/JAK1 inhibitor, Phase 2/3), [[hb-admsc|HB-adMSCs]] (MSC cell therapy, Phase 2), [[nly01|NLY01]] (GLP-1R agonist, Phase 2), and inflammasome-targeting approaches like [[selnoflast]] and [[dapansutrile]]
- Key modality distinction: [[bhv-8000]] is an oral small molecule with demonstrated ~50% brain penetration; SNK01 requires IV infusion of living cells that must traffic to the CNS -- the small molecule has a clear pharmacological advantage for chronic dosing
- Among cell therapies in PD, SNK01 is conceptually distinct from dopaminergic neuron replacement programs ([[bemdaneprocel]], [[anpd001]], [[raguneprocel]]) -- those aim to replace lost neurons; SNK01 aims to modulate the immune environment to slow further loss
- [[hb-admsc|HB-adMSCs]] is the closest comparator: both are IV-infused cell therapies targeting neuroinflammation via immune modulation, but HB-adMSCs are allogeneic (off-the-shelf) MSCs with Phase 2 data showing MDS-UPDRS Part III improvement, while SNK01 is autologous (patient-specific) NK cells with no PD clinical data
- If [[bhv-8000]] succeeds in Phase 2/3, the neuroinflammation thesis is validated via the easiest-to-administer modality (oral), potentially diminishing interest in cell-based neuroinflammation approaches

## Analysis

The scientific hypothesis underlying troculeucel is biologically plausible: neuroinflammation contributes to PD progression, NK cells have demonstrated immunomodulatory and protein-clearance capabilities in vitro, and the AD Phase 1 biomarker data (GFAP reduction, alpha-synuclein improvement) provides directional support. But the gap between hypothesis and clinical validation is enormous, and the company's financial situation makes it unlikely the PD trial will generate meaningful data in the near term.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of PD trial generating a readout: 25-30%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for IND-cleared trials that never enroll: ~15-20% in small biotechs
- Adjustment upward: IND is cleared, FDA expanded access demonstrates some regulatory engagement (+5%)
- Adjustment downward: 21 months since IND clearance with no enrollment confirmation (-15%), Nasdaq delisting and sub-$25M market cap (-10%), no pharma partner (-10%), autologous manufacturing complexity (-5%)
- Net: ~25-30% chance the PD trial generates interpretable data

**Analytical estimate -- Clinical success probability (conditional on trial completion): 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for Phase 1/2 cell therapies in neurodegeneration: ~10-15%
- Adjustment upward: clean safety profile across ~100 patients (+5%), AD biomarker signals directionally supportive (+5%), novel mechanism with no prior failures to anchor expectations (+5%)
- Adjustment downward: no PD-specific preclinical efficacy data published (-5%), BBB penetration mechanism unvalidated in controlled studies (-10%), autologous manufacturing variability (-5%), tiny sample size (N=20 treated) underpowered for efficacy signal (-5%)
- Net: ~10-15%

**Signal analysis:**
- The company's behavior tells a cautionary story: IND cleared but trial not initiated, Nasdaq delisting, serial small fundraises at declining valuations. This is consistent with a company that has a potentially interesting biological asset but cannot execute on it. The Japanese strategic investor ($2M at $0.25/share in July 2025) may signal residual interest in the NK cell platform but is not a scale of investment that enables PD clinical development.
- The FDA expanded access authorization (July 2025) covering PD alongside other neurodegenerative conditions may be a pragmatic strategy -- generating real-world case reports in PD patients while the formal trial remains unfunded. This is not unusual for resource-constrained biotechs but produces unreliable evidence.
- The AD Fast Track designation (February 2025) and conference presentations at AD/PD 2025 demonstrate the company is generating enough data to maintain regulatory and scientific engagement, at least in Alzheimer's. Whether this momentum extends to PD depends entirely on funding.
- For the broader PD field, SNK01 is worth monitoring as a signal of whether NK cell-mediated immune modulation can produce CNS effects. If the AD program generates larger-scale biomarker data showing alpha-synuclein and GFAP changes, the PD hypothesis becomes more credible even without a PD-specific trial.
