# SAR446159

> [!note] Canonical entry
> SAR446159 is Sanofi's internal designation for [[abl301|ABL301]]. They are the same molecule. The canonical note with full analysis is [[abl301]]. This entry exists as an alias for cross-referencing under the Sanofi code.

## Summary

SAR446159 is Sanofi's (big pharma) designation for [[abl301|ABL301]], a bispecific antibody developed by ABL Bio (Korean biotech, ABL.KS) using the Grabody-B IGF1R-based blood-brain barrier shuttle platform — the first brain-shuttled alpha-synuclein antibody to reach the clinic. Phase 1 in 91 healthy adults (NCT05756920, completed April 2025) showed clean safety with no SAEs, but no human CSF penetration or target engagement data have been published, and Sanofi deprioritized the program in January 2026 before Phase 2. The Grabody-B platform itself has attracted $5.4B in cumulative pharma deals (Sanofi $1.06B, GSK $2.8B, Lilly $2.6B), yet the first clinical payload stalled. If [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 succeeds and Sanofi's Phase 1b biomarker study demonstrates brain target engagement, SAR446159 could re-enter active development with a differentiated BBB-crossing mechanism. If [[prasinezumab]] fails, the extracellular alpha-synuclein antibody modality is likely exhausted regardless of BBB enhancement.

## Notes

### Science
- Bispecific antibody format: one arm binds the C-terminal region of aggregated alpha-synuclein with high selectivity for fibrillar/aggregated forms over monomers; the other arm (Grabody-B) binds IGF1R on brain endothelial cells to trigger receptor-mediated transcytosis across the BBB
- Grabody-B exploits IGF1R's 98% cross-species homology (vs. 76% for transferrin receptor), enabling strong preclinical-to-clinical translation, and critically does not interfere with endogenous IGF1R signaling — a key safety advantage for chronic dosing in a neurodegenerative disease
- Preclinical data showed significantly higher brain and CSF penetration vs. monospecific anti-alpha-synuclein antibody alone, and prevented alpha-synuclein seeding capacity in vitro and in vivo (npj Parkinson's Disease, Jan 2025)
- Key differentiator vs. [[prasinezumab]]: standard mAbs achieve only 0.1-0.2% brain penetration; Grabody-B aims to increase CNS exposure by an order of magnitude — testing whether the 0-for-6 antibody failure record in alpha-synuclein reflects insufficient brain drug levels rather than target invalidity
- IGF1R shuttle has a safety advantage over TfR-based shuttles (Roche Brain Shuttle, Denali Transport Vehicle, BioArctic BrainTransporter) — TfR targeting causes reticulocyte depletion and anemia risk at high doses, while IGF1R has neuroprotective signaling properties
- Core scientific question remains: does delivering more antibody across the BBB matter if alpha-synuclein pathology is predominantly intracellular? The prion-like spreading hypothesis says clearing extracellular aggregates blocks cell-to-cell transmission, but no antibody has clinically confirmed this

### Clinical

**SAR446159 Phase 1 (SAD/MAD)** | NCT05756920 | N=91 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety profile. No deaths, no SAEs
- **Key secondary:** SAD (N=56): 7.1% treatment-related AEs (half in placebo group), all Grade 1-2. MAD (N=35): 5.7% treatment-related AEs, all Grade 1-2. 69.6% of SAD participants had mild TEAEs overall
- **Status:** Completed (December 2022 – April 2025)
- **Interpretation:** Safety validated for the Grabody-B IGF1R shuttle in humans. The critical gap is the absence of any published human CSF concentrations, brain PK data, or target engagement biomarkers — if the shuttle delivered impressive brain penetration, Sanofi would be advancing, not deprioritizing

**SAR446159 Phase 1b (biomarker study)** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | PD patients (planned)
- **Primary endpoint:** Alpha-synuclein target engagement via CSF analysis and imaging
- **Status:** Announced by Sanofi; not yet registered. Timeline TBD pending "dedicated measurement equipment" development
- **Interpretation:** Sanofi is demanding proof of target engagement before committing Phase 2 resources — rational de-risking but cedes first-mover advantage to [[prasinezumab]], already in Phase 3

### Financial
- **Sanofi deal (January 2022):** $75M upfront, up to $985M in milestones (~$1.06B total). ABL Bio led preclinical and Phase 1; Sanofi responsible for Phase 2+ development, regulatory, and commercialization worldwide
- **$5M milestone received** (development milestone confirmed by Korea Biomed)
- **Sanofi deprioritized SAR446159 in January 2026** during Q4 2025 earnings call, alongside eclitasertib (RIPK1 inhibitor, Denali partnership) — part of broader portfolio churn as Sanofi acquired Vigil (TREM2), Blueprint, and Vicebio
- **ABL Bio stock:** Dropped 19.5% on deprioritization news (January 2026)
- **Grabody-B platform cumulative deal value:** $5.4B across Sanofi ($1.06B), GSK ($2.8B, April 2025), and Lilly ($2.6B, December 2024). Actual upfront payments total ~$165M — pharma is paying for cheap options, not deep conviction
- **Deal comparison:** $75M upfront (7.1% conviction ratio) was competitive for a preclinical bispecific in 2022 but looks generous given the deprioritization. GSK and Lilly licensed the platform for different payloads (non-alpha-synuclein targets), not to rescue SAR446159

- Sanofi's deprioritization partly reflects competitive timing — Roche has a 5+ year head start with [[prasinezumab]] including Phase 3 biomarker evidence. Sanofi may rationally be waiting for PARAISO readout (2027-2028) before committing Phase 2 resources to SAR446159

## Analysis

SAR446159 represents the central paradox of the alpha-synuclein antibody field: a scientifically differentiated asset (enhanced BBB penetration via IGF1R shuttle) stalled by the same target-level uncertainty that has produced six consecutive primary endpoint failures. Sanofi's deprioritization in January 2026 is a negative behavioral signal, but context matters — this was part of broader portfolio rationalization, not a data-driven safety or efficacy kill. The absence of published human CSF PK data after a completed 91-patient Phase 1 is the most informative signal: if the shuttle achieved impressive brain penetration, Sanofi would be showing the data, not shelving the program.

**Analytical estimate — SAR446159 clinical success probability: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: 0/6 alpha-synuclein antibodies have met primary endpoints, and SAR446159 was deprioritized before Phase 2 → starting point <10%
- Adjustments upward: BBB shuttle could meaningfully increase brain drug levels (+10%), clean Phase 1 safety enables regulatory flexibility (+3%), [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3 success would validate the target (+5%), IGF1R safety advantage enables chronic dosing (+2%)
- Adjustments downward: zero published human CSF data after 3 years (-5%), Sanofi deprioritization is a negative behavioral signal (-5%), alpha-synuclein pathology is primarily intracellular while antibodies work extracellularly (-3%)
- Net: ~15-20%, conditional on the program resuming active development

**Signal analysis:** The Grabody-B platform is validated by three major pharma deals totaling $5.4B — but GSK and Lilly licensed it for non-alpha-synuclein payloads. This distinction is critical: pharma believes the delivery vehicle works but the first payload (alpha-synuclein antibody) is on thin ice. Sanofi's simultaneous deprioritization of eclitasertib (Denali) and pivot toward acquired assets (Vigil TREM2 agonist, Blueprint, Vicebio) suggests portfolio-level capital reallocation rather than SAR446159-specific failure.

The decision tree is asymmetric and entirely dependent on external events: if [[prasinezumab]] PARAISO Phase 3 (2027-2028) succeeds and Sanofi's Phase 1b biomarker study demonstrates brain target engagement, SAR446159 becomes a compelling next-generation asset. If [[prasinezumab]] fails, the extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance thesis collapses and SAR446159 is permanently shelved. The Grabody-B platform survives either scenario — GSK and Lilly can pair it with [[biib122|LRRK2 antibodies]], GCase enzyme replacement, or neuroinflammation payloads — but SAR446159 as a product does not.
