# Emrusolmin

## Summary

Emrusolmin (anle138b) is an oral alpha-synuclein oligomer modulator developed by MODAG GmbH (German startup) and globally licensed to Teva (big pharma, TEVA). Phase 1a in healthy volunteers (NCT04208152) and Phase 1b in PD patients (NCT04685265) are complete with clean safety and exposures above preclinically efficacious levels, but no efficacy data has been disclosed from PD trials. The asset's most advanced efficacy test is the ongoing TOPAS-MSA Phase 2 (NCT06568237) in multiple system atrophy, with PD Phase 2 still pending. If TOPAS-MSA succeeds, it validates the oligomer-targeting mechanism across synucleinopathies and strengthens the case for PD Phase 2; if it fails, the approach faces credibility challenges — especially after [[minzasolmin]]'s Phase 2 termination removed the closest small-molecule competitor.

## Notes

### Science
- Small molecule identified via high-throughput screen for inhibitors of alpha-synuclein and prion protein oligomerization; diphenylpyrazole scaffold
- Mechanism: binds inside tubular alpha-synuclein fibril cavities via stable polar interactions, positioning its pyrazole core near the protein backbone to block oligomer formation at the earliest toxic species stage
- Key differentiator vs. antibody approaches ([[prasinezumab]], [[cinpanemab]]): targets intracellular/membrane-associated oligomers directly rather than extracellular aggregates — addresses the critique that antibody CNS penetration (~0.1-0.2%) limits efficacy
- Key differentiator vs. [[minzasolmin]]: emrusolmin targets oligomer formation broadly across alpha-synuclein, tau, and prion aggregates, while [[minzasolmin]] specifically targeted membrane-bound oligomeric alpha-synuclein pore formation. Emrusolmin's broader mechanism could be an advantage (multi-target) or disadvantage (less selective)
- Excellent oral bioavailability and BBB penetration — preclinical data showed 5-fold higher brain concentrations than plasma, a major advantage for a CNS target
- 12-hour half-life supports once-daily dosing
- Preclinical efficacy demonstrated in transgenic PD models (reduced alpha-synuclein deposition, preserved dopamine neurons) and MSA models (decreased oligomers, preserved motor function), even with delayed treatment initiation
- Also active against tau pathology in preclinical models, raising potential for broader neurodegenerative applications (Alzheimer's)
- Open question: whether oligomer modulation as a mechanism translates to clinical disease modification — [[minzasolmin]]'s Phase 2 failure (different target binding site) is a cautionary signal for the small-molecule anti-aggregation class
- PET tracer variant ([11C]MODAG-001, [18F]anle138b) in development for imaging alpha-synuclein aggregates — potential companion diagnostic

### Clinical

**Anle138b-P1-01 (Phase 1a)** | NCT04208152 | N=68 | Healthy volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean profile; all AEs mild and resolved; no clinically significant changes in any system organ class
- **Key secondary:** PK — single and multiple ascending doses (50-300 mg/day); 12-hour half-life; plasma levels significantly exceeded preclinically efficacious thresholds
- **Status:** Completed (December 2019 - August 2020)
- **Interpretation:** Established favorable safety and PK profile in healthy volunteers; confirmed oral bioavailability and brain penetration assumptions from preclinical models

**Anle138b-P1-02 (Phase 1b)** | NCT04685265 | N=70 | Mild-to-moderate PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → No compound-related AEs leading to early termination; no SAEs; one withdrawal due to unrelated SARS-CoV-2 infection
- **Key secondary:** PK in PD patients comparable to healthy volunteers (important given delayed GI transit in PD); exposures above minimally effective plasma levels from animal models
- **Status:** Completed (December 2020 - December 2022; presented at MDS Congress 2023)
- **Interpretation:** Confirmed safety and PK in the target patient population. Notably, no efficacy or biomarker data disclosed — the study was short-duration (7-28 days) and not powered for clinical outcomes. The absence of PD-specific PD endpoints leaves the efficacy question entirely open.

**TOPAS-MSA (Phase 2)** | NCT06568237 | N=200 | Probable/possible MSA patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Progression in modified UMSARS Part I score at 48 weeks
- **Design:** Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 300 mg emrusolmin vs. placebo once daily for 48 weeks; 50 sites across US, France, Italy, Germany, Spain, Israel, Japan
- **Status:** Recruiting (initiated October 2024; estimated completion June 2027)
- **Interpretation:** MSA is being used as the lead efficacy indication — rational because MSA is a faster-progressing synucleinopathy (detectable signal in 1 year) and has FDA orphan drug and Fast Track designations. A positive MSA result would de-risk PD Phase 2 design.

**PD Phase 2** | NCT TBD | Details not disclosed
- **Status:** Pending; announced as planned but not yet registered
- **Interpretation:** PD Phase 2 timing likely depends on TOPAS-MSA interim signals and/or Teva's portfolio prioritization

### Financial
- MODAG GmbH is a private German startup based in Wendelsheim, Germany; financial terms of the Teva deal were not publicly disclosed
- Total known funding: ~$16M raised (Series A), plus MJFF grants of $1.26M (2015, preclinical optimization) and $1.4M (post-Phase 1, additional development)
- Teva (TEVA, NYSE) holds exclusive global license to develop, manufacture, and commercialize emrusolmin and sery433 (related compound)
- FDA orphan drug designation for MSA (2022); FDA Fast Track designation for MSA (September 2025)
- No peak sales estimates publicly available; MSA market is small (~15,000-50,000 US patients) but orphan pricing could support meaningful economics
- The undisclosed deal terms make it difficult to gauge Teva's conviction level from economics alone — however, Teva's investment in a 200-patient, 50-site global Phase 2 and pursuit of Fast Track suggest material commitment
- Teva's neuroscience franchise historically centers on generic/specialty medicines; emrusolmin represents a disease-modification bet outside their traditional portfolio

- Emrusolmin occupies a distinct niche: oral small molecule targeting oligomer formation, vs. antibodies targeting extracellular aggregated protein ([[prasinezumab]]) or gene silencing reducing production ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[ly3962681|LY3962681]])
- Closest competitor was [[minzasolmin]] (UCB/Novartis) — also an oral small molecule targeting alpha-synuclein aggregation, but terminated after Phase 2 failure. This is both a risk signal (small-molecule anti-aggregation may not work) and an opportunity (emrusolmin is now the only oral alpha-synuclein aggregation inhibitor in active clinical development)
- The MSA-first strategy differentiates from [[prasinezumab]] (PD-first) and [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (PD-first) — an MSA proof-of-concept could provide disease modification evidence faster than a PD trial
- Oral dosing with high brain penetration is a significant practical advantage over IV antibodies ([[prasinezumab]]) and intrathecal delivery ([[ly3962681|LY3962681]]) for chronic neurodegenerative treatment
- If [[prasinezumab]]'s PARAISO Phase 3 validates extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance, it strengthens the broader alpha-synuclein target thesis, which benefits emrusolmin indirectly. If PARAISO fails, the field's focus may shift toward intracellular approaches where emrusolmin's brain-penetrant small molecule mechanism is better positioned

## Analysis

Emrusolmin's core appeal is its pharmacological profile: oral, brain-penetrant, once-daily dosing with demonstrated safety across 138 subjects. The 5-fold brain-to-plasma ratio is exceptional for a CNS small molecule and addresses the fundamental limitation of antibody approaches to alpha-synuclein. However, the asset sits in an uncomfortable gap — Phase 1 safety is clean but the complete absence of clinical efficacy data in PD means the investment thesis rests entirely on preclinical translation, which has historically been unreliable in neurodegeneration.

**Analytical estimate — Phase 2 success probability in MSA: 20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: neurodegenerative disease-modifying trials have <10% historical success rate
- Adjustments upward: MSA is faster-progressing (better signal-to-noise, +5%), orphan indication (regulatory flexibility, +3%), excellent PK/brain penetration (+5%), novel mechanism not yet failed in MSA (+5%), preclinical MSA model efficacy (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: [[minzasolmin]] Phase 2 failure in related small-molecule anti-aggregation class (-5%), no clinical efficacy data in any indication yet (-3%), relatively small patient enrollment for heterogeneous disease (-3%)
- Net: ~20-25%

**Signal analysis:**
- Teva's pursuit of FDA Fast Track (September 2025) and investment in a 50-site, 7-country Phase 2 signals genuine commitment — this is not a low-cost option being maintained cheaply. Fast Track also provides more frequent FDA interactions and potential for accelerated approval if interim data are strong.
- The MSA-first strategy is tactically sound: MSA progresses 5-10x faster than PD, UMSARS is a validated endpoint, and orphan drug status provides 7 years of market exclusivity. A positive TOPAS-MSA readout in 2027 would immediately de-risk PD Phase 2 design and potentially accelerate enrollment.
- The [[minzasolmin]] termination is a double-edged signal. It removes the nearest competitor but also raises questions about whether small molecules can achieve sufficient target engagement against alpha-synuclein aggregation in humans. Emrusolmin's different binding site (fibril cavity vs. membrane-bound oligomers) provides a mechanistic argument for differentiation, but investors and regulators will scrutinize this distinction.
- MODAG's thin capitalization (~$16M raised) means the Teva partnership is existentially important. If Teva deprioritizes emrusolmin — possible given their large portfolio — the program could stall. Conversely, MODAG has minimal overhead competing for Teva's attention, making this a low-friction collaboration.
- Decision tree: if TOPAS-MSA positive in 2027, expect rapid PD Phase 2 initiation and potential accelerated MSA approval pathway. If TOPAS-MSA negative, PD Phase 2 becomes unlikely and the program probably terminates, further weakening the small-molecule aggregation inhibitor thesis.
