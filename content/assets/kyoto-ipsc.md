# Kyoto iPSC-DA (CiRA Phase I/II Trial)

## Summary

The Kyoto University investigator-initiated Phase I/II trial (N=7) is the foundational clinical dataset for the iPSC-derived dopaminergic cell therapy field. Published in Nature (April 2025), it demonstrated that allogeneic iPSC-derived DA progenitors survived in human brain, produced dopamine confirmed by 18F-DOPA PET (44.7% average increase; **63.5% in the high-dose cohort**), and formed no tumors over 24 months. This trial data directly underpins the [[raguneprocel]] NDA filed by Sumitomo Pharma / RACTHERA with Japan's MHLW in August 2025, now under priority review with a committee hearing scheduled for February 19, 2026. If approved, iPSC-derived cell therapy is validated as a platform for neurodegeneration. If rejected, the small N=7 open-label design becomes the cautionary example for premature regulatory filing, and [[bemdaneprocel]] (BlueRock/Bayer, ESC-derived, Phase 3) becomes the unchallenged front-runner.

## Notes

### Science
- Allogeneic iPSC-derived dopaminergic neural progenitor cells generated from HLA-homozygous donor iPSC line established by the CiRA iPS Cell Stock Project at Kyoto University, using technology developed by Prof. Jun Takahashi's group
- The iPSC line carries the most common HLA haplotype in Japan as a homozygote -- HLA-A (24:02), B (52:01), C (12:02), DRB1 (15:02), DQB1 (06:01), DPB1 (09:01) -- matching approximately 17% of the Japanese population, enabling reduced immunosuppression with tacrolimus monotherapy
- Cells are CORIN-sorted (floor plate marker; 93.2% purity post-sort) to select dopaminergic progenitors, yielding a final product of approximately 60% DA progenitors and 40% DA neurons
- Transplanted bilaterally into the putamen via stereotactic surgery through burr holes, where they engraft, mature into dopaminergic neurons, and produce dopamine
- Key distinction vs. [[bemdaneprocel]]: iPSC source (reprogrammed adult cells) vs. ESC source (embryonic stem cells); iPSCs avoid embryonic tissue sourcing concerns and enable HLA-matching from banked cell lines, but raise questions about epigenetic memory and reprogramming completeness
- Preclinical validation in cynomolgus monkeys (Nature Communications, 2020): CORIN-sorted iPSC-DA progenitors survived in MPTP-treated primate putamen, showed 18F-DOPA PET-positive graft formation, and improved motor function without tumor formation at 2 years
- Open scientific questions: (1) long-term durability beyond 24 months -- does a single transplant provide lasting benefit or is repeat dosing needed? (2) optimal dose-response relationship given the stark difference between low-dose (7% PET increase) and high-dose (63.5% PET increase) cohorts; (3) whether tacrolimus discontinuation at 15 months is sufficient to prevent late immune rejection

### Clinical

**Kyoto University Phase I/II (Investigator-Initiated)** | UMIN000033564 / JMA-IIA00384 | N=7 (6 evaluable) | Advanced PD patients (ages 50-69, disease duration >5 years)
- **Design:** Open-label, dose-escalation. Low-dose cohort (N=3): ~2.4 million cells per hemisphere. High-dose cohort (N=4): 5.3-5.5 million cells per hemisphere. First patient received unilateral transplant with 8-month safety evaluation before contralateral side; remaining 6 patients received concurrent bilateral transplants. One patient (PD07) excluded due to COVID-19, replaced by PD08.
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety (SAEs, tumor formation) over 24 months --> **No serious adverse events**; 73 total AEs (72 mild, 1 moderate dyskinesia); MRI confirmed no graft overgrowth; grafts contained <1% proliferating cells
- **18F-DOPA PET:** Overall 44.7% increase in putaminal uptake. **High-dose cohort: 63.5% increase; low-dose cohort: 7% increase** -- strong dose-response relationship confirming higher cell numbers drive greater dopamine production
- **MDS-UPDRS Part III (motor):** OFF-medication state improved 20.4% average (4/6 responders); ON-medication state improved 35.7% average with 4.3-point mean improvement (5/6 responders)
- **Dyskinesia:** Unified Dyskinesia Rating Scale increased 116.4% from baseline, occurring only during ON periods -- attributed to maintained medication doses (not dose-adjusted for graft-derived dopamine) rather than graft-induced dyskinesia
- **Immunosuppression:** Tacrolimus 0.06 mg/kg twice daily (target trough 5-10 ng/mL), reduced by half at 12 months, discontinued at 15 months. No clinically significant immune rejection observed regardless of individual HLA compatibility
- **Enrollment:** Patients recruited August 2018 - January 2019; transplantations 2018-2023; 24-month follow-up completed
- **Status:** Completed; results published Nature (April 2025)
- **Interpretation:** The trial was powered for safety, not efficacy, and succeeded on its primary objective. The 63.5% dopamine increase in the high-dose cohort is the headline number -- it provides the strongest evidence to date that iPSC-derived DA neurons can functionally engraft in human brain and produce meaningful dopamine. The dose-response relationship (63.5% vs. 7%) is biologically coherent and argues for high-dose in future studies. The dyskinesia signal (116.4% increase) requires attention -- it is likely manageable through levodopa dose reduction, which was not done in this fixed-protocol trial, but graft-induced dyskinesia plagued earlier fetal tissue transplant trials and will be a regulatory focus.

## Analysis

The Kyoto trial represents the single most important clinical dataset in the iPSC-derived cell therapy field. The Nature 2025 publication accomplished three things simultaneously: demonstrated safety (no tumors, no SAEs), proved biological function (44.7-63.5% dopamine increase by PET), and showed clinical signal (20.4% motor improvement off medication). For a Phase I/II safety trial with N=7, this is about as good as the data could look.

The dose-response relationship is the most analytically significant finding. The stark difference between the low-dose cohort (7% PET increase, minimal clinical benefit) and the high-dose cohort (63.5% PET increase, meaningful motor improvement) provides a clear design rationale for future trials: high-dose (5+ million cells per hemisphere) is the minimum viable dose. This finding explains why an N=7 trial could support an NDA -- the high-dose cohort showed consistent, biologically coherent results across imaging and clinical endpoints.

**Analytical estimate -- Data strength for supporting regulatory approval: moderate-to-strong for Japan's conditional pathway, weak for FDA/EMA.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Japan's Regenerative Medicine framework explicitly allows conditional approval based on smaller datasets with post-marketing confirmatory requirements. The safety profile is clean (primary objective met), the PET imaging provides objective biological evidence (not subjective patient-reported outcomes), and the dose-response relationship demonstrates internal consistency. However, N=7 open-label with no control arm would be insufficient for FDA or EMA full approval. The US program (UCSD investigator-initiated trial + Sumitomo company-sponsored study) will need to generate randomized controlled data for global regulatory success.

The dyskinesia signal (116.4% increase) is the primary risk factor. Historical fetal tissue transplant trials (Freed et al., 2001; Olanow et al., 2003) showed graft-induced dyskinesia as a major complication that ultimately derailed that approach for decades. The Kyoto investigators attribute the dyskinesia to maintained levodopa dosing rather than graft pathology, which is plausible given it occurred only during ON periods. But regulators and clinicians will scrutinize this closely. If [[raguneprocel]] is approved, the post-marketing protocol will almost certainly require levodopa dose-adjustment algorithms and long-term dyskinesia monitoring. This becomes a commercial execution challenge -- neurosurgeons transplanting cells must coordinate with movement disorder neurologists adjusting medications, requiring a multidisciplinary clinical pathway.
