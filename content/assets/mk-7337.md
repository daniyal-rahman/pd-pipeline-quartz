# MK-7337

## Summary

[11C]MK-7337 is Merck's (big pharma, MRK) first-generation alpha-synuclein PET tracer, and the first radioligand to demonstrate signal in the substantia nigra of idiopathic Parkinson's patients. First-in-human data presented at AD/PD 2025 showed uptake in 6/8 PD patients in the substantia nigra and 7/8 in the brainstem, with no signal in healthy controls. However, MK-7337 is discontinued due to off-target cortical/thalamic/cerebellar binding and the requirement for carbon-11 labeling (short half-life, requires on-site cyclotron). Merck is advancing the successor compound MK-0947, which uses fluorine-18 labeling and eliminates the off-target binding issues. If MK-0947 validates, it becomes a critical enabling technology for the entire alpha-synuclein therapeutic ecosystem -- patient selection for [[prasinezumab]] Phase 3, target engagement measurement for [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]], and eventually commercial PD diagnosis. If alpha-synuclein PET tracers fail broadly, clinical trials remain reliant on insensitive clinical endpoints and CSF-based assays, perpetuating the heterogeneity problem that has sunk multiple alpha-synuclein programs.

## Notes

### Science
- High-affinity radioligand with Kd = 0.4 nM and Ki = 1 nM for alpha-synuclein aggregates -- within the ideal range for PET ligands (sub-nanomolar binding required)
- Selective for aggregated alpha-synuclein fibrils: no binding to monomeric alpha-synuclein, no affinity for amyloid-beta, and only weak affinity for neurofibrillary tangles (tau)
- Discovered through high-throughput screening and optimized for affinity, physicochemical properties, transporter selectivity, and discrimination against other proteinopathies
- Preclinical validation in A30P alpha-synuclein-overexpressing mouse models and non-human primate PET imaging confirmed brain penetration and clearance
- Critical limitation: substantial off-target binding in cortex, thalamus, and cerebellum in both NHP and humans -- hypothesized to reflect phospholipid binding due to the compound's lipophilicity
- Carbon-11 labeling requires an on-site cyclotron (20-minute half-life), severely limiting clinical scalability. This is why MK-0947 was developed with fluorine-18 compatibility (110-minute half-life, can be shipped regionally)
- Key scientific question for the field: alpha-synuclein aggregates in PD are far less dense than amyloid-beta plaques in Alzheimer's, making PET detection inherently harder. The signal-to-noise challenge explains why alpha-synuclein PET tracers have lagged tau and amyloid PET by a decade
- Developed in collaboration with the Michael J. Fox Foundation (MJFF), which has funded multiple competing alpha-synuclein PET tracer programs through the Ken Griffin Alpha-Synuclein Imaging Competition

### Clinical

**First-in-Human PET Study (Phase 1)** | NCT TBD | N=12 (8 PD, 4 controls) | Mild-to-moderate idiopathic PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Feasibility of alpha-synuclein PET imaging in idiopathic PD → Demonstrated signal in substantia nigra and brainstem
- **Key findings:** 6/8 PD patients showed substantia nigra uptake; 7/8 showed brainstem signal; 0/4 healthy controls showed signal. All six patients with substantia nigra retention had anosmia/hyposmia. Two PD patients with preserved olfaction showed no substantia nigra binding. Signal was asymmetric (lateralized), consistent with known PD pathology patterns. Smell-impaired patients also showed olfactory epithelium signal.
- **Enrollment criteria:** 3+ years PD diagnosis, ages 63-84, positive CSF alpha-synuclein seed amplification assay. Controls aged 61-76.
- **Status:** Completed (presented AD/PD 2025, Vienna)
- **Interpretation:** Proof-of-concept that alpha-synuclein fibrils can be visualized in living PD patients. The anosmia correlation is biologically compelling -- olfactory dysfunction correlates with alpha-synuclein Braak staging. However, off-target cortical/thalamic/cerebellar binding limits clinical utility and prevents this specific compound from advancing.

**Lead researcher:** Robert E. Drolet, PhD (Merck senior principal scientist); Helen Mitchell (presenter at ACS); Jason Uslaner, PhD (Merck VP overseeing program).

### Financial
- Internal Merck program -- no external deal economics. Development supported by MJFF grant funding
- Merck is a $275B+ market cap company; alpha-synuclein PET tracer is a minor pipeline item relative to their oncology/vaccine franchises
- Alpha-synuclein PET tracer market potential is significant but indirect: the commercial value is as a companion diagnostic enabling patient selection for alpha-synuclein-targeted therapeutics (a combined >$5B market if any disease-modifying therapy succeeds) and as a stand-alone diagnostic tool for PD, MSA, and DLB
- PET tracer market comparator: amyloid PET tracers (florbetapir/Amyvid, florbetaben/Neuraceq, flutemetamol/Vizamyl) generated ~$150-300M annually at peak but were transformative for Alzheimer's trial design and FDA approval of aducanumab/lecanemab
- Merck's decision to develop MK-0947 rather than abandon the program signals continued commitment to the alpha-synuclein imaging space despite MK-7337's limitations

## Analysis

MK-7337 is a proof-of-concept compound, not a clinical product. Its importance lies in what it proved: alpha-synuclein fibrils can be visualized in the brains of living idiopathic PD patients using PET imaging. Before this, alpha-synuclein PET was considered potentially impossible at PD-level aggregate densities (orders of magnitude lower than amyloid plaques in Alzheimer's). MK-7337 broke that barrier.

The anosmia correlation in the first-in-human data is notable. All six patients with substantia nigra signal had lost their sense of smell, while the two without signal had preserved olfaction. This maps onto Braak staging of alpha-synuclein pathology (olfactory bulb involvement is an early stage marker) and suggests the tracer may be detecting biologically meaningful pathology rather than nonspecific binding in the regions where signal was observed. The lateralized (asymmetric) signal pattern further supports specificity, since PD pathology is characteristically asymmetric.

**Analytical estimate -- probability that Merck delivers a validated alpha-synuclein PET tracer (via MK-0947) by 2027: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: MK-7337 proved target engagement is possible at PD-level aggregate densities (base +30%). MK-0947 addresses both known MK-7337 limitations (off-target binding, carbon-11 labeling). Merck has deep radiochemistry capabilities and MJFF collaboration infrastructure. Adjustments downward: alpha-synuclein PET tracer development has a high attrition rate across the field (-15%), and MK-0947 is untested in humans (-10%). Net: 40-50%.

The strategic question is whether Merck will commercialize MK-0947 as a stand-alone diagnostic product or license it to a specialized PET radiopharmaceutical company. Merck's core business is therapeutics, not imaging agents. The amyloid PET precedent (Lilly/Avid, GE/Piramal, Lantheus) suggests that big pharma tends to spin out or license PET tracers rather than commercialize internally. However, Merck may retain the tracer for internal trial use -- enabling their own alpha-synuclein therapeutic programs (if any) or as a bargaining chip for partnerships. The broader ecosystem impact is clear regardless of Merck's commercial strategy: a validated alpha-synuclein PET tracer transforms trial design for [[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[ly3962681|LY3962681]], and every other alpha-synuclein program by enabling patient stratification, target engagement measurement, and response monitoring. This is the "all-weather" investment thesis identified in the PD landscape analysis -- diagnostics win regardless of which therapeutic modality succeeds.
