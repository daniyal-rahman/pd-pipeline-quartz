# UCB7853

## Summary

UCB7853 is an anti-alpha-synuclein monoclonal antibody targeting extracellular pathological alpha-synuclein, developed by UCB (big pharma, Euronext: UCB.BR) with a Novartis (big pharma) opt-in agreement for co-development. Phase 1 (NCT04651153) completed July 2023 with safety/PK data presented at AD/PD 2025 in Vienna -- specific results remain undisclosed beyond conference abstracts. The key near-term catalyst is whether Novartis exercises its opt-in right: if Novartis opts in, the $1.5B deal framework funds Phase 2 jointly and signals big pharma conviction in extracellular alpha-synuclein clearance despite [[minzasolmin]]'s Phase 2 ORCHESTRA failure in the same partnership. If Novartis declines, UCB would need to advance alone, and the program's future becomes uncertain given UCB's pivot away from [[minzasolmin]] and the broader antibody failure track record in the alpha-synuclein space.

## Notes

### Science
- Anti-alpha-synuclein monoclonal antibody designed to prevent extracellular alpha-synuclein spread by binding pathological alpha-synuclein species and flagging them for immune clearance
- Mechanism is passive immunotherapy: exogenous antibody binds misfolded/aggregated alpha-synuclein in the extracellular space, blocking prion-like cell-to-cell transmission and promoting microglial clearance of toxic species
- The specific epitope targeted by UCB7853 has not been publicly disclosed, unlike [[prasinezumab]] (C-terminus) or the failed [[cinpanemab]] (N-terminus, aa 1-10) -- this opacity makes mechanistic differentiation difficult to assess
- UCB positions UCB7853 as complementary to [[minzasolmin]] (intracellular misfolding inhibitor): UCB7853 targets extracellular spread while minzasolmin targeted intracellular aggregation. With minzasolmin's ORCHESTRA Phase 2 failure, the "complementary" framing is now moot -- UCB7853 stands alone
- Core scientific question shared with all alpha-synuclein antibodies: standard mAb CNS penetration is ~0.1-0.2% of serum levels. Whether sufficient antibody reaches the brain parenchyma to meaningfully block extracellular transmission remains unproven for this class
- Competes in a crowded antibody modality space where no anti-alpha-synuclein antibody has met a primary clinical endpoint to date

### Clinical

**UP0092 (Phase 1)** | NCT04651153 | N=62 | Healthy males (single ascending dose) + PD patients (multiple ascending dose)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of single ascending doses in healthy males and multiple ascending doses in PD patients
- **Key secondary:** Pharmacokinetics (Cmax, AUC of UCB7853 in serum after IV infusion)
- **Status:** Completed July 2023. Enrollment was briefly suspended in June 2021 for "further evaluation" before resuming. Results presented at AD/PD 2025 (Vienna, April 2025) as poster/oral presentations
- **Sites:** London, UK and Leiden, Netherlands
- **Interpretation:** Phase 1 data appears supportive of continued development given UCB presented both preclinical evaluation and Phase 1 results at AD/PD 2025, but no detailed efficacy or safety data has been publicly disclosed beyond conference abstracts. The June 2021 enrollment suspension raises a minor flag, though resumption and completion suggest the issue was resolved

No Phase 2 trials have been initiated. Progression depends on Novartis opt-in decision.

### Financial
- **Deal economics:** The $150M upfront payment covered both minzasolmin (UCB0599) and the UCB7853 opt-in right. With minzasolmin terminated after ORCHESTRA failure, the remaining deal value is concentrated on UCB7853 -- but Novartis has not publicly confirmed whether the opt-in has been exercised
- **UCB profile:** UCB is a ~EUR 48B market cap biopharma company (Euronext Brussels: UCB.BR) with >EUR 7.6B in 2025 revenue guidance. Parkinson's is one focus within their broader neurology franchise. UCB7853 is not a material asset for UCB's current valuation
- **Novartis context:** Novartis separately paid $2.2B ($200M upfront) for [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (Arrowhead, siRNA alpha-synuclein production inhibitor) in September 2024 -- a much larger commitment to a different modality targeting the same protein. This raises the question of whether Novartis views UCB7853 as redundant or complementary to ARO-SNCA
- **Commercial split if approved:** UCB leads in Europe and Japan; Novartis leads in US and rest of world
- **Deal comparison:** The $150M upfront for two assets (one Phase 2, one Phase 1) contrasts with Novartis paying $200M upfront for ARO-SNCA alone at preclinical stage -- reflecting the premium Novartis placed on Arrowhead's TRiM delivery platform vs. a conventional antibody approach

- UCB7853 enters the most crowded target space in PD therapeutics. [[prasinezumab]] (Roche/Prothena) is the alpha-synuclein antibody class leader, now in Phase 3 (PARAISO) after mixed but encouraging Phase 2b (PADOVA) results -- UCB7853 is several years behind
- The antibody modality for alpha-synuclein has a 0/6 track record on primary endpoints. [[cinpanemab]] (Biogen) was terminated after Phase 2 SPARK failure. UCB7853 would need to demonstrate a differentiated mechanism or epitope to justify continued development in this context
- [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (siRNA, Novartis/Arrowhead) targets alpha-synuclein at the production level rather than clearance -- and is backed by the same partner (Novartis) with a much larger financial commitment. If Novartis views production inhibition as the superior strategy, their enthusiasm for UCB7853 may be limited
- Brain-shuttled antibodies represent a next-generation approach -- Roche's trontinemab uses a transferrin receptor shuttle to achieve ~20x higher brain penetration than standard mAbs. If UCB7853 uses conventional CNS penetration (~0.1-0.2%), it faces a generational disadvantage
- If [[prasinezumab]]'s PARAISO Phase 3 succeeds, it validates the extracellular antibody approach and UCB7853 could benefit as a follow-on. If PARAISO fails, the entire antibody modality for alpha-synuclein is likely exhausted, and UCB7853's path forward would be untenable

## Analysis

UCB7853 is a Phase 1 anti-alpha-synuclein antibody in a field where the antibody modality has produced zero primary endpoint successes across six programs. The asset's most important feature is not its biology -- which remains largely undisclosed -- but its deal structure: Novartis holds an opt-in right contingent on Phase 1 completion, and that decision will reveal big pharma's internal assessment of the data.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of clinical success (approval): 5-8%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for Phase 1 neurology assets reaching approval is ~8-10%. Adjustments downward: 0/6 alpha-synuclein antibody primary endpoint track record (-3%), undisclosed epitope/mechanism prevents assessment of differentiation (-2%), partner (Novartis) has already made a much larger bet on a competing modality in the same target space via [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (-2%). Adjustments upward: UCB presented data at AD/PD 2025 suggesting continued interest (+1%), large deal framework provides funding runway if Novartis opts in (+1%).

The signal to watch is the Novartis opt-in decision. If Novartis exercises the option despite having ARO-SNCA in-house, it signals they believe a multi-modal alpha-synuclein strategy (production inhibition + extracellular clearance) has value -- a scientifically rational "belt and suspenders" approach. If Novartis declines, it confirms the field's consensus that antibody approaches to alpha-synuclein are being superseded by gene silencing. The [[minzasolmin]] ORCHESTRA failure already cost the partnership its lead asset; whether Novartis doubles down on the backup or walks away will be informative for the entire alpha-synuclein competitive landscape.

UCB7853's ultimate fate is also coupled to [[prasinezumab]]'s PARAISO Phase 3 readout (2027-2028). A positive PARAISO result would revitalize interest in anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies broadly and could accelerate UCB7853 into Phase 2 with a validated endpoint and enrichment strategy. A negative PARAISO result would effectively close the antibody modality chapter for alpha-synuclein, redirecting capital toward [[aro-snca|gene silencing]] and [[pariceract|genetic PD targets]].
