# ACT-02

## Summary

ACT-02 is a first-in-class oral prolyl endopeptidase (PREP) inhibitor from Accure Therapeutics (startup, Barcelona) targeting alpha-synuclein aggregation via a novel enzyme target -- distinct from the direct alpha-syn binding approaches that have struggled clinically ([[minzasolmin]] terminated, [[emrusolmin]] unproven in PD). GLP toxicology and safety pharmacology are complete, with IND filing targeted for 2026, funded by a $1.12M MJFF grant (project MJFF-025939) plus a separate EUR 0.48M Luxembourg FNR grant for iPSC-based precision medicine work. The dual mechanism -- blocking PREP-mediated alpha-syn nucleation AND enhancing autophagic clearance -- is mechanistically differentiated from every other alpha-synuclein small molecule in the pipeline. If IND-enabling studies confirm a clean safety profile and the Phase 1 proceeds on schedule, ACT-02 would be the first PREP inhibitor to enter PD clinical trials. If preclinical-to-clinical translation fails (a common outcome for novel enzyme targets), the program stalls at a stage where Accure's limited capital makes recovery difficult without a licensing partner.

## Notes

### Science
- PREP (prolyl endopeptidase / prolyl oligopeptidase) is a serine protease that directly binds alpha-synuclein and accelerates its dimerization, serving as a nucleation point for aggregation -- importantly, this aggregation-promoting activity is independent of PREP's enzymatic (proteolytic) function
- PREP inhibition operates via dual mechanism: (1) conformational shift of PREP to a compact monomeric form that cannot nucleate alpha-syn aggregation, and (2) upregulation of beclin-1 and enhanced autophagy, increasing clearance of existing alpha-syn aggregates
- Preclinical efficacy demonstrated in Thy1-aSyn ("line 61") transgenic mice: neuroprotection of dopaminergic neurons, reduced alpha-synuclein aggregation, reduced neuroinflammation, improved mitochondrial function, and enhanced motor and cognitive performance
- Key differentiator vs. [[minzasolmin]] (terminated): minzasolmin targeted membrane-bound alpha-syn oligomers directly; ACT-02 targets an upstream enzyme that promotes aggregation. This is mechanistically distinct -- targeting the catalyst rather than the substrate
- Key differentiator vs. [[emrusolmin]]: emrusolmin binds inside alpha-syn fibril cavities to block oligomer formation; ACT-02 prevents the initial nucleation step by removing the PREP scaffold. Different intervention points in the aggregation cascade
- Key differentiator vs. [[mor-a-syn|Morphomer alpha-synuclein]]: Morphomer targets intracellular alpha-syn fibrils directly; ACT-02 targets the enzymatic machinery upstream of fibril formation
- Prior PREP inhibitor research (KYP-2047, academic tool compound) showed reduced alpha-syn oligomers and increased striatal dopamine levels in multiple PD models, but KYP-2047 was never drug-optimized for clinical development -- ACT-02 represents the first advanced, drug-like PREP inhibitor designed for IND
- Open question: PREP is widely expressed and has physiological roles in neuropeptide processing (substance P, TRH, vasopressin) -- whether chronic PREP inhibition produces off-target effects from blocking normal peptide metabolism remains to be established in longer-term studies
- Oral bioavailability and brain penetration reported (consistent with PREP being an intracellular CNS target), though specific PK parameters have not been publicly disclosed

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. IND-enabling studies are underway with GLP toxicology and safety pharmacology completed. Key preclinical milestones:

- **GLP Toxicology:** Completed (as of April 2025 press release). Results described as part of "comprehensive positive" preclinical data set
- **Safety Pharmacology:** Completed
- **Efficacy (Thy1-aSyn mice):** Neuroprotection, reduced alpha-syn aggregation, reduced neuroinflammation, improved mitochondrial function, enhanced motor/cognitive performance
- **iPSC Validation (planned):** EUR 0.48M FNR-funded Transcend-PD project with Luxembourg Centre for Systems Biomedicine (LCSB) to test ACT-02 in patient-derived iPSC models and develop precision medicine strategies
- **IND filing target:** 2026, with Phase 1 expected to follow

- ACT-02 is the **only PREP inhibitor** in the PD pipeline -- no direct target competitors. The competitive question is whether an upstream enzyme inhibitor can outperform direct alpha-syn binders that have larger datasets but mixed results
- Vs. [[prasinezumab]] (Phase 3, antibody): entirely different modality and mechanism. Prasinezumab clears extracellular aggregates; ACT-02 prevents intracellular aggregation formation. Theoretically complementary rather than competitive, though a prasinezumab PARAISO success would validate the alpha-syn target broadly and benefit ACT-02's thesis
- Vs. [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (Phase 1, siRNA) and [[ly3962681|LY3962681]] (Phase 1, ASO): gene silencing reduces all alpha-syn production at the mRNA level. ACT-02 is more targeted -- it blocks PREP-mediated aggregation while preserving physiological alpha-syn function, which could be an advantage if total alpha-syn knockdown produces safety issues
- Vs. [[buntanetap]] (Phase 3, translational inhibitor): buntanetap reduces alpha-syn production via IRE-mediated translational inhibition but has significant credibility challenges. ACT-02's mechanism is more targeted and scientifically cleaner
- Vs. [[emrusolmin]] (Phase 1b, small molecule): closest modality competitor. Both are oral small molecules targeting alpha-syn aggregation, but via different mechanisms. Emrusolmin binds fibrils directly; ACT-02 targets the PREP enzyme upstream. If emrusolmin's TOPAS-MSA Phase 2 fails, it could cast a shadow over all small-molecule alpha-syn approaches -- or alternatively highlight the need for mechanistically differentiated approaches like PREP inhibition
- The [[minzasolmin]] ORCHESTRA failure (December 2024) is both a headwind and an opportunity: it killed the most advanced small-molecule alpha-syn competitor but also raised questions about whether any small molecule can modify alpha-syn pathology. ACT-02's differentiated mechanism (enzyme target vs. direct alpha-syn binding) is the key argument for why the minzasolmin failure does not apply

## Analysis

ACT-02 occupies a unique niche in the alpha-synuclein landscape: a first-in-class mechanism (PREP inhibition) with strong preclinical rationale but no clinical proof-of-concept. The dual mechanism -- blocking aggregation nucleation AND enhancing autophagic clearance -- is the most scientifically compelling feature, as it addresses both production and clearance of pathological alpha-syn species. The Thy1-aSyn mouse data showing neuroprotection, reduced aggregation, and improved motor/cognitive function is encouraging but standard for PD preclinical programs that subsequently fail in clinical translation.

**Analytical estimate -- probability of reaching Phase 2: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for IND-enabling to Phase 1 initiation is ~60-70% for funded programs with completed GLP tox. Adjustments upward: MJFF grant provides external scientific validation (+5%), completed GLP tox removes a major derisking hurdle (+5%), oral route and brain penetration confirmed (+5%). Adjustments downward: small startup with limited capital (-10%), no disclosed partner for clinical development (-5%), novel target with no clinical precedent in PD (-10%). Net: ~40-50% to reach Phase 2 (which requires Phase 1 success plus additional capital raise or licensing deal).

**Analytical estimate -- probability of ultimate clinical success if it reaches Phase 2: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for novel-target small molecules in PD Phase 2 is ~10-15%. The PREP mechanism has strong preclinical data but zero clinical validation, and the alpha-synuclein aggregation field has a near-100% clinical failure rate for approaches that showed preclinical efficacy. No adjustments materially change the base rate at this stage.

**Signal analysis:** The MJFF grant is the most important external signal. MJFF is the single largest non-governmental funder of PD research and their grants undergo rigorous scientific review. The $1.12M award for IND-enabling work signals that MJFF's scientific advisors believe PREP inhibition is a credible disease-modification strategy worth testing clinically. The separate FNR grant for iPSC validation adds a precision-medicine dimension that could enable patient stratification in future trials -- a lesson learned from [[minzasolmin]]'s failure to enrich for likely responders. Accure's successful licensing of ACT-01 to Oculis (NASDAQ: OCS) demonstrates the team's ability to advance assets to clinical stage and execute licensing deals, which is the most likely path for ACT-02 given Accure's startup scale. The key strategic question is whether Accure can generate enough Phase 1 data to attract a pharma partner before running out of capital. The 2026 IND filing timeline means Phase 1 data would likely be available in 2027-2028, placing ACT-02 in a competitive licensing window alongside readouts from [[prasinezumab]]'s PARAISO (Phase 3) and [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s ongoing trials -- a PARAISO success would make the alpha-syn space hot and increase partner interest in differentiated approaches like PREP inhibition.
