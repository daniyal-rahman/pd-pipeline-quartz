# LY3962681

## Summary

LY3962681 is Eli Lilly's (big pharma, LLY) internally developed intrathecal siRNA targeting SNCA mRNA, currently in Phase 1 (PROSPECT trial, NCT06565195) with estimated completion in 2029. Preclinical data shows dose-dependent SNCA mRNA and alpha-synuclein protein suppression in substantia nigra and dorsal striatum, plus NHP CSF alpha-synuclein reduction — mechanistically aligned with [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (production inhibition) but burdened by intrathecal delivery requiring lumbar puncture. Biogen's discontinuation of [[ion464|ION464]] (also intrathecal, also alpha-synuclein knockdown) in February 2025 despite clean safety is the most relevant precedent. If PROSPECT demonstrates meaningful CSF alpha-synuclein reduction with acceptable tolerability, LY3962681 advances but remains structurally disadvantaged vs. [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s subcutaneous route for chronic dosing. If PROSPECT fails or shows insufficient knockdown, it reinforces the market verdict that intrathecal delivery is nonviable for PD and strengthens [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]'s competitive position.

## Notes

### Science
- siRNA that binds SNCA mRNA and triggers degradation via RISC complex, reducing alpha-synuclein protein production upstream of aggregation — same production-inhibition thesis as [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] but different delivery platform
- Intrathecal injection delivers drug directly into CSF, bypassing the BBB entirely — guarantees CNS exposure but limits distribution to CSF-bathed surfaces rather than deep parenchymal penetration
- Preclinical (NHP): dose-dependent, sustained suppression of SNCA mRNA and alpha-synuclein protein in substantia nigra and dorsal striatum; alpha-synuclein reduction confirmed in NHP CSF
- GLP toxicology: intrathecal administration well tolerated with no notable adverse clinical or pathological findings
- Key scientific distinction vs. [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]: Arrowhead's TRiM platform claims subcutaneous delivery to deep brain regions via receptor-mediated transcytosis; LY3962681 relies on intrathecal CSF diffusion, which has historically shown a gradient — high concentration near injection site, diminishing in deeper structures
- Genetic validation is strong: SNCA gene dosage is directly causal (duplications cause late-onset PD; triplications cause early-onset aggressive PD), supporting the production-inhibition rationale
- Open question: whether CSF-delivered siRNA achieves sufficient knockdown in the substantia nigra (the critical degenerating region) vs. cortical/superficial structures that are more CSF-accessible
- The synucleinopenia concern applies equally here: if functional monomeric alpha-synuclein is neuroprotective, chronic knockdown could theoretically worsen outcomes; alpha-synuclein knockout mice show subtle cognitive deficits at 100% loss, but programs target 50-80% knockdown

### Clinical

**PROSPECT (Phase 1)** | NCT06565195 | N=108 | Healthy volunteers (SAD) + PD patients (MAD)
- **Design:** Two-part, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; SAD in ~48 healthy volunteers, MAD in ~60 PD patients; 3:1 randomization (active:placebo); up to five dose levels tested sequentially
- **Dosing:** Intrathecal injection (lumbar puncture); SAD = single dose; MAD = two doses 12-24 weeks apart
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, PK/PD; effect on CSF alpha-synuclein levels
- **Follow-up:** 52 weeks post-dose (SAD), 52 weeks post-Dose 2 (MAD); optional extended monitoring for additional 24 weeks
- **Locations:** United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Netherlands, Japan
- **Status:** Recruiting (as of May 2025); estimated completion 2029
- **Interpretation:** Standard SAD/MAD dose-finding design. The MAD dosing interval of 12-24 weeks implies Lilly expects prolonged duration of action from a single intrathecal dose — critical for commercial viability if the drug requires only 2-4 lumbar punctures per year rather than monthly

### Financial
- LY3962681 is an internally developed program within Prevail Therapeutics, a Lilly subsidiary acquired in January 2021 for ~$1.04B ($880M upfront + $160M CVR)
- No external partnership on this specific asset; Lilly bears full development cost and risk
- Part of Lilly's broader PD portfolio: Prevail/PR001 (GBA1 gene therapy, ~$1B acquisition), Capsida partnership (AAV platform, $665M+), Ventyx/VTX3232 (NLRP3 inhibitor, $1.2B acquisition), ABL Bio Grabody-B license ($2.6B for BBB shuttle platform)
- Total Lilly PD/neurodegeneration investment exceeds $5B across these deals — signals strategic commitment, not a single speculative bet
- No peak sales estimates available for this early-stage asset; commercial viability depends on solving the intrathecal compliance problem
- Comparison: Novartis paid $200M upfront ($2.2B total) for [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] at preclinical stage — the premium reflects subcutaneous delivery advantage. Lilly's internal development avoids premium deal economics but does not solve the delivery disadvantage

## Analysis

LY3962681 has sound science attached to a problematic delivery route. The production-inhibition thesis — reducing alpha-synuclein at the mRNA level before aggregation occurs — is the most genetically supported approach in the PD field, backed by Mendelian SNCA dosage causation. The preclinical package (NHP SNCA knockdown in substantia nigra and dorsal striatum, clean GLP tox) is adequate for Phase 1 entry. The problem is not the drug; it is the needle in the spine.

**Analytical estimate — probability of advancing to Phase 2 with positive target engagement: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for Phase 1 safety/tolerability in intrathecal programs is ~70% (antisense and siRNA drugs generally tolerable); adjustment downward for the [[ion464|ION464]] precedent suggesting intrathecal alpha-synuclein knockdown may face unexpected challenges (-15%); adjustment downward for the long dosing interval (12-24 weeks between doses) which may not sustain adequate knockdown (-10%); adjustment upward for Lilly's clean GLP tox data and NHP CSF engagement (+5%).

**Analytical estimate — probability of eventual approval as intrathecal product: 5-10%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: even if the drug works, chronic intrathecal delivery in a population of elderly PD patients creates insurmountable compliance and safety barriers at commercial scale. Lumbar puncture every 3-6 months for decades is a burden that severely limits the addressable market. Nusinersen (Spinraza) proved intrathecal dosing can work commercially in SMA, but SMA is a rare pediatric disease with no alternative disease-modifying therapies — PD is a common geriatric disease where oral and subcutaneous competitors will exist.

The strategic read on Lilly's behavior is nuanced. They are not betting the franchise on intrathecal delivery. LY3962681 sits within a diversified PD portfolio (PR001 for GBA-PD, VTX3232 for neuroinflammation, Grabody-B for BBB shuttling of biologics). The Grabody-B deal with ABL Bio ($2.6B) is particularly telling — Lilly is building a BBB shuttle platform that could eventually deliver siRNA subcutaneously or IV, making intrathecal delivery unnecessary. LY3962681 may function as a proof-of-concept for alpha-synuclein knockdown while Lilly develops a more commercially viable delivery route. If PROSPECT demonstrates that reducing CSF alpha-synuclein is safe and correlates with biomarker improvement, that pharmacological validation is worth pursuing even if the intrathecal route is ultimately replaced.

The [[ion464|ION464]] discontinuation is the critical unknown. If Biogen saw adequate SNCA knockdown but no downstream biomarker benefit, it implies that production inhibition is insufficient regardless of delivery — devastating for LY3962681 and [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] alike. If Biogen saw insufficient knockdown due to intrathecal biodistribution limitations, LY3962681 faces the same problem unless its siRNA chemistry achieves better tissue penetration than Ionis's ASO. The market has defaulted to the delivery-failure interpretation, which is the favorable read for [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] and neutral-to-negative for LY3962681.
