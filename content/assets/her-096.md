# HER-096

## Summary

HER-096 is a first-in-class subcutaneously administered CDNF mimetic peptide from Herantis Pharma (biotech, HRTIS on Nasdaq First North Finland) that completed Phase 1b with all primary and secondary endpoints met, demonstrating safety, brain penetration, and biomarker evidence of biological activity across proteostasis, mitochondrial function, and neuroinflammation pathways. The program is unpartnered and the company is small-cap (~EUR 50M), making it an early-stage opportunity with high binary risk. Phase 2 is planned for Q2 2026 with 300 mg twice-weekly dosing in early PD patients. If Phase 2 shows motor or biomarker efficacy, HER-096 would validate a novel neurotrophic mechanism distinct from all other PD disease-modification approaches (anti-synuclein antibodies like [[prasinezumab]], gene silencing like [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], kinase inhibitors like [[biib122|BIIB122]]). If Phase 2 fails, the neurotrophic factor thesis for PD -- which has a poor track record from GDNF/neurturin failures -- remains unvalidated.

## Notes

### Science
- HER-096 is a small peptidomimetic derived from the active site of cerebral dopamine neurotrophic factor (CDNF), an endogenous ER-resident protein that promotes dopaminergic neuron survival
- Mechanism is multimodal: (1) modulates the Unfolded Protein Response (UPR) pathway via interaction with ER sensors PERK and IRE1-alpha and the master regulator GRP78, (2) reduces alpha-synuclein aggregation, (3) dampens neuroinflammation
- Key advantage over prior neurotrophic factor approaches (GDNF, neurturin): HER-096 crosses the blood-brain barrier after subcutaneous injection, reaching pharmacologically active CSF and brain interstitial fluid concentrations -- eliminating the need for intracranial surgery or convection-enhanced delivery that plagued GDNF trials
- Extended CSF and brain ISF half-life compared to plasma, enabling twice-weekly SC dosing
- Preclinical data published in Cell Chemical Biology (2024): subcutaneous HER-096 protected dopamine neurons, reduced alpha-synuclein aggregates, and modulated UPR pathway activity in aged synucleinopathy mice
- CDNF and its relative MANF form a structurally distinct neurotrophic factor family -- unlike classical neurotrophic factors (GDNF, BDNF), they primarily act intracellularly in the ER lumen rather than through surface receptor signaling
- Open question: whether UPR modulation can produce clinically meaningful disease modification given the multifactorial nature of PD neurodegeneration

### Clinical

**HER-096 Phase 1b** | NCT06659562 | N=36 (12 healthy volunteers + 24 PD patients) | Early-to-moderate PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of repeated SC doses (200 mg and 300 mg twice weekly x 4 weeks) → All primary and secondary endpoints met. Adverse events mainly mild, transient injection site reactions
- **Key secondary:** Pharmacokinetics and CSF penetration → Both dose levels achieved sufficient CSF concentrations for expected pharmacological activity, confirming BBB penetration in PD patients
- **Biomarker data (Jan 2026):** Analysis of >2.5 million datapoints showed clear biological response -- HER-096 exposure associated with changes in proteostasis, mitochondrial function, and neuroinflammation pathways, consistent with expected mechanism of action
- **Motor outcomes:** MDS-UPDRS remained stable in both active and placebo groups (trial not powered for efficacy)
- **Status:** Completed (last patient visit August 2025; topline October 2025; biomarker data January 2026)
- **Interpretation:** Clean safety profile and confirmed brain penetration are the key de-risking milestones. Biomarker evidence of target engagement across multiple pathways is encouraging but short treatment duration (4 weeks) limits conclusions about disease modification. 300 mg twice-weekly selected for Phase 2.

**Phase 2 (planned)** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | Early-stage PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Expected to include efficacy endpoints (likely motor and/or biomarker-based)
- **Status:** Planned for Q2 2026 initiation; 6-month GLP toxicology study completed November 2025 to support longer dosing
- **Interpretation:** Pivotal proof-of-concept study. Will determine whether UPR/CDNF pathway modulation translates to clinical benefit.

- HER-096 occupies a unique niche: the only clinical-stage CDNF-based program and the only neurotrophic factor approach with subcutaneous delivery (bypassing the intracranial delivery limitation that undermined GDNF trials)
- Historical context: GDNF (Amgen/Medgenics) failed in multiple Phase 2 trials despite promising preclinical data -- primary issues were delivery (intracranial catheter complications) and inadequate tissue distribution, not necessarily target biology. HER-096's SC delivery could address these failures
- Neurturin (CERE-120, Voyager/formerly Ceregene) also failed in Phase 2 -- AAV-delivered NRTN did not reach target tissue sufficiently. Same delivery-versus-biology question
- Orthogonal to the dominant PD disease-modification approaches: does not compete directly with alpha-synuclein targeting ([[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]), LRRK2 inhibition ([[biib122|BIIB122]]), or GBA1 activation ([[pariceract]])
- If HER-096 Phase 2 succeeds, it validates a completely novel mechanism and could become a combination therapy candidate alongside anti-synuclein or genetic approaches
- If Phase 2 fails, it further dims prospects for the neurotrophic factor field in PD, though the distinct mechanism (UPR modulation vs. classical receptor signaling) means it would not definitively close the door on CDNF biology

## Analysis

HER-096 represents the most credible attempt to date to translate the neurotrophic factor hypothesis into a viable PD therapy. The critical insight is that prior GDNF/neurturin failures were primarily delivery failures -- requiring intracranial surgery, suffering from poor tissue distribution, and creating compliance barriers for chronic dosing. HER-096's subcutaneous route of administration eliminates this bottleneck entirely. The Phase 1b confirmation that HER-096 achieves pharmacologically active CSF concentrations after SC injection is arguably the most important data point so far, as it proves the delivery problem is solved.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 2 success probability: 15-20%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: neurotrophic factors in PD are 0-for-6 in clinical trials (GDNF x4, neurturin x2) → starting point ~5%
- Adjustments upward: novel mechanism (UPR/ER stress, not classical receptor signaling) partially distinct from GDNF failures (+5%), solved delivery problem with SC administration (+10%), biomarker evidence of target engagement in Phase 1b (+5%), multimodal mechanism (proteostasis + anti-synuclein + anti-inflammation) provides multiple pathways to efficacy (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: 4-week treatment too short to conclude disease modification (-5%), micro-cap company with limited resources to run large trials (-3%), no partner validation (-2%)
- Net: ~15-20%

**Signal analysis:** The MJFF and Parkinson's Virtual Biotech funding is notable -- these foundations have sophisticated scientific advisory boards and fund selectively. The EIC Accelerator grant and equity investment add another layer of external validation. However, the absence of a pharma partner at this stage is a gap -- if the Phase 1b data were unambiguous, one would expect partnership interest. The company's explicit statement that it is "seeking pharma partnerships" suggests deal discussions may be underway but not closed. A Phase 2 partnership announcement would be a meaningful positive signal for the program's credibility and the company's financial runway.

The decision tree is clear: if Phase 2 shows efficacy signals on motor endpoints or validated biomarkers, HER-096 becomes a potential first-in-class disease-modifying therapy with a patient-friendly dosing route, likely triggering partnership interest and significant revaluation. If Phase 2 fails, the micro-cap company faces existential risk given its single-asset portfolio. For the broader field, a positive result would open an entirely new therapeutic axis for PD beyond the dominant alpha-synuclein and genetic targets.
