# IPX203 (CREXONT)

## Summary

IPX203 (CREXONT) is an FDA-approved (August 2024) extended-release oral carbidopa/levodopa capsule from Amneal Pharmaceuticals (biotech, AMRX) that uses a mucoadhesive polymer technology to sustain levodopa plasma levels longer than any other oral CD/LD formulation. The pivotal RISE-PD Phase 3 trial showed a statistically significant 0.53-hour increase in daily Good ON time vs. IR CD/LD, with IPX203 dosed 3 times/day vs. 5 times/day for IR -- a meaningful quality-of-life improvement through dosing simplification. Zambon (EU partner) submitted to the EMA in June 2025 and is running the Phase 3b ADIP trial in advanced PD across Europe. If European approval follows, CREXONT becomes a global standard-of-care upgrade for motor fluctuation management; if the EMA requires additional data, the ADIP trial provides a bridge but delays EU revenue by 12-18 months.

## Notes

### Science
- Oral capsule combining **immediate-release (IR) granules** of carbidopa and levodopa for rapid onset of action with **extended-release (ER) pellets** containing levodopa coated in a mucoadhesive polymer
- The mucoadhesive polymer keeps ER pellets adhered to the proximal small intestine -- the primary site of levodopa absorption -- extending the duration of therapeutic plasma levels beyond what Rytary (IPX066) or standard ER formulations achieve
- After a single dose, levodopa concentrations remain above 50% of peak for **4.6 hours** with IPX203 vs. **1.5 hours** with IR CD/LD (p<0.0001), representing a ~3x improvement in duration of therapeutic exposure
- This is a symptomatic therapy -- it does not modify disease progression. The target is optimizing dopamine replacement to reduce OFF time and motor fluctuations in patients already on levodopa
- Key differentiator vs. Rytary: PK data shows longer duration of levodopa plasma concentrations; PD data shows longer duration of motor effect; pharmacodynamic comparison showed 0.9 hours less daily OFF time with IPX203 vs. Rytary
- Open question: whether the mucoadhesive technology advantage holds across the full spectrum of GI motility impairment seen in advanced PD (gastroparesis, constipation) -- this is what the ADIP trial in advanced PD aims to address

### Clinical

**Phase 2 (PK/PD Study)** | NCT02814916 | N=28 | PD patients with motor fluctuations
- **Primary endpoint:** Pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics comparison vs. IR CD/LD (open-label, crossover)
- **Key secondary:** OFF time reduction: 19.3% vs. 33.5% of waking hours (IPX203 vs. IR CD/LD; p<0.0001), translating to 2.3 hours less OFF time, with 1.9 hours gained as Good ON time
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Established the PK/PD profile showing prolonged levodopa exposure and clinically meaningful reduction in OFF time; enabled Phase 3 progression

**RISE-PD (Phase 3)** | NCT03670953 | N=506 randomized (630 enrolled) | PD with motor fluctuations
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in daily Good ON time → **+0.53 hours** vs. IR CD/LD (95% CI 0.09-0.97; p=0.02), with IPX203 dosed 3x/day vs. 5x/day for IR
- **Key secondary:** Good ON time per dose increased by +1.55 hours (95% CI 1.37-1.73; p<0.001); OFF time reduced by -0.48 hours (p=0.025); PDSS-2 sleep quality improved (-2.35; p<0.0001); 29.7% vs. 18.8% reported "much improved" or "very much improved" on PGI-C
- **Safety:** Well tolerated. Most common AEs: nausea (4.3% vs. 0.8%), anxiety (2.7% vs. 0.0%)
- **Status:** Completed (November 2018 - June 2021); NDA filed August 2022; CRL received July 2023 (carbidopa PK data insufficient); resubmitted with additional healthy volunteer PK study; **FDA approved August 2024**
- **Interpretation:** The 0.53-hour absolute Good ON improvement is modest, but the real clinical value is the +1.55 hours Good ON time per dose with 40% fewer daily doses -- this is a compliance and quality-of-life story, not a raw efficacy story

**RISE-PD OLE** | Extension of NCT03670953 | Ongoing
- **Key finding:** Sustained efficacy of comparable magnitude to end of double-blind study; favorable long-term safety and tolerability profile
- **Status:** Completed; published December 2023
- **Interpretation:** No signal of efficacy waning or new safety concerns with chronic use

**RISE-PD Sleep Analysis (Post-hoc)** | Same trial | Presented AAN 2025
- **Key finding:** Statistically significant improvements across all PDSS-2 subdomains: disturbed sleep (-1.07; p<0.0001), nighttime motor symptoms (-0.62; p=0.006), PD symptoms at night (-0.65; p=0.002). More patients woke in ON state and fewer recorded OFF upon awakening
- **Interpretation:** Night-time benefit differentiates CREXONT from IR dosing regimens and could be a key commercial message

**ELEVATE-PD (Phase 4)** | NCT06765668 | N=~220 planned | Moderately severe PD with motor complications
- **Primary endpoint:** Real-world efficacy of switching to CREXONT from existing oral LD-based regimens
- **Interim results (N=55, Dec 2025):** Switching from IR CD/LD: +3.13 hr/day Good ON time; from IR + COMT inhibitor: +2.31 hr/day; from Rytary: +1.80 hr/day. OFF time decreased by -2.83, -2.36, and -2.57 hours respectively
- **Status:** Active, enrolling; full data expected 2026
- **Interpretation:** The interim switch data is compelling, particularly the +1.80 hr improvement even vs. Rytary. Open-label and small N, but if the full dataset holds, this is the head-to-head evidence CREXONT needs for formulary displacement

**ADIP (Phase 3b, Zambon)** | NCT TBD | N=92 planned | Advanced PD (Europe)
- **Primary endpoint:** Efficacy and safety of IPX203 vs. IR LD/CD in advanced PD
- **Design:** 25 weeks (1-week screening, 12-week treatment, optional 12-week extension); sites in Italy, Spain, Poland
- **Status:** First patient dosed December 2025
- **Interpretation:** Supports Zambon's EMA submission by generating European clinical data in a population (advanced PD) not specifically studied in RISE-PD; may also address the gastroparesis/GI motility question in sicker patients

- **Rytary (IPX066):** Amneal's own prior-generation extended-release CD/LD, approved 2015. CREXONT shows superior PK duration and ~0.9 hr/day less OFF time in pharmacodynamic comparisons. ELEVATE-PD interim data shows +1.80 hr/day Good ON improvement when switching from Rytary. Rytary's US patent expires 2028, creating a generic erosion timeline that CREXONT is positioned to capture
- **IR carbidopa/levodopa (Sinemet generics):** Still ~80% of the oral levodopa market by volume. RISE-PD demonstrated superiority, but the modest absolute Good ON time difference (0.53 hr) means CREXONT must sell on dosing convenience (3x vs. 5x/day) and per-dose efficacy (+1.55 hr)
- **Subcutaneous and infusion pumps (Duopa, foslevodopa/foscarbidopa):** Target advanced PD with severe motor fluctuations. CREXONT's oral route is a major advantage for the large population between "IR not working well" and "needs a pump" -- a commercially underserved segment
- **COMT inhibitors (opicapone, entacapone):** Often added to IR CD/LD to extend duration. ELEVATE-PD interim data suggests CREXONT monotherapy may replace IR + COMT inhibitor combinations, simplifying regimens
- CREXONT does not compete with disease-modifying candidates ([[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]); it is complementary -- patients on those trials still need optimized symptomatic therapy

## Analysis

CREXONT represents an incremental but meaningful advance in the most prescribed drug class in Parkinson's disease. The 0.53-hour improvement in daily Good ON time sounds modest in isolation, but the real value proposition is the per-dose story: +1.55 hours Good ON per dose with 40% fewer daily doses. For patients taking IR CD/LD 5 times/day who can switch to 3 times/day with better per-dose duration, this is a genuine quality-of-life improvement. The sleep quality data adds a differentiated benefit that IR regimens cannot match due to their short duration.

**Analytical estimate -- US peak sales probability of reaching $300M+: 55-60%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: Base rate for symptomatic PD oral therapies gaining significant share is moderate (~30%) given the dominance of low-cost generics. Adjustments upward: strong early launch trajectory (22K patients, 3.2% share in Year 1) (+15%), ELEVATE-PD switch data showing superiority over Rytary (+10%), sleep quality differentiation (+5%), expanding payer coverage (+5%). Adjustments downward: the 0.53-hour primary endpoint improvement is clinically modest and makes formulary arguments harder (-10%), Rytary generic entry post-2028 could cannibalize the extended-release segment (-5%), price sensitivity in a market dominated by $0.30/tablet generics (-5%). Net: 55-60%.

The Zambon partnership for Europe is strategically sound. Zambon has deep European neurology commercial infrastructure and existing PD presence. The ADIP Phase 3b trial in advanced PD serves dual purposes: generating local clinical data for EMA approval and expanding the evidence base into sicker patients. The EMA submission (June 2025) means a decision is likely by mid-2026. European launch would significantly expand the addressable market, though the path to peak sales in Europe depends on reimbursement negotiations in each major market.

The ELEVATE-PD Phase 4 interim data (December 2025) is the most commercially important dataset right now. The +1.80-hour Good ON improvement even vs. Rytary, if confirmed in the full 220-patient dataset expected in 2026, gives Amneal the head-to-head evidence needed for formulary positioning. The switch-study design mirrors real clinical practice -- neurologists moving patients from one therapy to another -- making these results immediately actionable for the sales force.
