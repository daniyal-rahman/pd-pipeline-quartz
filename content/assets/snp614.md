# SNP614

## Summary

SNP614 is a locked nucleic acid (LNA)-modified antisense oligonucleotide targeting LRRK2 mRNA, being developed by SciNeuro Pharmaceuticals (startup, private) with $5M in non-dilutive MJFF funding and $53M in equity financing led by ARCH Venture Partners and LAV. NHP data presented at MDS 2024 (late-breaking abstract LBA-20) showed potent LRRK2 mRNA knockdown in CNS regions of therapeutic interest with an acceptable safety profile. SNP614 enters the LRRK2 space after Biogen/Ionis discontinued their competing LRRK2 ASO (BIIB094/ION859) in February 2025, meaning SNP614 is now the only known LRRK2 ASO in active development. If the MJFF-funded IND-enabling studies succeed and [[biib122|BIIB122]]'s LUMA Phase 2b validates LRRK2 as a target, SNP614 becomes a differentiated second-wave asset offering mRNA-level knockdown (eliminating both kinase and GTPase functions) vs. kinase-only inhibition. If LUMA fails in idiopathic PD, the case for any LRRK2 therapeutic narrows to mutation carriers, and an IND-stage ASO faces a difficult fundraising environment.

## Notes

### Science
- LRRK2 (leucine-rich repeat kinase 2) is a large multifunctional protein with both GTPase and kinase domains; gain-of-function mutations (most commonly G2019S) cause 1-7% of PD in Europeans and 20-40% in Ashkenazi Jewish and North African Arab populations
- Elevated LRRK2 kinase activity has also been observed in post-mortem brain tissue of idiopathic PD patients, extending the therapeutic rationale beyond monogenic LRRK2-PD
- SNP614 uses a **Locked Nucleic Acid (LNA) scaffold** developed through a partnership with Secarna Pharmaceuticals (March 2023), which designed ASO candidates using their LNAplus platform -- LNA modifications enhance binding affinity, nuclease resistance, and potency relative to earlier ASO chemistries (e.g., 2'-MOE used in BIIB094/ION859)
- Key mechanistic distinction vs. [[biib122|BIIB122]] (small molecule kinase inhibitor): ASO-mediated mRNA degradation eliminates the entire LRRK2 protein, knocking out both kinase AND GTPase activity, plus any scaffold/protein-interaction functions. Small molecule inhibitors only block kinase activity, leaving GTPase and scaffold functions intact
- Key mechanistic distinction vs. BIIB094/ION859 (discontinued Biogen/Ionis LRRK2 ASO): SNP614 uses LNA chemistry vs. the 2'-MOE gapmer chemistry in BIIB094, potentially offering improved potency and CNS exposure per dose
- Open question: complete LRRK2 protein knockdown raises safety concerns -- LRRK2 knockout models show pulmonary and renal effects, though heterozygous knockdown (partial reduction) appears well-tolerated. The therapeutic window between efficacy and peripheral toxicity is critical
- Delivery is presumed intrathecal, consistent with CNS ASO precedent (nusinersen, tofersen) -- this is a disadvantage for chronic dosing in PD relative to oral agents like [[biib122|BIIB122]]

### Clinical

No clinical trials initiated. IND-enabling studies are underway, funded by the $5M MJFF Therapeutics Pipeline Program grant (November 2025).

Key preclinical milestones:
- **MDS 2024 Late-Breaking Abstract (LBA-20):** "Nonhuman Primate Efficacy and Safety of SNP614, a LRRK2 Antisense Oligonucleotide as Potential Therapeutic Agent for Parkinson's Disease" -- presented September 28-30, 2024, Philadelphia
- NHP studies demonstrated potent LRRK2 mRNA knockdown in CNS regions of therapeutic interest with acceptable safety profile
- In vitro and in vivo efficacy confirmed across multiple models
- SciNeuro joined MJFF's LRRK2 Investigative Therapeutics Exchange (LITE) program, connecting them with 30+ academic/clinical centers and a dozen companies developing LRRK2 therapies, implemented by the University of Dundee (Dario Alessi group)

- [[biib122|BIIB122]] (Denali/Biogen, Phase 2b) is the most advanced LRRK2 asset -- an oral small molecule kinase inhibitor. Different modality: kinase inhibition only vs. SNP614's total protein knockdown via mRNA degradation. If LUMA validates the LRRK2 target, SNP614 benefits from a proven target with a differentiated mechanism
- BIIB094/ION859 (Biogen/Ionis) was the direct ASO competitor -- discontinued February 2025 after Phase 1 (REASON trial). This leaves SNP614 as the only LRRK2 ASO in active development. The discontinuation raises both a concern (is LRRK2 ASO viable?) and an opportunity (SciNeuro inherits the entire LRRK2 ASO thesis)
- Biogen's stated rationale for discontinuing BIIB094 was not fully disclosed, but came alongside broader pipeline cuts. Whether the issue was target-related, chemistry-related, or portfolio prioritization matters for SNP614's thesis -- if it was 2'-MOE chemistry limitations, LNA may solve the problem; if it was LRRK2 knockdown safety, SNP614 faces the same risk
- [[arv-102|ARV-102]] (Arvinas) represents a third LRRK2 modality -- PROTAC-mediated degradation of LRRK2 protein. Like SNP614, it eliminates the whole protein rather than inhibiting kinase only, but uses a small molecule oral approach rather than intrathecal ASO
- The intrathecal delivery requirement is SNP614's primary competitive disadvantage vs. oral agents ([[biib122|BIIB122]], [[arv-102|ARV-102]]). Chronic PD patients require decades of treatment -- monthly or quarterly intrathecal injections are a significant burden, as demonstrated by the clinical experience with tofersen in ALS (Biogen)

## Analysis

SNP614 occupies an interesting niche: the only remaining LRRK2 ASO after Biogen/Ionis exited, with a next-generation LNA chemistry that may address whatever limitations drove BIIB094's discontinuation. The MJFF grant and LITE program membership provide validation beyond just investor capital -- MJFF's Therapeutics Pipeline Program is selective, and Dario Alessi's endorsement from the University of Dundee (a leading LRRK2 biology lab) carries scientific weight.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching Phase 1: 50-60%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate for IND-enabling programs reaching Phase 1: ~65-70%
- Adjustments upward: NHP data already in hand with positive efficacy/safety (+5%), $5M dedicated MJFF funding for IND-enabling work (+5%), experienced CNS leadership team (Min Li ex-GSK neuroscience head) (+5%), Secarna's validated LNA platform (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: BIIB094 discontinuation creates regulatory and scientific uncertainty for LRRK2 ASO class (-10%), intrathecal delivery adds manufacturing and clinical complexity (-5%), company has multiple programs competing for resources (-5%)
- Net: ~55%

**Signal analysis:**
- The Novartis deal ($165M upfront for the BBB shuttle program) fundamentally changes SciNeuro's financial position. The company is no longer a cash-constrained startup -- it has big pharma validation of its platform technology and resources to fund multiple programs through IND. This makes SNP614 less likely to be killed for financial reasons and more likely to be advanced on scientific merit.
- ARCH Venture Partners' continued investment through the $53M round (after originally co-leading the $100M Series A) is meaningful. ARCH also backed Denali Therapeutics, the developer of [[biib122|BIIB122]] -- they have deep conviction in the LRRK2 target and are now backing two complementary approaches (kinase inhibition via Denali, mRNA knockdown via SciNeuro).
- The LUMA Phase 2b readout (March 2026) for [[biib122|BIIB122]] is the single most important external catalyst for SNP614. A positive LUMA result validates LRRK2 as a PD target and makes SNP614's differentiated ASO approach far more attractive to potential partners. A negative result would make it very difficult to advance any LRRK2 therapeutic, regardless of modality.
- Decision tree: if IND-enabling studies succeed and LUMA validates the target, SNP614 becomes a potential partnering asset for a large pharma seeking a differentiated LRRK2 approach (complete protein knockdown vs. kinase inhibition). If LUMA fails, SNP614 likely gets deprioritized in favor of SciNeuro's AD programs (SNP622 APOE4 ASO, Novartis BBB shuttle collaboration), which have clearer paths.
