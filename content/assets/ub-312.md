# UB-312

## Summary

UB-312 is an active alpha-synuclein vaccine from Vaxxinity (biotech, formerly VAXX on Nasdaq, now private after voluntary delisting in May 2024) that demonstrated the first published evidence of target engagement in PD patients — a 20% reduction in CSF alpha-synuclein seeding vs. a 3% increase on placebo (p<0.05), published in Nature Medicine in 2024. The Phase 1 (NCT04075318) showed 92% immunogenicity (12/13 patients developed anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies) and a Phase 1b (NCT05634876) at NYU in synucleinopathies (PD + MSA) completed in July 2025 with continued safety signals. However, Vaxxinity is a micro-cap company that went private under financial distress — the critical question is whether they can fund a properly powered Phase 2 trial. If Phase 2 confirms the CSF seeding reduction translates to clinical slowing, UB-312 would validate the active vaccine modality alongside [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] and offer a low-COGS alternative to [[prasinezumab]]'s monthly IV infusions. If Vaxxinity cannot secure funding, this program stalls despite promising biology and the active vaccine thesis rests entirely on [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]].

## Notes

### Science
- UB-312 is a synthetic peptide vaccine using Vaxxinity's UBITh platform: a **10-amino-acid C-terminal epitope** of alpha-synuclein conjugated to a proprietary T-helper peptide via a small peptide linker, combined with adjuvant for intramuscular injection
- The C-terminal epitope was selected from a screening campaign of **>60 peptide epitopes** — chosen because it is immunosilent on its own but highly immunogenic when conjugated, is present on pathological alpha-synuclein forms, and is accessible to antibodies on the protein surface
- Induced antibodies show **preferential binding to aggregated (oligomeric/fibrillar) alpha-synuclein** with almost no binding to normal monomeric alpha-synuclein — critical selectivity that avoids interfering with physiological alpha-synuclein function
- Key mechanistic distinction vs. [[prasinezumab]]: UB-312 is **active immunotherapy** (induces the patient's own polyclonal antibody response) vs. prasinezumab's **passive immunotherapy** (exogenous monoclonal antibody). Active approach generates sustained antibody production without repeated IV infusions, potentially achieving broader epitope coverage through polyclonal response
- Both UB-312 and [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] target the C-terminal region, but differ in immunogen design: UB-312 uses the actual alpha-synuclein peptide sequence conjugated to UBITh T-helper peptide, while ACI-7104.056 uses a synthetic mimotope conjugated to KLH carrier protein
- The UBITh platform overcomes immune tolerance toward self-proteins by driving a targeted B-cell humoral response while avoiding T-cell-mediated autoimmune toxicity — this is the core engineering challenge for vaccines against endogenous proteins
- Open question: whether vaccine-induced antibodies achieve sufficient CNS penetration for meaningful target engagement. Phase 1 showed CSF antibody detection in only **5/13 patients** (38%), though those with detectable CSF antibodies showed the clearest biomarker improvements

### Clinical

**Part A — First-in-Human (Phase 1)** | NCT04075318 | N=50 | Healthy volunteers (age 40-85)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability → Clean safety profile; AEs mostly mild and transient (headache, vaccination-site pain, fatigue)
- **Key secondary:** Dose-dependent anti-alpha-synuclein antibody induction in serum and CSF across seven dose cohorts (40-2000 ug); all participants in target dose group showed detectable antibodies
- **Status:** Completed
- **Interpretation:** Established immunogenicity across dose range and clean safety profile in healthy volunteers. Selected 100 ug and 300 ug doses for PD patient study. Published in Movement Disorders (2022).

**Part B — PD Patients (Phase 1)** | NCT04075318 | N=20 | Early PD (Hoehn & Yahr ≤ III)
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability and immunogenicity → 92% (12/13 completers) developed anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies in serum; 38% (5/13) in CSF
- **Key secondary (exploratory):** 300/100/100 ug group showed **20% decrease** from baseline in aggregated alpha-synuclein in CSF (by SAA) vs. **3% increase** in placebo (p<0.05). Post-hoc: patients with detectable CSF antibodies showed improvement in MDS-UPDRS Part II (activities of daily living)
- **Status:** Completed (June 2023)
- **Interpretation:** First demonstration of target engagement (CSF seeding reduction) for an active alpha-synuclein vaccine. The 20% reduction in toxic alpha-synuclein aggregates is biologically meaningful but the sample size (N=20) and post-hoc nature of clinical correlations limit conclusions. Published in Nature Medicine (2024).

**Phase 1b — Synucleinopathies** | NCT05634876 | N=7-8 (4 MSA + 3-4 PD) | PD and MSA patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety/tolerability and immunogenicity in synucleinopathies
- **Key secondary:** Anti-alpha-synuclein antibodies detectable in CSF in 3/4 MSA patients and 2/3 PD patients at week 29
- **Status:** Completed (July 2025); immunogenicity and safety analyses through week 72 pending
- **Interpretation:** Extends safety and immunogenicity data to MSA, broadening potential indication scope. Presented at MDS 2024. Very small sample size — primarily supportive of further development rather than proof of efficacy.

### Financial
- **IPO:** November 2021 at $13/share, raised $76M on Nasdaq (originally targeting $14-16/share range)
- **Voluntary delisting:** May 2024 from Nasdaq after failing to maintain $1.00 minimum bid price; transitioned to OTC markets then effectively went private. Leadership described public markets as an "abusive relationship" for a pre-revenue biotech
- **Non-dilutive funding:** ~$10M from leading organizations including MJFF grant (January 2022) for pharmacodynamic marker development in collaboration with Mayo Clinic and UTHealth Houston
- **Pre-IPO funding:** $27M Series B (2021); key investors include United Biomedical (18.9% post-IPO stake) and Prime Movers Lab Fund (5.9%)
- **Financial distress signals:** Going concern language in filings, sub-penny OTC trading ($0.02), market cap ~$14K as of late 2025. The company's ability to fund a Phase 2 trial independently is highly uncertain
- **Cost advantage thesis:** If efficacious, an active vaccine would have dramatically lower COGS than monthly antibody infusions ([[prasinezumab]] estimated $30-50K/year) or quarterly siRNA injections ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]). Vaccine manufacturing is scalable and infrequent dosing (quarterly-to-annual boosters) reduces healthcare delivery burden

- UB-312 is one of two active alpha-synuclein vaccines in clinical development, alongside [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] (AC Immune, Phase 2). Both target the C-terminal region but use different immunogen designs and carrier systems
- [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] is ahead operationally — in a Phase 2 adaptive trial (VacSYn, N=150) with interim data showing 100% immunogenicity and early biomarker signals. ACI-7104.056 also has a better-capitalized sponsor (AC Immune, publicly traded, ACIU)
- The active vaccine modality offers theoretical advantages over passive antibodies like [[prasinezumab]]: sustained polyclonal response, broader epitope coverage, lower COGS, less frequent dosing (IM injections vs. monthly IV infusions), and better scalability for global PD population (~10M patients)
- Key disadvantage vs. passive antibodies: less precise dose control, variable immunogenicity across patients (8% non-responders in Phase 1), and irreversibility if adverse immune response occurs
- If [[prasinezumab]] succeeds in PARAISO Phase 3, it validates the extracellular alpha-synuclein target and creates strong rationale for vaccines as the scalable, low-cost follow-on approach
- If [[prasinezumab]] fails, active vaccines face the question of whether any extracellular alpha-synuclein approach can work, though the distinct mechanism (polyclonal vs. monoclonal) preserves some differentiation

## Analysis

UB-312 presents an unusual profile: compelling early biology in a financially distressed vehicle. The Nature Medicine Phase 1 data — 20% CSF seeding reduction with preferential binding to pathological alpha-synuclein forms — is genuinely encouraging and represents the first published target engagement for an active alpha-synuclein vaccine in PD patients. The UBITh platform's ability to overcome immune tolerance against a self-protein while avoiding T-cell autoimmunity is a meaningful technical achievement. But the asset's trajectory is entirely dependent on Vaxxinity's ability to fund development, and the company's financial situation is dire.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of reaching Phase 2 initiation: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: the science supports advancement, MJFF and non-dilutive funding provide partial support, and the Phase 1b completion at NYU suggests academic collaboration can offset some costs. But going private with sub-penny stock and going-concern warnings means the company needs a licensing deal, acquisition, or substantial non-dilutive funding to run even a modest Phase 2. The voluntary delisting was framed as strategic but reads as forced by Nasdaq compliance failure.

**Analytical estimate — Conditional probability of Phase 2 success (if initiated): 20-25%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning: base rate for alpha-synuclein targeting in PD is poor (0/6 antibodies have met primary endpoints); active vaccines are mechanistically distinct (+5%) and showed early target engagement (+10%); but small Phase 1 samples and post-hoc clinical correlations require substantial confirmation (-5%); the 38% CSF antibody detection rate is a potential efficacy ceiling (-5%).

**Signal analysis:** Vaxxinity's decision to go private rather than do a dilutive raise suggests either (a) the founders believe they can secure better terms privately, possibly through a licensing deal enabled by the Nature Medicine publication, or (b) they had no viable public-market options. The MJFF grant and NYU academic collaboration suggest the scientific community sees value in the program even if capital markets do not. The key watch item is whether a larger partner (pharma or biotech) licenses or acquires UB-312 — the Nature Medicine publication and the growing active vaccine dataset from [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] could make this an attractive low-cost pickup for a company wanting optionality in the alpha-synuclein vaccine space. United Biomedical's 18.9% stake provides a potential funding backstop, but their commitment to the PD program specifically is unclear.
