# Morphomer Alpha-Synuclein

## Summary

AC Immune (biotech, ACIU) is developing Morphomer alpha-synuclein, a first-in-class oral small molecule aggregation inhibitor targeting **intracellular** alpha-synuclein aggregates -- a critical differentiator from antibody approaches like [[prasinezumab]] and vaccines like [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] that only address extracellular pathology. The program uses AC Immune's proprietary Morphomer platform (~10,000-15,000 CNS-optimized compounds) and has demonstrated reduced alpha-synuclein accumulation in neuronal cell models with favorable brain-penetrant pharmacokinetics. Lead candidate selection was expected around 2025, with IND-enabling studies to follow. However, the program faces a headwind from [[minzasolmin]]'s Phase 2 failure (ORCHESTRA, December 2024), which terminated the closest small-molecule alpha-synuclein competitor and cast doubt on the modality. If Morphomer alpha-synuclein can demonstrate a differentiated mechanism from [[minzasolmin]] -- particularly if it targets intracellular seeding rather than membrane-bound oligomers -- it could revive interest in the small molecule approach. If preclinical data fail to differentiate from [[minzasolmin]]'s failed biology, the program will struggle to attract clinical development capital.

## Notes

### Science
- Morphomer platform enables rational chemical design of CNS-penetrant small molecules that bind with high specificity to misfolded proteins, working to break up neurotoxic aggregates and inhibit aggregation and seeding
- Library of ~10,000-15,000 CNS-optimized compounds curated specifically for targeting neurodegenerative disease proteins in the brain
- Multiple distinct chemical series identified that significantly decrease alpha-synuclein aggregate formation in cellular assays by interfering with the fibrillation process
- Key mechanistic distinction: targets **intracellular** alpha-synuclein aggregates, addressing the compartment where pathology actually accumulates -- antibodies ([[prasinezumab]], [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]]) and siRNA ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]) address extracellular clearance or production inhibition respectively, but neither directly disrupts existing intracellular aggregates
- Iterative medicinal chemistry optimization produced compounds with favorable CNS-penetrant pharmacokinetic properties; oral bioavailability expected
- MJFF-funded research (Fall 2021 grant) by Elpida Tsika (AC Immune, Lausanne) and Ruth Luthi-Carter (University of Leicester) accelerated in vivo proof-of-concept studies in alpha-synucleinopathy models
- AD/PD 2025 presentation: "Discovery and optimization of therapeutic small molecules targeting alpha-synuclein aggregation" (Elpida Tsika, virtual OO-187)
- The same Morphomer platform produced ACI-12589, AC Immune's alpha-synuclein PET tracer now in clinical development -- the diagnostic and therapeutic programs are scientifically synergistic, enabling target engagement measurement in future clinical trials
- Critical open question: how does this mechanism compare to [[minzasolmin]] (which targeted membrane-bound oligomers) and [[emrusolmin]] (which binds inside fibril cavities)? If the intracellular aggregation inhibition mechanism is biologically distinct, [[minzasolmin]]'s failure may not be predictive. If the mechanism overlaps, the modality is in serious trouble
- Second open question: can a small molecule achieve sufficient intracellular concentrations at tolerable doses to meaningfully inhibit aggregation in the context of progressive protein misfolding?

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. IND-enabling studies were expected to begin around Q3 2021 based on early disclosures, with lead candidate selection anticipated around 2025.

Key preclinical milestones:
- **Cellular assays:** Multiple chemical series demonstrated significant reduction of alpha-synuclein aggregate formation in neuronal cell models
- **In vivo PK:** Favorable CNS-penetrant pharmacokinetic properties confirmed in animal models
- **In vivo proof-of-concept:** Studies in alpha-synucleinopathy animal models initiated (MJFF-funded, 2021-2022)
- **Lead optimization:** Ongoing medicinal chemistry refinement; BioWorld reported the compound ACI-21018 as a novel alpha-synuclein aggregation inhibitor presented at ADPD 2025

### Financial
- **Wholly owned program** -- AC Immune retains full economics with no partner sharing
- **MJFF grant support:** Fall 2021 grant under "Accelerating Early Proof-of-Concept Testing of Promising PD Therapies" -- amount undisclosed but typical MJFF grants in this program range $250K-$2M
- **AC Immune cash position:** CHF 127.1M as of June 2025; runway through Q3 2027 after 30% workforce reduction
- **Market cap:** ~$365M (January 2026); ACIU stock ~$3.20
- **Strategic priority:** AC Immune restructured in 2025 to focus on three clinical-stage vaccine programs ([[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]], ACI-24.060, ACI-35.030), suggesting the Morphomer alpha-synuclein therapeutic program is a lower priority than the vaccine platform despite scientific merit
- **Context:** AC Immune has >$4.5B in potential milestones across partnered programs (Roche/Genentech, Lilly, Janssen), but the Morphomer alpha-synuclein program -- like [[aci-7104|ACI-7104.056]] -- is wholly owned, meaning AC Immune must self-fund or find a partner for clinical development
- **Comparable deals:** [[minzasolmin]] was partnered by UCB with Novartis before Phase 2 failure; [[emrusolmin]] was licensed to Teva by MODAG. A Morphomer alpha-synuclein partnership would likely require differentiated preclinical data post-minzasolmin failure

- Gene silencing approaches ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[ly3962681|LY3962681]]) reduce alpha-synuclein production but do not clear existing intracellular aggregates -- Morphomer alpha-synuclein could complement production inhibition by addressing accumulated pathology

## Analysis

The Morphomer alpha-synuclein program occupies an intellectually compelling but commercially precarious position. The scientific thesis -- that intracellular alpha-synuclein aggregation is the pathological driver and can be inhibited by a brain-penetrant small molecule -- directly addresses the compartment that antibodies, vaccines, and even gene silencing approaches cannot reach. In theory, this is the most direct approach to disease modification in PD. In practice, the program is early-stage, unfunded by a partner, and operating in the shadow of [[minzasolmin]]'s comprehensive Phase 2 failure.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of clinical entry (IND filing): 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: discovery-stage programs from publicly traded biotechs with validated platforms reach IND at ~30-40% rates
- Adjustments upward: Morphomer platform has already produced a clinical-stage compound (ACI-12589 PET tracer) from the same chemical library (+5%), MJFF grant funding validates scientific approach (+3%), AC Immune has institutional experience in IND filings across multiple programs (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: AC Immune's 2025 restructuring prioritized vaccines over small molecules, suggesting reduced internal investment (-5%), [[minzasolmin]] failure creates pharma reluctance toward small molecule alpha-synuclein programs (-5%), cash runway through Q3 2027 creates urgency to advance clinical-stage assets over preclinical programs (-3%)
- Net: ~40-50% probability of reaching IND; conditional probability of eventual approval given IND filing would be very low (~5-10%)

**Signal analysis:**
- AC Immune's decision to restructure around three clinical-stage vaccine programs while maintaining the Morphomer alpha-synuclein program in the preclinical portfolio suggests measured scientific conviction without urgent commercial commitment. The program is being kept alive but not prioritized.
- The MJFF grant is a validation signal -- MJFF's Therapeutics Pipeline Program is selective and specifically targets industry programs with translational potential. However, grant funding alone is insufficient for IND-enabling studies.
- The AD/PD 2025 presentation by Elpida Tsika on "Discovery and optimization of therapeutic small molecules targeting alpha-synuclein aggregation" suggests active medicinal chemistry work is ongoing, which is a positive signal for eventual lead candidate nomination.
- The strategic value of this program may be as a **partnership asset** -- if [[prasinezumab]]'s PARAISO Phase 3 validates extracellular alpha-synuclein targeting, pharma attention turns to the intracellular compartment as the next frontier, making Morphomer alpha-synuclein a logical combination partner. If PARAISO fails, the entire alpha-synuclein field contracts and this program's prospects diminish.
- The synergy with ACI-12589 (PET tracer) is underappreciated: AC Immune is one of few companies that could run a clinical trial with both a therapeutic Morphomer compound and a diagnostic Morphomer compound for the same target, enabling direct target engagement measurement that [[minzasolmin]] lacked.
