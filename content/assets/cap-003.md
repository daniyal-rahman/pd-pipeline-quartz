# CAP-003

## Summary

CAP-003 is Capsida Biotherapeutics' (startup, private) IV-administered AAV gene therapy for PD-GBA, using a proprietary BBB-crossing capsid engineered via directed evolution from the Viviana Gradinaru lab at Caltech. IND cleared June 2025, with Phase 1/2 dosing initiated Q3 2025 (NCT07011771, ~31 patients). NHP data showed brainwide GBA1 RNA expression 200-fold greater than IV AAV9, up to 6-fold GCase protein increase, and GCase activity 250% above untreated levels -- well above the 30% threshold expected to normalize enzyme activity in PD-GBA patients. AbbVie (big pharma) exercised its option on the program in January 2025 with a $40M license payment, and the broader collaboration totals $90M upfront plus milestones. If Phase 1/2 shows safe, durable GCase restoration in the brain, CAP-003 becomes the leading one-time IV gene therapy for PD-GBA, leapfrogging the intracisternal approach of Lilly/Prevail's PR001. If safety signals emerge (DRG toxicity, liver off-target, immunogenicity), the case for gene therapy in PD-GBA weakens and capital flows back to oral approaches like [[pariceract]].

## Notes

### Science
- CAP-003 delivers a functional copy of the human GBA1 gene packaged in a proprietary engineered AAV capsid, administered as a single IV infusion intended to provide a permanent source of GCase protein throughout the brain
- The capsid was engineered via directed evolution from the Gradinaru lab at Caltech, screening libraries of >60 billion capsid sequences in NHPs and human cell lines using machine learning and structural biology
- Key differentiator is the **IV route with BBB-crossing**: achieves brainwide distribution without intracranial or intracisternal injection, unlike Lilly/Prevail's PR001 (intracisternal) or traditional AAV9 (poor CNS penetration from IV)
- Proprietary detargeting: 19-fold less liver biodistribution and 17-fold less DRG expression compared to IV wild-type AAV9 -- addresses the two major safety concerns of systemic AAV gene therapy (hepatotoxicity and sensory neuropathy)
- GBA1 mutations are the most common genetic risk factor for PD, present in 5-10% of PD patients (15-20% in Ashkenazi Jewish populations), conferring 20-30x increased disease risk
- GBA1 encodes glucocerebrosidase (GCase), a lysosomal enzyme; loss of function leads to glucosylceramide and glucosylsphingosine accumulation, impaired lysosomal-autophagy pathways, and accelerated alpha-synuclein aggregation
- Gene therapy approach restores the enzyme itself -- mechanistically distinct from substrate reduction (failed: Sanofi's venglustat), chaperone approaches (ambroxol, limited results), and enzyme activation ([[pariceract]], in Phase 2b)
- Open question: will single-dose IV gene therapy achieve sufficient and durable transduction across all relevant CNS regions to meaningfully alter disease trajectory over decades?

### Clinical

**Phase 1/2** | NCT07011771 | N=~31 | PD patients with GBA1 mutations, ages 21-75
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety and tolerability of single IV infusion across ascending dose cohorts
- **Key secondary:** GCase enzyme activity, biomarker changes, immunogenicity, clinical efficacy measures (MDS-UPDRS)
- **Design:** Part 1 is dose escalation in small cohorts; Part 2 uses selected dose for expansion. Two-year follow-up per patient
- **Status:** Active; IND cleared June 2025; first patient dosing expected Q3 2025
- **Interpretation:** First-in-human for an IV BBB-crossing AAV gene therapy in PD. The dose-escalation safety profile, particularly DRG and liver signals, will determine whether the engineered capsid's preclinical detargeting translates to humans. Initial safety/dose data expected H2 2026.

**IND-enabling NHP studies (Preclinical)**
- Single IV infusion produced brainwide GBA1 RNA expression **>200-fold greater** than IV AAV9
- Up to **6-fold increase** in GCase protein vs. untreated animals across relevant brain regions including substantia nigra
- GCase enzyme activity increased **up to ~250%** above untreated levels, well exceeding the 30% threshold expected to normalize enzyme activity in PD-GBA
- GLP toxicology study in NHPs demonstrated "potential best-in-class" safety profile per Capsida (presented at Society for Neuroscience, October 2024)
- 19-fold less liver biodistribution and 17-fold less DRG expression vs. wild-type AAV9

- **vs. [[pariceract]] (BIAL, oral GCase activator, Phase 2b):** Most direct competitor for GBA-PD. Pariceract is oral/chronic dosing with ACTIVATE Phase 2b data mid-2026 -- if positive, validates the GCase mechanism and implicitly supports CAP-003's thesis, but also creates a convenient oral alternative that competes on access. If pariceract fails, gene therapy becomes the last viable path for GCase restoration and CAP-003's strategic value increases substantially
- **vs. PR001/LY3884961 (Lilly/Prevail, AAV9 gene therapy, Phase 1/2a):** Same gene therapy modality and GBA1 target, but PR001 requires **intracisternal** injection (neurosurgical procedure) vs. CAP-003's **IV** route. PR001 uses wild-type AAV9 capsid without liver/DRG detargeting. Prevail's PROPEL trial (NCT04127578, N=20) is active; Phase 3 (exPDite-2, N=102) planned for 2025. If PR001 shows efficacy but route-of-administration challenges limit uptake, CAP-003's IV delivery becomes a best-in-class differentiator
- **vs. venglustat (Sanofi, oral substrate reduction inhibitor):** Failed Phase 2 in 2021 -- no benefit over placebo. Validates that substrate reduction alone is insufficient; enzyme restoration (gene therapy or activation) needed
- **vs. ambroxol (academic, oral GCase chaperone):** Phase 2 showed modest GCase increases but unclear clinical benefit. Limited investment suggests this approach is deprioritized
- Route of administration is the key competitive axis within gene therapy: IV (CAP-003) vs. intracisternal (PR001). If Capsida's engineered capsid delivers on its NHP promise, the IV route with brainwide distribution and favorable safety profile would be a decisive advantage for commercial adoption

## Analysis

CAP-003 represents the convergence of two high-conviction theses: GBA1 as a validated genetic target in PD, and engineered AAV capsids as the enabling technology for non-invasive CNS gene therapy. The NHP data is genuinely differentiated -- 200-fold improvement over AAV9 in CNS expression with simultaneous liver/DRG detargeting is a step-change, not an incremental improvement. AbbVie's decision to exercise its option and commit $40M in January 2025, months before IND clearance, signals that their internal diligence on the preclinical package was compelling.

**Analytical estimate -- Phase 1/2 success probability (safety + biomarker signal): 45-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: AAV gene therapy Phase 1/2 programs in CNS have ~30-40% probability of generating usable safety/efficacy data
- Adjustments upward: exceptional NHP data with clear dose-response (+10%), novel capsid with demonstrated detargeting addresses main AAV safety risks (+10%), strong genetic validation of GBA1 target (+5%), AbbVie big pharma validation and manufacturing support (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: first-in-human for this capsid -- NHP-to-human translation of engineered capsids is uncertain (-10%), immunogenicity to novel capsid is unknown (-5%), single-dose paradigm means no ability to dose-adjust if underdosed (-5%)
- Net: ~45-50% for clean safety + meaningful GCase biomarker elevation

**Signal analysis:**
- AbbVie's opt-in behavior is informative. They structured the original 2021 deal as an option, preserving the right to walk away. Exercising that option in January 2025 -- pre-IND, before any human data -- and paying $40M means their preclinical review was convincing. AbbVie has significant neuroscience infrastructure and the financial commitment to run a large program if Phase 1/2 succeeds.
- Capsida's unconventional funding model (pharma collaborations > traditional VC) means they are less dependent on public market sentiment and more insulated from biotech funding cycles. This is structurally advantageous for a long-duration CNS program.
- The competitive timing is critical: [[pariceract]]'s ACTIVATE Phase 2b data arrives mid-2026, overlapping with CAP-003's initial Phase 1/2 safety data. A pariceract success validates the GCase target (good for CAP-003's thesis) but creates an oral competitor (challenging for commercial case). A pariceract failure would dramatically increase CAP-003's strategic importance as potentially the only viable GCase restoration approach.
- Decision tree: if H2 2026 data shows clean safety and dose-dependent GCase elevation in CSF/brain, expect AbbVie to accelerate into expansion cohorts and potentially a registrational design. If safety signals emerge (particularly DRG toxicity or hepatotoxicity despite detargeting), the engineered capsid platform thesis is challenged and the entire Capsida pipeline is affected, not just CAP-003.
