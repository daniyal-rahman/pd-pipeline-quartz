# LYS241 (Glunozumab) — Lys Therapeutics

## Summary

LYS241 is a first-in-class fully humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody from Lys Therapeutics (French startup, private) that blocks the interaction between tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) and NMDA receptors on vascular endothelial cells, restoring blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity and reducing neuroinflammation without needing to cross the BBB itself. A January 2025 publication in *Journal of Neuroinflammation* demonstrated that systemic IV glunomab rescued dopaminergic neurons and restored sensorimotor function in an alpha-synuclein overexpression mouse model, establishing a direct mechanistic link between tPA-NMDAR signaling and PD neurodegeneration. MJFF has funded the program twice — most recently a $5M Therapeutic Pipeline Program grant (September 2025) — to advance through IND-enabling studies toward first-in-human trials. If LYS241 demonstrates clinical efficacy, it would validate a novel neuroinflammation/BBB-restoration axis orthogonal to all current PD disease modification approaches ([[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[biib122|BIIB122]], [[pariceract]]). If it fails, the tPA-NMDAR hypothesis as a druggable node in PD is likely shelved, though the stroke indication provides a second path.

## Notes

### Science
- LYS241 is a full-length, Fc-silent, fully humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that selectively blocks tPA binding to the GluN1 subunit of the NMDA receptor on vascular endothelial cells
- **Core mechanism:** In PD, striatal GABAergic neurons projecting to the substantia nigra release tPA in response to alpha-synuclein overexpression. tPA binds vascular NMDA receptors (through a non-proteolytic interaction), triggering BBB breakdown, immune cell infiltration (both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells), microglial activation, and dopaminergic neuron degeneration
- **Key insight:** Proteolytically inactive tPA mutants retain full neurotoxic capacity — the pathology is driven by the tPA-NMDAR binding event, not tPA's enzymatic (plasminogen-activating) function. This makes the interaction a clean antibody target
- LYS241 does NOT need to cross the BBB — it acts from within blood vessels on endothelial NMDA receptors, which is a fundamental pharmacological advantage over CNS-penetrant antibodies like [[prasinezumab]] (which achieves only ~0.1-0.2% brain exposure)
- Fc-silent backbone means the antibody blocks the tPA-NMDAR interaction without triggering Fc-mediated effector functions (ADCC, CDC), reducing immune side effects
- Restores physiological NMDA receptor function and tight junction integrity without perturbing basal NMDAR ion channel activity — selectivity for the pathological interaction only
- Scientific validation comes from Prof. Denis Vivien's lab (Blood and Brain Institute, Caen-Normandie) with >300 peer-reviewed publications, and independent replication at University of Michigan (Prof. Daniel Lawrence)
- The mechanism is fundamentally different from alpha-synuclein clearance ([[prasinezumab]], [[cinpanemab]]), alpha-synuclein production inhibition ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]), LRRK2 kinase inhibition ([[biib122|BIIB122]]), or GCase activation ([[pariceract]]) — it targets downstream neuroinflammation and BBB dysfunction rather than protein aggregation directly
- Open question: the alpha-synuclein rAAV2 mouse model demonstrates the mechanism, but human PD neuroinflammation is chronic and multifactorial. Whether blocking one axis (tPA-NMDAR) is sufficient to meaningfully slow progression in patients remains unproven
- "Pipeline-in-a-drug" strategy: same antibody, same mechanism targets BBB dysfunction across PD, MSA, ischemic stroke, and MS — de-risks development by providing multiple shots on goal

### Clinical
No clinical trials initiated. IND-enabling studies are underway.

- **Preclinical efficacy (PD):** Systemic IV glunomab rescued dopaminergic neurons, reduced neuroinflammation (decreased microglial MHC-I/CD16/32 expression, reduced T-cell infiltration), and restored sensorimotor function in rAAV2-human-alpha-synuclein mice (Lawrence et al., J Neuroinflammation, Jan 2025)
- **Preclinical efficacy (Stroke):** LYS241 reduces ischemic damage, promotes tissue recanalization, and works synergistically with standard thrombolytics (alteplase, tenecteplase) while mitigating their off-target vascular toxicity (Blood Advances, March 2024)
- **Preclinical efficacy (MS):** Efficacy demonstrated in validated preclinical MS models (data presented at ACTRIMS Forum 2024)
- **Regulatory progress:** Positive Scientific Advice received from national drug agencies (EMA pathway); GMP-compliant antibody production and regulatory toxicology studies underway
- **Conference presentations:** Poster at MDS International Congress (Copenhagen, August 2023); six presentations at major conferences in 2024 including Gordon Research Conference, International Stroke Conference, ACTRIMS Forum, AD/PD Congress, European Stroke Organisation Conference; AD/PD 2025 presentation
- **Expected FIH timeline:** First-in-human clinical trial initiation targeted, likely 2026-2027 based on current IND-enabling trajectory

- LYS241 occupies a unique niche: it is not competing directly with alpha-synuclein-targeting agents ([[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]]) or genetic-PD targets ([[biib122|BIIB122]], [[pariceract]]) — it targets a downstream neuroinflammatory/BBB mechanism
- The closest mechanistic comparison would be anti-neuroinflammation approaches, but LYS241's specific target (tPA-NMDAR interaction) has no direct competitors in clinical development for PD
- If alpha-synuclein clearance approaches ([[prasinezumab]]) fail to show disease modification, neuroinflammation-targeting approaches like LYS241 could gain attention as the field pivots away from protein aggregation hypotheses
- The BBB-restoration mechanism could theoretically be complementary to (not competitive with) alpha-synuclein or LRRK2 approaches — combination potential if early clinical data is positive
- Multi-indication strategy (stroke, MS, MSA) provides optionality: even if PD indication disappoints, stroke represents a large unmet-need market where BBB restoration synergizes with existing thrombolytics
- Being a French startup with no US clinical presence yet is a competitive disadvantage for speed — US-based programs with FDA relationships may reach clinic faster in the same space

## Analysis

LYS241 represents a genuinely novel mechanism in the PD pipeline. Unlike every other clinical or late-preclinical PD asset — which targets alpha-synuclein, LRRK2, GBA1, or symptomatic pathways — Lys Therapeutics is targeting the neurovascular axis: the tPA-NMDAR interaction that drives BBB breakdown and neuroinflammation. The January 2025 *Journal of Neuroinflammation* publication is the key scientific anchor, demonstrating neuroprotection via systemic IV antibody in an alpha-synuclein-driven PD model. The fact that MJFF has funded the program twice, including their top-tier $5M Therapeutic Pipeline Program grant, provides meaningful external validation — MJFF has domain expertise and access to proprietary data that informs their funding decisions.

**Analytical estimate — Probability of clinical success in PD: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: preclinical-stage antibodies for neurodegenerative disease have ~5-8% probability of reaching approval
- Adjustments upward: novel, non-crowded mechanism with clean target biology (+5%), independent replication across two labs (Caen + Michigan) (+3%), Fc-silent design reduces safety risk (+2%), does not require BBB penetration — fundamental PK advantage (+3%), MJFF validation and repeat funding (+2%), strong board (ex-Biogen CEO, OrbiMed partner, neurologist-neuroscientist) (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: no human data yet — IND-enabling stage (-5%), single mouse model (rAAV2-alpha-syn) may not predict chronic human PD (-5%), tiny company with limited clinical execution track record (-3%), no disclosed partnership with large pharma for PD — execution risk (-3%)
- Net: ~10-15%

**Signal analysis:** The board composition tells a story. Michel Vounatsos ran Biogen through the aducanumab era and knows neurodegenerative drug development intimately. Anat Naschitz has OrbiMed-level deal evaluation experience. Shibeshih Belachew led clinical development at Biogen for MS — directly relevant to LYS241's MS and neuroinflammation thesis. These are not advisory-board-in-name-only appointments; they signal the company is positioning for a significant Series A or partnership. The "fully financed through early clinical development" statement suggests they believe current funding covers Phase 1 costs, but Phase 2 in PD will require either a pharma partnership or a $30-50M+ raise.

The pipeline-in-a-drug strategy is rational risk management. Stroke is a faster-to-read-out indication with clear endpoints and synergy data with existing thrombolytics. If LYS241 shows Phase 1 safety and stroke Phase 2 efficacy, the PD program becomes much more attractive to potential partners — the antibody is de-risked pharmacologically even before PD-specific efficacy data exists.
