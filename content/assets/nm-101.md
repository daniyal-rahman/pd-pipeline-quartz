# NM-101 (Tomaralimab)

## Summary

NM-101 is a first-in-class humanized IgG4 anti-TLR2 monoclonal antibody from Neuramedy (Korean biotech, private), currently in Phase 1 SAD in healthy volunteers and MSA patients (NCT06934941, recruiting at Seoul National University Hospital). The molecule was originally developed as OPN-305 by Opsona Therapeutics (Dublin) for transplant rejection and MDS, then acquired by Neuramedy in 2019 and repositioned for synucleinopathies. TLR2 sits upstream of the NLRP3 inflammasome -- extracellular alpha-synuclein oligomers are endogenous TLR2 agonists that prime microglial inflammatory responses -- making this a mechanistically distinct approach from the NLRP3 inhibitors ([[selnoflast]], [[dapansutrile]], [[vtx3232]]) and JAK-STAT inhibitors ([[bhv-8000]]) already in the neuroinflammation cluster. If Phase 1 safety/PK clears and Neuramedy advances to PD efficacy trials with biomarker evidence of CNS target engagement, this validates a novel upstream node in the neuroinflammation cascade. If PK shows inadequate CSF penetration or the MSA-first strategy stalls, the PD timeline extends well beyond 2028.

## Notes

### Science
- Humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that antagonizes Toll-like receptor 2 (TLR2), blocking a key pattern recognition receptor on microglia
- **Mechanism:** Extracellular alpha-synuclein oligomers are endogenous TLR2 ligands. When alpha-synuclein binds TLR2 on microglia, it triggers NF-kB signaling and primes the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to IL-1beta/IL-18 release and sustained neuroinflammation. NM-101 blocks this priming step
- TLR2 sits **upstream** of the NLRP3 inflammasome -- this is the "Signal 1" (priming) step, while NLRP3 inhibitors like [[vtx3232]], [[selnoflast]], and [[dapansutrile]] target "Signal 2" (assembly/activation). Blocking priming rather than assembly is mechanistically distinct
- A January 2026 Nature Communications paper demonstrated that alpha-synuclein propagates from neurons to oligodendrocytes via TLR2, driving glial cytoplasmic inclusion (GCI) formation and demyelination in MSA models. Anti-TLR2 antibody administration rescued the demyelination phenotype in transgenic mice
- IgG4 subclass is a deliberate choice -- reduces Fc-mediated effector functions (ADCC, complement activation), minimizing risk of antibody-mediated neuroinflammation upon microglial target engagement
- **CNS penetration is the critical open question.** Standard mAbs achieve only 0.1-0.2% brain penetration. The Bioasis xB3 BBB-shuttle deal (2022) indicates Neuramedy is actively working on an enhanced-penetration version, but the current Phase 1 program appears to use the unmodified antibody
- Prior clinical history as OPN-305: Phase 1/2 in MDS (myelodysplastic syndromes) and delayed graft function (2012-2018) under Opsona Therapeutics, establishing a baseline human safety profile. Neuramedy acquired the asset and repositioned it for neurodegeneration in 2019
- NM-101 has FDA Orphan Drug designation (likely for MSA)

### Clinical

**NM-101 SAD (Phase 1)** | NCT05790382 | N=56 | Healthy male and female volunteers
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, PK parameters (Cmax, AUC, Tmax, t1/2, CL, Vd)
- **Key secondary:** CSF concentrations of NM-101 (Cohorts 5-7 only); anti-drug antibody development
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-part (Part 1: single ascending doses; Part 2: multiple ascending doses), 3:1 active:placebo, dose range 10-60 mg/kg IV
- **Location:** Quotient Sciences, Nottingham, UK
- **Status:** Registered May 2023; estimated completion June 2024. Status listed as "not yet recruiting" as of March 2023 -- current enrollment status unclear
- **Interpretation:** The CSF sampling in higher-dose cohorts is critical -- this is the first human data on whether unmodified tomaralimab achieves measurable CNS penetration. The dose range (10-60 mg/kg) is broad, suggesting Neuramedy is exploring whether high-dose IV can overcome the BBB barrier

**NM-101 SAD in MSA (Phase 1)** | NCT06934941 | N=36 | Healthy subjects + MSA patients
- **Primary endpoint:** PK profile (Cmax, AUC, Ctrough, Tmax, t1/2, Vd, CL)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single ascending dose escalation, triple-masked
- **Location:** Seoul National University Hospital, Seoul, South Korea
- **Start date:** November 25, 2024 (actual)
- **Primary completion:** October 30, 2025 (estimated); study completion March 30, 2026
- **Status:** Recruiting
- **Interpretation:** This is the more recent and active trial. Including MSA patients alongside healthy volunteers in Phase 1 suggests Neuramedy is pursuing MSA as the lead indication (smaller population, faster enrollment, orphan drug economics) with PD as follow-on. The Korean trial site aligns with Neuramedy's Seoul headquarters

No PD-specific efficacy trials are registered. PD indication is described in corporate materials as a follow-on to MSA.

- NM-101 targets a **distinct node** in the neuroinflammation cascade vs. every other asset in the cluster. TLR2 is the alpha-synuclein sensor that primes NF-kB and NLRP3; the NLRP3 inhibitors ([[vtx3232]], [[selnoflast]], [[dapansutrile]], [[nt-0796]], [[nt-0150]]) act downstream at inflammasome assembly; [[bhv-8000]] acts on a parallel JAK-STAT signaling pathway; GLP-1 agonists ([[exenatide]], [[lixisenatide]], [[semaglutide]]) have indirect anti-inflammatory effects
- The closest mechanistic comparison is [[muna-kv13|Muna Therapeutics' Kv1.3 blocker]] -- both target upstream microglial activation, but via entirely different mechanisms (ion channel vs. pattern recognition receptor)
- **Modality disadvantage:** NM-101 is an IV-infused mAb competing against oral small molecules ([[vtx3232]], [[bhv-8000]], [[dapansutrile]]). For a chronic neurodegenerative condition requiring years of treatment, oral dosing has a significant commercial and compliance advantage
- If NLRP3 inhibitors ([[vtx3232]], [[selnoflast]]) succeed in PD trials, it validates the inflammasome pathway but does not directly validate TLR2 -- alpha-synuclein activates NLRP3 through multiple mechanisms (TLR2 priming, mitochondrial damage, lysosomal rupture), so NLRP3 could be the better intervention point. Conversely, if NLRP3 inhibitors fail, TLR2 blockade might still work if the pathology is driven by NF-kB signaling independent of inflammasome assembly
- **MSA-first strategy** is both an advantage and a risk. MSA is a faster, more aggressive synucleinopathy with prominent oligodendroglial pathology -- the Nature Communications 2026 data showing TLR2-mediated neuron-to-oligodendrocyte alpha-synuclein spread makes MSA a biologically rational lead indication. But MSA trial success may not translate to PD (different cell types, different disease tempo, different pathological burden)

## Analysis

NM-101 is the only anti-TLR2 antibody in clinical development for any synucleinopathy, making it a genuinely first-in-class asset. The mechanistic rationale is sound: alpha-synuclein is a validated endogenous TLR2 ligand, and TLR2-mediated microglial activation is well-documented in PD neuropathology. The 2026 Nature Communications paper demonstrating that TLR2 mediates neuron-to-oligodendrocyte alpha-synuclein propagation in MSA models provides fresh preclinical validation. However, "first-in-class" in neurodegeneration has historically been a warning sign -- novel mechanisms carry higher biological risk.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of reaching PD Phase 2 with positive biomarker data: 10-15%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: anti-inflammatory mAbs in neurodegeneration have a near-zero clinical success record --> starting point ~5%
- Adjustments upward: prior human safety data from Opsona's MDS/DGF trials reduces safety derisking risk (+5%), TLR2-alpha-synuclein biology is well-published and mechanistically distinct from failed approaches (+3%), orphan drug designation provides regulatory pathway advantages (+2%), MSA-first strategy enables faster proof-of-concept (+3%)
- Adjustments downward: CNS penetration of unmodified mAb is likely insufficient for deep parenchymal target engagement (-5%), private Korean biotech with limited disclosed funding raises execution risk (-3%), no PD-specific trials registered (-2%), the neuroinflammation causality question remains unresolved for all assets in this cluster (-3%)
- Net: ~10-15%

**Signal analysis:** Neuramedy's decision to acquire an antibody originally developed for transplant rejection and reposition it for neurodegeneration is a bold bet on TLR2 biology. The Bioasis xB3 deal signals awareness of the CNS penetration problem but also raises the question of whether the unmodified antibody in current trials can generate meaningful CNS data. The MSA-first approach is strategically rational -- faster disease progression enables shorter trials, orphan economics improve the commercial case, and the 2026 Nature Communications paper provides fresh mechanistic support for TLR2 in MSA specifically. But the path from MSA Phase 1 to PD efficacy data is long: even optimistically, PD Phase 2 initiation is unlikely before 2027-2028.

For the broader neuroinflammation cluster, NM-101 is a "watch" asset rather than a near-term catalyst. Its primary value is as a mechanistic probe -- if Phase 1 CSF data shows meaningful TLR2 occupancy in the CNS, it opens up TLR2 as a druggable target for PD and strengthens the upstream-node hypothesis that complements the NLRP3 inhibitor programs. If CSF penetration is negligible, attention shifts to the xB3-enhanced version (timeline unknown) or to small molecule TLR2 inhibitors (Yungjin Pharm collaboration). In either case, the data will be informative for the broader question of whether targeting the priming step (TLR2, NF-kB) is more or less effective than targeting the effector step (NLRP3, caspase-1) in PD neuroinflammation.
