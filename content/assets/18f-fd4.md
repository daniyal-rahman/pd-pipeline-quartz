# 18F-FD4

## Summary

18F-FD4 is the most advanced alpha-synuclein-selective PET tracer approaching clinical registration, developed by SynuSight Biotech (private startup, incubated by Mabwell [688062.SH]) with XingImaging as the US manufacturing and imaging analysis partner. Investigator-initiated trials at Huashan Hospital (Shanghai) demonstrated efficient BBB penetration, high-contrast signals in pathology-relevant brain regions for PD and MSA patients, rapid washout, and low off-target binding -- characteristics that distinguish it from the only other clinically tested alpha-synuclein PET tracer, AC Immune's ACI-12589 (which showed signal primarily in MSA but not PD). MJFF awarded $3.84M in September 2025 to support US clinical registration studies. 18F-FD4 is already licensed non-exclusively to ABLi Therapeutics for use in the [[risvodetinib]] ABILITY-PD and CAMPD trials, establishing it as a pharmacodynamic biomarker for therapeutic development. If 18F-FD4 validates as a reliable in vivo measure of brain synuclein pathology in PD, it transforms trial design for every alpha-synuclein-targeting therapeutic -- [[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[ly3962681|LY3962681]], [[abl301|ABL301]] -- and becomes a critical diagnostic tool for patient selection and treatment monitoring. If it fails to show reproducible PD-specific signal, the field remains dependent on CSF seed amplification assays and skin biopsies, which are less spatially informative.

## Notes

### Science
- Structure-driven drug discovery approach: SynuSight used Cryo-EM electron diffraction and helical filament imaging to determine the molecular architecture of alpha-synuclein fibrils, then designed 18F-FD4 with a novel core structure for selective binding to pathogenic alpha-synuclein aggregates over monomeric forms
- Key imaging characteristics demonstrated in investigator-initiated trials at Huashan Hospital: efficient BBB penetration, clear and stable imaging window with rapid washout, low off-target binding (critical for PET tracer specificity), and favorable safety profile
- In PD and MSA patients, 18F-FD4 produced high-contrast signals in pathology-relevant brain regions including the substantia nigra, with robust disease-differentiation performance between PD, MSA, and healthy controls
- Selectivity for alpha-synuclein fibrils over amyloid-beta and tau fibrils (~10-fold), which is essential because PD brains frequently contain co-pathology -- a tracer that also binds tau or amyloid-beta would produce confounded signals
- Alpha-synuclein PET has been the "missing biomarker" in the PD therapeutic field. Unlike amyloid-beta PET (which transformed Alzheimer's trials), there has been no validated in vivo measure of brain synuclein burden. CSF seed amplification assays (SAA) detect alpha-synuclein aggregates but provide no spatial information; skin biopsies measure peripheral pathology. PET imaging would enable both anatomical localization and longitudinal quantification
- Key open scientific questions: (1) whether the PET signal reliably tracks with disease severity and progression rate in PD (not just cross-sectionally distinguishes PD from controls), (2) whether the signal is sensitive enough to detect changes induced by alpha-synuclein-lowering therapies within a trial timeframe, and (3) whether PD uptake patterns are sufficiently distinct from normal aging

### Clinical

**Investigator-Initiated Trials (Huashan Hospital)** | NCT TBD | N=14+ | HC, PD, and MSA patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, brain uptake, and imaging performance
- **Key secondary:** Regional brain signal differences between PD, MSA, and healthy controls
- **Status:** Completed (China)
- **Interpretation:** Proof-of-concept data showing increased uptake of 18F-FD4 in substantia nigra of PD patients vs. healthy controls; high-contrast signals in MSA pathology-relevant regions. Formed the basis for MJFF grant application and ABLi licensing

**MJFF-Funded Analysis Study (Roger Gunn, PI)** | NCT TBD | N=14 | 4 HC, 4 PD, 6 MSA
- **Primary endpoint:** Quantitative PET analysis using advanced image processing pipelines to identify signal increases in brain regions consistent with PD and MSA pathology
- **Status:** Funded September 2025; planned
- **Interpretation:** Independent Western validation of Chinese investigator-initiated trial data; led by Roger Gunn (prominent PET imaging scientist) using XingImaging's NeuroEXPLORER ultra-high resolution brain PET imager in New Haven, CT

**MJFF-Funded Clinical Evaluation Study** | NCT TBD | N=TBD | PD patients and healthy controls
- **Primary endpoint:** Clinical evaluation of 18F-FD4 as alpha-synuclein PET imaging biomarker
- **Status:** Funded 2025; planned
- **Interpretation:** If successful, would establish radiochemistry network for larger studies and support regulatory path toward clinical registration

**Integration into ABILITY-PD ([[risvodetinib]])** | NCT TBD | N=120 | Re-enrolled 201 Trial participants
- **Primary endpoint:** 12-month longitudinal biomarker assessment of brain synuclein (via 18F-FD4 PET), blood, and tissue biomarkers
- **Status:** Planned Q1 2026
- **Interpretation:** First use of 18F-FD4 as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in a therapeutic trial -- measuring whether [[risvodetinib]] reduces brain alpha-synuclein pathology over 12 months

**Integration into CAMPD ([[risvodetinib]])** | NCT TBD | N=~450-500 | Untreated early PD
- **Primary endpoint:** Phase 2b/3 efficacy trial for risvodetinib; 18F-FD4 as exploratory/secondary biomarker endpoint
- **Status:** Planned 2026
- **Interpretation:** If CAMPD shows clinical benefit AND 18F-FD4 shows brain synuclein reduction, it would provide the most mechanistically complete disease modification evidence package in PD

### Financial
- SynuSight is an incubated company of Mabwell (Shanghai, 688062.SH) -- a publicly traded Chinese biopharmaceutical company with integrated R&D, manufacturing, and commercialization capabilities
- **$3.84M MJFF grant** (September 2025) for US clinical registration and development -- significant non-dilutive funding and credibility signal from the world's largest PD research philanthropy
- ABLi Therapeutics license: upfront payment + subsequent license-related fees; non-exclusive (SynuSight retains ability to license to other therapeutic developers)
- XingImaging: separate license for US-based FD4 manufacturing and PET imaging analysis; operates NeuroEXPLORER ultra-high resolution brain PET imager in New Haven, CT
- **Revenue model is dual:** (1) enabling biomarker for therapeutic clinical trials (ABLi is first, but every alpha-synuclein-targeting trial is a potential customer), and (2) eventual diagnostic/patient selection tool for clinical practice
- **Comparable:** AC Immune's ACI-12589 alpha-synuclein PET tracer -- published in Nature Communications (Oct 2023) showing MSA differentiation but limited PD signal, which is 18F-FD4's key competitive advantage
- **Market context:** amyloid-beta PET tracers (florbetapir/Amyvid, florbetaben/Neuraceq, flutemetamol/Vizamyl) collectively generate >$500M annually in Alzheimer's; an alpha-synuclein PET tracer validated for PD would access a comparable or larger market given PD prevalence

- **AC Immune ACI-12589** is the only other alpha-synuclein PET tracer with published human data. Key limitation: ACI-12589 showed strong signal differentiation in MSA (cerebellar white matter) but limited ability to distinguish PD from healthy controls. This is likely because MSA has glial cytoplasmic inclusions (higher alpha-synuclein density per volume) while PD has neuronal Lewy bodies (more diffuse). 18F-FD4's reported ability to show PD-specific signal (substantia nigra uptake) is the critical differentiator
- Multiple academic groups have attempted alpha-synuclein PET tracers (reviewed in Pharmaceuticals 2021, EJNMMI 2025) but none besides ACI-12589 and 18F-FD4 have reached human imaging
- 18F-FD4 is a platform-enabling technology, not a competitive threat to therapeutics. Every alpha-synuclein-targeting program benefits from a validated PET biomarker: [[prasinezumab]] (could show in-brain target engagement that CSF alone cannot), [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] (could quantify brain synuclein reduction), [[risvodetinib]] (already licensed), [[abl301|ABL301]] (Sanofi deprioritized partly because they could not measure brain target engagement -- 18F-FD4 could reopen this)
- The diagnostic/biomarker layer benefits regardless of which therapeutics succeed or fail -- as long as alpha-synuclein remains a relevant PD target, the imaging tool has value

## Analysis

18F-FD4 occupies a uniquely strategic position in the PD landscape: it is an enabling technology that is underpriced relative to its impact. The entire alpha-synuclein therapeutic field -- representing billions in R&D investment across [[prasinezumab]], [[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]], [[ly3962681|LY3962681]], [[abl301|ABL301]], and others -- is bottlenecked by the inability to measure brain synuclein pathology in vivo. Sanofi explicitly deprioritized ABL301 because they could not confirm CNS target engagement. Every alpha-synuclein antibody trial relies on CSF or peripheral biomarkers as proxies for brain pathology. A validated PET tracer changes the economics of the entire field.

**Analytical estimate -- Probability of achieving clinical registration as a diagnostic PET tracer: 40-50%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: amyloid-beta PET tracers achieved FDA approval after demonstrating correlation with autopsy findings and clinical utility in ~5-7 years of development; alpha-synuclein PET is ~3-5 years behind on this timeline
- Adjustments upward: MJFF grant is a strong validation signal from domain experts (+10%); early human data from Huashan Hospital showing PD-specific signal is encouraging (+10%); dual PD+MSA applicability broadens clinical utility (+5%); AC Immune's ACI-12589 PD limitation creates a clear competitive opening (+5%); structure-driven design approach (Cryo-EM-informed) is scientifically rigorous (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: early human studies are small (N=14) and from a single center (-10%); alpha-synuclein fibrils in PD (Lewy bodies) are lower density than amyloid plaques, making PET signal-to-noise inherently harder (-10%); regulatory path for alpha-synuclein PET is undefined (no precedent) (-5%); Chinese investigator-initiated data may not replicate in Western cohorts (-5%)
- Net: ~40-50%

**Signal analysis:**
- The MJFF $3.84M grant is significant because MJFF's grant review process involves expert panels with deep domain knowledge. Their willingness to fund 18F-FD4 over competing candidates signals that the early data passed rigorous peer review. MJFF has historically been a strong signal generator for PD program quality.
- SynuSight's incubation within Mabwell (688062.SH) provides manufacturing infrastructure that standalone startups lack. Mabwell's integrated biopharmaceutical capabilities could accelerate tracer production scale-up -- critical because PET tracers require cGMP manufacturing at or near imaging sites (fluorine-18 has a 110-minute half-life).
- The ABLi non-exclusive license is strategically smart: it generates near-term revenue and clinical data while preserving SynuSight's ability to license to larger pharma partners. If 18F-FD4 performs well in ABILITY-PD and CAMPD, Roche ([[prasinezumab]] developer) and Novartis ([[aro-snca|ARO-SNCA]] partner) become natural licensees.
- The China-US dual development path (Huashan Hospital + New Haven, CT) de-risks geographic regulatory dependence and builds the multi-center dataset regulators require.

**Decision tree:**
- If 18F-FD4 validates in registration studies: transforms trial design for all alpha-synuclein programs; creates a diagnostic franchise comparable to amyloid PET ($500M+ market); SynuSight becomes an acquisition target for major PET/diagnostics companies (Siemens Healthineers, GE HealthCare, Eli Lilly/Avid) or large pharma running alpha-synuclein trials
- If 18F-FD4 fails to show reproducible PD-specific signal: the field remains dependent on CSF SAA and skin biopsy as indirect measures; AC Immune's ACI-12589 (MSA-specific) would be the only clinical alpha-synuclein PET tool; therapeutic trial design remains hampered by inability to confirm brain target engagement
