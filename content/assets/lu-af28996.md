# Lu AF28996

## Summary

Lu AF28996 is an oral D1/D2 dopamine receptor agonist from Lundbeck (big pharma, Copenhagen: HLUN-B) targeting motor fluctuations and dyskinesia in moderate-to-advanced Parkinson's disease. The compound entered the clinic in 2018, completed multiple Phase 1 studies (NCT04291859, NCT06004180, NCT06277609, NCT06337110) characterizing safety, tolerability, PK, drug-drug interactions, and ADME through 2024, and Lundbeck's H1 2025 investor materials state that Phase 1b data supports planning of a Phase 2 proof-of-concept trial. The core thesis is that Lu AF28996 solves a decades-old pharmacology problem: apomorphine is the only clinically validated D1/D2 agonist in PD but cannot be given orally, requiring subcutaneous injection or infusion pump. If Phase 2 demonstrates oral D1/D2 agonism reduces OFF time and dyskinesia without the tolerability issues (hypotension, nausea) that killed earlier oral D1 agonists like dihydrexidine, Lu AF28996 could become the oral apomorphine equivalent the field has sought since the 1990s. If it fails on tolerability or efficacy, it joins a graveyard of D1 agonist attempts, and the space shifts further toward [[tavapadon]] (D1/D5 partial agonist, NDA filed) and continuous levodopa delivery systems ([[nd0612]], VYALEV).

## Notes

### Science
- Lu AF28996 is a small molecule with agonistic properties at both dopamine D1 and D2 receptors, plus an active metabolite with D1/D2 agonistic activity — enabling concerted stimulation of the direct pathway (D1) and indirect pathway (D2) in the basal ganglia
- The D1/D2 dual agonism distinguishes it from existing oral dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine) which are D2/D3-selective and from [[tavapadon]] which is D1/D5-selective. Only apomorphine shares the D1/D2 dual profile clinically, but apomorphine has poor oral bioavailability
- Historical challenge: D1 receptor agonists have been notoriously difficult to develop as oral drugs. Dihydrexidine (the first full D1 agonist, late 1980s) showed profound antiparkinsonian effects in MPTP primates exceeding levodopa and bromocriptine, but caused dose-limiting hypotension, tachycardia, and flushing in human trials due to unfavorable pharmacokinetics
- Preclinical data: Lundbeck investor materials reference NHP (non-human primate) data showing symptom relief without troublesome dyskinesia — a critical differentiator if it translates to humans, as levodopa-induced dyskinesia is a major limitation of current dopaminergic therapy
- The D1 component is the pharmacological innovation: D1 agonism on direct-pathway medium spiny neurons may provide levodopa-equivalent efficacy (based on dihydrexidine primate data) while the D2 component provides the conventional agonist benefit. The combined profile aims to match apomorphine's efficacy with oral convenience
- Open question: whether the active metabolite's D1/D2 agonism can be controlled for an appropriate therapeutic window — the PK challenge is matching receptor occupancy kinetics to avoid the pulsatile stimulation that drives dyskinesia

### Clinical

**Phase 1 — Safety/PK/Efficacy Exploratory (NCT04291859)** | N=~63 | PD patients with motor fluctuations
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics
- **Design:** Open-label, multi-part exploratory study. Part A: ascending oral doses OD (Cohorts 1-3) for 14 days + BID cohorts (A1 for 24 days, A2 for 39 days); Part B: BID dosing for 41 days (Cohorts B1-B3), all followed by investigator-guided down-titration
- **Key inclusion:** Idiopathic PD (UK Brain Bank Criteria), Modified Hoehn & Yahr score <=4 OFF / <=3 ON, recognizable and predictable motor fluctuations with >=1.5 hours OFF time in awake time (Part A) or >=3 hours OFF time + >=1 hour ON time with troublesome dyskinesia (Part B), MDS-UPDRS Part III OFF/ON amplitude >=30%
- **Concomitant meds:** Levodopa allowed; dopamine agonists required to be washed out >=4 weeks prior
- **Status:** Listed as recruiting/active on ClinicalTrials.gov; Lundbeck investor materials state Phase 1 completed with data supporting Phase 2 planning
- **Interpretation:** The study's Part B design (41-day BID dosing in fluctuating PD patients) goes beyond standard Phase 1 safety — the exploratory efficacy endpoints on motor fluctuations and dyskinesia likely generated the signal Lundbeck references in investor presentations

**Phase 1 — Japanese PD Patients (NCT06004180)** | N=TBD | Japanese PD patients
- **Primary endpoint:** Safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics in Japanese population
- **Status:** Started September 2023; completed 2023
- **Interpretation:** Bridging study for Japanese regulatory pathway — signals intent for global development including Japan, an important PD market

**Phase 1 — DDI/Enzyme Inhibition (NCT06277609)** | Healthy participants
- **Primary endpoint:** Effects of enzyme inhibition on PK of Lu AF28996 and metabolites
- **Status:** Started February 2024; completed
- **Interpretation:** Standard drug-drug interaction study required for progression; the focus on enzyme inhibition effects on metabolites confirms the active metabolite plays a pharmacologically relevant role

**Phase 1 — ADME (NCT06337110)** | N=8 | Healthy men
- **Primary endpoint:** Absorption, metabolism, excretion of radiolabeled 14C-Lu AF28996 (single 2mg oral dose)
- **Design:** Subjects confined to clinic Day -1 through Day 7 minimum; urine and feces collected for radioactive dose recovery
- **Status:** Started April 2024; completed
- **Interpretation:** Mass balance study characterizing metabolic pathways and excretion routes — a late Phase 1 activity that typically precedes Phase 2 progression

### Financial
- Lundbeck (~24B DKK / ~$3.5B annual revenue, ~$6B market cap) is a CNS-focused big pharma headquartered in Denmark, with growing revenue from Vyepti and Rexulti (14% CER revenue growth in H1 2025)
- Lu AF28996 is internally developed — no partner, no disclosed deal terms, no external milestone payments
- Lundbeck's previous PD symptomatic bet (foliglurax, mGluR4 PAM) failed Phase 2a (AMBLED study) and was written down for EUR 100M after Lundbeck acquired Prexton Therapeutics. That write-down provides context for Lundbeck's risk tolerance in PD symptomatic programs
- Target market: Lundbeck investor materials cite 7-10 million PD patients with motor complications globally, positioned ahead of invasive treatments (DBS, pump-based delivery). The global PD treatment market was ~$5.65B in 2024, expected to reach ~$7.6B by 2030
- Competitive commercial threat from [[tavapadon]] (AbbVie, NDA filed, PDUFA H1 2026): if tavapadon is approved before Lu AF28996 reaches Phase 3, Lu AF28996 must demonstrate superiority on dyskinesia or OFF-time reduction to justify a differentiated label
- Lundbeck's 2027 strategic targets include Lu AF28996 among programs expected to mature during the planning period, suggesting Phase 2 data by 2027

- [[tavapadon]] (AbbVie, NDA filed) is the primary competitive reference — a D1/D5 partial agonist with clean Phase 3 data, but it is D1-selective (no D2 component) and targets early PD monotherapy + adjunctive settings. Lu AF28996's D1+D2 agonism aims for apomorphine-level efficacy in advanced fluctuating PD, which is a different niche
- [[mesdopetam]] (IRLAB, Phase 3) addresses dyskinesia via D3 antagonism — complementary rather than directly competitive, as mesdopetam reduces dyskinesia while Lu AF28996 aims to avoid causing it in the first place
- [[nd0612]] (NeuroDerm/AbbVie) and VYALEV (AbbVie, approved October 2024) are continuous subcutaneous levodopa delivery systems for motor fluctuations — Lu AF28996's oral route is the key advantage if efficacy is comparable
- Apomorphine (Kynmobi sublingual, discontinued 2023; APO-go/MOVAPO subcutaneous) remains the only marketed D1/D2 agonist but is limited by route of administration and side effects. Lu AF28996 is explicitly positioned as an oral alternative
- The history of D1 agonist failures (dihydrexidine, ABT-431, SKF-38393) casts a long shadow — no oral D1 agonist has successfully completed Phase 2 in PD. Lu AF28996's approach (prodrug with active metabolite, BID dosing) may address prior PK failures, but this remains unproven
- [[aav-gad]] and [[eladocagene]] represent gene therapy approaches to the same motor fluctuation population via different mechanisms (GABAergic modulation, AADC enzyme replacement) — not directly competitive on mechanism but competing for the same advanced PD patients

## Analysis

Lu AF28996 is Lundbeck's attempt to solve a pharmacology puzzle that has defeated the field for over three decades: delivering D1 dopamine receptor agonism orally. The scientific rationale is strong — apomorphine proves that D1/D2 co-agonism delivers levodopa-equivalent motor benefit, and primate data with earlier D1 agonists showed efficacy exceeding levodopa in MPTP models. The problem has always been tolerability and pharmacokinetics, not target validation. If Lu AF28996's prodrug/metabolite approach has cracked the PK challenge, Lundbeck's claim of "first-in-class oral D1/D2 agonist" would represent a genuinely novel therapy.

**Analytical estimate — Phase 2 success probability: 25-30%.** This is our assessment, not from a published source. The reasoning:
- Base rate: Oral D1 agonists have a 0% historical success rate in PD clinical trials (dihydrexidine, ABT-431 both failed on tolerability) → starting point ~15%
- Adjustments upward: NHP data showing efficacy without dyskinesia (+10%), Lundbeck's stated Phase 1b data supporting Phase 2 progression (+5%), active metabolite design potentially addressing prior PK failures (+5%), improved medicinal chemistry tools since 1990s-era D1 agonists (+5%)
- Adjustments downward: No Phase 1 results publicly disclosed — unusual for a program entering Phase 2 (-5%), foliglurax failure shows Lundbeck's PD symptomatic track record is not clean (-3%), [[tavapadon]] approval would raise the bar for commercial viability (-2%)
- Net: ~25-30%

**Signal analysis:**
- Lundbeck's continued investment through four Phase 1 studies (2020-2024) after the foliglurax write-down signals genuine internal conviction based on Phase 1 data. A company that just wrote off EUR 100M on a PD symptomatic program would not continue investing in another unless the early data was compelling.
- The absence of a partner is notable: Lundbeck has not licensed or partnered Lu AF28996 despite having partnership infrastructure (Genmab for amlenetug). This could mean (a) the data is too early for a credible out-license, (b) Lundbeck wants to retain full economics, or (c) partners passed. The compound's position in "early development" on the pipeline page favors explanation (a).
- The H1 2025 statement that "Phase 1b data supports planning of Phase 2 proof-of-concept" is the most important signal. Lundbeck is a company that killed foliglurax after Phase 2a failure and wrote it down — they have demonstrated willingness to stop PD symptomatic programs. Their choice to advance Lu AF28996 to Phase 2 means the Phase 1b data showed a tolerable therapeutic window with motor benefit signals.
- The key decision tree: if Phase 2 PoC shows oral D1/D2 agonism reducing OFF time by >=1 hour with manageable dyskinesia, this becomes a major asset worth partnering or developing through Phase 3. If tolerability limits dose escalation to sub-therapeutic levels (the historical D1 agonist failure mode), the program likely terminates and validates [[tavapadon]]'s partial agonist approach as the ceiling for oral D1-family targeting.
