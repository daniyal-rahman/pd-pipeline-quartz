# CiRA/Kyoto University iPSC Ecosystem for Parkinson's Disease




**Research Date:** January 2026

## Overview

The Center for iPS Cell Research and Application (CiRA) at Kyoto University is the epicenter of Japan's iPSC-based regenerative medicine ecosystem. Founded in 2010 and led by Nobel laureate Shinya Yamanaka, CiRA has pioneered iPSC technology for clinical application, with Parkinson's disease as a flagship therapeutic target.

## The Kyoto PD Trial: Key Details

### Trial Design

**Type:** Investigator-initiated Phase I/II
**Sponsor:** Kyoto University Hospital
**Start:** August 2018 (first surgery October 2018)
**PI:** Jun Takahashi (CiRA)
**Collaborating Departments:** Neurology, Neurosurgery, Diagnostic Imaging

**Patients:** 7 (ages 50-69)
**Design:** Single-arm, non-randomized, open-label

**Cell Source:** Allogeneic iPSC-derived dopaminergic progenitors
**Dose:** 5 million or 10 million cells per side
**Procedure:** Bilateral stereotaxic transplantation into putamen

**Key Innovation:** CORIN marker sorting for dopaminergic progenitor purification

---

### Trial Results (Nature, April 2025)

**Safety:**
- No serious adverse events
- No abnormal cell proliferation (MRI monitoring)
- No tumor formation
- 73 mild-to-moderate adverse events

**Efficacy (n=6 for evaluation):**
| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| MDS-UPDRS Part III OFF | 4/6 improved; average 9.5 points (20.4%) |
| MDS-UPDRS Part III ON | 5/6 improved; average 4.3 points (35.7%) |
| Hoehn-Yahr Stage | 4/6 improved (1 patient improved 2 stages) |
| 18F-DOPA Ki (putamen) | +44.7% average; +63.5% high-dose group |

**Key Finding:** Grafted cells survived, produced dopamine, no tumors - "suggests safety and potential clinical benefits"
