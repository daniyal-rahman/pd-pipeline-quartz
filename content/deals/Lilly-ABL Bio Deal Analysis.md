## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Lilly's $2.6B Grabody-B license is **the third mega-deal for ABL Bio's IGF1R-based BBB shuttle** (after GSK's ~$2.5B and Sanofi's $1.06B), marking Lilly's most aggressive move yet to build **the deepest, most multi-modal Parkinson's Disease franchise in pharma**.

The deal signals:
1. **Platform > Product:** Lilly is betting BBB delivery will unlock value across multiple CNS payloads (antibodies, siRNA, ASO, gene therapies)
2. **PD Portfolio Convergence:** Combining BBB shuttles (Grabody-B) + AAV capsids (Capsida) + gene therapy (PR001/GBA1) + neuroinflammation (VTX3232) + neurotrophic factors (GDNF/Medtronic) creates the **only integrated PD franchise spanning genetics, delivery, inflammation, and neuroprotection**
3. **Market Validation of BBB Bottleneck:** Three pharmas paying $6.5B+ combined for ABL Bio's platform validates that **CNS delivery—not just payload—is the critical path to disease-modifying therapies**

**Confidence Level: 8/10** that Lilly is building the most comprehensive PD portfolio, **but only 5/10** that disease-modifying PD therapies will achieve meaningful clinical efficacy before 2030.

## PHASE 1: DEEP DIVE

### 1.1 Scientific Foundation: What Payloads Will Lilly Pair with Grabody-B?

#### The Platform's Proven Versatility

**Grabody-B Mechanism:**
- **Target:** Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 Receptor (IGF1R) on brain endothelial cells
- **Process:** Receptor-mediated transcytosis (RMT) across blood-brain barrier
- **Advantage over TfR shuttles:** IGF1R is highly enriched in brain microvessels vs peripheral tissues; does NOT interfere with iron uptake (avoiding dose-limiting anemia seen with transferrin receptor approaches)
- **Cross-species validation:** Works in mice, rats, non-human primates with broad epitope reactivity
- **Payload capacity:** Successfully tested with molecules from <10 kDa to 140 kDa

**Published Evidence:**
- **Antibodies:** Anti-alpha-synuclein + Grabody-B fusion showed superior neuropathology improvement in PD mouse models vs naked antibody
- **Peptides:** Galanin (analgesic) delivery achieved dose-dependent thermal hyperalgesia suppression
- **siRNA (via GSK/ABL Bio collaboration):** Preclinical data shows CNS gene knockdown + skeletal muscle delivery
- **ASO:** GSK's deal explicitly covers antisense oligonucleotides for neurodegenerative diseases

#### Lilly's Likely PD Payload Targets

| **Payload Type** | **Target** | **Rationale** | **Probability** |
|------------------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Antibody** | Alpha-synuclein | Compete with Roche/prasinezumab, Novartis/Arrowhead; Lilly lacks α-syn asset | HIGH |
| **siRNA** | LRRK2 knockdown | LRRK2 mutations in 1-2% PD; siRNA avoids kinase inhibitor toxicity | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| **ASO** | SNCA (α-syn gene) | Direct approach to reduce α-syn production in genetic PD | MEDIUM |
| **Enzyme** | GCase augmentation | Complement PR001 (GBA1 gene therapy); enzyme replacement for non-genetic PD | MEDIUM |
| **Antibody** | Tau (4R tauopathy) | PD patients develop tau pathology; BBB shuttle enables tau targeting | MEDIUM |
| **GDNF** | Neurotrophic delivery | Replace Medtronic implantable pump with IV delivery via Grabody-B | LOW (but strategic) |

**Key Insight:** Lilly's deal grants **multi-program rights**, meaning they can pursue **3-5 parallel payloads simultaneously**—unlike Sanofi's single-program ABL301. This is a **platform play**, not a single-asset bet.

## PHASE 2: THESIS EXTRACTION & EVALUATION

### THE THESIS: Lilly's PD Franchise Strategy

**Core Hypothesis:**
> **Parkinson's Disease is heterogeneous** (genetic variants, idiopathic, inflammatory subtypes), requiring **multi-modal interventions** targeting distinct pathways. Success depends on:
> 1. **Delivery platforms** (BBB shuttles, AAV capsids) enabling CNS access for diverse payloads
> 2. **Precision medicine stratification** (GBA1-PD for gene therapy, NLRP3+ inflammation for VTX3232, α-syn+ for antibodies)
> 3. **Combination therapy potential** (e.g., VTX3232 + α-syn antibody to reduce inflammation while clearing aggregates)

**What Must Be True for This to Pay Off:**

1. ✅ **BBB delivery is the critical bottleneck:** Alpha-synuclein antibodies failed in trials (Biogen, AbbVie, UCB/Novartis) potentially due to **poor CNS penetration**—shuttles could solve this
2. ✅ **PD heterogeneity requires multiple mechanisms:** No single pathway (α-syn, LRRK2, GBA1) affects >10% of patients—diversified portfolio mitigates risk
3. ⚠️ **Platforms are reusable:** Grabody-B and Capsida must work for **5+ different payloads** to justify $3B+ investment
4. ⚠️ **Disease modification is achievable:** VTX3232 showed motor improvement in Phase 2a (n=10), but **no PD disease-modifying therapy has ever succeeded in Phase 3**
5. ❌ **Combination therapies will be necessary:** Regulatory precedent for PD combinations is weak (unlike oncology); payers may resist

---

### EVIDENCE FOR THE THESIS

#### 1. BBB Delivery Validation is Accelerating

**Supporting Data:**
- **Market validation:** $6.5B+ in BBB shuttle deals (ABL Bio's 3 licenses) + AbbVie's $1.4B Aliada acquisition (TfR/CD98hc platform) signals pharma consensus that **delivery = value**
- **Clinical precedent:** Denali's Transport Vehicle platform demonstrated oligonucleotide BBB delivery in NHPs; Roche's BrainShuttle used in clinical trials
- **Failed antibody trials:** UCB/minzasolmin, Biogen's α-syn program, AbbVie's α-syn program **all failed despite strong preclinical data**—poor CNS penetration is the suspected culprit

**Counterpoint:** No BBB shuttle has **clinical proof-of-concept yet**—Sanofi's deprioritization of ABL301 (Grabody-B + α-syn antibody) in Jan 2026 raises questions about whether shuttles achieve **sufficient CNS exposure** to drive efficacy.

#### 2. PD Heterogeneity Demands Multi-Modal Portfolios

**Supporting Data:**
- **Genetic subtypes:** GBA1 (5% of PD), LRRK2 (1-2%), PINK1 (<1%), idiopathic (90%+)—no single target addresses majority
- **Subtype stratification:** Recent research identifies 3 "PACE subtypes" (Inching, Moderate, Rapid progression) with distinct biomarker profiles—precision Rx requires multiple tools
- **Polygenic risk scores:** Emerging ability to stratify by pathway (mitochondrial, autophagy-lysosomal, inflammatory)—supports Lilly's multi-pathway approach

**Counterpoint:** Heterogeneity could also mean **no therapy works broadly**—if each mechanism only benefits 5-10% of patients, commercial viability is limited (orphan-sized markets).

#### 3. Neuroinflammation is Central to PD Pathogenesis

**Supporting Data (VTX3232 Phase 2a):**
- **Biomarker validation:** VTX3232 reduced CSF IL-1β, IL-6, and plasma hsCRP to near-normal levels, demonstrating **target engagement**
- **Motor improvement:** Statistically significant improvement across MDS-UPDRS subscales (Part III: -5.2 pts, p=0.0054; Part I: -2.4, p=0.0118; Part II: -2.7, p=0.0471) in n=10 Phase 2a trial
- **Mechanism:** NLRP3 activation in microglia drives α-synuclein-induced neuroinflammation; inhibition breaks the **α-syn/inflammation feedback loop**

**Counterpoint:** Phase 2a was **open-label, n=10**—placebo effect cannot be ruled out. Phase 2b placebo-controlled trial will determine real efficacy (results pending).

#### 4. Integrated Platform Value

**Lilly's Unique Capability:**
- **3 delivery platforms:** Capsida (AAV capsids for one-time gene delivery), Grabody-B (BBB shuttle for chronic protein Rx), Medtronic (device for focal neurotrophic factor delivery)
- **Modality flexibility:** Can deliver antibodies, siRNA, ASO, enzymes, gene therapies—**platform-agnostic payload selection**
- **Muscle/obesity expansion:** ABL Bio data shows Grabody-B enables siRNA delivery to **skeletal muscle**, opening non-CNS applications (obesity, muscle wasting)—Lilly's GLP-1 franchise synergies

**Counterpoint:** Platform complexity = execution risk. Lilly must advance **5+ parallel programs** through clinical development—high probability of failures across portfolio.

---

### EVIDENCE AGAINST THE THESIS

#### 1. Disease-Modifying PD Therapies Have 100% Failure Rate

**Failed Programs (2020-2025):**
- ❌ **Alpha-synuclein antibodies:** UCB/minzasolmin (failed Phase 2), Biogen/BIIB054 (discontinued), AbbVie (terminated pre-Phase 2)
- ❌ **LRRK2 inhibitors:** Denali/BIIB122 in Phase 2 (results pending), but UCB partnership dissolved
- ❌ **GCase modulators:** Sanofi/venglustat (failed Phase 2), Gain/GBA-ambroxol (Phase 3 ongoing but slow enrollment)

**Why They Failed:**
- **Insufficient CNS exposure:** Antibodies don't cross BBB; small molecules have poor brain penetration
- **Wrong biomarkers:** Alpha-synuclein imaging/CSF levels don't correlate with clinical benefit
- **Heterogeneity:** Trials enroll mixed populations (genetic + idiopathic)—dilutes signal
- **Too late intervention:** Neurodegeneration is irreversible by symptomatic stage

**Implication for Lilly:** Even with BBB shuttles, **target biology may be wrong**—clearing α-synuclein or reducing inflammation may not stop motor decline.

#### 2. Platform Risk: Grabody-B is Unproven in Humans

**Key Unknowns:**
- **Sanofi deprioritization:** ABL301 (α-syn antibody + Grabody-B) was deprioritized in Jan 2026 before entering Phase 2—reason stated was "need better α-syn measurement tools," but could signal **efficacy concerns**
- **Dose requirements:** What dose is needed to achieve therapeutic CNS levels? TfR shuttles required **high doses** leading to peripheral toxicity
- **Immunogenicity:** Repeated dosing of Grabody-B fusion proteins could trigger anti-drug antibodies, reducing efficacy
- **Payload-dependent performance:** Grabody-B works in mice with α-syn antibodies, but will it work with siRNA? ASO? Enzymes?

**Risk Level: HIGH**—No Phase 1 safety/PK data; Sanofi's deprioritization is a **negative signal**.

#### 3. Execution Complexity: Lilly is Spreading Too Thin

**Current PD Portfolio Status:**
- **PR001 (GBA1 gene Rx):** Phase 1/2 PROPEL trial—slow enrollment, results not yet reported
- **VTX3232 (NLRP3):** Positive Phase 2a (n=10), but Phase 2b placebo-controlled trial needed (timeline unknown)
- **Grabody-B:** Discovery-stage; 3-5 parallel programs to initiate
- **GDNF/Medtronic:** Preclinical device optimization
- **Capsida AAV capsids:** Discovery platform—no PD-specific program disclosed

**Resource Allocation Risk:**
- Lilly's **neuroscience unit was restructured in 2023** to prioritize Alzheimer's (donanemab/Kisunla approved 2024, remternetug in development)
- PD portfolio is **secondary priority** vs Alzheimer's—may face budget constraints if Alzheimer's programs expand
- **Portfolio cannibalization:** If Grabody-B + α-syn antibody works, does PR001 (GBA1 gene therapy) become redundant? Or do they compete for the same patient pool?

## PHASE 3: ADVERSARIAL CHALLENGE (3 Cycles)

### CYCLE 1 — ATTACK: Unfocused Spending or Strategic Hedging?

**The Bear Case:**

**Argument:** Lilly is **over-diversifying** in PD, spreading $3B+ across 5+ mechanisms without a clear lead asset. This looks like **unfocused R&D spending** driven by FOMO (fear of missing out), not a coherent strategy.

**Evidence:**
1. **No clinical validation:** VTX3232 is the only asset with Phase 2 data (n=10, open-label)—the rest are Phase 1 or preclinical
2. **Platform over product:** $2.6B for Grabody-B is a **platform bet** without knowing what payload will work—Sanofi's deprioritization suggests first payload (α-syn antibody) may have failed
3. **AbbVie's focused approach is winning:** AbbVie has tavapadon (NDA filed) + PINK1 (mitochondrial disease modification)—**2 shots on goal** vs Lilly's **5 moonshots**
4. **What if DMTs don't work?** If disease modification is impossible (all trials failed), Lilly wasted $3B while AbbVie captured symptomatic market with tavapadon

**Provocation:** Is Lilly building a PD franchise or a **failed R&D graveyard**?

---

### CYCLE 2 — DEFEND: Why Multi-Modal Beats Monotherapy

**The Bull Case:**

**Rebuttal:** Lilly's multi-modal portfolio is **strategic hedging against PD heterogeneity and pathway uncertainty**—it's the ONLY way to derisk a disease with 0% DMT success rate.

**Evidence:**
1. **Heterogeneity requires optionality:** PD is **not one disease**—it's a syndrome with genetic (GBA1, LRRK2), inflammatory (NLRP3+), and idiopathic subtypes. A single-mechanism bet (like AbbVie's PINK1) has **90% probability of failing** if mitochondrial dysfunction isn't the primary driver
2. **Platform reusability reduces cost:** Grabody-B is a **multi-payload platform**—once validated in humans (Phase 1 safety/PK), Lilly can test **5+ different payloads** (α-syn antibody, LRRK2 siRNA, GCase enzyme) for ~$50M each vs $500M+ for de novo programs
3. **Combination therapy upside:** If neuroinflammation (VTX3232) + α-syn clearance (Grabody-B antibody) work synergistically, Lilly captures **the entire disease-modifying market**—not just a 5% genetic subset
4. **AbbVie's tavapadon is symptomatic, not curative:** D1/D5 agonists are **another levodopa**—they improve motor function but don't stop neurodegeneration. Lilly is betting the **$7B PD market** will shift to DMTs by 2030-2033

**Provocation:** AbbVie is selling **better symptom relief**; Lilly is selling **a cure**—which would you rather own in 2035?

---

### CYCLE 3 — RESOLVE: Balanced Assessment

**The Nuanced View:**

**What Lilly Gets Right:**
1. ✅ **BBB delivery is the bottleneck:** All α-syn antibodies failed—shuttles are the logical next step
2. ✅ **Platform thinking:** Capsida + Grabody-B create **option value** to test multiple payloads without $1B+ acquisitions per mechanism
3. ✅ **Neuroinflammation is underexplored:** NLRP3 pathway is mechanistically sound (α-syn activates inflammasome → IL-1β → microglial damage → dopaminergic neuron death)—VTX3232's positive Phase 2a biomarkers are encouraging

**What Lilly Gets Wrong:**
1. ❌ **Execution risk is HIGH:** Advancing 5+ parallel programs strains resources—Lilly's neuroscience unit already prioritizes Alzheimer's
2. ❌ **Clinical timelines are LONG:** PR001 started Phase 1 in 2020, still no results. Grabody-B won't reach Phase 2 until 2027 at earliest—**10+ years to commercial launch**
3. ❌ **Sanofi's deprioritization is a RED FLAG:** ABL301 (Grabody-B + α-syn antibody) was the **first clinical test** of the platform—if Sanofi deprioritized due to **poor PK or efficacy signals**, Lilly's $2.6B bet is at risk

**Probabilistic Outcomes:**

| **Scenario** | **Probability** | **Outcome** |
|--------------|----------------|------------|
| **Best Case:** VTX3232 + Grabody-B α-syn antibody combo shows disease modification in Phase 2/3 | 15% | Lilly dominates DMT market; $5B+ peak sales |
| **Base Case:** 1-2 programs succeed (e.g., VTX3232 monotherapy OR GBA1 gene therapy), others fail | 50% | Lilly captures niche markets ($500M-$1B peak sales); portfolio justifies investment |
| **Bear Case:** All programs fail Phase 2/3; BBB shuttles don't achieve sufficient CNS exposure | 35% | $3B+ write-off; Lilly exits PD development |

**Verdict:** Lilly's strategy is **rational but risky**—the multi-modal approach is the RIGHT bet given PD heterogeneity, but **execution and clinical validation are the gating factors**. Confidence in strategy: 8/10. Confidence in execution: 5/10.
