**Action:** Monitor tech transfer from these institutions. Licensing deals from Broad/Stanford/UCSF in neuro = high-conviction sources.

**6. Stealthy Flagship ProtoCos**

Flagship's model: incubate in-house (ProtoCos) → spin out with funding. [Roughly 8-10 ProtoCos/year](https://www.pharmavoice.com/news/Flagship-Pioneering-failures-successes-Pfizer-Pioneering-initiative/691219/).

Vesalius was founded 2019, unveiled 2022. **There are likely 2-3 stealth neuro ProtoCos in Flagship Labs today.**

**Action:** Cultivate Flagship relationships. Their ProtoCos are pre-Series A opportunities not visible on Pitchbook.

---

### What NOT to Chase

**1. "Me-Too" LRRK2/GBA1 Programs**

Unless you have:
- Novel patient stratification (e.g., LRRK2 + polygenic risk score)
- Differentiated modality (e.g., brain-penetrant ASO vs. small molecule)
- Compelling preclinical data (e.g., reversal in human iPSC + primate PK)

...you're replicating Denali, Prevail, etc. **Low differentiation = low exit multiples.**

**2. AI Platforms Without Wet-Lab Validation**

Many "AI drug discovery" companies are **software companies cosplaying as biotech**. If the team is 80% engineers, 20% biologists, **run**.

Vesalius has iPSC labs, genetics teams, med chem. The AI is a **tool**, not the product.

**3. Undifferentiated iPSC Modeling Shops**

Lots of CROs offer "PD patient-derived iPSC neurons for your screen." That's a service, not a company.

Vesalius differentiates by **integrating iPSC with genetics, AI, and patient stratification**. The iPSCs aren't the moat; the **integrated workflow** is.

**4. Companies Betting on "Earlier Treatment" of Known Targets**

Some argue: "α-syn antibodies failed because we treated too late; let's treat prodromal PD."

Problem: **prodromal PD trials take 10+ years** (slow progression). Regulatory/reimbursement nightmares. Avoid unless the company has:
- Breakthrough Therapy designation  
- NIH/MJFF co-funding
- Validated prodromal biomarker (REM sleep behavior disorder + DAT scan)

---

### Summary: The GSK/Vesalius Deal as Market Bellwether

**This deal is a **5-alarm fire signal** that traditional PD drug development is broken.**

When a top-10 pharma pays $80M upfront for an undisclosed preclinical target from a 5-year-old biotech with zero clinical assets, it means:

1. **Known targets are tapped out** (at least for unselected populations)
2. **Platforms are worth more than assets** (because assets targeting known biology are replicable)
3. **Precision medicine is mandatory**, not optional (heterogeneity is acknowledged as the core problem)
4. **Sourcing window is NOW** for genetics-first, platform-driven neuro companies (before the field gets crowded)

**For VCs/BD:**
- **Thesis:** Invest in platforms that define PD subtypes + discover subtype-specific targets
- **Stage:** Series A/B (platforms need 3-5 years to generate data; early entry = best terms)
- **Risk:** High (14% failure rate even at Flagship), but **option value** (multi-target potential) justifies it
- **Comp:** Vesalius $75M initial, likely $200-300M total raised → $650M partnership. **~2-3x platform value creation in 5 years**, assuming clinical validation

**The meta-signal:** Pharma has **$5B+ in biobucks** allocated to PD target discovery platforms in the last 18 months. The capital is there. The question is: **who builds the next Vesalius/Valo/Denali before the window closes?**
